Quote: TheWigginator, 2008-02-29T19:35:58Z
How does it work? I assume you both play the map at the same time, so:

Correct - all players play the map at the same time.

Quote: TheWigginator, 2008-02-29T19:35:58Z
Are there two starts and two finishes? Is it for only two players, or can you have more?  Can you play multiplayer on just one computer?

The map editor provides the ability to place individual start locations for each player (and you can have up to 4), so it's possible to make a map where all four players are in their own sections. Multiple exits can be placed as well, but most maps I've seen only have one exit.

Multiplayer is either online or local (or system link, but I don't know anyone who is able to arrange that). Most of my experience has been online.

There are three kinds of multiplayer modes you can play: co-op, race, and survival. I'll talk briefly about the latter two before going into details about co-op.

RACE: Race maps are like normal maps, except there is now a 'race timer' at the bottom, and you respawn at your start location after a few seconds if you die en-route to the exit (which is quite common, as I've seen). The first player to the exit wins the race and the next race level commences (or it's a draw if nobody makes it to the end by the time the timer runs out). Getting gold has no effect in this mode.


SURVIVAL: Survival maps give each player their own individual timer of 60 seconds. These levels have no exit. Getting killed reduces the time on your timer (by 5, maybe 10 seconds) and you respawn a short while afterwards. Collecting gold increases your timer, and gold on these maps respawns after a short time, so I suspect that a poorly designed survival level might last forever, but the default maps don't have that problem. If your timer reaches 0, then you are eliminated from the game. Last player with time left is the 'winner' and the next level commences.



Having done both modes, I'd like to say that regular co-op is still my favorite, though these competitive games are a fun break. All the stuff I talk about after this point applies only to regular co-op mode.

For regular co-op, you have the option to play co-op specific maps or the singleplayer maps. Both are great fun to do, and while the single player maps can be completed by a single person, using cooperative techniques can make the maps much easier. Some of the co-op maps are devilishly hard and will required at least a pair of reasonably good individuals working together to complete. Many of the co-op maps require at least two people to be alive to make it past the level (the door switch being in one section and the door in the other is very common).

For start locations, in singleplayer all the ninjas start in the same spot. For co-op maps starting locations are slightly dependant on whether you're playing local multiplayer or online - I haven't confirmed this, but I have heard that local co-op games will start two players in one section and two players in the other if you are playing with four people. For online matches, the host starts in one location and all the other players start in the other start location - so that if other players leave, the host and remaining players have all the bases covered. No co-op map I've seen so far requires more than two people to complete (and some can be done by one), but you could definitely design user levels that do.

User levels can of course be played co-op, but I have heard that you must invite friends to the match instead of hosting a public match if you are hosting your own user levels online.

Quote: TheWigginator, 2008-02-29T19:35:58Z
Do you have seperate timers?

The timer is a single communal timer - anyone that gets gold may add to it, though in the 10 coop episodes time usually isn't a big factor.

Quote: TheWigginator, 2008-02-29T19:35:58Z
Are you racing to get to the end first?

Your objective is to get just one Ninja to an exit - the level is completed as soon as any player reaches an open exit. The level ends at that point and you all proceed to the next one. This of course does not stop people from trying to get there first.

Quote: TheWigginator, 2008-02-29T19:35:58Z
What happens if one player dies?

If a player dies during a co-op map, they are dead until the level is completed or everyone else dies or the timer runs out. When your three comrades are good and deceased, this is your chance to be a hero and win their acclaims as they witness your prowess!... or you die horribly as everyone watches and laughs.

Many co-op maps are impossible to complete with only one person left (or if the host dies in his section, it's impossible for the three remaining people to complete it). This necessarily means that the other Ninjas must kill themselves and you restart the level.

Quote: TheWigginator, 2008-02-29T19:35:58Z
What happens if the two players collide with each other?

Players cannot collide, but you can interact with them indirectly. If you're feeling malicious you can guide a rocket into someone or disturb the bounce-block they're standing on. Co-op with turrets means being aware of someone else activating a turret or floorguard that may hit you - More than once I've run past a gauss turret with a friend trailing me, only to see the turret barely miss me and hit him instead.

Quote: TheWigginator, 2008-02-29T19:35:58Z
What happens if a homing zap drone sees two players in opposite directions at the same time?

Enemy behavior that I've seen so far tends to be that when given a choice of two targets, the enemy will attack the closer one. This is certainly true of all the targeting turrets - machineguns, gauss, laser, and rocket turrets will all target the closest ninja that is within their line of sight.

Floor guards will dart at whatever ninja they can see, but will go in the opposite direction if they are at the limits of their movement. Two co-op levels use this principle, laid out as such:

N-Ninja
s-Switch
G-Floorguard
____________________________
_N_ ___s____G____s___ _N_

This floorguard will move constantly, going after one Ninja and then the other, enabling the Ninja on the far side to jump in and quickly hit the switch while getting out alive.

Some other stuff....
The next level doesn't start until everyone hits 'A' to get ready. Nobody can pause the game, but hitting 'start' brings up the game menu, so you want to do that when dead or safe. People can only join a game when it is starting - people cannot join mid-game, but they can leave and you can continue so long as at least two people are still playing. If the host leaves everyone else drops. Voice chat works quite well and coordination of your actions is very helpful. Everyone can set the color of their ninja when the game starts, so you can be quite distinctive. I prefer being black or white.

The most amusing co-op level is also one of the most difficult. While in most levels you can suffer a death and move on, there's this one in the later sections that consists of a chain of drones moving around a small room. In the center of the room are several mines and bounce-blocks. The (up to) three non-host players start in this room, which has the level door. If any one of the three die, their body will hit the mines and drones and be blown to bits that fly around the room. This bits send the bounce blocks moving like crazy, which practically always results in the swift death of the other two people in the room. Insidious and hilarious to watch. Everyone in the room must survive long enough for the guy on the outside to open the door, which involves bounce-blocks and a rocket. I've only ever completed this level with a full four people once - while most coops are easier with more people, this one is easier with less.


Useful techniques:

Here's some of the things you can do in co-op that I've found helpful:

1. Distraction. Every turret will go for the closest target, and so you can keep a turret occupied by getting close and dodging it. This most often occurs where one player ties down a rocket in one place, preventing the turret from firing and letting the other players move safely.

2. Suicide bombing. Mines go away when you or your body parts hit them. A suicide (X button) will blow up your body and send the pieces flying which can clear a lot of mines and pave the way to an easier level completion for the other players. One co-op level necessitates this, and for several other levels doing this makes those levels easier.

3. Shared load. In a level with multiple switches, or a level where you normally have to go 'back' to reach the door, one person can stay by the door while the others run off to hit the switch, making the level easier and faster (most common on the singleplayer maps.) In addition, if people are handling the level itself just fine, you can remain busy by collecting hard-to-reach gold.

4. Learning. Watching other people's methods of doing a level can really help you out later.

5. Bitching. Being able to complain about how hard a level is can be theraputic.




Bad things:

A couple complaints about multiplayer. Overall I love it way more than singleplayer due to the fun, but these are things that could improve it or people might have issues with. Overall they're really pretty minor.

First is the lag. There is definite lag with most online games and this causes two things:

One is death - lag can very occasionally cause you to not see where your ninja is (you 'warp') and miss a jump or something. This doesn't happen very often, and the vast majority of my deaths are still due to mistakes, but once in awhile if I try to jump off a wall and die because I wasn't actually on the wall, then I died due to lag. Occasionally a missile will warp around at high speed and catch you unexpectedly. I've heard that playing zoomed in will reduce your lag, and while I think that seems true I can't confirm that.

The other is percieved death. Often you will see your online friends walk into a mine that explodes or get zapped by a drone and suddenly emerge intact. This is due to lag on your screen making your game think they hit the mine when on their screen they did not. This is confusing to many new players, but something you learn to get used to. Mines that weren't really hit will remain there and the 'apparent' death is corrected very quickly. And watching the host navigate a minefield is made all the more harrowing by the apparent explosions.

Apparent death doesn't happen to you much - but at one point I do recall getting 'zapped', warping, and not being dead, so it's possible.


Second, the 'Hit A to ready up' has no countdown timer or way for the other players to force the game to start, so if someone doesn't hit 'A' you're stuck between levels. Most people are very good about not leaving the game or telling you if they are taking a break, so it's not too big an issue, but I've dropped from one or two games that were stuck on the 'waiting for players' screen.

Finally, the fact that nobody can join a co-op game in progress is something I'd love to see changed. People often drop from co-ops after awhile, and it would be nice to simply invite a friend and keep trying from the level you were on. As it is, to get more players you have to start a new game.

Ok, I'm here to give some advice with level 42-4. I beat it a couple days ago and I'm a pretty good N player, and I died a whole lot. I haven't finished the game yet (taking my time with the last episodes), but I do think that this is definitely the hardest level so far (but still not as hard as 88-4 in the PC version).


The key is planning - you need to find a 'safe' route and 'safe' methods for hitting each switch. By 'safe' I mean 'reliably survivable' - your routes will all involve rockets chasing you, and possibly mines and floorguards.


The first switch is relatively easy and you've probably gotten at least that far in your attempts. My 'safe' route to get to it is to jump up at the start and fly in an arc over the top middle mines.

Come to think of it, this will be easier with diagrams. I'm reconstructing the level from memory here, so forgive me if I slip up. These rough drawings should give you a decent idea of the routes I used.

1-5 are the switches you have to hit in order. D is the door, light blue things are floorguards, dark blue things are rocket turrets. Anyone who has glimpsed N knows what the small red circles are.


Once you arrive at switch 1, you'll have a couple rockets following you. Jump at the right time to shake them off into the wall, then head for switch 2.

I used an arc similar to the one I used to get to switch 1 to go back, with an alteration at the end to get to the high switch. If you get your jump off the slope just right, you can nail switch 2 with plenty of time to get out. If you don't nail the jump (which is likely, I only nailed it a few times), you can still get switch 2 but you absolutely need to not be greedy - those rockets will follow you into the hole the switch is in unless you shake them off first, and you will die. In any event, you will most likely only hit the switch and escape from an oncoming rocket by the skin of your teeth - if you escape at all.


Switch 3 is insidiously placed and an easy place to die if you don't shake the rockets first. Jump down to the bottom section (you could also go straight down from switch 2 and traverse the floor), avoiding floorguards and mines until you get to the pink circle. Hang out there (dodging floorguards) until you can shake any rockets following you, and you'll be in a spot where the rockets won't fire. Only then should you attempt to jump up, hit switch 3, and get out of that spot before a rocket hits you.

Traverse the very exciting floor and hang out in the pink circle area again until the rockets go away. Hit switch 4 the same way you hit switch 3.

For switch 5, I move back up to the door and do the exact same thing (an arc) that I did to get from switch 1 to switch 2.

To get back to the door, I dropped down to switch 1 and did the same sort of arc that got me from the door to switch 1 in the first place.




Now that you have these advised 'safe' routes, you're still going to die, a lot. It took me many, many lives to discover these routes and many more to execute them all properly, but I managed in the end. Practice and persistence will see you through. If you find out that one of these paths isn't working out, then try a path of your own - you can practice each path without going through the whole level by going to one switch and following the path to the next. That's how I figured out how to safely get switches 3 and 4 - I just dropped right down to switch 4 several times and discovered the pink circle area method of getting them.

Fortunately you don't need to be absolutely perfect in this level - some mistakes you can make are possible to recover from if the rockets don't get you. I fell down to the door on my successful run when I tried to go from switch 1-2, but recovered and still got the switch a bit later.


I have yet to play a harder level in N+, but I've only recently finished episode 43. There's several more levels that could potentially be harder, but we'll find out.


Go ahead and add me as a friend and we could attack this level in co-op sometime if you still have trouble with it - GT is Comassion. The great thing about N+ is that we can give people demonstrations in person, so to speak.

The floorguard would pick the first ninja to chase based on these criteria, in order:

1. Whichever one it saw first.

2. If it saw them at the same time (was revealed by doors or starting the level there, perhaps), it would chase the closer one.

3. If it saw them at the same time and distance, it would chase in player order - so it would chase second player before the third player.


With one caveat for floorgoards - it will not attempt to chase ninjas beyond the extent of it's movement, so a ninja cannot 'hold' a floorguard in place by being close to it - it will promptly move in the other direction if it is already at one extent of its movement. It will always move the full extent of it's movement once it starts moving, and will only 'see' ninjas that are in a direction it can move (causing it to move back and forth if two ninjas are on either side of it).


As for the level editor, I didn't mess around much with the level editor in N, but the N+ editor allows you to move, copy, or delete entire sections of your level, including both tiles and objects. I have found the copy function very useful in numerous levels that I've made.
