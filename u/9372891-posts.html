First off, thanks a lot to the developers of this lovely game called N. Thanks not only for creating this fun little game, but also for putting up these extremely nice tutorials about the tile-based collision detection.
Now let me get to the point. I'm in the middle of creating my own little N clone using C++ and OpenGL. I was able to get everything described in the tutorials working quite nicely and in general the collision detection and response works really well.
However there are a couple of nasty "special cases" where the system fails. This happens only about 0.01% of the time playing but as you might see it is kinda annoying, and it's bad for my ego. I'd really like to have a smooth working system at any time.

Let me get a little more precise now. Instead of trying to describe the problems by hand i'll just give drawings of 2 of such cases:



My main problem is the following. As soon as a collision occurs i set the velocity that's pointing in direction of the projection vector to 0. This means for example if I'm falling onto a regular box tile from the top that the vertical velocity will be set to 0. This is needed so the player doesn't keep accelerating into the tile.

Now when looking at figure A, which is a situation that can happen when the player (red bounding box) is running along 2 regular tiles, in some cases it might happen that the bounding box ends up in this position before the collision detection. Now the player will be projected vertically up by box 1 and his vertical speed will be set to 0. This is all fine and just how it should be. But, the collision detection will also find that box 2 is projecting the player to the right to move him out of his right side because it's the shortest way. Additionally it will set his horicontal speed to 0.
And that is the part where all of a sudden the player will be starting to move choppy for the blink of an eye.
A similar thing occurs with figure B, but I'm not gonna get into details with this as it's pretty similar.

My main question would be if there was a clean way to fix this issue. How should the position and the velocity of the player behave when colliding against 2 or more tiles to get around these issues?
Among the things I've tried was calculating a summed projection vector (like the sum of the projection vectors from box1 and 2) and cancelling the velocity along that vector. Another version was to project the player immediately after collision with tile 1 and then calculating the projection vector of box 2 with this updated position and so on and so on.
As soon as I fixed one special case with one of those methods another similar problem would occur.
So before wasting any more time I was wondering if anybody would have a good solution to treat these cases. Does a general solution even exist or do I have to catch all of those special cases with a set of if clauses all by one and react to them separately?

@Raigan:

Quote: Raigan, 2006-08-07T18:40:14Z
to start with, you absolutely should always be updating the object position after collision with each tile. this is how this sort of method works.. you're finding the global solution (move object out of all tiles) by interatively finding local solutions (move object out of tile X).. the assumption being that finding the global solution all at once is hard/impossible, and that repeatedly applying local solutions will converge to the global solution.

now i see where the whole algorithm comes from. thanks for that input.

Quote: Raigan, 2006-08-07T18:40:14Z
the _real_ solution is to switch from a convex-chunk-based method (i.e a set of tiles that describe the area of the world) to a surface-based method (i.e a set of linesegs that describe the surface of the world)..

yeah, that's what I was thinking when i ran into my problems. In fact, when looking at slide nr. 76 of your "FITC_beyondHittest07.swf" I got a little confused and was thinking that maybe you have been using line-segs already in N.

Quote: Raigan, 2006-08-07T18:40:14Z
i'd love to know how other people handle this, because all the literature says to handle concave shapes by splitting them into several convex chunks, and colliding vs each chunk individually (which is what tile-based collision does).. but noone ever mentions your problem. but they MUST be solving it somehow..

The main problem i see with the tiles are mainly the "cracks" between 2 coplanar surfaces. When starting out with a concave tile you probably wouldn't start a split in the middle of a straight ramp. Instead you'd prolly split at some corner where 2 edges with different angles meet. And at such a spot you'd probably not notice a little glitch in the colission detection as easy as a glitch that occurs when running along the middle of a straight surface.

Quote: Raigan, 2006-08-07T18:40:14Z
one idea that's just occurred to me is this: if you look at both your cases, it is the case that the solution you desire is also the single projection which moves the shape out of collision with BOTH shapes at once. for A that's vertical projection out of tile1, and for B it's projection out of either of the tiles' diagonal faces.
