Depressed for 7 years? I have a difficult time believing that you've not experienced any significant periods of joy in the last seven years of your life. Are you sure that this experience of sadness isn't linked with a specific event or series of events in your life?

Before you contemplate seeing a therapist or psychiatrist (who may misdiagnose you), ask yourself:
  • What is the antecedent? What has caused this depression? If you can find a reasonable and recent cause for your sadness, it probably is not a disorder.
  • How is this affecting your behavior? Are you acting differently than before? Recall how you were before you became depressed. Is your current state having a large effect on the way you act?
  • Are you experiencing related bodily symptoms, like a change in sleep or apetitie?
Don't be quick to assume you have a case of major clinical depression. Unless you're properly diagnosed, you're situation will only worsen. In fact, assuming you have some kind of depression may be a self-fulfilling prophecy that leads to an undue amount of strain applied to your life in the way of "stabilizing the mood disorder" (that you may not have).

First off, I will state that although it may feel that your depression has lasted a long time, it may have been no longer than a month or two. Think back two years ago. Can you remember being happy? Clear your mind and be honest with yourself. When was the last time you were happy? Be honest. Are you experience a state of emotion that directly corresponds to an event in your life? If so, you can likely overcome your sadness or grief without medicine or therapeutic intervention--which may end up doing more harm than good. Are you in a different mood right now? Can you remember being in energetic or happy moods recently?

If you can trace back to a point more than two weeks ago in which you experienced no sustained amount of enjoyment through the course of the day and have been unable to find pleasure in the meantime, it may be time to seek help. I advise you not to rush it, though. Find ways to enjoy yourself. Seek out a group in your school that may cater to your interests. Expose yourself to others. Don't worry about "making friends". Just talk to people more often. Try to be assertive. In essence, see if you can find a way to get over it. I don't mean that to poke fun at the gravity of your situation. That's just the easiest way to say it. We all have ways to get over things. Reading (or writing) books, playing (or making up) games, watching TV, et cetera. You have the ability to cope without external help. You just need to focus your effort in restoring your way of thinking and behaving to the way it was before. Remember what things made you happy and focus on those events. Whatever caused you depression will likely become less severe and will just become another neutral aspect of your past.

If you do not have the ability to cope with your current stresses on your own, you should seek out social coping mechanisms, that is, ways that others can assist you in your ability to cope. Find an adult or a group of people whom you trust and can share your feelings with. See the school counselor or someone whose job it is to help you. Seek out a therapist.
