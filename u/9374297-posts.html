You can't use mathematics with infinity. A graph is the only way to work TRULY infinite #s; everything else rounds off. Now that I realize, you're right about the graph needing to be concave But there's still a gap nonetheless.

If you want a verbal explanation, here's a brilliant one:

Quote
There's no way to make use of an infinitely accurate infinitely repeating number, for several reasons.
First, there's no way to BE infinitely accurate.
Second, there's no way to handle infinite numbers, except by placeholders.
Third, it's totally useless to have actually infinite numbers, and use them as such, for the above reasons.

So a compromise is reached by rounding the numbers off at a sufficiently high digit. This way, normal algebra rules can still apply, and a useful number can be found.

[...] while that one [here, nevermore's post] seems to favor being proof, you can never actually complete the [math]. So, what's the square root of pi? It's impossible to determine, so we approximate to whatever decimal point of accuracy we need.

Besides. For all the limit-at-infinity (or at zero resulting in infinity), you're using a different set of rules than standard algebra. And any decent math teacher will tell you that it isn't actually real numbers you're finding, you're just finding what they APPROACH WITHOUT EVER REACHING. So .333... isn't actually equal to 1/3, it's just infinitely close (which, for basically all forms of math, is close enough to say it is the same). Due to that, by saying 1/3 = .333..., you're assuming what you're trying to prove is correct, which is a fundamental flaw in trying to prove something like this.
The guy in your quote is talking about applied math. This is the kind of math you'd find in physics or in any lab setting. If you're doing an experiment, calculating pressure in a water tower, designing a bridge, manufacturing machine parts, etc., you don't want infinite precision. You don't need it, and you can't use it. For simplicity's sake, you round off to a significant number of figures and work with that.

In pure math, you can use infinite precision. If you couldn't, we wouldn't have concepts like pi. We wouldn't have limits and asymptotes. Infinite precision is what allows us to say that a curve approaches its limit without ever touching it. Otherwise, you'd have to say, "Okay, after 50 miles, these lines converge," or "After 50 miles, these lines become parallel." In applied math, you can do that - in fact, you'd probably do it a lot sooner than 50 miles - but in pure math, you can't just change a line because you're tired of handling its quirks.

0.999... = 1 is an axiom. It's just a concept that you accept and add to your available math tools. There are special cases where the difference is important. For example, 1 / (1 - 0.999...) is solvable. It calculates to be infinity, actually. 1 / (1 - 1) is unsolvable because you have divide by 0. But besides these special cases, you can usually take 0.999... to be the same as 1.

(Also, I don't know what you mean about using graphs for truly infinite numbers. Graphs are finite by definition - if for no other reason than available space in the universe. i.e. You can never draw a graph of infinity because you would fill up the universe trying.)

Actually, when you divide something by 0 it effectively works out as infinity anyways, so even there the difference is indistinguishable.
