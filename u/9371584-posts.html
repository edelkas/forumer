Well after my latest map got sniped, I went to bash and trash to vent some pent up frustration. I read the comments and looked at the different view points and I just want to summarize some of them before I get to my point:

1. Ratings don't matter, will never matter, and have never mattered (Nevermore)

2. Ratings used to matter and were a good representation of skill. They are now corrupted. Numa has lost its purpose as a breeding ground for talent and map making improvement. (Atramentis)

After considering both of these highly acclaimed authors opinions, it hit me: a new version of NUMA, laid out in the same format, but regulated by a mod, much like "bitesize" is. Users email a moderator to become part of this new database and are judged on their contributions in the original NUMA. If they are deemed worthy of admittance to the group, they will be given the right to a user name and can post all of their maps on the new NUMA. People with a history of badly made maps, sniping, offensive behavior, or other actions detrimental to the overall health of NUMA will not be allowed to participate and will be restricted to the open NUMA database.

Unfortunately, I am incapable of making a website of this magnitude, so I cannot carry out this great plan (oh how I wish that I could). Thusly, I want comments, feedback, suggestions, and volunteers for this project. This is my new mission.

#144365 6 2006-02-16T03:08:22Z wcvandal
I try to do certain things to get noticed. ceratainly, when you begin, don't expect to have your first maps rated. i would suggest that you take some time to look at the best authors on numa and look at the techniques that they use, so that you can get a feel for what people look for in a map. good authors to look at are: Maximo, wellsj, formica, nevermore, sendy, stepself, tktktk, lucidium, astheoceansblue, etc. try to playtes your maps for fun. a good tileset that catcehs peoples eye will get them to vote more quickly, but if it isn't fun, then they probably won't rate it highly. It's important to remember that this is a game, and that above all other things, it should be fun. Another way to get yourself noticed is to play and comment on alot of maps. if you are seen as a participating and enthusiastic member of the user community, chances are that they will check you out. good comments aren't just things like "nice" or really cool", but should contaitn some insight as to your veiws on the map. make suggestions or tell the author what you liked and didn't like about their map. by making insightful comments, you show yourself to be a smart, proficient user, and you will be able to flatter your own expertise, making more people check out your maps. if it makes you feel any better, i'm still being discovered =)
