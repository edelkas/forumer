If i might respond briefly to a couple of 'trends' in the midst of these comments. The first is I think there is a problem overall with a somewhat reductionist view of God. I'm not always convinced that those who commented grasped the nub of my argument- which may be my fault rather than theirs- so let me pick up the line that I think sums it up best and you can chew it over- 'evil and suffering are only a problem if there is a God. Cossack- thanks for the technical advice. I of course accept that our ideas of justice change- that is of course a fact. The issue that is at stake is what determines what is just. If what is 'just' is determined by the majority or consensus then you are opening the door to totalitarianism e.g. the Nazis. The minority are always wrong because there is nothing either intrinsically right or wrong.
I agree that people can come to similar moral conclusions from different backgrounds. But morality is, I believe, only authentic if there is an absolute standard called 'morality' otherwise we have no basis for morality other than personal opinion. Take the example of the person who says a pig and a child have the same intrinsic worth ( I can't quite recall who takes that view, perhaps Peter Singer). Either we reduce that debate to personal opinions and so introduce the door to infanticide should its advocates gain enough support. Or we say there is an absolute standard from which we acknowledge that animal life is not on a par with human life.
Also I must disagree with your statement- although it may be a discussion for another day- that the Bible is just a book of human origin. As for ancient civilisations they did have their form of justice, some of which we would recognise. But they had otehr standards which as they embraced Christian morality they rejected e.g. the exposure of infants, which again I would want to argue is intrinsically wrong but you would not.
I have no doubt from what you have written that we share much in terms of our moral framework. I think our discussion is partially about the true nature of morality. My case is that only an absolute moral framework actually is morality and secures morality.
