I'm an... "abnormal mormon"
I was "born in the covenant." that's the term used in official records to describe those that are born into families that are members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints (better known as "The Mormons")

I don't, however, oft follow what could be called "mainstream mormonism." Although I believe in the doctrine of the church, I think that the church members tend to misinterpret (more like over-interpret) doctrine, stretching it to reflect what they are comfortable with. Due to this, the mainstream LDS churches political views don't tend to reflect real doctrine. I believe that the LDS church's political views are far from doctrine or the teachings of Jesus. In the words of Mahatma Mohandas Gandhi, "I like your Christ, I do not like your Christians. Your Christians are so unlike your Christ."

So although I'm mormon, I'm strongly liberal. I support gay marriage and believe in evolution. Really, I find residents of Utah just plain creepy; I don't like them at all.

Why do I believe what I do?
Faith is hard to explain... in the words of Elder Dallin H. Oaks, a member of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles of the LDS Church, "Faith settles like the dust on a bureau." For those "Born in the covenant" there is often not a single deciding moment - you just realize one day the bureau is dusty. LDS doctrine teaches us that faith comes from The Holy Spirit, who confirms to us the truth.

As a note, I do not believe that science and religion conflict. Religion is the why, and Science is the how.

Us Mormons have an interesting view on this issue. We believe that there are several stages of resurrection. First, you will be resurrected back on earth, but in a non-corporeal form. This is called the spirit world - it's a sort of waiting room. Spirits that knew the gospel in life will teach those that didn't, this way everyone will have the opportunity to know the truth. Spirits remain here until the second coming. At this point comes judgment. Spirits will stand before the bar and their lives will be reviewed by various important persons including Jesus and Moroni. After this they will be sent to their respective heaven. We do not believe in a "heaven" and "hell," as most religions do. There is a "hell" of sorts, called Outer Darkness. however, very few people will go there. The only way to go to outer darkness is to know of a surety of Jesus and deny him. This is something that as few as 100 people will ever have the opportunity to do.

Those that don't go to Outer Darkness (read: virtually everyone) will go to one of three heavens. In order of glory, least to most: Telestial Kingdom, Terrestrial Kingdom, Celestial Kingdom. All of these realms have a degree of glory - all are heaven. the sinners of the lot will go to the Telestial kingdom, where they will be happy. But they will never be as happy as the good people, which will go to higher kingdoms. Those in the Celestial kingdom will dwell with god - the highest of exaltation.

There is mention that we will all be gods - that is, we will eventually be resurrected as gods of our own worlds. However, this is not spoken of in any detail.

Indeed, I've been linked there many times, and have read the whole thing. Many of his points are entirely incorrect: many are passages taken out of context; and in many places he is just plain incorrect (the Jews in 600 BCE did have churches, in fact).

However, it is worth noting that he is also correct on many points. But most of these are caused by taking the BoM entirely to literally - I don't take any religious documents as being a literal historical record. The BoM is meant as a repository of spiritual information. As a result, many historical elements have likely been modified to emphasize the point being made.

You must also account for translation errors. We believe the Bible and Book Of Mormon to be the word of god "as far as it is translated correctly." Over time many, many errors have been introduced in to both documents. The Book of Mormon has been translated fewer times, and is therefore presumable more accurate than the Bible, but it is also susceptible to error.

And the rest? well, it is quite likely that parts of the BoM were originally written incorrectly.

I've been diagnosed with high functioning autism, and among my symptoms are OCD.

It's not inhibiting for me, but it is noticeable and sometimes annoys me and others.

Among my behaviors:
  • All stacks of items must be of the same height
  • Cabinets and doors must be opened to the full extent that their frame permits. If they aren't, they should at least be at an angle which is a multiple of 45
  • Certain items, such as plates, newspapers, and books, must be placed where I cannot see them. Otherwise, I will not be able to concentrate on other things
  • My hands always feel wierd. I will go through a variety of odd motions, particularly squeezing my fingers, to make them feel normal
  • I often get a strong desire to squeeze something or push on it very hard.
  • I must always know the exact time. If I do not have my watch on, I will check the clock every couple of minutes.
  • I'm much happier if I have even numbers of things.
  • My hands always feel dry. As a result I will wash them constantly. If they are dry, I will not be able to concentrate and I won't want to touch anything.
  • Things set in rows must be properly aligned.
Along with those standards, sometimes random errors will just really bug me. For example, in my Mandarin classroom, there was a Russian dictionary that was shelved upside down. I was unable to concentrate until I turned it right side up.

I think this has been said before, but I must reiterate it.
(Note: link are to the WorldCat global interlibrary catalog. You can enter your location in the box under the "libraries" tab below the summary and it will list nearby libraries that have a copy)

Ender's Game by Orson Scott Card
I think it's been said before, but it's a fantastic book that everyone should read.

Red Mars by Kim Stanley Robinson
A fantastic book (the beginning of a trilogy) about the colonization of Mars.

Man's Search for Meaning: An introduction to Logotherapy by Dr. Viktor E. Frankl
A book on Logotherapy, a system of psychological trauma therapy developed by Dr. Frankl while he was an inmate at the Auschwitz Jewish internment camp. Follows his time at Auschwitz and how he developed the concept, as well as an introduction to the concepts of Logotherapy. Considered by many to be one of the most important writings in psychotherapy since Dr. Freud.

The Google Story by David A Vise and Mark Malseed
Just as it sounds, the story of the internet titan Google Inc.
