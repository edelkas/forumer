Gilbert Magnus was apprenticed to a moderately successful alchemist back on the surface. He is a fairly intelligent young man who would usually do just enough work to stay out of trouble with his master - his apprenticeship was a matter of his parents' influence, not any love for the art. However, things worked out quite well for the lad; there are all sorts of herbs out there which can provide hours of amusement to (and for) those who know how to use them. Such entertainments along with the promise of eventually being able to use the retort all on his own (snicker) kept him from giving up entirely on the whole alchemy thing.

His teacher, unbeknownst to him, was a member of a rebel force fighting against the iron grip of the empire. Being a master of the more subtle arts, it was quite a while before suspicions were directed in his general vicinity. The completely outrageous behaviour of a member of an officer in the district's senior staff at a grand ball (he had imbibed a certain potion in the hopes that it would improve his dancing as much as... other things, and so instead of merely terrorizing his lady wife which would have caused her family to withdraw their support from the proposal to further restrict... well, you get the picture, it merely caused the man to make a public spectacle of himself) as well as the officer's continued protests of being possesed, somehow, led to a rather thourough investigation - which inevitably turned up Gilbert as the primary suspect, as the boy had been known as something of a prankster with his herbs in the past. (also his master, a very devoted man, didn't really like his casual attitudes very much and decided that framing Gilbert was the best thing "for the cause")

Anyway, it went to trial, evidence that didn't exist the previous week was presented and accepted, and Gilbert was banished, much to his bewilderment.

So, for actual gameplay stuff:

He's proficient with simple ranged weapons (slings, chucking rocks, etcetera) and the staff, both mainly for protection against wild animals while hunting down feral herbs. He has no clue as to the use of most bladed weapons, though he has been known to have dinner without stabbing himself to death by accident.

Most of his skill is in alchemy; however, the herbs of the surface are much different from those of Avernum, and it is unlikely (though possible, he was quite interested in the properties of certain mushrooms) that he will recognize any herbs down here. With a proper laboratory he could probably work out a couple basic recepies in a few months, but why bother when there's already alchemists down here? The first thing he's likely to do after getting his bearings is consult with them about the properties of the local flora.

He is a mage of some small skill; all alchemists are magi on the side (the magi think it's the other way around) but that was never really the focus of his studies. He can cast spells of the first rank, and quite often manages to get the words to those of the second rank right. The gestures are another story entirely.

He is quite weak, physically, because of all that time he's spent inside not picking up heavy things. He's slightly dexterous, because if he weren't there are several alchemical operations which will (and have) quite happily explode(d) in his face. He doesn't have an exceptional amount of endurance, but wandering around looking for wild herbs has trained that a bit.

On the surface he would have generally been a fairly cloudy, ditzy person unless he were making potions, which he has learned from rather explosive experience must be done with both feet firmly on the ground; however, the recent turn of events has given him a perpetual surprised look, with the subtle overtones of hunted rabbit.

Anyway...

TheDailyLlama, w.r.t your post on how us being here is impossible: It's obviously not. The thing is, that sort of thinking is sort of self-defeating (not to mention usually leads up to the initial creator argument). I mean, you go on and on about how it's nearly impossible for us to be here, when the fact that we are is quite self-evident. For an answer (sorta) as to why we are here as opposed to an infinitesimal amount of nothingness, you might want to read Stephen Baxter's (I think) Manifold: Time. I think that most of what he says has been shown to be incorrect, but that involves physics and maths that make my brain hurt. His point is still quite interesting. (it's a really good science fiction book too, of course, otherwise I wouldn't have read it in the first place.) What he says, basically, is that this might not be the first universe there has ever been, nor the only one there will ever be. Even without delving into the whole multiverse thing, well, you'd basically have an infinite amount of time in which things can happen. And, though you might not know this, even chances that "are sorta below nil" become certainties when exposed to infinite amounts of time; that is, the probability of an event with a nonzero probability of happening at any given time happening as time goes to infinity is 100%.

Wow, that sentence doesn't make any sense. Ummm... How about, "everything that can happen will happen if you give it enough time to happen in"? I think that works better.

And, of course, that might be why we're here. We had a nonzero probability of happening, and there was a lot of time for us to happen in...

Which actually segways nicely into the reason I have for believing in reincarnation. Even if one were to discard the possibility of there ever being any other universes ever, well, this universe is still gonna last for a while, and it's my opinion that eventually someone or something will figure out how to transplant psyches across time, somehow. I have no clue how it could happen; you could die one day and wake up surrounded by Cthulu-like beings in a body which is not your own, or by the beautiful priestesses of Nymph 3 or something. The means and manner don't matter; the important part is that if it's possible, it might happen in the fullness of time.

Now, I realize that this isn't what most people think of when they think of reincarnation - there's no cockroach for a body involved, after all, and you get to keep all your old memories. That's a different argument, but I don't really think that sort of reincarnation is very fun.

Besides, the word "reincarnation" taken literally just means "being made flesh once more" - nothing about memories in that.

One could, of course, argue that you might not be you at that point for whatever reason, so I've looked up a quote from Neil Gaiman:
