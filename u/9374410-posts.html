Type 2: These can be formed by putting in a ^ into N. They have the appearance of a vertical half-tile, but they interact with N in strange ways (i.e., stopping him, propelling him, and/or killing him). See any Glitchtastic level for examples.

About this contest
This competition focused not on creating new maps, but on finding an old map that fit a given theme. For example, when I named a theme of "drones" (round 17), people found maps of theirs that they had already made that were focused on drones. The judges then judged the maps based on how well they fit the theme.


Scoring
Scoring was divided into two categories.
Playability: This category encompasses everything from how fun the map is to how well thought-out it is. Essentially, judges graded this the same as they would rate it on NUMA.
Suitability: This category was graded based on how well the map fit the theme.
Also, if the map had been submitted before, it lost 1/2 point after the rest of the scoring had been completed.

Each category had a maximum of five possible points, with half-points possible.

If two maps tied for first, they both earned first place and there was be a third map as runner-up. If two maps tied for second, they were both listed as runner-ups.
If a player would have placed twice in one round, only their better map was displayed.


Prizes
Signatures were awarded to all winners each round.
To see all prizes made, go here.
To see a certain member's prizes, go to http://s256.photobucket.com/albums/hh173/flashback_winners?action=tags&current=(username)

Also, if anyone figures out what levels are shown in a set of five signatures (currently, 6-10, 11-15, or 16-20), I will make them a personalized signature.

I have a few 25 corrections and additions:

1) With overlapping locked doors, you only need to get the switch of the door that was put on first, not last. Also, if you get any other switch after getting the first, the door will reclose. Closing the door in this way can propel N in the same way trapdoors can.

2) The "laser goes through tile" glitch is the exact same thing as the "rocket sees through walls" glitch. Both of them are based on the smallest side of the three tile. See http://numa.notdot.net/map/113688 for an example of this with lasers.

3) Drones and thwumps will not move if their direction is set to NaN (with drones, the same thing happens if their pathfinding algo is set to NaN). NaNed thwumps will look like they are facing downwards, but will only zap you if you touch their right side. NaNed seeker drones will move if you start within their field of view. If you are in their field of view when they reach a wall, they will continue to move. If not (and you set their algo, not their direction, to NaN), they will stop moving. See http://numa.notdot.net/map/103577 for an example of this.

4) Drones slow down only when they are within a certain distance of the right side of the map. I believe the cutoff is slightly over halfway across.

5) Tower loops- N can be accelerated to high speeds (around one tile per frame) and then easily go through tiles, being propelled on as he comes out. See "Tower Loop" in the original userlevels for an example.

6) It is possible to stand on a one-way panel that is entirely in a wall if you are holding an arrow key towards that wall. (GForce20's collection of one-way bugs only covers how to go through them, not stuff like this.)

7) If a thwump is placed correctly to make it back up but is not facing into a tile, it will start going forward when you approach it from behind. See http://numa.notdot.net/map/109486 for a demonstration.

8) If a gauss or rocket turret is placed with a c-snap so that it is just barely touching an 8-tile, it will not fire unless N is in a specific portion of its field of view. See the exit switch area of http://numa.notdot.net/map/113020 for an example.

9) If a trapdoor switch is placed on top of a mine, it will be hidden by that mine in-game. This means that you can place a trapdoor in the way to block you from hitting the mine. See http://numa.notdot.net/map/113380 for an example (hold left at the start).

10) If N is standing on a 5a tile, he will not be seen by seeker drones or (most) thwumps. This occurs for the same reason that drones cannot pass through seemingly very wide passages- as long as there is some sort of tile in a section, they cannot enter it.

11) A gauss turret placed outside the border of the map can shoot anywhere within the map, through any number of tiles. If it is placed a long distance away, it will take a long time to fire, but when it does, it will kill N. A gauss turret with either coordinate of NaN will take a few seconds to fire, but will kill N. If it is placed in a tile, it can shoot out of that tile and the tiles surrounding it, but not through any other tiles.

12) Floorguards placed in midair will not move right (much), but, if they see N to their left, they will move infinitely to the left, through tiles and the edge of the map.

13) If two thwumps back up into each other with N in between, they can push N into and through a locked door nearby. See http://numa.notdot.net/map/105003 for an example.

14) A launchpad placed 1/2 snap under or above a locked door can propel N directly through the door at much greater speed than the launchpad alone could provide. See http://numa.notdot.net/map/8071 for an example of both simultaneously.

15) A launchpad placed on the ground will not always* propel N if its power has been manually decreased.
*If N walks over it, it won't, but if he lands on it, it will.

16) If N is just barely touching an exit, he cannot be killed by rockets (in Ned only). For an example, load |5^540,564!11^540,588,540,564!10^540,540 in Ned.

17) Gold delay can be used on thwumps. It is most frequently used in DDAs to launch N, but if enough gold is on a thwump, N can go right through it. See http://numa.notdot.net/map/105003 for an example.

18) Locked door + door- if a normal door is placed on top of a locked door, it will let N through after he opens the locked door with the normal door open, let enemies go and see through, but become a wall to everything when N opens the normal door again. For an example, see http://numa.notdot.net/map/81669.

19) In addition to pushing you between 5 tiles, thwumps can push you backwards through all launchpads, all bounceblocks, right- and up-facing one-way pads, down-facing one-ways if you jump, and sometimes left-facing one-ways (this is, however, not as reliable as the others).

20) It is possible to get a switch within a wall or behind a door if you are moving fast enough toward it when you hit the wall/door. If the switch opens the door in the way, that door will have no effect on your motion. For an example, see http://numa.notdot.net/map/98869 (watch GForce's demo).

21) Well-placed launchpads can propel N through tiles. See http://numa.notdot.net/map/97982 for some examples.

22) An unedited launchpad facing into a large stack of bounceblocks can act as a teleporter. See http://numa.notdot.net/map/97982 for an example.

23) Drones cannot leave the walls unless they are going into a completely empty tile. See http://numa.notdot.net/map/97982 for an example of what can be done with this.

24) A laser drone's laser does not change once it begins firing, even if the door it was firing at opens or a trapdoor closes in the way.

25) It is possible to stand on a normal door if it is vertical, N lands on it from above, and N does not have much speed. Also, if N starts the level exactly above a normal door, he will balance on it until you decide to move.


Those are the majority of additional glitches I could think of. Just about all of the others are related to NaNed doors.

This thread is an attempt to explain how glitched doors work and what they can do.


The Basics

Creating them:
Glitched doors are created when they are set to face in a direction that the program doesn't recognize (anything other than 0, 1, 2, or 3). I generally set it to NaN, because if it is set to something like 5, NUMA will display it as a 1-tile, alerting players to the glitch.

What they are:
Glitched doors are particularly hard to understand because they behave in hard-to-understand ways. They kill you if you approach them in the wrong way, and they will not stop you in any way you'd expect.
This is because the door has been broken up in a way- it acts like two half-doors in different places. These will only stop you from going in one direction, and will kill (or propel) you if you approach from the opposite side.

In the attached map, a locked door is represented by the one-way pads and normal door in the first room. I highly suggest playing around on the glitched door to see how it behaves.

Note that these doors only do this when closed; for example, in the second room, jumping over the trapdoor switch will do nothing until the switch has been triggered. Also, the normal door will only affect you when you are far enough from the center that it is closed.

Going into tiles:
Probably the most important thing these doors can do is allow you (among other things) to go into tiles. If an open glitched door is placed directly above a tile, you will fall in instead of walking over it. If there are no objects inside the tile to catch you, the tile will kill you.
Drones can also enter tiles through openings such as these, and will not be affected by other tiles once inside. Open the top door in the third room to see this.

Note: all glitched doors will do this, provided they are open. Therefore, trapdoors can be used to do this before they are closed, which allows you to execute this glitch without showing people where it will happen. See room four for an example of this.

Go here and here to see the applications of this glitch.

This glitch will also allow you to go through closed doors with no resistance. See room five for a demonstration.

Closed when open:
If either of the "halves" of a glitched door is inside (or partially inside) a wall, the other "half" will always be closed. This is true for open locked doors (like those in room 6) and not-yet-closed trapdoors. This glitch creates problems when you want to allow things through near a wall. If you are having such problems, you'll probably need to delete the nearby tile entirely to fix this.

Edit: actually, though the above is always true in the case of drones, it is only sometimes true for N. As shown by BuddyLee and Lord_Day, these doors can be set up to trap drones but not affect N.
To clarify: one part of the door will only affect N if there is a wall along the entirety of the other side of where the door appears to be. For clarification, see room 6 and play around with the various doors. Note that each side is independent; one side of the door next to the gold is open, but the other is closed.

Advanced

Playing with floorguards:
Because these doors can make the game not register the floor in an area, if they are put under a floorguard, they can make that floorguard act as if it's floating on air. However, because of how floorguards work, they will only do this if the door loads before them and if the gap hasn't been covered by another door. You can see the results of all this in room seven.

Playing with tight spaces:
If a row of these doors is created and one-way pads are placed underneath, N can travel while part way in a tile. This will, for example, allow him to avoid the mines in room eight and reach the switch.

Playing with drone paths:
Highly advanced drone paths can be made by taking advantage of the fact that these doors will let drones through in one direction, but stop them from going the other. This can be done without putting an obstacle in N's way, as explained in the description of room six and as shown in room nine.
Also note that only the side opposite the one in the tile affects the drones; when in the tiles, they can travel through that part of the door.


I hope you found this useful.

In discussing abiogenesis, I feel obligated to bring this up:
Sidney W. Fox has done some research regarding abiogenesis and has demonstrated that microspheres can be reliably formed under conditions potentially very similar to those of Earth in its early stages. (See http://www.theharbinger.org/articles/rel_sci/fox.html for more detail.)



Regarding the probability discussion:
If something has a 50% chance of happening (say, a fair coin landing heads-up), then the chance of it happening at least once after n trials can be modeled by the formula P = 1 - (1 - .5)^n because each trial would cut in half the chance of the event not occurring.

Therefore, if something has a 1 in 10^125 chance of happening, the probability of it happening at least once after n trials is P = 1 - (1 - 1/10^125)^n. According to my calculations, the probability of this event would reach 50% after about 6.9 x 10^124 trials and would reach 99% after 4.6 x 10^125 trials.

By the way, if you want to check my work, I suggest downloading this or something similar.

Now, let's consider what a "trial" would be in context. In order for a protein to have even the slightest chance of forming, a reasonable number of amino acids would have to be in a small area. I'll be nice and limit the number of trials per cubic millimeter at a given point in time to one hundred. I'll also assume that a maximum of one hundred chemical reaction can occur (or not occur) per second.

The earth has a surface area of 510,065,600 square kilometers, 70.8% of which is water. It seems to me a fair assumption that this percentage has not changed much in the Earth's past, as the amount of land area is determined by the amount of rock light enough not to sink into the mantle. I'll make the assumption that only the top 5 kilometers got enough sunlight to sustain chemical reactions.

This gives 1.8 x 10^9 cubic kilometers, or 1.8 x 10^18 cubic millimeters of space for potential trials, ignoring any space around ocean vents. Using the estimate of 100 million years (3.16 x 10^17 hundredths of a second), this gives 5.698 x 10^37 trials for Earth.

The Universe is generally accepted to be 1.37 x 10^10 years old and 92 billion light-years wide. Allowing a gigayear for planets to begin forming and 20 light-years between reasonable planets gives 1.21 x 10^28 planets like earth.

This gives the result of 6.93 x 10^65 trials total. The probability of this occurring is 1 in 6.93 x 10^60 according to Meyer's estimate (and ignoring several things that would make this much more likely).

Using other estimates give different results.

Using the first result on Google (1 in 5 x 10^19) gave me an overflow error on a calculator that is able to handle hundreds of digits. I decreased the estimate to 1 in 5 x 10^55, and it began saying that the probability of proteins having formed was 1. I had to bring the estimate down to 1 in 5 x 10^63 before it began registering anything lower than one, and it still displayed 61 nines after the decimal point.

I didn't bother trying the lower estimates, such as 1 in 10113 and 1 in 1067.


Conclusion: unless you are willing to go way overboard with your estimate, probability is not on your side.

Well, I think I've found a program that will handle the recording nicely. CamStudio is an open source screen recording program with no watermarks but plenty of functionality. The default settings produce poor video, so you'll want to make a few changes:
  • Under video options, set quality to 100 and compression to ffdshow (well, you probably don't have to set it to that, but it seems to produce smaller, higher-quality files).
  • Under cursor options, select "hide cursor."
  • Under options, check enable autopan.
  • Bring "autopan speed" down to ~12 or so (anything too much higher is jerky, but bringing it too low makes it hard to keep up with the ninja).
  • Under region, select "fixed region" and modify the width and height to an appropriate size.
  • Do not record to .swf format. Doing so will dramatically reduce quality and increase filesize. Also, YouTube prefers .avi files (as far as I know).
  • Look up the shortcut keys that will start and stop recording. If you don't, you'll end up needing to cut out certain parts. (Of course, you may need to do such editing anyway. If you do, I recommend VirtualDub, because I've had issues with over-compression in Movie Maker. Plus, VirtualDub supports ffdshow encoding.)
