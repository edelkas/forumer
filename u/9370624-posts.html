Since N is a live or die game (no damage or hit points), there isn't much to add to him. But, what if there were simple things N could get that would allow him to live just a little longer under certain circumstances. Such as:

* Mine immunity - next mine N hits will explode but not kill N
* Thwump immunity - immune to thumps for a period of time
* Invulnerability - N will not be affected by robots of any kind for a period of time (2 to 5 seconds, for example). N will simply pass through them (or they through him) instead of blowing up or doing damage.
* Missile immunity - for next missile to hit N
* Laser immunity (laser drones and gauss turrets) - for a period of time
* Jumping power - for a period of time add 25% to 50% to N's jumping power
* Landing perfection - N can jump from any height and live for a period of time
* Stop time - For a fixed period, all robots will stop their motion and N's lifespan will not be reduced (and as a penalty, no gold picked up during this time will add to his lifespan either).
* Etc.

Nothing too overpowering and yet still useful to N and can create some unique map desings. Each "power up" would only be able to be used once and then it is gone. All could be set to expire after a period of time (use within the next 10 seconds or lose it, for instance).

As an example, I can see mine immunity being used to create a map where N has to pick the right mine to blow up without dying just to advance further in the level. Or maybe N has to pick up jumping power to make the jump accross the chasm in order to reach the exit. It's all about having more tools to make more interesting maps (and play them, of course).


Also, there could be "cursed power ups" that have negative effects such as:

* Seeking zap drone now have a 360 degree line of sight (they can see N even from behind)
* N loses his ability to jump for a period of time (or reduces it by 50%)
* N moves at half speed
* N will pass through bounce blocks as if they were not there
* Floor guards move at twice normal speed when they sense N is near
* Etc.

Some of these cursed effects could be permanent (until N dies, that is) while others might be only temporary.

I think you get the idea.

If the mode rating (the rating given most often, for those not familiar with the term) were given more weight than the other ratings given, how would that affect the overall rating? Basically, it would give an overall rating that would more reflect what most people think instead of a pure average.

Maybe it could work like this:

The rating used most would be x10, next would be x8, next would be x6, then x4, x2, and the one used least would be x1. If two ratings had the same number of votes then they would split the difference of their value. So, something like this:

2 people rated the map 0
No one rated the map 1
3 people rate the map 2
5 people rate the map 3
3 people rate the map 4
1 person rated the map 5

5 of the people rated the map a 3, which was the highest total. That would be 5 x 10 for 50 points. The next highest was 2 and 4, both of which received 3 votes. The next multipliers used are 8 and 6, averaged would be 7. So all of the 2 and 4 votes are multiplied by 7. That would make 6 x 7 = 42. The next highest is 0, so those 2 votes are multiplied by 4 which makes 8 points. One person rated it a 5 and that makes 10 points. No one rated the map a 1, so no points are added. The total so far would be 50 + 42 + 8 + 10 = 110. This value is then divided by the total number of votes which is 14. 110 / 14 = 7.86.

Another example:

12 people rated the map 0
15 people rated the map 1
23 people rate the map 2
30 people rate the map 3
21 people rate the map 4
18 people rated the map 5

Calculated as above, the points would be:

#3 = 30 x 10 = 300
#2 = 23 x 8 = 184
#4 = 21 x 6 = 126
#5 = 18 x 4 = 72
#1 = 15 x 2 = 30
#0 = 12 x 1 = 12

For a total of 724. Divide that by the total number of votes (119) and you get 6.08.

Every value should be on a 0 to 10 scale, if I've done my math correctly.

Yes, this is more complicated, but it would be far more like a consensus rather than just an average. It gives you a value that is much more like what the overall community feels about the map and gives a lot less weight to the rebel without a clue, be it a high rating or a low rating.

Delayed doors - hit the switch and have a period of time before it closes or opens. Time could be zero to make it instant.

Vertical/Horizontal Auto Doors - The idea here is that if the ninja is lined up with the door either vertically or horizontally (depending on the door type) then it will open or close (chosen when the door is placed), otherwise it is in the opposite state.

Black Hole Door - When the ninja gets too close, he is sucked through the door and it promptly closes. It has a switch to reopen it, presumably accessible from the other side.

#471557 14 2008-09-19T11:49:55Z HeartView
DDA's can be very simple, like Staying Busy. My first DDA, Close Call, took a very long time to make. One of my better rated DDA's is The Path Less Traveled. Also well received was Infiltration - The Outer Limits, except that it seemed to fail for some folks, no clue why.

DDA's take a LOT of time to make. The way I do it is to make small steps. Set up a sequence and watch it play a few times. Add some spice, change things a little, extend the path and make it longer. Think small steps and watch out for changes that might accidentally break other sequences. Classic example is that when you add too many turrets some may not fire in a timely manner like they did when there were fewer. Machine gun drones are unpredictable and will break most DDA's.

Make sure every moment you can is filled with close calls and narrow escapes. Shooting quickly through a lot of bad guys over a long distance is amazing to watch. If your DDA is long, make sure it isn't boring. The last one I linked above is very long, but has one spot on the left where the ninja just hangs around way too long. I had already advanced the map too far past that point and didn't bother to fix it when I made. Probably a mistake, but overall I think the DDA was a success.

Again, DDA's take a LOT of time to make. It might take you two hours to make a 30 second DDA. I probably spent 8 or more hours on The Path Less Traveled.

Splitting Hairs is an example of a fully open DDA, and my personal favorite of the ones I made. It did not receive high marks, but I like it.

I've also done a few "standard maps made into DDA's". This can be a VERY good way to get used to how to move N around effectively. Some people prefer using fewer, modified, jump pads over scattering them all over. I say do whatever makes the action great and keeps the map working.

Some people start with a tileset and then fit a DDA to it. Others start with a theme like "only using Thwumps to move N around". Just start simple, dream big. Don't expect your first DDA to be your best and don't give up just because some idiot on NUMA or the forums gives you a hard time. Making DDA's is an art, not a science, just like regular map making. If you take the heart out of it then it will most certainly feel stale.
