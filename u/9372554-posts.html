I used to hate this level. Its actuallt one of the easiest for me now, but it'll probably take you a while.

Okay, This time you do the green bit first. Speed is of the essence here, so jump onto the first wall and do a quick wall-hop onto the ground. Run. When you go off the edge, the chaingun will see you. Without getting shot (duh) do a small jump onto the entrance wall. Get the first switch and come back down, then immediately jump towards the far wall. I don't think you should be able to get the switch in one jump, so get it as you start going back. Land as close to the mine near the entrance as possible, then go straight down. The gun should miss you.
You'll want to land on the slant to break your fall, and then run. Jump onto the left wall bit of the next entrance, and run to the right. This is the fastest way I know of. Jump up the wall, (should take one jump to get under the thwump, then a second to get to the switch.) and land on the block. Very quickly, hop up again, and go straight down next the block. It'll kill you if you hit the floor, so either glance off the corner of land at the bottom of the room.
If you did all of this quickly, the chaingun will still be in the right corridor. Run to the gold, and collect it, (doube duh) in blue. Then just glide up to the exit.
Remember, you have to do the first bits fast so the chaingun can't see you.

Phew! Hope this helped.

Just getting this out of me, really, but I'd like to hear what you have to say on it.

Okay. A few years ago, I was in sixth grade. It was the first year my class had done any true writing, so all of our essays and stories had to follow a rather specific format. Now, I've been writing for a few years at this point, and I know some of my classmates have as well. I was really pissed off with the constraints, so I went and talked to my teacher about it. She explained how the aim of the format was to help introduce kids who would otherwise slop some crap out and hand it in to the basics of writing. She understood that I didn't really need any sort of assisstance, but asked that I conform with the group so as not to alarm anyone. I accepted that, though it wasn't what I had hoped for. We were formulaic throughout the year.

Skip to next year, seventh grade. As our first essay comes around the corner, we are shown the format that is expected for this and all future assignments. So, I ask my teacher about it again. I had a fantastic teacher that year, and I think she actually understood where I was. She basically siad the same thing, but let me try my own style of writing. She told the whole class that as we progressed into eighth grade, forulas would stop being required on written works. I was extremely happy, as you can imagine. This year, I was unformulaic and happy.

Then comes this year. I really disliked our reading teacher from the start, but I figured it couldn't be too bad. We didn't have to use any format for our writing this year, right? Wrong. My class finished up To Kill a Mockingbird a week or to ago, and was promptly given a sheet with the formula we had to follow. The teacher explained,

A report on Fort Ross.

Fort Ross was built by the Russian-American Company to serve as a base for hunting sea otter and for growing crops to send to Russian settlements in Alaska. The fort is located in Sonoma County on Californias Pacific Coast north of Bodega Bay.
The 40 Russians and 80 Aleuts that started Fort Ross in March 1812 finished in September of that year. It was built of redwood and was surrounded by a wooden palisade. Connected to the palisade were two block houses with cannons that could control the whole area. Inside the palisade was the Managers house, the officials quarters, a barracks for Russian employees, storehouses, a well and a chapel added in 1824. Outside the palisade were houses for company laborers, Native Americans and Aleuts.
By 1820, the Russians had nearly depleted the sea otter population so they turned farming into their main occupation. Their farms were not very successful because of the fog, gophers, mice and lack of interest of the men who thought of themselves as hunters. Because of the farms failure, there was not much extra food to send to the outposts in Alaska.
In 1839, the Russian-American Company signed an agreement with the Hudson Bay Company saying that they (the Hudson Bay Company) would supply the Russian settlements in Sitka with foods. Soon afterwards, the Russians decided to abandon Fort Ross. They first tried to sell it to the Mexican government, but ended up selling it to John Sutter in 1841 for $30,000, which was never fully paid.
Sutter took the valuables and animals back to Sutters Fort. The buildings were used for various purposes until they became a national park in 1906.
