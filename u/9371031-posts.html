Why submit a .sol, and how would it help?

Lately, the One-Life Challenge (or OLC for short) has been the target of some dispute, since the nature of the challenge means that scores cannot be proven as there is no such option within the game. As such, the contest has been sustained purely by trust for a long while. However, recently, the emergence of several players as well as their incredible scores has led the community wondering: is there a way to verify an OLC run?

Then, fellow community member Knu, himself a disbeliever of some of the more extraordinary scores, suggested making an entirely new .sol file just for the purpose of verification. This would work, as the userdata records personal bests, as well as death count. By having the claimed score correspond with the personal bests and death counts, OLC runs can finally be verified. Of course, there is the issue of .sol hacking, but it should be assumed that not many people would go to such lengths just to fake a score on a usermade contest.

How do create and submit the .sol?

1. Now, before you start an OLC run, go to your directory that has your userdata in it. For Windows XP users, which I assume to be the majority of the population, the pathway is

C:\Documents and Settings\YOURACCOUNTNAME\Application Data\Macromedia\Flash Player\#SharedObjects\RANDOMNUMBERSANDLETTERS\localhost

I have no idea about earlier versions of Windows, or other OSes.

2. Anyway, once you locate your userdata.sol, back it up. Do so by creating a folder that is easily accesible (say, a folder named N USERDATA on the desktop), and copying the userdata into that folder. You could also upload other copies onto the internet via email. This is very important, as your userdata contains all your progress in the game, and it would be a sad case if you lost everything.

3. Once you are sure you have your original userdata SAFE and SECURE, delete your userdata in your localhost folder (ie, the folder that you originally found your data). If N starts up and finds no data in that folder, it would obviously be a completely blank game, just like when you first started. A new blank userdata (which would be very small, like 45kbs or so) will also be created in your localhost folder).

4. Start the OLC. Play from whatever column you want, and just...erm...play. Yeah. It would help later if you kept your localhost folder open.

5. When you finally die during a level (ie, not after finishing the level), stop. If you don't find your run satisfactory, or if it just isn't a personal best, then go on and delete your current userdata in the localhost folder, and start from step 4. again.

If it is a new personal best, or you just wish to submit it, then stop the game, go to your localhost folder, and rename the OLC userdata into something that is recognisable, like YOURNAMEn_v14b_userdata.sol, like Sammage has done. Come to this topic, and attach that file to your post.

If you have died in the OLC run because you whacked the floor after finishing the level, please indicate where in your post. This is to facilitate score verification, since the .sol will record ALL deaths, including those inflicted after level completion.

6. If you wish to keep this OLC .sol for future purposes, keep it in a separate folder (eg, a subfolder "OLC runs" in your "N USERDATA" folder). Either way, restore your original userdata into the localhost directory, by dragging or copypasting or whatever, and you are set to continue normal gaming.


How do I verify other .sol files?

Let' use Sammage's .sol as an example.

1. Do the same thing as step 1 above. Backup your original userdata, and shift it somewhere else. The backup is the most important part, of course, since you can just override your original .sol with the OLC one anyway.

2. Download the attachment.

3. It would likely have been renamed, so after putting it into the localhost folder, rename it to n_v14b_userdata.sol

4. Open up N. It should only show up to the level that the scorer claims he has reached before death. Check his death count, and verify that with what the scorer claims regarding deaths after level completion and stuff like that.

5. After verifying, delete that OLC .sol and restore your old .sol into your localhost folder. Continue normal gaming.
