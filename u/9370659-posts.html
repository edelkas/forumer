The sun aligns with the fallen;

Now, you may think you know the answer to this, but there is more than one answer (Particularly directed at Trogdor).

Okay. The poem. Everyone has pretty much ignored the poem so far, or thrown out random guesses that don't connect to anything.

First of all, Kablizzy uses a QUOTE around the poem. Whether he uses it to separate the text or if he's actually quoting from something, we don't know. I'd be interested to see if we could find a source for this poem, or if it's something Kablizzy made up.

One thing we do know is that Trogdor knows something about this poem. He seems to either not know what Kablizzy thinks he knows, or he just isn't saying. Perhaps we should look back at what he says on this.

We also know that there is more than one answer to the poem. Which strikes me as odd, because there is no question in it. It's just description. Perhaps he means there is more than one interpretation, but maybe that is a conclusion that should not be jumped to. I don't really know where to go on this, or if it's a non-clue.

Okay, so the poem itself. I'll go line-by-line.

"The sun aligns with the fallen;"

The fallen what? Is this a reference to something in the metanet community, or something outside?
Are these things that have literally 'fallen', or things that have figuratively 'fallen'?

There are a heck of a lot of definitions for fallen. Which one(s) is Kablizzy using?

Is the sun what it seems to be? Maybe it's a play on 'son'. Maybe it really refers to something else. Hrm.

'Aligns'. What are we talking about here? A geometrical alignment? A philosophical alignment? Some other sort of alignment?

"Hundreds exist, yet within each, Four meet as one."

Hundreds of what? There are lots of sets of hundreds of things. This is really vague.

'Four'. Kablizzy capitalizes this in the middle of the sentence. This means that it is important in some way. In fact, I think just about everything Kablizzy has capitalised in the middle of a sentence will be somehow important in solving this. Maybe Four really refers to something else. I'm thinking maybe COFFEE here, but that's just a wild guess.

'within each' i.e. each of the hundreds, right?

'Four meet as one.' This is quite odd. Again, this sounds like COFFEE, but I'm not certain. Four what? Something tangible or not? And the one, same thing, is it tangible or not? What is this referring to?

"Below, the watcher protects;"

This is also odd - usually a watcher watches from above. Maybe it's not one who watches, but in fact, someone who uses a watch in some fashion? Doesn't really seem to fit.

'the watcher protects'. What are they protecting? How? For what reason? Why are they below? This is also very vague.

"While the blue vein brings life to them all."

Presumedly this means that this line is happening while the watcher protects.

'blue vein'. What is this referring to? There are multiple definitions for vein. Which are we talking about here?

The 'blue vein brings life to them all'. Them all being the hundreds? How does it bring life? Are we talking literally, or figuratively?

"Millions golden, sun-glinted and windswept."

This is worded somewhat weirdly. I would have switched windswept and sun-glinted. Better rhythm.

'Millions' of what? Same as the 'hundreds', or different?

'golden, sun-glinted and windswept.' What things are golden, sun-glinted, and windswept? Hair? Plants of some sort? Sand grains? Something else? There are millions of them, whatever they are.


This is all very terribly vague to me. Maybe it ties into some of the other clues we have uncovered. Whatever it is, I believe it's important in some way.
