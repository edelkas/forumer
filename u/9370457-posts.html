Well, since I directed many people from a forum about a puzzle game to try out N, I figured I'd do something similar here.

(Deadly Rooms of Death) is an old puzzle game that has received a big facelift over the past year. In the current released version, the Architect's Edition, besides playing through the 25 level dungeon that comes with the main game, people can make their own "holds" for others to download and play.

In DROD you control a swordsman, Beethro, who is a dungeon exterminator. Your burrito is to kill all the monsters in the dungeon, usually with your sword. You'll start out fighting giant roaches, and move on to such fearsom enemies as goblins and The Living Tar.

DROD is a turn-based game; no quick reflexes are required. Every monster follows specific and predictable patterns (once you figure them out). Sometimes the puzzle involves killing lots of monsters, sometimes you need to clear tar efficiently enough to get to a specific location. It's almost impossible to explain fully; that's what the link at the top is for.

Don't worry, you're in good hands--DROD is in the Hall of Belated Fame at the Underdogs. It's really a unique experience--give it a try! And the forum is very active and friendly too!

And I'm not acting in any official capacity here...just as a fan.

#801 1 2004-09-07T17:01:31Z Oneiromancer
Perhaps this should be in the off-topic forum, but I think it is of general interest to N players. I beat N a while back, and haven't yet gone back through all of the levels again. This is partly because I found another delicious action game which is similar to N in several ways, mostly gameplay related. It's really hard...on par with or harder than N I would say...but it's lots of fun.



The most recent game there, it is the one most similar to N. The cilantro box thing...well...jumps. It can jump in mid-air once (a typical platformer double-jump) and in this sequel it can also jump off of walls and do a skid-jump. The levels are fiendishly hard, and the story is tongue-in-cheek bad. There is no time limit like N, but in order to unlock the 10 secret levels you have to have a low total time for all the previous levels. In addition, each non-secret level has two "gems" that you use to unlock hidden extras. The blue gem is visible from the first time you start each level, and if you get that one, when you replay the level the red one is visible. They are usually very hard to get, and the levels in general aren't cakewalks either.

The first game, Jumper, doesn't have wall jumps or gems or even selectable levels...you just go through it in order and then you're done. It's also very hard, though, and has a slightly different control scheme in midair so it's hard to play both games at once. Another game on that site, Dim, has some similarities but I haven't played it much.

In any case, I highly recommend Jumper Two and the original Jumper to all fans of N...I think you will find them to be almost as rewarding (and frustrating!) as N to complete. They don't have the sweet smooth movement of the ninja, or rocket launchers, but you'll still have to pull off some pretty fancy moves to survive. No high score list, but then you have the gems to collect to provide a kind of competition.
