#334214 10 2007-06-22T00:13:48Z Jason
I figured out the projection to a single axis, its accually alot easier that it seems. There is no need to project to planes first. I simply need to project each point to the axis, and take the smallest and largest values, and I then have the single axis interval. With an ellipsoid, all I need to do is project the center point, then find the raduis along the projection axis, and have the interval be plus and minus the radius from the center. The cylinder was trickier, but it turns out to be similar to the ellipsoid, I simply project the center of the circle at the top, and add the plus and minus points of that point, and do the same with the bottom. Then its simply the interval between the smallest and largest one dimentional point. This way I can simply apply a rotation matrix to the points to get the axis alligned orientations I need.

Next step is to determine the finite number of axies that need to be tested with any orientation. The circle and cylinder will be somewhat tricky, they are the only ones that have infinite normals, forcing me to test every point and line. Thats where VRs will be helpful once I figure out how to iterate though them all, and accociate the point/line/plane they are closest to.

Here's a demo a made awhile back, it has a system for multiple collisions with tiles. Really it just finds the tiles the player collides with in a single frame, then sorts them by "most significant overlap".
