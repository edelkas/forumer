I found a small correlation between weapons and the points I earn. This is NOT a correlation between the weapon and the health damage it causes. Here are my observations:

1) I've noticed that the speed of the projectile when it strikes an opponent has a lot to do with points. The slower the projectile upon impact, the less points

2) I've also noticed that if a projectile "bounces" or touches ground, it scores less points when it impacts an opponent (presumably because it slows down)

3) Additionally if I can hit multiple opponents at once (like all three), without bouncing it gives me the most points. I've scored as much as 2900 points with one throw.

4) A good shot that kills, seems to give more points. I notice I start out with low-scoring shots near the beginning, and end up with respectable scores by the end of the round.

5) Some projectiles, while they do great damage, give abysmally low points. Rollers and slow-moving clusters are such weapons. Quote: robiroboter, October 04, 2007 01:36 pm
How do you define 'good players'? What do you think about their strong points?
I cannot offer a definition of "good player," but I can offer some characteristics I've found as admirable in a player:

1) never gives up - just because your health is 3, doesn't mean you should run and hide. Be aggressive! If you're unpredictable, the other players won't be able to anticipate your actions.

2) able to "place" the ball well. I'm not talking about "accuracy," because it's hard to be accurate when you generally do not know where the opponent will end up. But I'm sure you've had shots in which it was placed well, and it did damage to multiple opponents and brought you high scores. Now - I'm not a shabby player, and I place my shots so that I attack the entire opponent field with one shot. I'm good enough to aim that well on the first throw. The rest of the shots are just rote - and so people might think I'm a noob because of that. For those reasons, I also have different aims to pick off opponents one at a time.

3) able to choose the correct ball for the correct application. Obviously choosing a bouncer in a confined space near teammates is a poor choice, UNLESS you're absolutely sure that your ball won't bounce into them, and kill only oppenents instead. Or choosing a cluster over a bouncer so that it'll fragment at or around the opponent before travelling off the board.

4) able to make minute (pronounced my-noot) adjustments to hit a target that moves slightly. Again, I'm not touting my own greatness; but I make pixel-sized adjustments to my shots. It makes a difference on the other side as I hit the opponent on the near-side or far-side of a small hill.

5) able to decide which oppenent is an immediate threat and which aren't. If he thinks YOU'RE his biggest threat, he'll come after you first. This may piss you off as you may be quickly eliminated. But stay on and watch the rest of the game to see what he does after he gets you.

6) understands that health points are an asset of his team, but knows that reducing opponent health is also important. Sometimes you might walk into the firing line of this "great" teammate and he may TK you. If you don't move, he may continue to do so in order to quickly eliminate the competition. If he is able to aim alternately, he might do so.

7) is aggressive when warranted. I know some players who actively come over onto the opponent side of the grid. This is confusing to them as all get out. But it's 5 times more dangerous as well, since their teammates are aiming for that general vicinity too.

8) knows that getting hit is inevitable, and can "weather" the damage. There are some boards in which he will be pummeled, but won't move, because he knows he is able to beat down the opponents faster than they can hit him... He may end up with 10 life points, but may have won the round. As a corollary, this person would know WHEN to move, to live for another volley.

9) knows his weapons and uses them weapons creatively. A roller is not the ONLY weapon which rolls, a bouncer is not the ONLY weapon which bounces... Even the spanner, when used properly, can kill a 100 health opponent in 1 shot (without help from anyone else). Things like that.

10) can place his weapon, anywhere on the map. He would know which weapon would get there and how, so as to obtain max points and chooses it with care.

The chat bar is fine, but in my opinion using the chatbar detracts from your accuracy - especially when pixel-level adjustments make a great diffenrence. I never use the chatbar, and rarely change balls in the middle of a board unless I'm moving from one position to another. I'm sure some of the great players you've played against were very "quiet" in that they never had a retort - you may have even thought them as bots who came to destroy your fun. It might just be a really, really good player who is intent on good aim and strategy rather than conversing...

There may be others, if I can think of them.
