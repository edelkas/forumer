map theory, eh? there's no theory that you can write down that, when applied, will make a good map. the parameters for a good action map are way too broad to be confined to just a written theory. mapmaking also takes innovation, which cannot really be anticipated by theories. In the end, if you try to create a "map-making theory" it either attempts to demonstrate theoretically the concepts that compose a good map (which it certainly cannot do) or it demonstrates practically the basic concepts that go behind making a map, in which case it is useless, as it is not a theory anymore, and the basics are already known to almost everyone. this latter course is the path this "theory" has taken. it explains basic concepts of mapmaking, and in so doing cannot improve the skills of any author beyond the basic stage. to explore a higher stage of mapmaking, the one that the true masters are on, is impossible for any theory to do. practical arts are always dictated by theories, but this practical art of mapmaking has a theory which cannot be written with words. it can only be felt. it is the theory of innovation, and of creativity. the usage of creativity follows a theory, but what theory does it follow? it cannot be written with words. innovation is also a practical art, and thus has a theory behind it, but what is that theory? it cannot be written with words. all in all, this attempt of ours to create a mapmaking "theory" is doomed to fail and in the end, can serve no purpose besides fueling our egos with the fact that we, the masters, have created a mapmaking theory for ourselves.
