#2324 1 2005-03-27T18:14:29Z Kobus
It seems to me that there are two basic types of N levels (not counting things like bug maps and DDAs): Perfection, and Analysis.
Perfection levels give you a very clear objective and a very clear way to accomplish it, but make that one method extremely difficult: pixel-perfect jumps, barely avoiding lasers and missiles, all that jazz. Trying to beat a perfection level frequently involves inputting almost the exact same series of actions, over and over, hoping that your jumps and dodges will land on exactly the right frames. Sometimes hundreds of times in a row. The distilled essence of perfection levels is tktktk's Simple Challenge series (take a look at #6, #7, and #12 in particular). More often than not, these levels are far more frustrating and annoying than they are fun.

Analysis levels give you a large, frequently open-ended area, and make you figure out for yourself what you have to do. Enemies are used more as puzzle items than twitch opponents, forcing you to figure out which route to take to avoid them or where to hide to survive hails of gunfire. Trying to beat an analysis level frequently involves altering your path each time, until you find one (possibly of many) that will let you get to the goal. A good (if easy) example of an analysis level is 8-1 (Plan plan plan). There is what at first seems like a perfection segment trying to avoid the two chaingun drones, but it's a matter of getting their attention, then hiding behind blocks, and running through open areas while they're distracted.
Analysis levels, to me, are far more interesting and engaging than perfection levels. Unfortunately, it seems like about 95% of the levels (in metanet AND numa) are perfection.

I don't know where I'm going with all this. Just making some observations.
