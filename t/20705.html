#390541 6 2007-12-18T05:16:22Z astro
Don't establish the whole plot structure at the start of the book (eg. in Lord of the Rings, Frodo doesn't initially go off with the intent of destroying the ring, he just goes to see the elves). Be sparing with adverbs (eg. said John angrily) and instead try to set the scene with actions and events. Use the active voice (eg. John broke the window, rather than the window was broken by John). Be sparing, creative, interesting. The internet is full of good writing advice, as are good novels.

I would also, like condog, be prepared to read through and offer advice. I know how hard it can be to judge how good your own book is because you know what you mean and what's going to happen but other people don't.

A few tips: Don't have the entire story from the perspective of one character (unless it is in first person) as it can get quite repetitive. Instead do some sections from different people's point of view and emphasise how different their views on the world are. (Does that make sense?)
Also try not to make the storyline too predictable but don't make it the other extreme either. Books can lose there interest if everything that happens is too unlikely.
