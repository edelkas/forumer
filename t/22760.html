These represent the opinions that I could remember when I wrote this post. I have plenty of other ideas about what should be where on which list, but I couldn't remember them at the time. My memory is going at sixteen.
Also, things people already don't like (say, oil companies) and things people already like (say, Wikipedia) are not included. What's the point of me saying "I agree"? This is full of "I disagree", damnit.
Things scythe33 approves of:
1. Patagonia
If environmentalism were a competition, they'd outrun everyone and refuse to compete. Patagonia can claim partial responsibility for more revolutionary ideas than most companies have products; they were the first to break the traditional corporate culture, the first to use polyester for climbing and hiking wear, the first to use epoxy in surfboards, and the list goes on. They're also brutally honest; despite being one of the cleanest companies around, they'll admit upfront that every product you buy from them causes environmental harm, so they ask you to limit your purchases. I could say more, but I don't damn feel like it, and they can really speak for themselves.
2. Linux
Needs no introduction, does it? Continuing to outperform everyone else in just about everything, and doing it for free, allowed this one to have a high spot on the list. Serving as the inspiration for dozens more open-source projects doesn't hurt, either, and neither does compatibility with the wonderful music player Amarok.
3. XKCD
One would expect something titled with large, capitalized nonsense to suck, but this doesn't. Randall impresses every time. It absolutely defies explanation, even more so than Penny Arcade.
4. Democratic countries in Asia
Take a look at this picture right here. Note how India and Mongolia are surrounded by red and yellow. Both set what is often called "a good example". India faces the challenge of controlling a billion people, and Mongolia is surrounded by two of the worst (from a "danger to the free world" standpoint) dictatorships in the 21st century.
5. Project Wonderful
It's wonderful! Really, if I had a site, these are the ads I'd put up.
Things scythe33 disapproves of:
1. The People's Republic of China
I never really liked communism, I never really liked totalitarianism, and I never really liked dishonesty, so the PRC's totalitarianism branded as communism leaves a bad taste in my mouth. Extra raspberries for the block on Wikipedia. Add counterfeit products and child labor, and they've jumped ahead of...
2. Apple Computer
Apple earned its spot the easy way: piss me off. It's taken weeks to configure my music library to play on my cell phone, and it has presumably taken weeks for others, too. See, when Apple came out with iTunes, I thought they were finally over their "our software only works on proprietary hardware" nonsense, but music you buy from them will only play on iPods or a few Motorola (ick!) phones. To make matters worse, they sent the makers of the program QTFairUse (which allows music to be played on my phone), which was relatively little-known and hardly posed a threat, a cease-and-desist letter they can't afford to fight, even though QTFairUse does not violate the DMCA. Posing as the ideal alternative to Windows when Linux is several times better isn't cool, either, nor is their attempt to sell things on brand name and flashiness.
3. Gaia
As the second-biggest online messageboard after 2channel, Gaia is full of-you guessed it-4chan memes, and unlike the hate machine, they don't try and keep people from their site from spreading that crap all over the Internet. Which is exactly what happens, and all the noise drowns out the signal. Serving as the perfect bad example, they win the Third Worst Forum Award, after d2jsp and 4chan, but get on the list higher because of their popularity. Who would have thought the new Myspace would have been even worse?
4. Second Life
SomethingAwful can easily make my point for me: people on Second life are weird. Check the Weekend Web article on Second Life; I don't need to say any more.
5. Vladimir Putin
Everyone's favorite ruthless dictator with access to nearly half the planet's nuclear missiles. That's just what the doctor ordered.

Okay, I'm bored now. Writing this was fun, and damaging enough to my reputation ("but I like apple!", "hey, i visit Gaia!") as it is.

In fact, maybe I just wrote this to give Dave more reasons to make fun of me. You'll never know, will you?!
