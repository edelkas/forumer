An argument that I see so frequently used by theists is that atheism leads to moral relativism, and that theism does not.

There are some problems with this.

1. The Euthyphro dilemma.

"Is what is moral commanded by God because it is moral, or is it moral because it is commanded by God?"

The first part of the dilemma is that if morality is commanded by God because it is moral, then there is an external basis for morality, beyond God, and human kind should also be able to discern this, given enough time and knowledge.

The second part of the dilemma is that what is moral is moral because it is commanded by God, then "moral" and "good" are just arbitrary values and subject completely to God's whim.

2. Inherent moralities exist.

Even if God descended to Earth right now, and commanded upon us that slavery was morally good, most of us would instantly realize that this is not so. This could mean, as far as I can discern, one of two things.

a. There is some rational or intuitive external basis for morality, and humans have knowledge of it.

b. Morality is completely arbitrary and subject to emotional values, and we are all therefore emotionally similar, while God is emotionally different.

3. The indeterminate nature of divine mandates.

Divine mandates are not useful as the only source of morality, because they are intrinsically vague and indeterminate.

For example, the sixth commandment says "Thou shalt not kill," but this is far too vague to be the only source of morality concerning killing. Does it mean that you can't kill fetuses? Or is God referring only to people? or Plants? or all three? or None?

These types of laws cannot be the only basis for morality, because they leave gray areas and complete blanks.
