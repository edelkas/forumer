#47785 2 2005-01-24T18:55:48Z Tsukatu
I should think so, unless the problem is within your hard drive. For example, and this is using a Windows machine, actually...
One day my computer said to itself, "I think I'll delete crucial system files."
And it did.
I couldn't start Windows, and I was stuck in DOS. Because I know how to get around in DOS, I started looking for problems. Well, as it turned out, some system files weren't in their correct positions, and others were missing altogether. Actually, one wasn't being correctly read, despite its normal extension and correct folder placement.
So anyway, the rest of my hard drive was fine... I just needed some method of transfering it.
So basically, if you can figure out where exactly the problem is, you can (using DOS, Macs have that, right?) see if the rest of your hard drive is intact. Then what you do is put in a removable hard drive in addition to your old one, install it through DOS (quite possible, though sometimes frustrating), and then transfer all of your files, except for all that C:\Windows crap onto the removable hard drive. Then... buy a new computer. Or get your old one fixed by professionals. But sometimes it's cheaper to just buy a new one. Then just stick your removable into the new computer, and you're set.
