This is a competition based around the well known phrase, "Less is More".
In my opinion, this is quite true, and this challenge will test your skills in creating great maps that are very *very* simple.

Here are the rules:

1) No more than 5 tiles
2) No more than 5 objects
3) No more than 3 entries per person.
4) Demo must be posted by author of under 500 frames

N counts as an object
A door (and its respective switch) counts as one object

Judging:

A very basic judging system: Out of 50, we will rate the map, taking into consideration how fun and exciting it is.

I'd like to preceed my judging with a short note.

The rating I give the maps is roughly based on what I'd give the map in the NUMA ratings system. That means a 50 is equivalent to a 5 - so, basically, I'd just like people to be prepared for lower ratings. Making small maps is hard, and as such the maps are less likely to rate well on NUMA, and therefore likewise for me. So, good luck to everyone.

Nevermore, Capcom
This is going sound ridiculous, but I actually rather like the tileset in this one. Well, I think it maximises the use of tiles rather well considering only 5 could be used. But the tileset isn't important, it's the gameplay that it's all about. Well, this map definitely suffers from the lack of objects. It doesn't feel complete. The drone usage is an interesting but slightly uneconomic use of objects - and in this contest ecocomy is really important. 3 objects for just the one small section - well, it could have been spent better otherwise. But nonetheless, this isn't bad for a first entry. 23 out of 50

47percent, The Slaughter of the Inner Child
This success and also the failure of this map is the use of the entire landscape. It's obvious really - who needs 5 tiles when you can just make the big border round the outside part of the level? Now that's what I can economic. But unfortunately, one rocket is just too small to cover such a large space effectively and to your little ninja's danger. However, the top part of the map is more interesting, and overall the map does work. Just about. But there is no question that it feels incomplete. 27 out of 50

tl, i need more space!
Well, it's impressive that this really does function as a puzzle. Unfortunately, solving the puzzle is more a case of persistance as opposed to deep thought. Just keep on jumping until you discover the ideal route - or at least, that's what I did, and it didn't take too long. It's a nice little map though, and the way the thwumps have been arranged poses an intruiging challlenge. 28 out of 50
