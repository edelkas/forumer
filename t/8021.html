#151599 16 2006-03-04T19:50:31Z iangb
@Evil:That's sort of what I meant. If you've ever played around with UnrealEd, you'll understand it a bit more.

In that, the bot's pathfinding works thusly: In the map is an invisible network of 'nodes', linked together. The bot's 'midn' cycle works something like this:
1)Find where the bot is, and the status of the bot, and the positions/distances (via the node network) of all items.
2)Decide what the bot wants to do - ie get health, shoot a player, etc...
3)Using the network and a relatively simple algorithm, find the shortest route, going from node to node, between the bot and it's target node.
4)move bot along the path to the target node. This is the hard bit (with jumping calculations, etc),
5)redo from start.

With N the ninja can get to different places depending on it's speed, so the 'potential' network would be much, much harder to lay out.
#151600 17 2006-03-04T19:54:52Z Teh Dude
Quote: Evil___, March 04, 2006 12:06 pm
iangb's got the point there. A pathfinding algo for drones works fine because the drones can only move in 4 directions. The ninja can move down to 1 pixel! The only way around this would be to have the map-maker program the AI themselves - something that might take ages and then would be just following a set path.

That is not why pathfinding is hard. In any given frame, there are only three things (excluding kill and boss mode) that the ninja (i.e. the player) can do; press left, press right, press jump. This gives three moves, while drones have four. The drones do not use pathfinding, they just move in a straight line until they hit a wall or detect the ninja.

The pathfinding difficulty is that the places you can get to from the current frame, depend on things like how fast the ninja is moving, in which direction, how long the jump button has been held for, etc. If the ninja was like a chess piece and moved in fixed jumps from square to square, then it would be easy to create a graph for path finding as this graph would be the same for all possible sequences of previous moves. In N, the possible next moves depend on what moves have been made in the past.
