A: First off, the universe exists. It is here now in its present state.
B: Actually, we can't know that for certain. The universe is only experienced through human sensation, and our human senses are imperfect. Even our thoughts aren't perfect, so even if our senses were objectively accurate, we could still be processing those senses incorrectly.

A: Alright, how about we look at it this way: you exist, even if I don't, and even if the rest of the universe doesn't. Not your body, not even necessarily your senses, but your consciousness exists.

B: It's true!
A: There is no point continuing this. You need to make the assumption that the way we perceive reality is faithful to what is real before we can gain any ground in this discussion.


A: I'm going to prove to you that 2 + 2 is equal to 4.
B: I'd love to see you try.
A: First off, we can see that 2 is really just twice the number 1, or two individual units.
B: Actually, we can't know that for certain. Mathematics is, after all, an invention of fallible humans, and there could easily be some glaring error in mathematics that we haven't discovered yet that makes even statements like 1 + 1 = 2 defunct.
A: ...
B: What? It's true!
A: Alright, how about we look at it this way: there is a dot, and then there is another dot. This is the total number of dots you have on one side. We're going to do this by counting.
B: The problem here is that you're going to be forced at one point to represent the situation with mathematical terms. Your entire argument is futile because it is based on something that is impossible to prove infallible - mathematics.
A: ...
B: It's true!
