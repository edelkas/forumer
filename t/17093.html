As many of you may have noticed, maestro threw together an interesting compilation of NUMA map rating trends. I know it says not to read to deep in to these graphs, but I think they are nonetheless interesting and warrant some discussion.

One of the most interesting things I noticed is that 27% of all the votes have been 5's. The next nearest percentage grouping is 4's with a meager 16%. This could be interpreted in several ways. I suppose some people will say it's because people tend to overrate maps, and looking at the graphs, this is an argueable statement. The vast majority of ratings are over 2.5's, and I don't think most maps on NUMA deserve this high of a mark.
However, another way this could be interpreted, and the way I prefer to believe, is that the better maps are being rated the most. For instance, look at the top-rated maps, such as AfternoonDragon (which is a 4.5 with over 680 rates). Other top-rated maps also have a large amount of rates associate with them. Surely this means that the best maps on NUMA are receiving the most attention, as would be expected.
Feel free to share your opinion about the trends, and thanks a lot to maestro for taking the time to put this all together.

#316344 18 2007-04-28T01:56:51Z Atilla
I rarely rate below 2.5, because I don't often play maps which don't seem interesting. After all, when I browse NUMA I'm looking for maps I'll enjoy. I think a lot of people do the same thing, which would explain the above-average average. It's a selection effect - people look for good maps, more good maps are rated than bad ones, and thus the average is higher than you would expect. This can be used to explain the average rating graph:

Atilla's Speculative History of Ratings:

In the beginning, there were few maps, and most of them got rated. This lead to a relatively low average rating. As more maps became available, people stopped voting for maps which were obviously substandard, which caused the average rating to become higher. Unfortunately, because people were now seeing only high ratings, it became perceived that maps must be rated higher and higher, which lead to an overly-lenient period in which the average rating almost reached four. After numerous complaints and the introduction of rating guidelines, the average descended to a more reasonable level.

As for the high number of fives and zeros, I would say the fives are mostly the result of people browsing the top-rated, with a bit of multi-accounting and "mates rates" on the side. The zeros are probably due to the bombardment of offensive or stolen maps with 0s, and sniping. I note that the proportion of 0s and 5s has decreased since the arrival of Maesweep, which I believe is due in part to their efforts to stop abuse, as well as the introduction of the voting guidelines.
