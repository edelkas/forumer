#24843 30 2004-07-13T22:54:50Z Guest
The reason that you can't gain much height by jumping off of a bounce block is just the way that N (as a game) is. When the ninja is plummeted down onto a bounce block, it goes down quite a way, because the ninja has weight and velocity to push the block down. When the block is coming back up though, it only has the velocity transferred to it by the ninja from the original landing, and friction opposes this force. Thus it only moves up a tiny bit, even though it moved down alot (most of the force was expended in moving the block down). And you will only gain as much height in your jump as the block gained height when moving up.

This to me is a good illustration how N (as a game) differs from say, Mario Bros.

Well.. If you want to get technical, it's the energy that matters as to how far the block compresses. One could explain the block phenomenon by saying that the block simply lost energy to friction or other forces, as n00854180t has pointed out. HOWEVER, this does not explain the block's movement. If you jump onto the block, it quickly sinks, and IDEALLY it would then oscillate about a center point which is defined by the distance it would compress if the ninja was placed on the block and stood still. It would be reasonable to expect in real life for the block to oscillate, but for the oscillation to slow down exponentially. The block should come up at approximately the same speed at which it same down.

When you jump back off the block in the game, it undergoes this oscillation as it should when the ninja stands on it, an inconsistency. But changing this property of bounce physics is not really worth it if anyone wants to use demos from past versions of games.

Even with this all said, one could argue that the block doesn't experience simple harmonic motion. It's ninja-harmonic motion.
