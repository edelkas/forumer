Are you serious, southpaw?
"Awareness of transfer of energy?"

While the definition of consciousness is hotly debated, the definition of "awareness" really isn't. "Awareness" only comes about when something has the right amount of cortical matter, which plants don't. Plants are an excellent example of an organism that is completely mechanical - the plant has absolutely no decision-making abilities or thoughts, consciousness, or awareness of any kind; all of its structure is devoted entirely to growing physically.
(For example, here's how vision is processed by your brain:
1) Your occipital lobe receives the input from your optic nerves, and sends it (mostly) in two different directions: the ventral pathway (toward the parietal lobe) and the dorsal pathway (toward the temporal lobe).
2) The ventral pathway identifies the object and the dorsal pathway determines its context. They both forward the information to your prefrontal lobe so you can actually make use of these identifications.
3) Your prefrontal lobe receives that information, decides its importance, and shifts it higher on your priority list based on how unlike the object is from what you were expecting. The entire process has so far been entirely unconscious.
4) AWARENESS: Information is sent back to your occipital lobe, specifically to your V1 area, which is in the extrastriate cortex. If this information does not get back to the occipital lobe, you are not aware of the visual stimulus.
...and that's how visual awareness comes about. People who can perform all of steps 1-3, but not 4, exhibit the "blindsight" phenomenon: they think they are completely blind (which makes sense because they have no awareness of any visual stimula), and yet if asked to guess about what they're looking at they'll get it right with a very, very high accuracy, as though they were able to see the stimulus normally, but they still insist that they're just "blindly" guessing.)
In short, "awareness" is achieved through a specific pattern of neural activity. Since plants don't really have any meaningful amount of nerves, it's really not very reasonable to say that they have any sort of "awareness" whatsoever. They are purely mechanical; they have as much "awareness of energy transfer" as a clock does.
