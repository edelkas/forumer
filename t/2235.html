So, to start out, an explanation of determinism.

We will start with the dictionary definition: Determinism is the philosophical doctrine that every state of affairs, including every human event, act, and decision is the inevitable consequence of antecedent states of affairs.

What this means is that everything that is happening right now is 100 percent a consequence of what happened immediately before. And that chain will continue right on back as long as time itself.

Which means that 5 billion years ago, when presumably all that existed was just gasses and rocks and other inanimate stuff, the entire universe is governed completely by immutable physical laws of nature. So so the position, velocity, composition interaction etc. of everything that existed at that time is completely and 100 percent determined. There can be no deviance from what the laws determine.

Well, then naturally, we have a nice chain that will continue to lead right on up to today, tomorrow, and the rest of time. Which means that what you are doing right now was already set in stone by events that happened 5 billion years ago.

So, since you don't have the power to change the laws of nature, or the distant past, then you don't have any ability to change what you do right now either.

And, of course, if you don't have the ability to change what you do right now, how can you possibly think that you have free will?

Here's the argument in logical form jsut so you get an idea of what you have to argue against to save your free will (if you want to).

P= the distant past
L = the laws of Nature, whatever they may be.
F = the future

Determinism states P + L --> F (the arrow stands for "entail", so read the past plus the laws of nature entail the future)

So, the argument goes as follows

P+L-->F
P, and P is not up to you
L, and L is not up to you
therefore F, and F is not up to you.

If F is not up to you, then you do not have free will.

#50343 41 2005-02-14T02:25:05Z Daddaluma
So, a few points to make.

Determinism isn't really the same thing as "predetermined". Predetermined has a sense of all things being destined to be the way that they are, as if something outside of natural physics has set the course of things.

Whereas determinism just states that the current state of affairs is a natural logical consequence of the previous. So really, this "time slice" is the direct and inevitable result of the previous "time slice". It just happens that that previous time slice was the direct and inevtiable consequence of the next previous "time slice".

There's nothing mysterious about it. You can just follow the causal chain back forever.

So, once again, what this all means is that once you have a starting point, which can be any time slice from before humans were around, then my initial argument will go through to its conclusion.

Some of you are saying that your actions are based on your choices. You are completely right. My point is, though, that at the time you made the choice, the other options available to you were illusory. It was not physically or metaphysically possible for you to choose anything other than the option you in fact picked.

Where the problem lies for most of you is in the fact that the path always and necessarily follows your choices, and thus you still want to say that you have free will.

So, for ease of disscussion I will give an example and then bold the real question at hand.

You are deciding between drinking coffee and orange juice in the morning. You go with coffee, but you could have just as easily gone for orange juice, or so you think. Your choice of coffee could have been determined 100,000 million years ago. You couldn't have chosen the orange juice, no matter how easily you think you could have gone the other way. It's been set in stone by the laws of nature since 100,000 million years ago.

So, I do believe that the argument i posted is pretty much inescapably airtight, with the exception of the final step (i.e. the "therefore you have no freewill" part).

As I pointed out before, the core of this problem lies in the question, "If you could have done other than you in fact did, then how can you say you freely did what you did?" This question clearly implies that for an action to be free, we have to have been able to do other than we did. It seems like a valid definition of free will, which is why the initial argument against free will holds so much force. It's easy to accept that you need to have been able to make other choices.

But I don't think that that is actually what's important for free will. Rather, I would define a free action in this way.

"If I had wanted to choose option 2, then I would have." Where option 2 is any option that you did not in fact choose.

If you can say this about a choice you made, then I contend that this is enough to make that choice a free one. It took me an entire semester to come around to this though. I was really convinced that if we actually cannot make the other choice, then that's really all that matters. I wouldn't have any of this semantic "would have" bullshit. It was "could have" or unfree for me.

But, if you think about it, this new "redefining" of free actions fits our normal usage perfectly. It rules out scenarios like being in the locked room as unfree, which it should. Even if you had wanted to leave the room, you wouldn't have been able to. It's locked. You can't freely choose to stay in a locked room. And it keeps in all the actions we would normally consider free, like deciding between coffee and orange juice.

It basically comes down to contraint. If a choice you made wasn't constrained, then it was free.

There you go. That's my take on this whole shindig.

How would any past occurences of nature predict our future actions? (This is what I've interpreted from Daddaluma.) That makes absolutely no sense to me.
"Unbreakable laws of physics" is the answer.
Let's say you have a pool table with a bunch of balls on it, and you hit the cue really hard into another ball. Based on the velocity and the angle at which it hit the other ball, the balls would roll around and hit each other in a certain way, and every single one of these is predictable with simple math and a little understanding of mechanical physics. If you were to then set up the table exactly as before and hit the cue ball exactly the same way, then you'd notice that exactly the same thing happens: the end state is the same. No matter how many times you do this, the end state will always be the same as long as you put the table back into the starting state and hit the cue exactly the same way.
Now think of your brain as a pool table hundreds of thousands of miles to a side, with hundreds of billions of balls. Some stimulus comes along, grabs a pool stick, and hits the cue a certain way, and this causes a flurry of motion among the balls. But the thing is, the paths of every single ball is still quite predictable, and if you were to set each and every one of the balls exactly the same way and ask the stimulus to hit the cue the same way he did the first time, you would always end up with the same end state.
So if you're standing in a room, picking your nose and staring at a wall, and then someone throws a baseball at your crotch, you'd react to the ball by covering your groin, widening your eyes, releasing adrenaline into your bloodstream, etc. And then if you were to turn back time so that you were back in the same position again, picking your nose exactly the same way, staring at exactly the same spot on the wall as before, and with no memory of the ball, and then the ball is thrown at your crotch again in exactly the same way and at exactly the same time as before, then your reaction will be completely, 100% identical to your previous reaction: you'll take just as long to put your hands over your groin in exactly the same manner, your eyes will widen just as wide as before in exactly the same amount of time, and your adrenal glands will swell just as much and just as quickly as they did last time. This will happen no matter how many times you reverse time and play it forward.
THIS MEANS that your outcome is defined entirely by your constituent bits (namely the subatomic particles that make up every atom of every cell of every tissue that you'd consider part of your body) and how they react to the quite rigid laws of physics. If put in such a situation, your quarks will react exactly the same way every single time. You have as much a say in what you do as the 7 ball does when it's hit by the pool cue, which is to say you have no choice in what happens to you whatsoever.
And by extension, every stimulus was caused by something else and moved in a predictable way to stimulate you, and if you trace back all of those causes, you'll realize that things could only have ended up a single way since the beginning of time, just as everything that happens on the pool table is determined the moment the cue ball is struck (the balls can only move a certain way, and that's exactly what they'll do). Everything about you, every thing you think, every emotion you feel, every nervous tick and virus you get, every way you influence your surroundings and people around you, has been determined at the beginning of time.

That's determinism in a nutshell, and science can back it up fully so far (and that doesn't look like it's going to change).
