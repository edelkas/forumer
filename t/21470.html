#409620 13 2008-02-15T06:37:33Z Keron Cyst
YES.

(I mean, to what T3chno posted above. )

Don't make the same mistakes we did! Join clubs as SOON as possible and as much as possible. Try to become presidents of activities and stuff (be shameless in what you do, perhaps). Be known in the school. Join student council. For example (I don't know if you're a super-overachiever like the rest of the people I tend to mingle with or not), all the Ivy League universities' applicants already have straight-As, 2100+ on their SATs, etc. and there is no distinguishment academy-wise. Be the first white dude to lead Asian Student Union, as one of my friends just achieved this year, or something. START a club on your own, in fact, as one of my friends did three years ago (the Middle Eastern Club; incidentally, he's Syrian). THAT's the stuff people look for.

Or volunteer. Accumulate hundreds of hours of community service after school and/or on the weekends at a hospital or something. I stupidly did that only during one summer, filing, typing, sorting away like a drone, and achieved a mere 126 hrs. Community service & extracurricular activities are number 1.5 on your to-succeed-in list. (Marching band is required? That's really interesting...)

You're really smart to ask this. Get all the stuff out of an old senior like me, ha.

The lit. books are fine. Difficult stuff like Homer's The Odyssey can be adapted to once you force yourself through the first four chapters or so; by then you basically mold to the writer's style, and they become easier to read. Summer reading is simple; a chapter or three a day suffices, with a notebook next to you to track neat quotes. Some of the books may be boring, but some of them are nice choices and you may become interested on your own in your reading.

I too took nothing but Honors or AP courses for literature. We seem to go through somewhat less than ~7 novels every year, 2 or 3 being instantly covered via summer reading, from what I can recall. It's not that much, considering the months.

Yo puedo hablar espaol un poco. I took three years. The textbooks are very... well, academic-feeling. It's pretty much memorization, although some words and sentence formats interlace through the languages. I'm not sure of what "advice" I can give in particular on that, than simply reviewing vocabulary and grammatical concepts every other night. If all else fails (usually it did in the case of my classmates and I), learning fell to raw memorization of the stuff. But languages are big in today's world; if you feel you can really make something practical out of it, by all means pursue it.

Health and P.E. are required (for me, P.E. goes only to sophomore year... heh). In health (which I took over the summer with a friend right before we incame as freshmen), we just learned about drugs and stuff. High school P.E. (well, mine anyways) is rather similar to middle school P.E.



Put simply, I found high school to be like this, in comparison to middle school: MS teachers are aware that you have others who give you homework, so you don't have very much. But HS teachers are tougher, in fact a bit more like elementary school, simply concentrated to their particular field (of lit., history, science, math, foreign lang.... a little less: drama, music, electives - computer courses, ceramics, etc.). And to eat up your remaining time, you've got plenty of clubs & other activities, which almost always meet weekly during lunch, to check out; Interact and Key Club (community service clubs) in particular come to mind. You'll want to head for those two first.
