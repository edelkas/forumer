#57648 3 2005-04-01T23:22:49Z Guest
hi,
we chose that map because:
a ) we thought it was a really clever and cool idea which we'd never seen before
b ) we wanted to include one map from everyone
c ) while other maps had the same idea, they were also much more difficult and/or jam-packed with other stuff.


we thought that level was excellent because it was a cool, original idea, and a nice, not-too-hard level. we gave preference to maps with fewer enemies because we feel those are maps which are more "fun" to play, especially for people who aren't super-skilled. also, they run a lot faster. and, we needed more of them -- super-hard maps are a dime a dozen on NUMA, but moderately-hard maps are much more rare, so we used every good one we could find.

we heavily biased our choices towards the easier levels, because (even WITH that bias) the user maps have far more super-hard levels than usual. for instance, out of the (i beleive) 3 or 4 maximo maps, only one of them is super-hard; the others are easier and/or more interesting than difficult (i.e "Up Down" is, i think, really nice: it's simple yet effective, and not too hard).


i'm surprised, because "full circle" was one of my favorite user maps. it was rather challenging the first few times i played it, and i really liked the idea -- it was clever and used the game engine in different and weird ways.. and (perhaps most importantly) it was subtle. it was a bit serene and minimal, and basically just a cool application of that concept, with nothing superfluous.

yes, there are other rocket-chasing-you maps, but this one is _just_ rocket-chasing-you, without any other enemies or traps or anything to distract from the idea. it's just you and the rocket.


the thing with a lot of user maps is that they tended towards the "97241687536 enemies that you just have to run+jump like crazy through". they were very cluttered, super-difficult, and not so much fun as purely frustrating.

while a couple maps like that are fun, once we had chosen a few of our favorites of that type, we couldn't keep piling them on. we had to diversify, especially for the non-hardcore players. those maps may be fun when you're playing them one at a time in debug mode, but.. an entire episode of those and you'd either kill your computer, or us


so, as a result, we often chose simpler, less difficult maps because that's what we needed more of.

raigan

#57889 13 2005-04-02T19:50:57Z Guest
hi,
about 80-4: this _is_ hard in that it's very tense and there are a lot of things to do, however each individual task is rather easy, so we thought that a novice would have a better chance against it than some of the other levels.

"mooney suzuki" was (we thought) very different from the maps we make. it was great because of how it used thwumps as the main focus of the level -- not just as an enemy or to guide the player in a direction, but as the actual geometry of the level, instead of tiles. "leap of faith" was an easy level, which also happened to be different/clever, and something we thought players may not have seen.


about AI: we just preferred coalition. AI was very similar to maps we make, whereas coalition had several parts we thought were cool: some interesting skill-jumps, along with some enemy evasion.


pressed for time: we were blown away by this level. we just thought that it was incredibly cool that someone made such a great, well-made level which was also totally unlike anything we'd ever seen before. yes, it used bugs, but unlike the maps we cut due to bugs, you didn't need to know about them (i.e a new user wouldn't get "teleporters", or know that you need to move horizontally on bounceblocks to get a big jump off of them, whereas it's very clear what's going on in this map). we just thought it was great.


PROBABLY we should have swapped 88-4 and 89-4. 88-4 was the last level for a long time, however we (stupidly) caved to popular opinion and moved "mount doom" to the end. we really should have done what we thought was best, but in this case we figured what's the harm in letting people get what they want. lesson learned.


bouncy blast, trap, and something were our favorite levels by those authors. also, again, they were not-crazy-hard, so it was easy for them to get in.


i'm not sure if anyone understands the problems faced in terms of getting the difficulty correct. we started with 100 levels, 56 of which were "crazy hard". in our columns, we only use 2-4 of that difficulty, usually as the 4th level in the last few episodes. having +50 of them is simply not going to work.

so, this skewed our choices. hard maps were competing with ~80 other maps to get in, while easier maps were competing with ~20. any map which was pleasing to play, not too hard, and by an author we hadn't yet included basically got in automatically out of necessity. we always tried to choose the easiest, yet "best" (most inventive and/or fun) level from each author; as it is, we still ended up with too many hard levels. had we not given preference to easy levels, the 80s would be unplayable.


the bottom line is that the vast majority of user-made levels were ( a ) to repeat yet again, made to be played individually with no time limit, which made them not work in episode format, and ( b ) very importantly, made for an audience of hard-core players, who understood bugs and trick-jumps and didn't mind having 98756 enemies going after them all at once.


we wanted as many people as possible to be able to see what our users are doing. the vast majority of N players can't make it past mid-column, so we felt it was important to make sure they had a fair chance of beating at least a few user-column episodes.


anyway.. i should probably get back to work
