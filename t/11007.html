My contention is that the statement "atheists believe they are above God" is at best misleading and at worst totally innaccurate and meaningless.

To keep things clearer, let us focus on the first of these for the moment; that is, I shall begin with an attempt to demonstrate that "atheists believe they are above God" does not give an accurate impression of the beliefs of atheists.

Firstly, if you asked atheists whether they believed they were above God, I'm fairly sure most would say something like "No, I don't believe in God." Furthermore, defenitions of atheism rarely refer to believing you are "above God"; atheism simply refers to the lack of belief in gods, or belief in lack of gods. The statement "atheists believe themselves to be above God", then, is not useful in understanding an atheistic worldview, because it does not describe their core beliefs.

To demonstrate this point further, let us consider other worldviews. If atheists can be said to think themselves above God because they do not believe in Him, then it must be equally true that Christians think themselves above Ganesha (a Hindu god) because they do not believe in him. However, what does the statement "Christians believe they are above Ganesha" tell us about Christian doctrine? Next to nothing. If you were asked to describe the beliefs of Christians, I doubt you would say "Christians believe themselves above Ganesha" at any point, because this is only an incidental consequence. If Hinduism had never existed and there was no concept (note: concept, not the thing itself) of the god Ganesha for Christians to believe themselves above, Christianity would remain unchanged. So it is with atheism - when you say "atheists believe themselves to be above God", you appear to be connecting atheism with the concept of a Christian (or at least monotheistic) god. This is extraneous to atheism; as in the analogy with Christianity and Hinduism, atheism can exist perfectly well without the concept of any specific god.

Finally, the statement "atheists believe they are above God" is misleading because it implies that this is a belief specific to atheism, which is untrue. All non-Christians (or non-Abrahamic religion followers, depending on your intepretation) also fail to believe in your God, and by the same logic must believe themselves above Him - though they may, of course, believe in their own gods.

My contention is that the statement "atheists believe they are above God" is at best misleading and at worst totally innaccurate and meaningless.

To keep things clearer, let us focus on the first of these for the moment; that is, I shall begin with an attempt to demonstrate that "atheists believe they are above God" does not give an accurate impression of the beliefs of atheists.

Firstly, if you asked atheists whether they believed they were above God, I'm fairly sure most would say something like "No, I don't believe in God." Furthermore, defenitions of atheism rarely refer to believing you are "above God"; atheism simply refers to the lack of belief in gods, or belief in lack of gods. The statement "atheists believe themselves to be above God", then, is not useful in understanding an atheistic worldview, because it does not describe their core beliefs.

To demonstrate this point further, let us consider other worldviews. If atheists can be said to think themselves above God because they do not believe in Him, then it must be equally true that Christians think themselves above Ganesha (a Hindu god) because they do not believe in him. However, what does the statement "Christians believe they are above Ganesha" tell us about Christian doctrine? Next to nothing. If you were asked to describe the beliefs of Christians, I doubt you would say "Christians believe themselves above Ganesha" at any point, because this is only an incidental consequence. If Hinduism had never existed and there was no concept (note: concept, not the thing itself) of the god Ganesha for Christians to believe themselves above, Christianity would remain unchanged. So it is with atheism - when you say "atheists believe themselves to be above God", you appear to be connecting atheism with the concept of a Christian (or at least monotheistic) god. This is extraneous to atheism; as in the analogy with Christianity and Hinduism, atheism can exist perfectly well without the concept of any specific god.

Finally, the statement "atheists believe they are above God" is misleading because it implies that this is a belief specific to atheism, which is untrue. All non-Christians (or non-Abrahamic religion followers, depending on your intepretation) also fail to believe in your God, and by the same logic must believe themselves above Him - though they may, of course, believe in their own gods.

You know, if you want to get technical, atheists that say:

"I don't believe in God".

"I don't believe in something that doesn't exist".

Considering they believe that God doesn't exist.

So saying that they are above God=

"I don't believe in something that doesn't exist but I'm above it, contradicting what I just said and makes me sound like in fact I do believe in God but I am above it. Therefore I am not an atheist even though I am an atheist..."

Which should be what Atilla was trying to argue.
