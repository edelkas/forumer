Here's just some ideas I had while playing n. The likelihood of these being implemented are infinitesimally slim, I realize; if I post 'em maybe they'll go away and I can start thinking about new stuff. Maybe they'll do some good and inspire new ideas in someone else. I dunno.

1) Vector-based maps, instead of block-based. Give map creators the ability to create completely freeform maps using tools similar to the pen tool(s) in Illustrator, including a pathfinder for boolean operations to create complex shapes easily. For an example map, see attached image.
Why it won't be implemented: I'm going out on a limb and guessing one would have to rewrite n entirely to do this. That, and giving users increased ability to create maps gives them increased ability to create bad maps.

2) Wrap-around levels. Deleting the borders in ned open up those sides to create bigger levels. Removing the sides creates a "cylindrical" level, removing the bottom creates a bottomless pit, removing the top creates a sky.

3) Attaching "inert" objects to enemies. This would allow level designers to create moving landscapes, like the sliding platforms in Super Mario Bros. Likely combinations would be attaching a vertical wall of one-way platforms to a thwump (crushtastic!) or surrounding a zap drone (non-seeking) with one-way platforms.

4) "Duck Hunt" style 2 player action.
One of my fondest NES memories is playing Duck Hunt with my brother. At that time it was a somewhat of a secret that the on-screen ducks can be controlled with the 2p controller. Using certain manuevers, I rarely had more fun confounding my brother's attempts to shoot some skeet.
This would be a hoot in n. While "player 1" uses the keyboard to control the ninja, "player 2" would use the mouse to target missles and gauss turrets, clicking to trigger zap drones, floor drones and thwumps to dash.

Sorry, I had to get that out. Different sized tiles have been suggested many times before and probably will be in the future. The most commonly requested is a one-tile curve, and thats also the one with the best reason not to exist--it would mean double the polygons which would mean twice as much physics to generate which translates to lag on even the best computers. The second-most is the top row, and while they wouldn't change the physics they would still cause some problems (I can't say for sure, but I think they would have to change the way level data is coded if another tile was added). The 2nd and 3rd rows are just. . . odd.
