Actually, your system does not eliminate the power of sniping. It just reduces the power of sniping. Sniping will still change the set of sampled numbers to a new grouping (of lower numbers) producing a lower average. In fact, in many cases, it will yield results quite similar (or the same) as the current system.

Quote: sweep, 2005-10-06T15:52:50Z
Also, I'm aware we need to change the way people behave in a perfect world instead of changing the system, but this is akin to trying to control a stampede of bulls. It's not gonna happen. What do you do? Slap a brick wall in front of them. Problem solved.

As for this statement, we don't need to do anything. That's the big problem. I never said there was any circumstance in which we could say "problem solved." That's what everyone seems to be looking for. Most everyone who has some complaint about NUMA belives that there is some perfect idea. The belief is that this elusive idea can be manifested through the power of common thought and will turn NUMA into a utopian place. Problem solved.

Well, this diea doesn't exist. There's no idea that will circumvent the "human problems" of NUMA. The reasons the rating system "is busted" is because people are responsible for its upkeep. There's no way, whatsoever, to remove people from the process. There's no way to make people vote right or behave properly. Any operations that NUMA acquires to modify its systemic processes will only serve to make NUMA more systemic and less humanistic. Classical management flaw. The very spine of NUMA is that users have the sole responsibility for its upkeep. Unless some extreme advancements occur in the field of map-judging robotics, I would expect we will continue to have to relie on people to vote for maps, forever.

So, how do you manage people? With people. Moderation. Supervision. That's always how you manage people. That's the first rule of management. You want to be able to discern which votes are bad and which are not? You want to be able to discriminate between snipers and typical voters? No amount of computer programming will generate that. Only human effort can disseminate among and rule on those kinds of issues. There's a need for moderators who can veto votes, monitor user activity, and that sort of thing.

So, is there a quick fix for NUMA? No. People think there is because it's a website. In actuality, it's a community. It seems most of you don't know how to properly deal with communities. It's not by changing numbers and instituting requirements. It's by monitoring and managing the behaviors and activities of people.

*sigh*

That needed to be said. I hope a few folks read this and reconsider their "quick fixes" and oh-so-necessary suggestions for NUMA.
