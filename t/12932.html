Episode One, directly translated into Acceptable English by maestro:
---
The channel of stairs reached down into the cellar of the archaic wooden cabin, obtaining a sense of apprehension that drifted steadily in the dense air. Suspension had consumed the place for over a hundred years, abandoned cobwebs swaying in the moldy corners of every room and closet. The beating wind would not relieve the cabin, as it croaked and moaning with each gust, the rotting wood threatening to break apart. The old legend that belonged to the house was an uncertainty, older than time and changing every occasion it was told. No soul dare mock the tale, no matter how absurd, for fear of being dragged from their beds into the night by some unknown creature that protects the fable, making sure people knew they should not fool with it. Once a majestic palace, it now stands in ruins, defeated by time and nature, without the care of an owner, living or dead. Indeed, someone did once live there, a pathetic, detestable old man who died without a family, buried in the cemetery that lay just beyond the hills to the east near the old military encampment. The mental keenness of his life is still uncertain, as no one wanted to associate with the likes of such a man. It is said that nothing but the airy exist in the darkness of the basements. The unassuming pass by the house from time to time, stopping shortly to ponder what the truth being the myth really was. It was considered a thrusting feat for some, and foolish to others, to even walk past the sagging gate and slam the old iron door-knocker that hung on the door. In fear of angry spirits who wished no visitors, no one stepped inside. Reported to be true to some eye-witnesses, it is said that one day a man accidentally strolled into the residence, and came out but five minutes later spitefully beaten, with bleeding wounds lining his face and chest. Now every time that event is retold, it is looked upon with much vexation, as many believe it to be an unimaginative hoax. Only the pretentious would say that they arent afraid of the cabin, their false courage wiped away when dared to live up to their words and venture into the house. The impassive, dead appearance of the house remains, no matter what people could do to liven it up. Now the large cabin lay abandoned, no one had even walked by it for years. People went on with their lives, no longer unwavering to the tradition of telling the shocking tale of the old man and his home. The doctrine in the ghost stories was unenthusiastic and dull, because no one bothered about it. But something happened that would release all of the legends and tell the truth behind the house. Now we know the true story, and despite our beliefs in the lack of liveliness that lies inside the house, there is always more than meets the eye.
