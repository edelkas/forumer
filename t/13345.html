Planck time isn't a product of string theory, it's a necessary consequence of the uncertainty principle and quantum mechanics.

Planck time is equal to 5.4 x 10^-44 seconds. Any two events separated by less than that amount of time are coincident. So there's your answer.

Interesting note: we can currently only measure down to the attosecond (10^-18 seconds), so we could never know for sure whether two events are even approaching the Planck time.

And also: The definition of "touch" may be vague, but I think you could come up with something reasonable for the experiment (ie. two objects are touching when the applied force on the block plus gravity equal the electrostatic repulsion of the table's molecules).

#245293 20 2006-10-24T08:03:14Z Atilla
@stepself: I believe you're talking about energy/time uncertainty rather than a time quanta. Uncertainty does not mean there is a minimum time which we can measure accurately; it just means that when such a time is measured any measured energy will have a huge margin for error. Such relations can be obtained by applying unit conversions to the uncertainty principle, without reference to string theory. Many such pairs of variables can be found this way. Position/momentum and time/energy are just the two most commonly known.

EDIT: Actually, now that I think of it, it's possible to show that angular momentum, space, and energy are quantised using quantum mechanics. Time may also be possible, though I can't recall having seen it done.
