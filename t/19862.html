Thanks for reading this before you answer. A couple weeks ago I randomly tried holding my breath while sitting at the computer. I got up to about 1:30. I thought there must be special techniques that let people get to 9 minutes. There are, and since then, I've now hit 3 mintues. If you want to try it my way, follow these instructions:

Of course there are risks associated with doing this, so, um, please don't kill yourself.

1 - For a minute or so, breath slowly in the nose and out the mouth.
2 - Then, take 7 or 8 really deep, really quick breaths through the mouth. This is called hyperventilating.
3 - You'll likely be slightly lightheaded, so wait just a couple seconds until this passes.
4 - Sit up straight and take the deepest breath possible. Start your watch or timer.
5 - Hold the breath and relax your body. Try not to think about much. Once you feel you've gone as far as you can go, try exhaling nearly all the way. I find I can hold it about 20 seconds more after I've done this. If you've gone as far as you can safely go, your legs should tingle when you finally inhale.
6 - Stop the watch or timer and place your vote.

The Science Behind holding your breath

Interestingly, after holding your breath, the reason we are forced to breathe is not the body's need for oxygen to power muscles, but the need for oxygen to increase the pH in your blood. (If you don't already know this, oxygen causes your blood pH to rise (become basic), while carbon dioxide causes your blood pH to drop (become acidic.)) As carbon dioxide levels build up, the pH drops. Oxygen is needed to correct this, so your brain makes makes you inhale. So, now your saying, if only we could find a way to start with the pH higher. Then, as carbon dioxide builds up, the pH is first brought back down to normal levels before it starts becoming acidic. Logically, we could probably double our total time. This is precisely what hyperventilating is and as you have seen, I saw nearly a two-fold increase. Lastly, my idea of exhaling towards the end of your trial was built on this idea. I figured that exhaling the carbon dioxide in your lungs would raise the pH slightly. I'm guessing that getting rid of this carbon dioxide outweighs losing whatever oxygen is leftover in your lungs.
