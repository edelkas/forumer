If we're all waving our country credentials around, I was raised in a cattle farming district. My parents used to be farmers. My grandparents were farmers. Many of my uncles are also farmers.

Quote: smartalco, 2007-10-05T20:59:20Z
1. farming != sheep, in fact farming is almost entirely referring to the whole plow the field and plant the grain part, not livestock (in other words, they said farmer, not shepherd)

They are referring to a small, more-or-less self-sufficient farm, not the large-scale cash crops which are common today. They would want a mixture of produce, including grain, vegetables, fruit, milk, eggs, and meat. Until tractors became readily available they would also need horses or oxen. Many farms also keep dogs and cats, which also require meat of some description. There's also the fact that without modern fertaliser, fields become depleted unless you allow them to lie fallow and/or rotate your crops. As such, many farmers owned a number of sheep, goats or other grazing animals, as they are useful for fertalising fields which aren't currently producing a crop.

Furthermore, I disagree with the assertion that farming is almost entirely based on crops. Cattle, beef, milk, wool and lambs are among Australia's most valuable agricultural products. Livestock may be less common in the US; I know Australia exports an obscene amount of beef to you.

Quote: smartalco, 2007-10-05T20:59:20Z
2. no one ever said you can't have internet on a farm, it will probably just cost a little to have your provider run a cable out there for you

I live on a residential property in a medium-to-large country town. It would cost several thousand dollars to install cable broadband. It's not going to be cheap on a farm.

Of course, Australia's internet infrastructure sucks badly. It might be cheaper in the US. Or you might be able to get wireless or satellite instead.

...which is that we're discussing literature. People were considering farms idyllic long before the invention of modern machinery. Much more manual labour was require in the good old days, and you had to keep horses/oxen/etc., which created a different kind of work. You also had to haul your goods to market if you wanted to sell them, or at least go and barter with your neighbours. These days, people sometimes forget just how long it takes to travel by foot. And, of course, if you wanted heating, or wanted to cook, you needed a fire, which meant cutting firewood...
