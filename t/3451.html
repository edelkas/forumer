I have no problem with anything you said, apart from this ^^^^.

It shows a very closed mind to believe games are only addictive if the graphics are 3d... really, that's one of the most irrelavent aspects of gameplay you'll find. I could trawl off a list of many 2d games you could get addicted to, that many people did get addicted to before the years of several million bit graphics and flashy lights and stuff.

Let's take, as an example, the best football game ever, without a shadow of a doubt... Sensible World of Soccer. It's gameplay was far better than any football game anyone can muster these days. FIFA - fun, yes. Not the same though. Pro Ev - better than FIFA, yes. Not the same as SENSI though.

What is particularly interesting about Sensible World of Soccer, as you'll find if you read the description, is that the game lost it's appeal entirely when it 'succumbed' to the 3d graphics fad brought about by it's lesser cousin FIFA. As the description says, the 3d version was 'a dismal end to the famous series'. It was a sad sight to see.

Alright, I lied. Maybe SENSI isn't clearly the best football game ever... it ties with... no, not FIFA... no, not Pro Ev... but Championship Manager, the definitive football management game without a doubt. In past years the creators of the game have split, causing the new Championship Managers to be awful (it split into two games... one got to keep the name, the other got to keep the 'engine'... the engine one was far superior!).

Still though, the classic championship managers had something about them that people found hard to grasp... there were no graphics! Well, there were MENUS and stuff but not graphics of things moving. You found out what was happening by text. In the past few years they have updated it a bit, making it so you can see the pitch... however, each player is a dot in a very basic view of what is happening (it works though). So, before this game had ANY graphics whatsoever it was addictive... and they made the right choice to add very minimal graphics later...

I rest my case.

What's this? Someone who thinks 2d games aren't addictive? I would actually say the opposite is closer to being true: it is probably a lot HARDER to find an addictive 3D game than a 2d one. I mean, the only things that are ever addictive in that realm are:
1) mmorpgs, which are stupid and shouldn't count for this discussion because they require you to either play 24/7 or not play, even if its not fun at all. The entire goal of the game is to become the most powerful by throwing the most time into it.
2) fighting games, by which I mean 1 on 1 games like soul caliber. I really don't have or want to have any reason to belittle this genre, in my opinion any good game in this genre should be addictive, although they can sometimes be hard to find amidst the sea of. . .
3) fps deathmatches, which usually either offer something unique or do something better than competitors to make them stand out and be played longer, but the problem is that there's so many of them that there's no reason that you should be "addicted" to any (since there's a;ways three more just as good of the same type).

You notice a pattern, don't you? All of those (except #2 sometimes, though everyone still prefers multiplayer) are addictive on the sole principle of being multiplayer. You aren't addicted to the game, you're addicted to live opponents. If you had to play them against the computer, 90% of the time you would get bored pretty soon.

2d games, however, are usually MADE to be addictive. Every genre is made for the purpose of being addictive--platformers like n, platformers not like n (I think the gap between games like N and Jumper and games like castlevania and megaman is big enough to count as two genres, in my opinion), racing games, scrolling shooters, and. . . oh who am I kidding, you aren't going to read half of this.
