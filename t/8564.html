I have one or two things to get us started... on

Gardening.
If you're growing spring onion or lettuce (Actually, this rule applies to most salads) don't, no matter what the label says, pull them out root and all.

Wait untill the spring onion is pretty big, and then just cut off some of the multiple branches that are evident at where the plant splits... basically, you'll get the same spring onion as you would otherwise, without killing the plant and preventing it from growing any more.

On lettuce:
Grow it as per usual, and when you harvest it, cut it off at the lowest point to the ground. Worst case scenario? The plant dies anyway, but you get just as much salad. Best case scenario? It grows back entirely, and you get twice as much salad. (Though, I don't know much about the quality of this salad...) Typical, to the best of my knowledge, scenario? It grows back to a certain extent- not enough to give you another meal, but enough to go to seed and, hopefully, give you a lot of salad the next year.

DISCLAIMER!! I dunno why people tell you to pull it out roots and all, but they do, and there may be a good reason for this- so don't blame me if your spring onion or salads spontaneously explode the night after only cutting small sections off.
