Quote: maestro, 2005-10-11T12:08:46Z
Do you know of any techniques to encourage lucid or even vivid dreaming? I rarely remember my dreams, and when I do they're never vivid at all.
Drink a lot of water through the course of the day, but not right before you go to sleep. Water helps the brain function as it does during normal, perceptive, waking times. Have you ever felt like you weren't noticing things or focusing enough during the day? You might want to drink more water.

As for dreaming, water will help you focus on your thoughts (which become your mental reality) rather than drawing back into an empty sleeping state. Sleep deprivation often causes an increased need for your mind to pursue dreams. Just make sure that you get a lot of sleep on a comfortable bed. REM sleep is the stage of sleep where you "rewind" from very deep sleep into lighter stages of sleep (from 4th stage sleep to 2nd stage sleep). During REM sleep, you will have the most vivid dreams. However, once REM sleep is over, memory of those dreams stored in your mind begins to fade and is typically gone before the next REM cycle--therefore, difficult to remember.

However, if you wake during or soon after an REM cycle, you will typically remember you dream. Have you ever noticed how you either wake up in the middle of a dream or you wake up having no dreams at all to remember? Well, that's because of the quickly fading nature of dreams from the memory. Your REM phases are typically about 4, 6, and 8 hours after sleeping. If you wake up close to one of these times, you're more likely to recall a dream. After 8 hours of rest, the periods of REM sleep grow even longer and you are even more likely to recall dreams (by waking up during such a period).

Lucid dreaming comes from the determination that a dream is truly a dream and the will to alter it. Remember that a dream itself is just a memory of a dream--just the fleeting images of what you just experienced in your mind. Becoming more bold in real life or more willing to take risks can drastically influence your ability to have lucid dreams. People who seem to "have the whole world at their fingertips" tend to have more lucid dreams, where they can control their activities. It may all just be a part of perception. If you feel like you're not controlling your dreams, you won't. If you realize that your mind is producing these dreams, you can more easily shape their events.

Photons do have a rest mass of zero. However, as an object approaches the speed of light, it gains mass (because of a divisor that slowly approaches zero from one). This property has been proven and displayed in an equation: The Lorentz Factor.

At the speed of light, the transform suggests that the rest mass of the object is divided by zero to determine its relativistic mass. An object with any positive mass would therefore become infinite. Because an infinite mass would require an infinite energy to propel, objects with mass cannot reach the speed of light.

However, as an object with a mass of zero reaches the speed of light, its equation simplifies to zero divided by zero, which is suggested to equal one in relativistic physics. One what? Good question. It is theorized that this "one" would be the lowest possible mass for any object, that is, the mass by which all masses could be divided. An identity of mass, to be exact. A photon's "collision" has been determined to affect matter as if it had a mass; so these theories have been thoroughly tested.

But I digress.

The fact of the matter is that, although photons have a rest mass of zero, they gain an immeasurably small mass when moving at the speed of light. When a photon stops moving, it loses its mass and becomes nonexistent (neither a particle nor a wave) in a physical sense. Metaphysics would bring up further issues on this subject, however. Anyhoo, because the have gained a mass, they are affected by gravity.

It's a status symbol. Like many things in life, owning it serves no purpose but to show that it can be owned. We assume that possessions should be practical or entertaining to be worth purchasing. However, every time you make a purchase, you buy not only what is physically present, but a series of social and psychological benefits attached to that product or service.

Take a new video game console. Buying the video game console will allow you to play its games and have enjoyment. Moreover, however, buying a new video game console will privelege you to the social gratification of being part of that console's culture. Every product creates an illuminatus of sorts--that is, a group of people who know something the rest of us don't know. People who own golf equipment know a more refined sportsman's lifestyle. People who own an SUV know the feel of glory (even if the feeling is entirely intangible) that owning something larger than everyone else can provide.

Marketing is 10% product and 90% psychology. That's why people buy brand name products. Are Nike shoes better than Reeboks? No. They both look attractive and are quite durable. So why do Nikes sell more? The brand name. It's not about owning shoes--it's about owning Nikes. Why does McDonalds make more money per business than Burger King? Again, the psychology of business attracts individuals to buy certain intangible components of the things they are buying anyway. Marketing has convinced people they need to buy McDonalds, even when other options are available--parhaps subconsciously.

So, why buy an SUV? Because:

It makes a person part of a group of upstanding and middle-upperclass Americans--the type of folks who enjoy baseball, hot dogs, and apple pie. Anyone who sees the person in this vehicle will understand that the person knows what it is to be part of the SUV dream.

People are going to buy a vehicle anyway and the extra price of the SUV is worth the experience of owning an SUV.

The person who owns the most stuff, the most expensive stuff, and the biggest stuff wins life. Buying SUVs helps stake that claim.
