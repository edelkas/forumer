Quote: Shark, May 12, 2008 05:54 pm
Any chance of getting some help, guys? (*cough*cough*SCYTHE33!*cough*cough*)

Just treat the complexes as if they were metal ions. Diaminesilver is (NH3)2Ag+, but we'll call it Da+.
DaCl + HNO3 <----> DaNO3 + HCl
Diaminesilver should be easy enough to figure out, by the way.
Di + amine + silver
^ ^ ^
2 * NH3 + Ag.
Ammonia coordinates to silver (I) because ammonia is a Lewis base, while silver (I) is a Lewis acid.
Complex formation is a whole different ball game. As I recall, only two amines coordinate to zinc, but it should be on your information sheet.
Zn(OH)2 (s) + 2NH3 (aq) <------> Zn(NH3)[2+](aq) + 2(OH)- (aq).
Just find the complex ion, determine the charge on the metal, and you have this basic equation:

MeAn (s or aq) + Lg (aq) <---> Me(Lg)[n+](aq) + An[n-](aq)
where Me is the metal, An is the anion, and Lg is the ligand. You'll need multiple metal ions, anions, or ligands depending on the equation, of course.
Or, that's how I recall it.
