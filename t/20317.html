How to Become a Retro Gamer

by Ravenclaw


Have you ever wanted to appreciate gaming in its earlier years? Have you ever wanted to maybe enjoy the golden oldies that set the foundation for gaming today? Well this guide is here to help. I'm going to attempt to teach you (yes you!) how to become a Retro Gamer.

Things you will need:

1. An interest in old games and systems
2. Money (I know, it's hard to come by, but this is a key element in obtaining such game consoles)
3. A room with a TV that has rca jacks or whatever the A/V things are called (doesn't have to have cable)
4. A place to store this stuff (for example, the room mentioned above is a good place)
5. Optional: a bulletin board (to be explained later)

OK, so you've got the things listed above. Before we begin, you should know that you won't be able to become some uber-leet retro gamer in a few days. It takes money, flea markets, pawn shops, and commitment. If you have a girlfriend, break up with her, she will only distract you. I assure you these video games are much more important than reproducing. Also, becoming a retro gamer doesn't mean you have to stop playing today's fancy consoles. OK, so by now you've realized that I'm just showboating. Gathering up a few consoles and sitting down and enjoying them really isn't that hard. Here's where I'm going to start the step by step process.

Step 1. Get some money (notice how I said "get" and not "earn")

I suggest you either get a job, or try to leech off relatives.

Step 2. Take a look at the retro gaming world

Before you start collecting, you need to know what you want to collect. I, personally, collect mainly for the Atari 2600. But you may think that the Nintendo 64 or Playstation are cooler (fool!). Check out sites such as AtariAge.com or the Nintendo Classic section for a look. But of course, no website has more info on this than Wikipedia. Click that link for the history of all systems.

Step 3. Get what you want

This is where the money comes into play. Sites such as ebay and Amazon for great deals on all old consoles. If you're collecting for the Atari 2600, there's a very useful Rarity Guide that has pictures of every game for the Atari 2600, no matter what comany made it, or what kind of label it has. It also tells you how rare the game is on a scale of 1 to 10, or whether it's a prototype or a homebrew game. Extremely useful. You don't have to buy these things online though, your local pawn shop or flea market should have plenty of them. Scrounge for some good titles, and don't get ripped off. Back when I was inexperienced, I paid $15 for a game worth $.50, and that guy knew how much it was worth. What a bastard, stealing from kids!

Step 4. Set it up

So you've got some classic systems and some games, and are ready to play. If you have a system that connects to your tv using the A/V jacks, you're in luck. But before TV's had those, the consoles have these weird coaxial cable box things, these you have to screw into the back of your tv. And even before those, there were these prong box things, which you need a screw driver to slide the prongs under this weird coaxial cable box, which you then screwed into the back of your crappy 80's tv. So, figure out what kind you have, and I'll show you how to set it up.

A. A/V rca jacks (or whatever they're called)

Plug the cable into the back of your console, now the other end of the cable should have a yellow prong, a white prong, and most likely a red prong. You tv should have holes in it that match the color of those prongs, the holes may be in the back or the front of your tv. Take the yellow prong, and insert it into the yellow colored hole in your tv. Do the same for the white one. Congratulations! Turn on your console and tv, and chage the channel to either Input, Video 1, Video 2 , or Video 3. One of these channels is where your console is plugged in.

B. Coaxial Box things

These should look something like this:
In the back of your tv, there should be a hole with threads, this is where your cable normally goes in. Carefully screw the cable coming out of your box thing(it should have a thin prong) into this hole. Now take the other end and put it in your console. The console or the box may have a switch on it where you can choose channel three or four. Four never works, so put it on three. Now turn on your console and tv, and put it on channel 3.

C. The fork shaped prong-screwdriver box things (sorry, but I have no idea what they're called)

This website explains it better than I could

So, let's say you have more than one console hooked up to the same tv, head over to radio shack, and they have some coaxical cable transmitters or whatever that wire all your systems to the same tv, no switches, whatever console you press the power button on is the one that comes up on the screen. With A/V cable though, they have a switches so you can swith between consoles. You're also going to need some power strips. That tv of mine you saw above? It has three power strips behind it. Plus all those A/V cables are back there, plus my ipod speaker cords. It's a jungle behind there.

Step 5(optional). Put up a bulletin board

These things can hold your gaming info. Pictures of your favorite system, guides you printed off GameFAQs, or just reminders about gaming. Pretty useful.

Step 6. Enjoy!

Build up your collection and enjoy these fine pieces of video game history. As seen above, you can add game paraphernalia, and neon and flashing lights to pimp out your setup. Be creative and have fun with it.

UPDATE!!
