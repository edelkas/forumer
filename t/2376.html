Was it not just moment ago I witnessed a man fall, knife pointing high out of his ribs? Yes, I wasnt delusional, I did see the man fall, and he did have a knife wedged in his now carcass. So how could three faces be smiling so?

The other two fell, their suits cushioning the fall; a gentle thud accompanied them on their way down. I then finally stopped looking at the face, and looked past it to see two eyes staring at me. I looked down again, at my silhouette still in his shoes, but this time I was not perfectly portrayed. A drop of blood fell in my figures way. I looked for its source, and found it not concealed.
The knife, no, a knife, as corpse number one still held his trophy high. The man must have brought a pair. This one was stained so red, an apple would look pale. As the blood from his like dressed friends slid off its edge, I finally started to piece what had happened together. The man took no time ending this however, as his thirsty knife met my quenching flesh.
I felt no pain as the knife drowned itself in my ribs, they say your life flashes before your eyes, and they apparently dont lie.

Not trying to be mean or anything, but it's really poorly written. There are sentences I like, but they are far overshadowed by the sentences that either grate on me or are just plain incorrect.

Quote
Was it not just moment ago I witnessed a man fall, knife pointing high out of his ribs?

"Knife pointing high out of his ribs" strikes me as very awkward and doesn't easily bring out a clear image. The wording makes it sound like the knife came from inside of him and the blade is jutting out from his ribs.

Quote
Yes, I wasnt delusional, I did see the man fall, and he did have a knife wedged in his now carcass

"Yes, I wasn't delusional" sounds awkward to me. It might just be me though. I know what you want to say, but "his now carcass" is not grammatically correct, and it doesn't sound right either. Also, and far less important, "I wasn't delusional, I did see the man fall" is a comma splice. It should be a period.

Quote
The answer wouldnt come for some moments, perhaps two or three,

This just sounds comical. Maybe that's intentional though. If it's not meant ot be funny, then lose the "perhaps two or three".

Quote
I turned and faced those three smiling faces, to find them attached to a neck and body


I know your trying to make the sentences interesting, but this wording just strikes me as awkward.

Quote
I looked at their clothes, clean suits, freshly pressed.  Small black lines ran up their slacks and suits, pinstripes I believed they are called.

I like this part. It's the first clear image you've given us.

Quote
As I looked down at my silhouette reflecting in their shoes.

I like this too.

Quote
It twisted over, with the heel being the load bearer of the leg.

This is just bad. Poorly phrased, and created absolutely no image of what's going on. I have no idea what "twisted over" means, and I have no picture of what this should look like or what's happening.

Quote
The smiles were gone on all but one face, and the one smiling was the only one still in his place.

This isn't terrible, but it's vague. It's sort of boring to read, and doesn't create the sharp vivid images that good writing is supposed to create.

Quote
I then finally stopped looking at the face, and looked past it to see two eyes staring at me.

Looked past the face to see eyes? Seems like awkward phrasing.

This is another boring sentence, especially as it doesn't go anywhere because the guy immediately looks away after this. No reaction, no description of the eyes or how he felt. Nothing. It makes this sentence a throw away sentence. And particularly in a story this short you really don't want any throw away sentences.

Quote
but this time I was not perfectly portrayed

Might just be a personal preference, but I don't like the wording. Feels wrong or awkward or something.

Quote
A drop of blood fell in my figures way


This is also really awkward. The phrasing you used takes away from the image your trying to convey. Otherwise, this would be a good part. A clear picture of the man seeing his image in his soon-to-be-killer's shoe, marred by blood. That's a good crsip image that you don't want fudged up with awkward phrasing.


Quote
I looked for its source, and found it not concealed.

Too many "it"s referring to separate things. The phrase "found it not concealed" is awkward. Sounds funny. It's distracting to read and blocks the reader from getting really into the story.

Quote
As the blood from his like dressed friends slid off its edge, I finally started to piece what had happened together.

If you use "its" in this way, the reader expects the "it" to be the subject that comes right after the comma. So, at the very replace "it" with an actual object. But even after that, the whole sentence is a bit confusing. There are too many objects cluttering up the sentence makign it hard to interpret immediately. You got "his" (and we haven't been using a "he" as a subject yet, so this "his" is unlcear), "it", "like dressed friends" (which isn't horrible, but is a bit awkward sounding), and then the real subject of the sentence, the narrator. Just too much going on in one sentence.

I've got a few style issues with the rest, but nothing really worth mentioning.

Overall, this is a confusing piece to read, and the tons of awkward phrases of word choices make it frustrating to read. It's just not very good.
