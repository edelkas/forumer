#244439 16 2006-10-22T16:00:35Z blue_tetris
You usually don't need to say "that that". In most instances where you say "that that", you could omit that first that. For instance, "I think that that man is correct" could be rewritten as "I think that man is correct". It's evident that that "that that" that that "that" could be substituting is undesirable for the sentence. It should be relatively ironic that that "that that 'that that' that that 'that'" that that "that 'that that' that 'that'" was replaced by used two too many "that"s.

Had "had" had had my support on this construction (because it can be omitted in similar circumstances to "that"), I'd had had "had" too an unreasonable extent. Had "had 'had' had had" had had "had"s supernumerous to the amount of "that"s that that previous paragraph used, I'd had had this one up there instead of down here. Also, this paragraph has improper (or at least less savory) grammar.
