Okay, all that said, here's what I had:



Christians

They say their god is all-loving and all-powerful (amongst others). Yet, they also say that if you're bad, you go to hell. Some say that if you don't believe, you go to hell. However, an all-loving god would never send someone to hell. You could say "tough love", or that "he only does it because he loves them so, so they have to atone and be cleansed and come out good people", but hell is supposed to be eternal torment and damnation. Loving someone and sending them to corrective punishment, maybe, but sending them to eternal torment? Never.

It could be that no-one goes to hell. It could be that their god has no power over who goes to hell. (They says that "it's an example of your free will, that you chose to go to hell", but if you love someone, you stop them from doing something that will hurt them, even if they chose to do so. Also, by definition of hell, no-one would ever choose to go there. No jokes about masochists; they'd go to some heaven where they get whipped or whatever, not hell; their hell would be the eternal torment and damnation suited for them. Some may say that by choosing your actions, you choose to accept the consequences, but you don't. That's why there are clauses in thing that make you agree to accepting the consequences of your actions. You can never know the exact consequences of your actions, anyway. You can't say that "if I do this, then I'd go to hell, so I'm either going to choose to do it anyway, or not". This also leads to another point; if an action of yours has the consequence of sending you to hell, then that means your life is predetermined, else you could do something else later on which undoes the future sending-to-hell, in which case you can't say that the consequence of action x is going to hell, and if you don't know the consequences of your actions, or if there are no actual guaranteed consquences, or if everything is always a choice, then you can't actually go to hell; either the "they chose to go to hell; it was the consquence of their actions" argument falls apart, or they just have to make the choice, at the very end, to not go to hell, which no-one would ever do; say at the end, when you died, you were asked "what do you want to do now?", whatever you said (well, whatever your true answer is, so no reverse psychology "I want to suffer eternal torment", Hell: "Well, I'll give you what you don't want. Live with fluffy bunnies & happiness FOREVER!") would not be given to you in hell, so you'd go to heaven, there'd actually be no choice.
Back to earlier, with "if an action of yours has the consequence of sending you to hell, then that means your life is predetermined", the other side of that (I went through the not-predetermined side earlier) is that your life is actually predetermined, and therefore your actions are not your fault, they're not your choice (this goes against the christian 'god give you free will' stuff anyway), and if you went to hell for your actions when you had no choice in what your actions were, that'd just be ridiculous, and again, not a trait of an all-loving god.

It could also be that their god isn't all-powerful, and can't stop people from being taken to hell. I haven't got much else to say on that right now, apart from that it debunks the 'all-powerful' aspect of the christian god.





My next point, I started thinking about, as I saw some results of some poll (in america), the results of which basically said that many religious americans think people of other religions are good people (was something about 90% for various religions), but only about 50% of americans though that athiests could be good people.

This seems very prejudiced, and doesn't really follow the "love thy neighbour" aspect of christianity. I suppose if they go round pre-judging atheists as bad people, then they can't be that good themselves.

Yes, I understand that polls aren't always the best things to draw conclusions from, but I think the "atheists are bad" idea is widespread enough to say the above anyway.
