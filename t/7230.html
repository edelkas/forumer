#138807 22 2006-02-03T00:28:50Z Atilla
Quote: Teh Dude, Feb 2 2006, 11:58 AM
That is the underlying assumption of having the map ratings. This measure is based on the assumption that in general maps are correctly rated. If the ratings really are all incorrect then why bother having them?

Let me explain in more detail the problem.

Calculating the root mean square of the distance of every vote by every user from the average on every map they've rated is impractical. There are 20000 maps and thousands of votes and users. Every time a map was rated, you would have to do the calculations again for every user that had rated that map, because the average is not different. Clearly this will not be implemented before the use of quantum (or at least optical) computers becomes widespread, being hideously complicated and requiring too many operations.

The alternative is to only compare the vote to the average at the time the vote was made, which hits the following problem:

Say Noob Map A is posted. Noobs 1-5 come along and vote it an average of 4.5. I then rate it 1.5, as it deserves. Skilled Mapmakers 1-5 then give it likewise poor ratings, dragging the average down to 2.5. Skilled Mapmaker 6 then rates it 1.5.

The problem is that I now have a deviation from the average of 3, while Skilled Mapmaker 6 has a deviation of 1 (ignoring RMS for the moment), even though we gave identical ratings, which makes this statistic innacurate, pointless and without merit.

Even with the first method, if I rate Noob Map A poorly and Skilled Mapmakers 1-7 don't appear, I look like a sniper even though my vote was legitimate. I dislike this because what it basically says is you must agree with everyone else or you're a sniper/don't know what you're doing.
#138965 23 2006-02-03T12:39:50Z Teh Dude
Atilla that is a much better and far more constructive criticism.

As far as I see it, you have identified two problems with my so-called "marvellous method":
1) Having to recalculate each time a map's rating changes.
2) Having n00bs rate a map then someone else rating it correctly.

To solve problem 1 is simple. Just calculate the difference once, at the time of voting, and recalculate the RMS. Remember, the RMS is for all differences over every map that you have rated.

To solve your problem of an average being skewed if few people have voted (problem 2); simply have a floor value of votes, below which you do not use the difference for calculating the measure. You could also have a floor for if you have only voted on a few maps. So below 5 map votes, your RMS is not available. Or it is available but with the caveat that it is unreliable.

Explicitly, in your example, you are forgetting that your difference would be averaged. So yes you may have a difference in this circumstance of 3. However, averaged over many maps this difference would be reduced.

Finally, I am glad that you are lending such a constructively critical eye to this. Thanks.
