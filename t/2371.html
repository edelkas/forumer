the source for the diagram is worse than useless, because it's much more complicated to SHOW the projection than to just do it.

here's some pseudocode; you can rearrange some of the steps for speed, but this will hopefully make it very clear what's going on:

given vectors v= (vx,vy) and d=(dx,dy), you want to find the projection p=(px,py) of v onto d

steo "zero" is: you want to have VECTORS, not points. if you have two points a=(ax,ay) and b=(bx,by), and you want to find the vector v=(vx,vy) from a to b:

vx = bx - ax;
vy = by - ay;


so, assuming all of your vectors are actually vectors and not points/positions..

first, you want to scale d until it's unit-length:
var dlen = Math.sqrt(dx*dx + dy*dy)
if(dlen == 0)
{
//this is a problem/error -- we can't divide by 0 so stop here and don't continue
}

dx /= dlen;
dy /= deln;//dx,dy is now a vector of length 1


now, we do the actual "projection"

var dp = vx*dx + vy*dy;//dp is the dotproduct of v and d

px = dp*dx;
py = dp*dy;


done!

the LENGTH of p is Math.abs(dp). the direction is parallel to d, so it can be in one of two directions along d, depending on if dp is positive or negative.
