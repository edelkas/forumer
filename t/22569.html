SkyPanda: If God exists outside the universe, He also exists outside the laws of causation as we understand them, because such laws are part of the universe. There is thus no basis to assert that God caused the universe to be created, because the very notion of something being a cause is undefined in those circumstances.

Also, time is internal to the universe. Anything which existed "before" the universe (for want of a better word - without time there is no "before") and which can be said to have "caused" the universe to exist (for want of a better word - without the laws internal to the universe, there does not necessarily have to be causation in the sense that humans understand), you can make exactly the same "unbound-by-your-petty-concepts-of-causality" argument as you can for gods. Think about it for a moment: you're saying that God escapes the infinite recursion problem because He is outside of the universe - but, by definition, isn't anything which exists "before" the universe outside it?

Another problem is defining things like "always existed" without concepts of time and space. How long is "forever"? Because, if you think about it, the universe - by definition - has existed and will exist from alpha to omega, from the beginning to the end of time. To look at it another way, there is no time in which the universe does not exist. That meets at least one definition of "eternal".

Even without these problems, it's quite possible that the universe has "always existed" and has neither beginning nor end. One possibility is that time is finite but cyclical - every event is a cause which ultimately leads to the same chain of events happening again in an endless loop. Another is that the universe has no beginning simply because time extends infinitely back from our current location. We're just a point on the timeline in the same way as (1, 2) is a point on the graph y = 2x.

I think I need to explain that my argument isn't trying to offer a concrete proof about God not existing. It's meant to do quite the opposite: demonstrate that the whole issue of the creation of the universe is fundamentally illogical (to us!) regardless of whether you've got a creator God, and that you can't explain things away so simply (or explain them at all, in fact). I suppose it's an argument in favour of strong agnosticism.

Let's say there is a God. He's outside of our universe, our reality, the limits of what we can understand, but if there is a God, he certainly exists.

So, God exists. If something exists, but was never created, then it has always existed. By definition, if nothing created God, he has always existed. This has nothing to do with God existing outside of 'time' and being able to make his own rules. My argument is about our logic.
