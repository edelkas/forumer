Quote
Stress is a First Person Shooter designed to be as realistic as we can make it. In the final release we plan to have:
-full interactivity with environments (opening doors, breaking buildings/trees, making craters with explosives, swimming etc)
-realistic bullet flight - bullets are effected by wind and gravity
-realistic damage to personnel and vehicles eg. inhibited movement from being shot in the leg, loss of control of a vehicle from having the tire shot out
-continuous bleeding (you will bleed and leave a trail on the ground if you are wounded)
-large, expanseful maps - 20-50 people will be able to play on one map without it feeling crowded if we get this working.
-15 channels/map (instead of people hosting their own server, channels are constantly open with rounds either constantly running or timed so that there will always be a channel you can get into straight away) - we might come up with compromises to this, like a map/channel tab and then hosted servers. we'll see how we go.
-more variety than 2 teams/map - some gametypes will be free for all, some will let players create their own teams, some will be 2 teams, some 4.
-pain and stamina meters - if pain gets above 100 you will black out and be a target untill you regain consciousness - stamina effects how fast you can/if you can sprint and if it drops too low you will start panting (easier to find you)
--this list will be expanded as people remind me of things ive left out.--

Stress came from us wanting a decent, realistic fps that was free (there will be a free and an "unsedated" (premium) version of Stress.), fun and not requiring a powerful compy to play it well, aswell as requiring a small amount of disk space(this last wish will hopefully be fulfilled, but we cant make many promises in this area).
We're aiming for an initial beta (with as many of the listed features as we can achieve) late this year or early 2009 (depending on interuptions/our ability to get this moving). Features we are ~almost~ certain will be in the beta include:
-all the normal things -hp, movement, etc
-bullet drop/wind
-pain/stamina
-vehicles
-interactivity (with a fair amount of objects)
-multiplayer (at least direct connect)
things that ~might~ be in the beta include:
-AI players
-realistic blood
-gui

#412629 9 2008-02-28T03:37:15Z TornadoTK
I gotta agree, this sounds like a horribly large project. What physics engine do you plan on using? What programming language? Do you have people on your team with a vast amount of experience with netcode?

-full interactivity with environments
To address all of the things that you have said, you need one killer physics engine. The guys behind the new Red Faction game have talked about their latest engine, which allows all of the effects you desire, and they talk about it being one hell of an engine to manage. Another issue they had when designing levels using that engine is that buildings had to be structurally sound; if a building wouldn't stand in real life, it wouldn't stand in the game. This required way more additional research.

-realistic bullet flight - bullets are effected by wind and gravity
This isn't so hard, as long as you can code well and you understand ballistic physics.

-realistic damage to personnel and vehicles eg. inhibited movement from being shot in the leg, loss of control of a vehicle from having the tire shot out
-continuous bleeding (you will bleed and leave a trail on the ground if you are wounded)
Both have been done in the past, and they were executed successfully, so this should not be a problem.

-large, expanseful maps - 20-50 people will be able to play on one map without it feeling crowded
This takes a lot of work. With the complexity of the engine/level design/physics mentioned before, designing levels that are this large will be tough to balance out well.

-not requiring a powerful compy to play it well
I don't know how you can pull off all of the above AND have it be friendly with aging computers, but if you can... Wow.


Not trying to kick you in the face, but these are all problems you will have to deal with at one point. Good luck with the project.
