Isnt it ironic how high school students do things theyre not supposed to when they clearly know that they shouldnt be doing those things? Most high school students have taken a drink of an alcoholic beverage, such as beer or vodka. It is illegal to buy alcoholic beverages if youre under the age of 18 or 21, depending on where you live. Many high school students get wasted and hammered at parties and after prom. But why? Whats the point of drinking? Do kids do it because they think it makes them fit in? Do they do it to think theyre better than everyone else? Do they think that they can have more fun by drinking? Do they think it makes them change their personality? Do they see it as an escape from their personal problems and stress?
Drinking is also a problem in our town as well as in many other communities. Every day in school, I hear stories or plans about kids who want to go to a friends house or a party to drink. I cant stand these sorts of conversations because they absolutely disgust me. They know they shouldnt be doing it and they know the insidious suicide drinking possesses, so why do they look forward to doing it? Everywhere I go, I cant seem to escape from these discussions of alcohol. When Im in school, I hear about it in the hallways, at lunch, sometimes even in class. At home, I am reminded of it on Facebook: in my news feed, I usually see messages from so and so to their friend about how drunk they or their friend was last night. It just doesnt make any sense to me. I find it utterly revolting and disrespectful that students get drunk when one of our fellow class mates was killed by a drunk driver. In United States history, there were several Temperance movements in the mid-to-late 1800s against the consumption of alcohol. During the 1920s, alcohol consumption was ruled unconstitutional by the 19th Amendment. However, Americans in general found means to obtain and consume alcohol, often creating their own whiskey and wine. Millions of Americans also attended speakeasies. This shows that even if drinking is against the law, there are many people who are willing to break the law. However, society and capitalism also contribute to underage drinking. Millions of children watch hockey, baseball, football, and other sports. At professional ice rinks, there are advertisements for Bud Light among the boards. At baseball stadiums, there are billboards for Coors. Most television networks contain beer and vodka commercials. Watch Sports Center on ESPN for an hour count how many beer commercials youll see.
What can you do about underage drinking? You dont necessarily have to sit there like a lame duck. You can join groups such as the Alliance Against Underage Drinking to help spread public awareness of the issue. You can also advise people who are underage drinkers to stop their consumption. Let them know the health damages alcohol has on their body, (such as liver damage and the disturbance of the balance of hormones like testosterone and estrogen in adolescents). You can make a difference. Be responsible and make good decisions.

I was somewhat in your shoes a few years back (as Dave can testify, in fact), but I moved past it.

I will agree with you on a few key points. Yes, voluntarily poisoning yourself is retarded. Yes, alcohol is damaging to your body. Yes, people find the most clever of ways to do stupid things. Yes, drunk driving is a serious problem.
Despite these things, however, I continue to drink. While I realize that poisoning myself is bad, I do plenty of other things that harm me - I eat fast food, I don't exercise regularly, I spend a lot of time out of the sunlight, I don't get enough sleep, I have an unbalanced diet, etc., and all of these things actually contribute to my enjoyment. Fast food is tasty, exercise is labor, the sun annoys me, and the things I do instead of sleep are also enjoyable . Alcohol is the same for me in that I do think it benefits me enough that I'll take the detriment as well. Like Demonz, I don't drink much when I do, and I don't do it often at all, but it actually does have a meaningful impact on my social skills. I remain easily sober enough to act reasonably and to react to emergencies, but I lose just enough of my social inhibitions that I'm friendlier, more patient, and all around more charismatic. I think that's worth it.
And I've been on the other extreme, and it isn't fun (for me, anyway) being completely trashed. I'm less in control of myself, I look like an idiot, and people lose respect for me. That's not something I'm interested in, so I don't drink so much. But I know plenty of people who drink to this point willingly, even intentionally, so I think I share your dislike of people who are all about getting wasted at parties. I'd advise you to avoid these people if I thought you weren't already.

As for the health risks, I don't drink enough to destroy any part of my brain that I'll miss. Maybe in 20 years I'll take an extra 0.3 seconds to multiply two two-digit numbers in my head or something, but I really don't care. And if you lose 90% of your liver before you're 30, you'll grow it all back. The psychological benefits are worth all of that to me.

But I do also agree with you that it's all very silly that alcohol became so popular in the first place. I think there are plenty of safer alternatives that people could use to reduce their social inhibitions that isn't quite so dangerous as alcohol, but our ridiculous legal system disagrees for all the wrong reasons. If said substances were legal, I don't think I'd even touch alcohol. I think that beer companies in particular have done an epic marketing job by injecting alcohol into our culture, sort of like what Coca-Cola did with Santa Claus. Driving under the influence should be legally tried as attempted murder.
