Could someone please explain what each type of maps mean and what they are.
#317300 2 2007-05-01T04:37:17Z usaswim
I'm 100% positive this is in the forums somewhere because I personally did this once to somebody.

Action
These maps come in a variety of different shapes, sizes, smells, etc. It is the simplest group with the simplest rules.

Requirements:
  • If your map doesn't fit into any of the categories below, it may be considered action
DDA
A DDA stands for "Don't do anything". Yes, in these maps, they were designed to let you sit back and enjoy the show. Kinda like watching a movie. A DDA guide provided by Clifty (the king of DDAs), gets more in-depth about the sub-categories of DDAs. Most DDAs usually require you to press "1" before playing. A good DDA includes the following:
  • Lots of close calls
  • A good number of enemies
  • All enemies are involved
  • N is not wandering away from enemies frequently
  • Teleporters are quite bad in DDAs unless crucial
  • A variety of propulsion
  • Minimal lag
  • No long waiting periods with no enemy actions
The list could go on forever.

Requirements:
  • Don't do anything, or hold left or hold right.
Incomplete
This is the category for, you guessed it! Incomplete maps. If you don't have time to finish a map, is too lazy, ashamed of it, giving others a sneak preview, or saving it for later, you can submit your map under Incomplete. Of course, there is NUPB (The N User's Paste Bin). It is a place where you can save your map data so you can continue working on it later. It can serve for many different purposes, not just that one. There is also Mapswap. If you just cannot finish a map, you can trade your unfinished map for somebody else's unfinished map. You can finish their map and submit it to NUMA. But be sure to credit them!

Requirements
  • Unfinished maps only
N Art
This is the category for masterpieces created using tiles or objects. Some people have experimented with blending colors (placing objects within close proximity so when viewed in a thumbnail, the colors appear blended) to make green, purple, orange, etc, etc. A good N-Art is neat, colorful, and looks good (duh). Some N-Arts you must load in order to see the effects. Some include N-Snake.

Requirements:
  • Should not be usable to create a map
Puzzle
This category contains puzzle maps. No, not a jigsaw puzzle. These are maps that force you to use your brain cells.

Requirements
  • They should be able to make you think
Race
These maps are the most fun (IMO) to play. They are usually destined to have 1 specific route you must follow. Some races are multi-route however. These races should have very good flow and lots of jumps. Enemies should be used throughout the race and spread evenly. Races with gausses in every corner aren't good races. Also, it doesn't look good. Good looking tilesets will attract the audience's attention to play it.

Requirements:
  • Must have a specific route
  • Flow is crucial
Survival
These maps have no exits. It is an endurance test to see how long N can survive while dodging many enemies. Overly difficult survivals aren't very fun.

Requirements:
  • No exit
  • Should have a good amount of enemies to keep N busy
  • No dead spots (places in which you can last forever or a very long time)
Test Level
This category is mainly for new concepts. They are never before seen concepts that have never been used in maps.

Requirements:
  • Must be a new(ish) concept
  • Can be playable or not playable
Tileset
Tilesets are tiles arranged to make different shapes and designs. It is partially like N-Art except the tilesets can be used to make maps. The better looking, the higher the rating. Of course, it also depends on the usability.

Requirements:
  • Should have empty space to use for a map
  • Good looking
Uncategorized
This category is no longer used. This category is used for maps that just cannot fit any of the above categories.
