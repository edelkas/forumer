Apart from the crappy subtitle... this contest is going to be the best one yet. Its easily the most open of anycontest. I'll try to keep this post short and to the point, and leave the random crap out.

The theme of this contest is Themed Maps. What I mean by this is, all submissions must carry a consistant theme through out the whole map. THat them can be ANYTHING you want. When you post your map, tell us your theme, so we know what to look for.

There are No restrictions except the following:
  • No manual code editing of ANY kind
  • No completly hidden items, all items must be partially/wholey visable
  • No purpusful usage of glitches/bugs... none, or I'll give you running zeros (there is one exception, you MAY do the trick to freeze a drone behind doors... I will NOT count out off for that bug)
The judges are: Maximo, Brickman, and Maniac. The backup will be Foxhunter.

Scoring Break down (i'll be specific on the differences within the catagories)

Theme (0-10): This is not on how unique the theme is. It could have been done 100 times over, but if it is used well, fits the tileset you made, and is consitant throughout their map, more points to you. This is for how good the theme was
Uniqueness (0-10): I'm tired of creativity being a blown off topic. I want to see unique maps. Make something that I've never seen before, and you'll be rewarded. This is a topic for how unique/new the theme is, not at all on its quality
Fun (0-10): How enjoyable was the map to play. This is strictly oppinion based, if the judge had fun, you get points accordingly
Difficulty (0-10): Was it too hard, too easy or just right. More for the latter. I want a consistantly challenging map, that is never frustrating.
Excitement (0-5): I liked how this worked last time, so its back. Was your map a thrill ride start to finish. Now don't let this discourage you from making no enemy maps. I gave Weary, an all mines map a 5 in excitement. Excitement is not limited to turret/drone maddness, simple near death experiences add to excitement (remember that judges)
Other (0-5): anything a judge felt like you deserved more points for, maybe less for, this is the place for that.
Bonus (0 or 5): This is a new thing I've concocted. This is factored in at the end of the contest. Each of the three main judges choses their single favorite map out of the whole contest and that map gets 5 points added to its score. Yes, you can go over a 50 with this. If two judges pick the same map, 5 points, and so on. This is to help factor out the times when one bad score from a judge takes a whole map out of a competition...

Okay, now the nitty gritty rules...
  • All maps must be submited on Numa with these details:
    • Your forum name as the author
    • a description of your theme
  • Any map submited with an error may be corrected ONLY if spotted by the author before a judge finds the mistake themselves. If this happens the map is DQ'ed and it counts as a submission
  • All three judges will judge every map except their own (which foxhunter will judge) and the scores are added up and the highest score wins
  • Any map violating any of the requirements will be DQ'ed with no exceptions
  • There will be NO protesting of scores
  • You are allowed 2 submissions only unless there are 25 total submissions, then you are allowed 3. If we reach 40 total submissions, I will increase it to 4.
  • As a personal favor, please attempt to make a winning map each time. Its really a strain on judes to have to weed through the dummy entries to find the ones that are honest tries. So please, no purposeful crap maps
  • Submission deadline of Saturday, Nov. 27 I'll post a reply stating the end of submissions
I think thats it, If I missed something critical, just PM me and I'll fix/add it...

Good luck, and submissions are open now...

The way I judged this contest was this: I made a standard with the first map which obeyed the rules (symetry), and maybe one or two after it. I planned to judge other maps in a way so that the scores were all relative. Thus is map a had 23 and map b was worse, it would have less than 23 points, with the difference depending on how much worse. And visa versa. This ingenous system was designed to make sure that standards for judging didn't gradually change as the contest went on, with a map that was better than most scoring better than another which was better than it because at the time the first appeared I had nothing to compare it too. Or visa versa. I wanted a system where maps were comnpared to those that already had scores, instead of arbitrary numbers that might vary by the number of crap/good maps or the mood of the reviewer.
This was an excellent plan except it had one fatal flaw: poor overall quality of maps.
When I set the standard (really symetry was pretty much the starting point for the standard), I was expecting that, like in ned 2, the maps would get better after the first week. Go check that thread, the earlier maps all scored lower because they weren't as good, and I figured the same would happen (especialy since there were only three maps when I set the scoring standard, and only one which qualified, the potential good ideas hadn't been tapped yet). I will say, however, that most or all of the maps with a single digit score fully deserve it.

I suppose this could all be avoided by waiting until the contest was half over before starting to judge, but that carries its own problems. The maps pour in with no feedback, so until the judges start people have to guess at what we want. Thus, waiting to judge actually does make the maps worse than if you offer feedback all throughout. But it decreases the quality of the scores proportionately, a problem which I had thought I had solved back in the first paragraph.
