Umami is one of the five basic tastes sensed by specialized receptor cells present on the human tongue. Umami is a Japanese word meaning "savory" or "meaty" and thus applies to the sensation of savorinessspecifically, to the detection of glutamates, which are especially common in meats, cheese and other protein-heavy foods. The action of umami receptors explains why foods treated with monosodium glutamate (MSG) often taste fuller.
