First of all, Happy New Year!

Today, it has come. Ten months ago, we began on a journey, entitled "The Legacy Project." It began as a simple map-pack, but grew into something that none of us had anticipated. Who could have predicted that maximo's "Return Pack" would sprout its own legs, and flower into something so massive as the Legacy Project? Surely, not I. In ten months, The Legacy Team has brought the community many things, but more importantly than that, many others have followed in The Legacy's footsteps to create their own map packs and their own projects, to amazing fanfare. The reason that we began Legacy was first, to contribute something large to the community, and second, and more importantly, to inspire others to do the same. And I, for one, am ecstatic to say that the contributions that you all have given the community have far surpassed my expectations. I know that we are looking forward to more things from all of you in the future.

And now, we are entering a new Golden Age of Metanet. 2007 will be the year that Metanet breaks out of its mold, sheds its cult status, and becomes a heavy hitter in the gaming world. And we are proud to say that this year will be a large year for The Legacy Team, as well. And to start 2007 off, we have a number of treats for you all.

First and foremost, we would like to announce three new additions to the Legacy Team; KinGAleX, blue_tetris, and krusch! As busy as our schedules are, as a team, we decided that we need some new faces in Legacy. As of today, KinGAleX, blue_tetris, and krusch will all be helping work on projects for the team on a permanent basis. I fully believe that there are few men in the community who have done the kinds of things that these three have been capable of - The P42 pack, the Pony Parade, the Camisade Collective, The Sunday N, the Real N, The Map Manifestos, The Super Hard Column, the Super Fluffy Map Packs... I could go on for days. Now, as much as LittleViking and I have been working with KinGAleX and Dave at The Real N, this is more of a formality, and we fully feel that krusch has the talent and the prowess to inject some new ideas into future projects of ours.

Secondly, starting today, we are announcing the beginning of NedCon Eight. The NedCon contest series, started by Maximo, has been a great inspiration to mapmakers and fans of N alike. NedCon has been around as long as the forums, and has gone through many different forms, and this season, we continue the tradition set by LLCoolDave and maximo with a new theme: ReMix maps! All of the information needed to begin participating is included within the thread, and all questions should be directed there. Once you're done here, head on over to the Neditor Contest Eight thread and check it out!

Thirdly, today marks the start of a new tradition here at Metanet. What initially began as a casual activity amongst those of us who frequent the IRC and the Ventrilo server, the concept of Monday Night Numa Maps is one that died out rather quickly after its inception. It was popular enough, but the idea simply faded from existence. However, just recently, Clovic brought the idea to me that we should make the Monday Night Numa Maps a tradition, and the MNNM League was born. For more information on the MNNM League, and how to join, visit this thread.

Fourthly, today marks the end of the Dronies 2006! Voting has ended, and we will be tallying the votes as soon as is possible, and we will be posting results as soon as they are counted. Good luck to all participants, and thanks for all of the enthusiasm!

Fifthly, the Legacy Team website is getting a makeover! Before now, the site has been rather simple, as Sweep is the only one who can update it. But within the coming weeks, we will be updating the site, both aesthetically, and functionally, so that updates will occur much more frequently there, to let you all know about upcoming Legacy projects, current endeavors, and perhaps even some Metanet News.

Sixthly, I know that I have yet to hand out prizes for past seasons of our contests, so if you are missing a prize, please me and let me know what I have yet to do for you. I have a bit of a surplus of money, so I can do the T-shirts now and get them shipped out within the week, provided our post-blizzard conditions don't stymie my progress further.

Next, I am proud to announce the long-awaited dedication maps for iangb, Lucidium, and spect! We have been saving all of our big guns for today, and we felt that hanging on to the dedication maps until today would likely be the best move for publicity purposes. We thought that these three should get as much exposure as possible, as they were the best of the best during Legacy's Origins contest. So, without further ado, you can find the dedication maps here;

iangb
Lucidium
spect

And finally, the coup de grace. The moment that many of you have been waiting almost a year for. Today, we are launching Expanse, our second of three map-packs in the Legacy Trinity, our map trio. Expanse adds 300 levels, all made by The Legacy Team's seven members - maximo, nevermore, LittleViking001, Sweep, tktktk, Lucidium, and myself - As well as the maps made by Pariah, iangb, Echarin, and Barabajagal for their magnificent showing during the Legacy: Origins contest and during NedCon 7. Expanse is meant as an expansion-pack, of sorts, to Legacy, continuing in the same vein as the original pack, with new ideas, new map styles, and hopefully, some brand-new perspectives upon the game. Over the past ten months, we seven have tirelessly played and re-played through the pack, tweaking and balancing, and arranging the pack to be in tip-top shape for you all to enjoy.

Alongside Expanse, we are proud to present the Legacy: Expanse Contest! Like the Legacy: Origins Contest, the Legacy: Expanse Contest is designed to provide incentive for you all to further enjoy the levels that we provide. The contest is a race, and a challenge, to find the fastest, most skilled and most dedicated players here. The first 20 people to complete Legacy will be awarded and recognized for their accomplishment. Please, please, please read all of the rules before entering, and follow the submission rules included here;

Contest Rules

Contest Sumbission: TheLegacyProject@gmail.com

Contest Deadline: March 1, 2007

Contest Rules:

- Once you complete the Legacy: Expanse pack in its entirety, you will submit your N userdata.sol file by e-mail to The Legacy Team for review. For help finding your .sol file, please consult the first post of this thread.

- E-mails must be marked "Legacy: Expanse Submission" in the subject line, along with your N username. (Ex. Legacy: Expanse Submission - Kablizzy)

- All Submissions Must include completed Demo data from each of the 300 Expanse levels. MAKE SURE YOUR DEMO DATA IS COMPLETE. Incomplete submissions may result in disqualification.

- Please be aware that your .sol file contains your username and password. The Legacy Team will not use, store, or even look at this information. If you are still not comfortable submitting a file with your password in it, you can change your info before submitting. Inside the Configure menu, set your username as LegacySubmission and your password as dummy (with the e-mail address left blank) and press "change user." You can then submit without any private information being sent.

- In the case that your .sol file is very large, you can upload your file to iHud.com and e-mail the link given to you to The Legacy Team. iHud is a free file-hosting service that allows for files up to 50 MB in size without requiring users to register.

A professional Review by an Idiot:

Legacy: Expanse is an interesting expansion to familiar love. It's like hooking up with your best friend's hot cousin again when she visits him. It isn't boring, it's fairly attractive, and you can probably get away with it on his bed as long as you aren't a sloppy aimer.

What I find the most intriguing is this by far one of the easiest mappacks to get into. I can pop onto any level and be content with playing it. No map stands out as bad or amazing; In fact, few levels in this pack aren't amazing.

It's incredibly easy at times, and incredibly difficult at others. What's noticable here is the All Gold Challenge. You can play through the first twenty levels without too much difficulty, but going for all the gold is where the real fun starts.

And I think that may be the only problem with Legacy's Second leg of this crazy Tripod that holds up their egos: IT ISN'T THAT DIFFICULT. There's no real drive to go for all gold, and I mean, why would you?

Because it's fricken' Legacy. You WANT to play these maps, and you do, and you aren't disappointed. It's like when B.B King and Eric Clapton put out that album together; You got exactly what you expected. And that isn't too shabby when you expect perfection.
