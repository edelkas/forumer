This an RTS game. (RTS stands for Real-Time Strategy, for anyone who doesn't know.) You have to colonize an area, build your economy, amass a military force, then wipe out your opponents. The point of most RTS games is to do that: wipe out your opponent. There are alternate ways to win, like force them to surrender, but it's much more satisfying to clear out all their units in a gruesome fashion..

The game is graphically amazing, but my computer lags unless I turn off some of the advanced graphics options like anti-aliasing and the "bloom" effect. Even then, it's still decent. There are 8 civilizations to choose from, they all have unique units, and strategic advantages and disadvantages. The Portugese get a covered wagon every time they reach a new age, (it transforms into a town center). The Dutch's settlers require money instead of food, but they counter this by having a unique building that makes money just by existing. (Just like gas stations)

If you've played the previous Age of Empire games before, the basic concept of the game is the same. However, the game's biggest new feature is the Home City. Because the game is based on imperial civilizations, you are supported by your mother country and you choose upgrades and units that are delivered to your Town Center. The units and upgrades are used in a card system, and you can only choose new cards when you advance in level. Whenever you play in a Skirmish, you gain experience, and that levels up your home city, (like an RPG) which you can use to your advantage to pwn the face of some newb online. Even with an advanced home city, if you can't actually play the game well, you'll be in trouble, because the game is still based on skill.

Another new feature is the ability to build trading posts. Settlers or your explorer can build a trading post. When built along a trade route, you can choose what resource you get every time the trader passes by. The trader strolls on by the trade route, and he delievers whatever resource you select at each trading post. Another new feature: trading posts are also built near Native Settlements. When you build one next to a Native Settlement, you gain them as an ally, and you can train soldiers from their settlement with no increase in your population. There are also lots of different Native settlements, and each one has their own unique units and upgrades to offer. It's almost like a new civilization.

You have three resources to manage: Food, Wood, and "Coin" (Yes, it's actually used like that in the game. ) All three can be used from cards from your Home City, or they can be collected from a trade route. However, the main way to earn them is explained here. You gather food from having settlers hunt wild animals, you can have sheep grow off a livestock pen and then use them for food, or you can also build a Mill, which is slower than the other 2 methods, but it's unlimited and you don't have to go anywhere. Wood can only be gathered from chopping trees. Coin can be dug up from a silver or gold mine. Silver mines start with 2000 coin. Gold mines are hard to come by but they start off with 5000 coin. Coin can also be collected by building a plantation, and having settlers gather coin from it. Like the Mill, it is slower than going into the wild, but it's unlimited, and you don't have to go anywhere. All of these resources are used to purchase military units and upgrades for your civilization.

The game is not a great leap forward in the RTS world, but it is a good step in the right direction. The game is pretty addicting, and it's quite fun to blow the crap out of unsuspecting villagers who just want to harvest their crops. I rate it an 8.7/10

What are your comments/feedback on the game?
