For those unlightened people who have not watched the DVD special features because they were very, very bored...

Yup, Shaun of the Dead _is_ a horror comedy, (or, as the box says, "A romantic comedy. With zombies."), but the two genres are merged- it doesn't make fun of horror or send it up, it merely aims to create a comedy taking place in a horror environment.

And much of the film- most of the second half- is pretty centred around the horror aspect of the film even moreso than the first. Many of the (in my opinion) funniest scenes in the film were cut out of the second half because the makers felt that it interrupted the flow and progression of the plot around that stage, where 'jokes' as such were no longer as appropriate.

Anyway, on the commentaries, the film makers specifically said that they didn't want to make fun of horror films (totally unlike, say, the Scary Movie series)- they just wanted to make a comedy that took place in a horror environment respectful to those that made the genre. So, "horror comedy" doesn't mean it's not scary.

It _does_ get pretty tense towards the end- lots of people dying, including many that you start off sure will live in the early, much lighter sections of the film. I can picture it being considered quite frightening, even if I've seen far worse. (Although the mixture of comedy and then brutal death- the flicking between genres- is often more shocking and entertaining than the straight genres themselves are, at least when pulled off correctly- and a good deal of my respect for Joss Whedon comes from how well he can do this. Take, for instance, the death of Wash in Serenity- one of the best deaths I've ever seen on screen.)
