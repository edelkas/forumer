Washington Post Article

I actually heard this on the radio on my drive to work (I like to listen to the Christian Satellite Networks broadcast in my area); the Washington Post article was one of the first Google results.

The issue here is, in brief:
The American Civil Liberties Union is threatening to file a federal lawsuit against the US Navy unless they put a stop to their 160-year tradition of holding a "voluntary" mealtime prayer in the Naval Academy.

This all started because a handful of shipmen complained to the ACLU that they felt pressured to join the prayer session.

My take on this:
All of the Christian organizations that oppose the ACLU here keep calling the event voluntary, and I think that's very misleading; that's in fact the issue here. Just because you are free to say a prayer from any religion you want, to recite the humanist manifesto, or to sit idly for the 60 seconds, does not make it voluntary. It isn't voluntary, and it is misleading to call it voluntary, because the food is still withheld for clearly religious reasons for 60 seconds, and those who don't want to partake in the purely religious event should be able to get their food right away. Even if they were to call it a pre-mealtime moment of reflection, it's still pretty clearly a time for religious activity.
What I don't think should happen: Religious actions should be strictly prohibited among service members.
What I do think should happen: Everyone sits down and gets their food, without a dedicated 60 seconds. At that point, those who don't want to do anything religious don't have to and can begin eating, and those who want to pray, recite the humanist manifesto, or have a moment of personal reflection may do so as part of their personal time when they might otherwise choose to eat.

Ultimately, it's a technicality; I don't think I'd actually care were I in the Navy Academy, but I do agree with the ACLU on this on principle.

Other thoughts?
