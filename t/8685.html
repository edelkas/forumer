I'm no grammar nazi, but it makes me mad when people make posts that use too many abbreviations or misspellings. Most of you use pretty good grammar, and some of you even use extremely advanced vocabulary and tricky word structure to make your posts sound awesome, which I can't do.

However, a few members use bad grammar because they are eithier lazy, or just don't like using proper punctuations and stuff. (*cough*Ermacrules29*cough* ) The exceptions are people from another country who don't speak english, yet they try their best to type it. They deserve some credit.

The following is a guide to all the punctuations and capitalizations you'll need to know, as well as some tricky apostrophe words. Here we go:

Section 1: Punctuation

1. Periods. Not the thing that women experience every month, the little dots that come at the end of each sentence. ;O EX: The dog jumped over the fence. You use it when the sentence has finished representing its thought.

2. Commas. These are pretty evil. They sometimes have to be used in places that look like they shouldn't have to be used. They represent a break in thought. EX: As the man ran towards the house, it started to rain.

3. Capitalizations. The first word of every sentence is capitalized. ALWAYS. Every proper noun like someone's name, or the name of a specific place, is capitalized as well. EX: The zebra lives in Africa. Africa is a specific continent, so it is capitalized even though it's at the end of the sentence.

4. Question marks. These represent a question that is asked. Most questions start with the 5 W's: Who, What, Where, When, and Why. How is also used at the start of a question, but it doesn't start with W. EX: Who was the last person to use the bathroom?

Added by Atilla: Questions may also be asked by altering the word order. For example, "That is true." is a statement and "Is that true?" is a question. Questions also often begin with "is", "are" ("Are you hurt?"), "do" ("Do you like my shirt?"), "does" ("Does this shirt make me look fat?"), "can" ("Can you read Japanese?"), "could" ("Could you please hold this for me?"), "would" ("Would you still support the Bill if these amendments were added?"), "will" ("Will you please leave me alone?") and "should" ("Should the engine be making that noise?")

A question mark followed by an exclamation mark indicates that a question is rhetorical (ie, that an answer is not expected): Eg. "Are you insane?!"

"have", "had", and "has" are also commonly used to start questions.~

5. Exclamation points. These are used to represent yelling, or sometimes they're used to represent to get the attention of someone, or even both. EX: Look out for that rock!

Section 2: Sentence structure

Commonly misused words/contractions. These are only a couple of the words that sound the same, but are used differently:

1. They're is the contraction of they are. Used to show that a group of people are doing something. Pretty self-explanatory. EX: They're going to the beach tomorrow.

Their shows possession for a group of people. EX: Their baseball went through the window.

There shows the location of something. EX: My Math book is over there.

2. We're is the contraction of we are. It shows a group of people on "your side" doing something. EX: We're swimming in a pool.

Where is asking the location of something. It's one of the 5 Ws. EX: Where is the guy who stole my sandwich?

Wear shows that someone has clothes on. It also means that soemthing has deteriorated. EX: You should wear the green t-shirt and black jeans. The other wear: The ship shows wear. (Okay, bad example. You won't use wear to show deterioration too often, anyways. )

3. Two is the full spelling of the number 2. It is a number, plain and simple. EX: I have two pairs of shoes.

To shows movement. EX: John is going to the dog track tomorrow.

Too shows an excessive amount of something. EX: I had too many slices of pizza.

4. You're is the contraction of you are. It is making a statement about someone. EX: You're stupid.

Your shows possession for someone. EX: Your car was impounded.

I think that's it for the "your" series...

5. It's is the contraction of it is. It's probably the most used contraction ever. EX: It's boring to sit through a lecture.

Its shows possession. It can be tricky to use. EX: Its legs are broken.

Added by mascote_ponto: You use the apostrophe when you are contracting the word "is"
(Example: It's raining)

But you also use it to indicate a possession.
(Example: Metanet's game is geat.)

Added by Atilla: Plural possesives should have the apostrophe after the s. As in, "The Swans' home ground is the SCG." Group nouns, however, should have the apostrophe before the s. So "The flock's breeding ground was nearby" means there is only one flock, while "The flocks' breeding ground was nearby" means there are multiple flocks.

Too can also be used to mean, uh, "also". For example, I could have said that too can mean "also", too.

We're is also often confused with were, because people are slack and leave the apostrophe out.~

Remember, these are only some of the commonly mispelled words/contractions. There are a lot more.

Section 3: Miscellaneous grammar

I know this is the internet and we all like to use shortcuts. However, when you have more shortcuts than full words, that's a problem. At least try to spell the whole word out, okay? This is annoying: "Wil som1 help me please, tis is annoyin k I rly need help cuz my comp is bein slow n shiz" It actually seems shorter to type the whole word out than to use the shortcuts, because you have to find the right letter to replace it with. Besides, how much time do you think it saves? A couple of seconds? Use common sense.

Punctuation, capitalization, and contractions are the 3 main problems when typing anyways. There are a lot of other things to learn about grammar, including, but not limited to: tenses, conjunctions, verbs, adjectives, nouns, and adverbs.

While I remember, I just wanna post about one of my biggest grammar pet peeves. When you're referring to a company or organization, you treat it as a singular, not a plural. In other words, instead of thinking of it as a group of individuals, think of it as a single entity. For example:

Wrong: Nintendo are releasing a game soon.
Right: Nintendo is releasing a game soon.

Wrong: They are a big company.
Right: It is a big company.

You wouldn't think it would be such a problem, but I see people make this mistake all the time, even in mailbag responses for videogame sites, when they should be used to referring to companies.

I'd like to add something to your post Twistkill.
Articles.
A, an, and the.
A is replaced with an if there is a vowel after the word a. EX: An apple.
The is used if you don't want to use an or a. EX: An elephant = The elephant. The is also used if you want to be more specific.

Quote: apg, 2007-03-24T12:18:34Z
When do you use its'?
That word does not exist.
(It's technically a possessive of a possessive, so it makes about as much sense as "my's.")
The English language has the words "it's" and "its."
"It's" is used as a contraction of "it is." You would use "it's" any time you would normally say "it is."
(e.g. "it's raining" or "it's done.")
"Its" is used as a possessive pronoun. It shows that the noun following is owned by "it."
(e.g. "I crushed its puny skull.")
But there is no word "its'."

Just one little nitpick and an addition.

Nitpick

Your example in section 1 point 2 should have a semi-colon, not a comma. This is because a semi-colon joins two sentences that could work as either one or two; a comma doesn't necessarily show a break in thought (although it can). They more often than not show where the reader should pause briefly. A more appropriate example should be: It started to rain, so the man ran towards the house.

Addition

Than/then.

This one really pisses me off. That one letter difference is very important.

Than is used when making a comparison, for example: I want a blue shirt rather than a black one.

Then is used in a sequence, for example: I will go to John's house, then the mall.
