As for the spelling differences, it probably stems from natural language evolution with importation of the language to the States. If you look at the evolution of language, you'll find that it progresses from fancy to time-saving. It used to take about a second to write down a single letter in an ancient alphabets. Then, the letters themselves started to be written faster and sloppier--thus conveying a longer message with more speed (and less filler). The same goes for spelling. As the English language evolved, letters were dropped from words to make them quicker to write. We totally got rid of the "thorn" letter and replaced it with "th" to make things easier, at some point. This general evolution continued when the language was imported to America. English evolved in two different ways within the two different countries. So, Commonwealth English and American English gained more distinction.

And like true evolution, the parent language was left behind. It's not that Commonwealth English is original English. It simply evolved in a different way (like chimps and humans). Nobody in either of our countries spells things exactly like they used to a century ago.
