#422999 8 2008-03-29T05:20:27Z Beo
Quote: squibbles, 2008-03-29T04:02:03Z
"The only person who this will make look bad is you."

It's grammatically correct. The reason it sounds so weird is that the verb 'look' has other words attached to it, specifically:

"make something look bad."

But because of the word order the "something" (which in this particular sentence is "you") has been moved to the end of the sentence, leaving just "make look bad."

It's the same sort of format as:

"The only person who this will hurt is you."

As for the other question, both are fine in normal speech at least, but if you're writing an very formal essay then I'm not too sure which one.

#423484 18 2008-03-30T07:18:54Z Atilla
Only you will look bad because of this.

You alone will look bad because of this.

You're the only one who will look bad because of this.

This will only make you look bad.

This will make only you look bad.

This will only make one person look bad - you.

Only one person will look bad because of this - you.

It works better if you omit "person", I think.




As for the first one, I think "George's and Lenny's rabbits" or "George and Lenny's rabbits" are both acceptable. The first states that the rabbits belong to two individual entities, George and Lenny, while the second state that the rabbits belong a collective, "George and Lenny". The second is easier to say and easier to read, but it's also slightly ambiguous - you could mean that George is but a dream, as are Lenny's rabbits. In almost all cases, however, the context will prevent this misinterpretation.
