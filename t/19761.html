This guide will teach you how to use different ways of using the animation software Pivot Stickfigure Animator to it's full potential and capabilities.

First, download it.

Link.

This is Pivot 3 beta. This one I recommend to be used based on the fact that it has a much nicer display, allows more than 1 background to be used, and allows sprites.
Spoiler: Beginners GuideNow, on to the actual animation. Open it up, and know I shall teach.

Regular animation:

This is the most basic style of animation used in this program. You will need:

-An imaging program (i.e. paint, photoshop, GIMP, etc.)

-Using the image program, make a background (any, I don't care.)
-Save your background as a .jpeg or .bitmap or .GIF (use bitmap better, image has better view.)
-Go to File, then Load Background.
-Look around for the background you made, and click on it. Make sure it is any of the 3 types of file I stated, or you can't use it.

And you have a background. (you could also get an image from the internet, save it as any 3 files I stated, and put it in there.)

Here is a sample background. If it ever asks you to change the size dimensins of the animation to the background, say yes.



Now, on to the stick figures.

You can either:
a) Use the default one.
b) Make one yourself.

If you chose a), skip this.

When you make a stickfigure, there are many options to keep mind of.



-Creates a new pivot stick. Just connect it with a red dot on your stickfigure.



-Creates a circle, moslty used for heads and wheels and etc.



-Makes a stick skinner or thicker, depending on your concept of a particular character.



-This duplicates a stick (any) you may have selected. It will be then duplicated in the exact size and postion. Good for creating even legs and arms.



-This is probably the most powerful instument. When you have selected a stick, press this button, and the stick must stay in that postion. The effects can ONLY be seen in the actual animation set-up. In edit mode, it will only appear grey, signifying that this will be a frozen stick. Use this when you have a stick limb that is not needed to be moved.



-The delete button. Select a stick, press this button, and delete it. Warning: You can only delete sticks by the one that is the most outside, not the ones in the center. There is 1 master stick that cannot be destroyed.

Once you've finished your figure, be sure you have it perfectly made with no mistakes. Save it, and then click "Add to Animation" in file.

Now the most important part: Frames.

Add your stickfigure by clicking on the stickfig's name in the list next to you. There are various things to do with it.

Delete

If you have spare stickfigures, select them by clicking on the red dots (or pivots) and press the delete button.

Edit

Use this if it didn't come out how you think it would, and then fix it.

Center

Centers the stickfigure.

Flip

Flips the stickfigure into a mirror image... literally.

Colour

You can actually change the color of the stickfigure into whatever you like. Pink, blue, red, green, anything. But it has to be 1 solid color.

Size lever

When you see a number (usually 100) in an option box, it can resize the stickfigure into whatever you want.

Front and back

This option usually applies when there is more than 1 stickfigure. Choose a specific stickfigure. Front makes him shown to the front, and back makes it go to the back, not to be seen.

Once you have your frame prepared, click on the next frame button to make another frame. Notice that when you move the stickfigure, a grey shadow of it appears. That is for keeping track of it's earlier position (much like a claymation, only easier.) Remeber that the more frames you have, the more smooth the animation appears.Once you are done, your animation should at least resemble a smooth animation like this:



You can always control the speed.





Choose the speed that best fits your animation. If there is a mistake in a frame, then go to the bad frame by clicking on one of the frames at the top, and edit it.

Always save it as a .*GIF (animated .GIF) A menu will pop out, with a resize option. Make sure the resize option is at 100%, or else, your animation will be shrunken or enlarged.

Upload it in Imageshack, and show it off.
Spoiler: Sprite animation.
Sprite animation. The animation software at it's best. You will need:

-Imaging software (paint recommended)
-Pivot open.
-A huge determination.

First, open pivot, with a background.

Here's mine:



Go look for a spritesheet. Google is your friend.

Now, here is the very complicated part. Transparency is allowed on characters only. If there is pure white in your character, replace it with a white substitute (1 pixel difference of white.)

I'll use this. Remeber to credit those whos sprites you use. They will get you.

http://rmc.technoized.com/sprites/misc-sp-dancingbanana.png

Place each sprite in a seperate program (ergo, 5 sprites in 5 paint programs.)

Save your edited sprites as a .gif or .bitmap

Edit them with the least spce that you can. The pivot points will be automatically placed as a pivot stick; 1 pivot spot in one corner and another in an opposite corner.

Add the first part of the sprite, click next frame, delete it, add the next sprite, repeat. It took me 15min. for 1 with this one, based on the fact that to make the animation realistic, you must place the the figure to the exact pixel of where it was earlier.

Once you are done (and credite the person who made the sprite), pick a good, flowy speed to show it off.

And then you're done!


I will add some more guides, like implementing sprite animation, and animated backgrounds, as well as dramatic gradients (seen in movies.)
