Ahem. Just what the topic says. I had this random crazy idea while I was playing one of the really annoying N levels with about 730 mines and as many drones: zero gravity. I told you it was a crazy idea.

The basics are the screen stops being stationary and rotates to accommodate (sp?) the fact that the Ninja is no longer stuck on what used to be the floor, and there is, of course, no gravity. Drones are not affected by this, continuing to go around in their same old loops.
I don't know how this would be attained, as in how you'd get it (unlockable feature? map-specific little checkbox in Ned? a new object that you have to touch to toggle gravity on or off in that level?), but the gravity toggle switch seems the most reasonable.

How this changes gameplay is that now the Ninja has to push himself off of walls--he can walk on surfaces, but not for long because by running he effectively pushes himself off anyway. (Running is jumping at an angle so gravity keeps you on the ground.) This comes with an obvious downside, because if you get stuck out in the open you're screwed and have to either wait for a drone or missile to come along, or kill yourself. How much lag this would add to the game I don't know, but somebody just gimme some feedback on how this sounds.

Good? Bad? Utterly retarded?

#154287 19 2006-03-13T06:20:20Z Raigan
interestingly enough, this very suggestion was the premise for "ultramagnetic", a game which we may or may not ever finish properly. you could run around in 0-g, with magnet boots which locked you to the ground when you weren't jumping.

0-gravity platforming seems really interesting, buthas a lot of problems, most of which are interface-related. for instance, the camera: if it rotates (to make sure your feet are always "down" and your head is always "up") it makes you dizzy, but if it doesn't rotate, the controls can get confusing (you hold right to run across the floor, keep holding right to run up the wall, and now you're running along the ceiling -- moving left on the screen, but holding right on the keyboard).

also, no gravity means no ability to fine-tune your jumps (you move in a straight line.. with any amount of gravity, you can release jump at different times to choose different jump-paths, but setting gravity to 0 flattens all possible paths into the same boring line). if you allow the player to change direction in midair (by pressing left/right, as in most platformers), it gets way too easy/stupid since they can essentially fly around the level. however, if you don't let them move left/right in the air, it feels frustrating since while you're in the air you have no control over your movement.. you're just watching.

but it seems like there's potential for fun in there somewhere..
