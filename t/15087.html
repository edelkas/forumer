#282675 3 2007-01-07T23:12:44Z Twistkill
This is a technique that becomes almost second nature with practice.

First, you must land on a bounceblock with a good amount of momentum. Too slow, you won't get the necessary height, but if your approach is too fast, you will either die on impact or you will survive but you will go through the bounceblock.

When you hit the bounceblock, wait until you're as far down as the block will stretch. When it starts to rise up again, jump up as hard as you can when it's at its peak. This will launch you high up.

There is also another variation of this that involves jumping multiple times. Get your necessary momentum onto the bounceblock, and when it reaches as far down as it will go, jump up twice the moment it starts to rise up again instead of at it's peak. If done correctly, you will get 2 little jumps up to get a huge boost. This can also be done up to 3 jumps if you can hit the jump button quickly enough.

There is a bunch of training levels with descriptions in a topic made by Atilla called St. Atilla's College for Ninja. This trick is described there, as well as many more advanced tricks necessary to become a good N player.

I hope I've been helpful to you.

#282800 6 2007-01-08T02:45:14Z wedgie
Yeah as jtbandes touched on, when you land on the top of the bounce block, the more movement there is while the bounce block lowers will result in the bounce block moving a lot lower than if you just landed on it and didn't move.

The amount of height needed doesn't necessarily need to be a lot either. You can still do a double jump with a surprisingly little amount of height. Although dropping from a greater heigh will almost always result in a higher double jump. Check out my top demo on this for a little example of a double jump from a small height. Note how much horizontal movement there is while the block moves downwards.
On the other end of the scale, as Twistkill mentioned, falling from too much heigh will make you fall through the block, but you can still double jump on the block as long as you hit the first jump before you fall through the block. In this type of jump, the amount of time between the 2 jumps will be greater than a double jump from a small height. My score on 66-0 is an example of this. (the drop on the left side)

#282814 7 2007-01-08T03:26:26Z LittleViking
Moving left and right as the bounceblock is lowering is the easiest way to make it go down farther. For whatever reason, they go lower when you move sideways on them.

If you can get the bounceblock down low enough, you can actually press jump twice before you leave it, and that sends you outrageously high. It's not a difficult skill to master, but it does take some practice.
