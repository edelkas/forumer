For my part:

I feel like the measures have done very little in the way of garnering more security. Do you feel like we've become more secure or stopped terrorist attacks since the Act was passed? Nearly all of the people who have been scrutinized and brought in for interrogation under the Patriot Act have been Americans with no association to terrorists. The people who have been brought in were talking generally about terrorism, discussing a distaste for the way the government has been operating, or running Internet searches about certain topics. These Americans lost the freedom to explore information and speak freely--two freedoms guaranteed by our Constitution for a reason.

The Patriot Act has certainly curtailed our freedom--and Ben Franklin may have predicted correctly, it hasn't endowed us with a heightened sense of security.

China has a pretty effective system of self-censorship. The Chinese government doesn't actually have to watch everyone. All they have to do is instill the threat of surveillance and assume that the people are staying in line. Chinese people aren't allowed to research terms like "democracy", "June 6th" (a reference to the protests at Tiananmen Square), and a plethora of words related to the religious group Falun Dong.

China is very safe. Seriously! If you're the right person, believing the right things, and exercising only a few of your civil liberties; you will be very, very safe in China. America is not China. We want freedom. And we have it at the sake of security, sometimes; but we need a small government which allows us to do these things.

And even so, there exists evidence which shows we had (and have) the intelligence to prevent terrorist attacks without this Act. Our infrastructure is tight enough as is. All of the security measures, including those in airports, have mostly harried the average Joe in attempt to make other people feel secure.

In truth, the panopticon is not there. The government is allowing us to be as free and unprotected as we ever were, for the most part. The Patriot Act exists only in the "hearts and minds"--as it were--of the American midwest, where it inspires those seeking knowledge and a broader worldview to instead, perhaps, sit down and do their homework, pay their taxes, and try not to do anything that might make them look akin to the camel-jockeys overseas.
