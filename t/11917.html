#215958 13 2006-08-16T03:08:45Z TornadoTK
Oh man, if you want game ideas, I've got endless notebooks and scraps dedicated to them. One of my favorite things to do is create trap-based games. Tripwires, button traps, and mind-games in FPS's always make me think. I have some ideas scratched down about an FPS strictly based on setting traps for other players to fall into.

Another one of mine is an FPS loosely based off of Smash Bros. Everyone starts out in some kind of floating arena. There are FPS-like weapons, but all of them are used to affect force, gravity, or time in some way. The object of this game is to knock people into a cube-shaped forcefield that surrounds the map. Normally, this isn't easy, but like Smash Bros., the more damage you take, the easier it is for you to go flying. To help this, players have jetpacks with a Soldat/Tribes-esque fuel supply. I wrote a lot of stuff about it, and I'm pretty sure it can be made into a mod, especially in the Source engine.

Hell, if anyone knows much about this kind of 3D game creation, I'd love to see this game come to life.

#217625 18 2006-08-20T18:06:11Z Bigblargh
In my game, the camera is third-person. The setting doesn't matter, I don't know what the plot would be. But wherever you went, such as an urban street, you can find weapons all over the place. Like stop signs, littered crowbars, bottles, bats from stores, or anything that a baddie might be carrying around. But every weapon has a "resonance," which is a vertical meter on the top right that will shoot up (but decrease gradually) every time you use the weapon. If the meter goes all the way up, the object will break and you have no choice but to scrap it. The amount the meter goes up per hit depends on what the object is. A wooden bad would be broken very quickly, but a crowbar would stick with you for a long time. But to balance out the game, if you get a really good weapon, you can bet there are a bunch of baddies right around the corner to "break in" that lead pipe you have there.
I've had this idea swimming around in my head for a while.

#217856 21 2006-08-21T09:17:52Z Atilla
"Virtual Politician", an epic game of strategy and extreme cynicism. You have to cheat, lie and swindle your way to the top. You start out with only some savings from you day job for campaign funding, and have to get supporters by making vitriolic speeches, whining about everything the government does, attending protests and kicking up a ruckus anywhere with media coverage. You could hire cronies to improve your powers of persuasion - for example, speechwriters would improve the impact of your public speeches (each would have a rating for things like "praise", "insults", and "diverting unwelcome questions"), clerks could dig up dirt on your opponents to descredit them, and conduct polls of the electorate. Managing your personal beurocratic empire would, of course, be a major part of the stategy. Things could be broken up with action sequences - for example, you could pay someone to plant forged evidence of bribery in your opponent's office, and would then have to guide them into the building and place the "evidence" without alerting anyone. As you gained influence, you would have to join a party and climb to the top of the heirachy (first state, then federal) by stabbing your "allies" in the back, yet still keeping enough people loyal - or at least suitably bribed or intimidated - to you to be selected as the party leader. You would have to carefully manage the selection of your party members to make sure they're not a threat to you, but still better than the opposiing parties. Once you managed to get into government, you goal is to stay there as long as possible. This may be achieved with your usual methods, but also by shrewd management of the economy, spewing forth mountains of government-funded propanganda, pork barreling and so on. Your score for the game will be determined by the following formula: score = time spent in each position * point value of position (the more important your job, the higher the score, obviously) + amount of personal benefits (bribes, taxpayer-funded benefits, etc) in dollars.
