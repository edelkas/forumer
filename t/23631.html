Here are my poorly-formed caffeine-inspired thoughts from 1 AM. You're Welcome.

It seems to me that the vast majority of philosophical disputes that I come across these days can be accounted for simply by linguistic confusion. Most "confusing" philosophical dilemmas only seem so because the language involved is confused, which results in poor thinking. The whole Idealism/Materialism debate is a good example of this. In this context, "perceptions" and "objects" are really just different-sounding labels for exactly the same phenomena. The meaning of the words is independent of the pressure vibrations in the air or the shapes of the written symbols that carry those meanings. What the Idealist refers to as "forms" or "thoughts" are perceptions of the same phenomena that a materialist calls "matter." Meaning is what ends up being significant, and not the terminology involved.

Pragmatism has a similar kind of issue. The pragmatic theory of truth is that that which is useful to believe is true. People think that this is somehow a separate idea from say, correspondence theory of truth (that which corresponds to reality is true), but that's clearly not true; it's just an unnecessary (and confusing) redefining of the word "truth." When people talk about something being true, typically, they mean what correspondence theory says. It's a semantic trick on the part of the pragmatists to call useful propositions true. Useful propositions are useful. Pragmatists are really people who advocate usefulness over truth. However, they do this in the most confusing way possible, by redefining a word that already has a commonly understood definition.

"Free Will" is another example of a linguistically confused concept. I can hardly find two people who agree on what those two words even mean. Most determinists agree completely on what exists, but are confused in their terminology. Most people, whether aware of it or not, believe in an essentially deterministic universe. Tsukatu/Strife and I talked about this a while back, we agreed, pretty much, about what existed in the universe, we were just arguing about what to call all of these things. That alone wouldn't be a big issue, except that for a while I thought there was some genuine disagreement - and that is why linguistic confusion is a real issue.

I'm just as guilty of this as the next guy. I dunno if Dave remembers, but we had a discussion-type-thing a while back. My entire argument was nothing more than some semantic contortionism. He saw that as bullshit, and pounced on that right away. That was an important lesson for me. I learned that confusing people with Latin and equivocation was no way to conduct a discussion.

Now, obviously, there are real disagreements in philosophy, but I'm starting to think that they're few and far between.

When I think, I've found that I don't usually think in words, but rather in abstractions and meanings. This is cool for when my thinking is stuck in my own head, but when I'm trying to communicate to others, it's not exactly helpful. I try to write, but it never comes out the way I want it to. I'm normally pretty happy. Or even-keeled at the least, but lately I've been feeling this sort of existential anguish without really understanding why. I feel like I know the answer to all of these questions I have, but there's something going wrong in the process that converts these abstractions into words, and that's adding to the frustration.

It feels like when I post in the SD, I'm only criticizing the ideas of others, and not really putting my own forward. I don't mean to do this, but it ends up happening because any time I try to put a thesis forward, it ends up being unrefined and sounding wrong when I read it over. This makes me think about whether it was ever even correct in the first place. It's not that I'm afraid of being wrong, it's more that I don't want to contribute something that I think is wrong or shaky.

I have more things that I'm thinking about, but they'll have to come later because I'm falling asleep right now. This topic is my thought toilet. Feel free to do the same - dump whatever's on your mind. I feel like if I can write down whatever I'm brooding over, it becomes less of an issue and my brain can relax a little more. If any moderator thinks this is a bad idea or this is in the wrong place, let me know and I'll shut up.

In conclusion, Bone Thugs-n-Harmony is amazing.

Words aren't owned by the majority, they're the "property" of the parties that use them. The meaning of a given word is situational - it depends on what linguistic framework is being used. If you're speaking English, words have true and objective definitions. They're the ones that you find in dictionaries. If you're speaking from some kind of specialized framework (the way most people talk) that isn't quite English as it's found in dictionaries, then words can mean whatever the people talking want.
