#466684 14 2008-09-03T04:45:24Z Mekkah
Beats like ours is the one that truly impresses me.

Good job Bracken......

Also, many bands in your similar genre often have extremely shitty singers that somehow make the cut, you however, were for some reason memorable - it fit the music well and was on key almost the whole time. Also, the sound is good. Work on the refinement in some areas and that can only make you better. The first song on that site had a little trouble from 1:00 - to about 1:15, where you were singing and it was overlapped by another person in the background. That area was a little sloppy in parts. I could see the effect you were going for there, where the person in the background kinda completes the line of the song OR repeats the same lyrics at a different time for a cool, droning, overdubbed effect. Maybe rehearse the timings in that part? I'm in a band by the way, and I've encountered similar problems...so yeah.

The guitar parts are nice, I think the phrasing (sp? wrong homophone?) at the intro of tetris for kicks was a little monotonous. The bass is great, but the guitar seems a little over droning during that part. Maybe, instead of bashing through the gates with that guitar, maybe a bit of an introduction, or a climb to the beginning of the riff. I'm a guitar player, and, when I have a riff that I don't feel should immediately start off the song, I'll use couplets or make chords out of the notes in the scale system - and THEN go to the riff. Let's say I'm playing in A Minor - I come up with a riff that uses notes in the natural minor scale, I don't want to lead off with it however. I'll make a chord progression or something that uses the notes in the riff. Two songs that show this are the Smashing Pumpkin's, "Cherub Rock", and the Strokes', "Reptilia". Both songs have great riffs and droning guiar pieces that function in a somewhat similar manner to your songs. In Reptilia, the bass starts off, the the feedback guitar which creates a nice opening over the low bass line. The bass part you play suits the riff great, maybe start off ONLY with the bass, have your guitarist come in with feedback, one of the methods I mentioned above, or whatever you want, really, and go from there. Cherub Rock has a great guitar piece that, "climaxes" into the song after the drums and the opening octave guitar parts. When you do this, you create a sensation of building tension, something that leads the listener up to the main guitar parts, which creates for a better song and a better listening experience.

Here are the songs -

Cherub Rock

and

Reptilia



Also, the other guitar parts introduced later in tetris for kicks were prefect, now you only need a little more variation at the beginning and go from there.
