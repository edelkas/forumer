Quote: Section One
~~~

Eyes closed, one palm facing the grey clouds above and one facing the gently blowing grass below Nijin meditated intently upon his training.  He reflected upon the last five years of his life here at the Doki Gardens.  Gardens is misleading, it was more of a shrine, temple even, located next to the Doki River.  The grounds were roughly five square miles of thick bamboo forest, five small white pagodas marking the corners of the gardens.  At the center was the Shrine of Doki, which had existed for many centuries as the center of Way. 

Nijin recounted his first impressions of the Shrines elaborate spires and floating rooms.  Parts of the Shrine sat directly above the Doki River below, levitating over the currents below, held in place by the monks who channeled their minds through the Shrine. 

A group of these monks were sitting on one of the concentration platforms in a traditional Way meditation stance, using their combined efforts to keep the platform stable in the air.  When the wind picked up the platform started to tilt and wobble but the monks did not break meditation and held the platform aloft.  As the winds continued to increase however they let the platform slowly drop back into its place and walked inside after ceremonially coming out of meditation.

Nijin remembered coming here as a teen, much older than most that arrive as infants, and he was looked at as an outcast by his peers.  He was however quick to learn the grounds and adapt to his surroundings.  He remembered skipping sleep to walk the grounds and memorize the gardens he now called home.  Within a few months he knew the gardens as well, if not better, as any other student his age. 

Nijin focused upon his teachers over the years; most notable of course was his Way Father, Kokije.  Every student had a Way Father or Mother, and they were the students personal instructors into Way.  Kokije had chosen Nijin shortly after he arrived in Doki.  It was Kokije who instilled the drive into Nijin to excel beyond his peers who had been here for many years more than he. 

Kokije was a member of the Higher Council at the time and was seated above all but two other monks.  Being selected by the third highest monk in Doki was an honor Nijin could barely understand in his ignorant youth.  Kokije had taught all but one of the grand monks of the current era, a feat unmatched in ages past.  Kokije was arguably the greatest teacher at the time of Nijins arrival, and Nijin would be his first student in nine years, a fact that did not escape the eyes of the Higher Council.

It was also Kokije who had given Nijin his name.  Nijin means fated one and many at the time speculated that Kokije saw Nijin as a special boy.  Nijins birth name was Hon-Burein, Burein being his hometown.  Burein is located about a thousand miles east of Doki in the province of Sorreina.  Hon-Burein had made the journey alone after escaping his home to follow Way.  Way was outlawed in Sorreina and practicing it by punishable with death.  Hon-Burein had heard about the Doki Gardens and strived for them since youth.  When he was old enough to make the journey he left under the cover of darkness and traveled west for six months until reaching the Doki River and a group of monks who pointed him upstream towards the Shrine.

The soft rain drops on his arms broke Nijins trance as he returned to this plane of thought.  He thought of his first feeble training sessions with Kokije as he walked back to the Shrine and his cot, he smirked at his past failures.  The rains came quickly and forcefully in Doki.

The dust flew up around Nijin as he was slammed and slid across the ground.  Kokije stood above him and looked down upon a now limp Nijin. 

Why not stand? Kokije asked, pressuring Nijin to regain his feet.  What holds you to the ground?

Nijin pushed against the ground with what little strength he had and raised his battered body no more than a foot when his wobbling arms gave out and he collapsed in a plume of dust.

Father, my imperfections hold me here.  I can not stand under their weight, came the answer from the bleeding lips of Nijin.

Kokije extended an arm to Nijin who grasped it loosely as he was pulled to a standing position. 

Defend yourself! Kokije demanded as his arm came around his body to strike Nijin.  Nijin raised a weak defense that Kokijes attack easily broke through, striking Nijin under his chin.  Again Nijin found himself on the dirt being looked down upon by his Way Father.

What is wrong Nijin; you know your flaws and yet do not correct them?  Until you take your knowledge and form it in your life you will never transcend.  Kokije again extended his arm to lift Nijin from the ground, but Nijin was too winded to grab it.  This is where you are now Nijin, you see my arm and are aware it will lead to you standing, yet you do not take it.  Your pain now will pale in comparison to what pain awaits you outside of these gardens, if you can not take my arm now under light pains how then can you transcend under the actual pain of the world?  You can not.  Now take my arm and stand.

Nijin willed every last bit of energy into his arm and raised it to grab Kokijes.  He was pulled to his feet and stood unsteadily before his Way Father.  He turned to look him in the face, I do not expect to suffer Father. 

Kokije stood to his full height and looked with compassion upon his student.  Placing his hand on Nijins shoulder he spoke, Then you have underestimated the task that is before you Nijin.  Kokije removed his hand and Nijin collapsed under his own weight.  Kokije knelt and left a wrapped bit of gold by his students side before turning and leaving him in the clearing.  Tonight was a Way passing, which signified the end of a month in Doki. 

A month lasts ninety days in Doki, and signifies the life and death of Gannei N, the founder of Way.  Gannei, when he embarked on the path to transcendence, did not consume anything in order to meditate without ceasing.  After ninety days of constant meditation he envisioned transcendence for the first time.  He came out of meditation and realized the futileness of this life and allowed one of his students to kill him so that he could enter blissful transcendence.  As he died he explained the path, but his exact words are lost to history, what remains is a belief that meditation can lead to transcendence, and only through intense training can it be achieved.

Gannei was dead, but in death he showed a way for those who follow him to reach transcendence without death.  Every Way passing was a celebration of his death and sacrifice by the necessary eating of gold.  Way monks did not eat at any other time, but instead fasted to focus their minds on their training and tasks.  This eating was an acknowledgement that their current bodies could not live without indulging, and only when they transcended could they refuse their bodies completely and be with Gannei N in Transcendence.  Gannei remains the only one to have seen transcendence in this life, still though, Way monks hold prophecy that there will come another who will possess the knowledge to not just see, but fully transcend in this life.

Quote: Section Three
~~~

Two hundred feet sounded reasonable when Kokije had explained the days training exercise earlier.  That was before Nijin was standing on a meditation two hundred feet above a churning Doki River.  Nijin was standing on the edge of the large stone slab, Kokije sitting behind him in the middle of the slab meditating to hold the platform in place against the winds.

Nijin knew what he was to do, and although he was taught to resist fearing his tasks he was afraid of this objective.  One hundred and fifty feet below him was bridge that had been built so many Way Passings ago Nijin doubted anyone knew exactly how long it had spanned the river.  Nijins goal was to jump from this platform and reach the bridge safely.

The spot Nijin was aiming for was the side of the bridgea mere ten feet widejust wide enough for Nijin to grab on and slow his fall.  Any error would lead to how many bruises and broken bones Nijin refused to think about and promptly dismissed that option. 

The top of the shrine was fifty feet below him to his right, the large golden spires reaching high into the sky.  The sun reflected off of them and blinded Nijin slightly.  He shielded his eyes and looked for a way down from his perch.  Kokije had not limited his student to how he could reach the bridge, and Nijin planned on using this freedom to its fullest extent.

He scanned the roofs of the shrine, all the angles too steep to jump from, even if he could make the jump to reach them.  He turned to his left and saw nothing but trees and paths, hardly a place to break a fall.  Behind him were the same thick green forests and dirt walkways lacing the grounds below. 

Nijin turned back to the shrine and saw something he missed during his first inspection.  Behind the spire closest to him was a small balcony to what he assumed was Way Master Aikikos room.  The ledge was small, but only sixty feet below the student and twenty feet out.  Nijin had found his way down. 

He closed his eyes and asked Gannei for strength.  Just as he crouched to jump Kokije spoke, as if reading Nijins mind, You will not make the jump to Aikikos balcony.

What? Nijin blurted out, not expecting to hear Kokijes voice until the training was over.

You will not make that jump, Kokije said again, the same stillness in his voice.  Nijin was taken back in the lack of faith his Father held in him.  Nijin had successfully landed much larger jumps than this, why would Kokije doubt him now?

Nijin put the doubts out of his mind and crouched to jump again.  Just as he left the platform, the point of no return, he realized the spire blocked his landing and Kokije had been right.  There was no angle present for Nijin to reach the balcony as he planned.  The fall took about a second before Nijins helpless body was flung against the golden spire and fell numb onto the roof of the shrine.

Kokije by this time had left the platform and was already waiting when his student hit the wooden shingles and slid to a bloody stop.  Nijins arm and both legs were broken badly and Nijin had no hope of standing.

Where it not for shock Nijin would have been writhing in pain but he lay motionless except for his heaving chest.  Face down on the roof his semiconscious state barely heard the words of Kokije, almost as if they were whispered to him through a glass window.

You may find it brave to dismiss your doubts, but within every doubt is knowledge to be extracted about your circumstances.  Do not dismiss doubts as foolish, instead learn from them and understand why they are in your mind.  To think any thought you have is useless is to underestimate the Ganneis plan to bring us to Transcendence.  His guidance placed those thoughts into your mind and those who do not examine every thought are like incompetent children.

You will receive much guidance in your trials.  This guidance is not a warning or a suggestion.  It is a demand that you are a fool to ignore.  A command issued from Gannei himself that only the blind can not see and the deaf can not hear.  You are neither blind nor deaf, unless by choice you make yourself to be.  You chose blindness today, and now you feel the consequences.

Kokije leapt down to the ground below as the large stone platform the two were on moments before crashed into the Doki river with such a thunderous crash birds miles away took flight in search of safety.  Nijin, still gasping for air, lay still on the rough wooden roof.  He drifted in and out of consciousness until finally he surrendered to it and let himself drift off.  His recovery would take months.

~~~

Blurred bamboo shoots shot past his face on either side.  The barely discernable shape of his goal could be seen a few hundred feet ahead, ducking and twisting to avoid colliding with the foliage around them.  At these speeds you dont have time to think, so much as just react.  Nijin could feel his lungs straining to match his bodys needs and he willed them to keep going.  His goal flashed before him again, just before it ducked off to the left, never once missing a stride. 

Nijin new this part of the garden from walks he had taken upon arriving in Doki.  While running he plotted the course for where his prey would most likely be going.  Just as he figured it out he collided with an outcropping of bamboo.  His chest turned to the right, his arms and legs flailing as they turned with his body.  He was lifted off his feet and fell on his back, his left leg bent awkwardly under his body. 

He gasped to regain his breath and looked down the path to the left of him.  He saw his prize duck in and out of the clearing a mere twenty feet ahead of him, oblivious to the fact its pursuit had ended abruptly.  Nijin slammed his fist into the ground, furious he had let it escape and failed his mission. 

He laid there for some time before he heard a rustling behind him.  He strained his neck to look and see who it was but before he could Kokije was standing over him speaking, Son, why did you fail?

The words were stern and guiding, like everything Kokije spoke.  Nijin responded, still winded, Father, my impurities blinded me to what lay ahead.

No! Kokije yelled as he reached down and struck Nijin across the face with an open hand, You did not want to succeed, and so you did not.  You must want what you desire so greatly nothing else can stop you.  What do you desire Nijin?

Nijin wiped his chin of the blood that had started to run onto it before speaking, I desire the end of impurities and transcendence.  Nijin coughed and spit the blood from his mouth onto the ground.

Then you must crave to the ends of the realm, only then can you reach your destiny, Kokije knew he would regret those words as soon as he spoke them.  He had desired to keep those thoughts private until Nijin was of age to begin his mission.  Kokije knew Nijins natural inquisitiveness would mill over the words until he had an answer. 

Nijin, its time for you to know your fate, Kokije spoke slowly and softly, not his typical fatherly tone, It is your destiny to follow in Gannei Ns path.

Nijins tired mind raced for an answer as it struggled to form words, Butuhdont we?  He trailed off as his mind attempted to digest the information.

The other members of the Council do not agree with my evaluation.  You are not to speak of this to anyone, Kokije spoke again with great concern in his voice for his students future.

Nijin was still scrambling for answers as he asked, But dont we all follow His path, isnt that the fate of all of us?

Kokije regretted this conversation but knew he had to answer Nijins questions.  We do, yes, but you must follow him in this life and transcend before death, Kokije spoke while extending a hand to pick up Nijin.

Nijin spoke while standing, But Father, why has this fallen upon me?

It is your fate, son.

Quote: Section Five
~~~

Later that evening, just after dusk, Nijin was walking through the gardens.  He took his favorite path, one he had found himself some years prior.  It wasnt a main path and as such was much more private than the main paths that led to and from the Shrine.  Nijin walked carefully along the unbeaten path, making sure he didnt disturb anything as he contemplated the last ten hours of his life. 

A bird called out from above Nijin as he turned to continue deeper into the woods.  Ten hours ago he was just a normal student of Way.  He searched his mind for answers. 

Sure Ive been an exceptional student, being able to surpass my peers after only five years of studying at the Gardens, but that doesnt enable me to transcend in life. 
But Kokije did choose me, his first student in years, that had to mean Kokije saw something in me that even I didnt see, let alone the rest of the Higher Council.

What if Kokije was wrong though, its possible.  He said the Higher Council didnt see what he saw in me.  But Kokije is the only one who knows me personally, and the only one who had ever seen me train.  Could that be why Kokije always seemed to push me so much harder than the others? 

Nijin came to a clearing and he stopped for a moment.  He looked out across the opening and saw no movement but the rhythmic swaying of trees in the evening breeze.  He walked out into the clearing and sat down to meditate.  This was his favorite spot to meditate, unlike most students who preferred the floating platforms over the river.  Nijin preferred the seclusion of this clearing and the silence it offered.

He turned his left palm up towards the star filled sky and his right faced the soft cold dirt below him.  The moonlight gave the grove a blue glow until a lonely cloud drifted in front of it.  As the clearing was under the shadow of the cloud Nijin felt a cool hand upon his shoulder.

He looked back and up and the two figures eyes met.  Nijin was speechless and instantly stood to look Gannei face to face.  Nijin had never seen a picture of Gannei N but he knew instantly who he was looking at.  The ephemeral form before him wisped in the breeze and shared the same blue aura the glen had moments before.  Nijin studied Ganneis body, which was frail and weak in appearance, apparently how Gannei looked in the last moments of his life.

My chosen son, why are you here? A voice came from the form though its lips did not move, its face covered with compassion.  The voice was deep and resonated in Nijins head and he was forced to think over what he heard multiple times before he could comprehend. 

Im seeking answers, Master, Nijin spoke as calmly as he could manage.

What answers do you seek? Gannei spoke again, seemingly speaking to Nijin from within the students mind.

I desire to know my fate.

Ganneis figure looked horribly troubled as is spoke more words into Nijins mind, Your fate is mine to know son.  Times are more pressing than you could ever imagine.  These gardens are my last refuge in this world, and there are those who seek to destroy them.  This shrine, these gardens, the students.  If they are lost nothing will remain of the Way.  Those who oppose us have not made much noise to their intentions, but their desire for our end runs deep in their bloodlines.  I have chosen you to defend my people, for reasons you can not yet understand.

Nijin grasped to retain all this information; the Gardens have always seemed safe.  Never before had the idea of them not existing crossed his mind, let alone the loss of the other students, or even Kokije.  Ganneis voice continued to reverberate within him.

You must adopt a new way, the way of N.  N is not a way of peace, nor is it of war.  Nor is it a way of life or death.  It is the way of surviving, of transcending; it is the way of the ninja.  You must reach transcendence before death Nijin; the fates of those you cherish rest in your hands alone.  The way of N is the only way you can achieve this, without it you will fail.  Do not expect this to be an easy journey, for those who are against you will stop at nothing to see you fail.

Ganneis form lost all definition and faded into the breeze.  Nijin stared silently at where his master had stood, unable to move.  He finally gathered the will to sit and collapsed onto the dirt unconscious. 

~~~

Quote: Section Six
~~~

The sun rose the next morning to a still and unconscious Nijin.  A bird floated effortlessly in the wind, gliding up and down with the drafts before settling on Nijins shoulder.  Nijin rolled over in the grass as he slowly came to.  Raising a hand to shield his eyes from the rising sun in front of him he sat up and began to recollect the events of last night.

His gut reaction was to reject the memories as a dream, something his mind had conjured up as a solution.  But the memory was too vivid to deny, Ganneis words still sounding in his mind like a chorus.  My chosen son, why are you here?

That was the question Nijin was seeking to answer.  Nijin came to Doki seeking meaning, seeking a purpose, seeking his fate.  Nijin thought all of this over carefully as he walked hurriedly back to the Shrine and Kokije.  What of this new path?  Since his arrival at the Gardens he has studied Way, he knew nothing of N or what it meant to follow it.  He only knew what little Gannei had said the night before. 

Nijin reached the Shrine and ran up the stairs, cutting through a group of monks holding training.  He yelled sorry over his shoulder but never broke stride, skipping two, sometimes three, steps as his pace quickened.  Kokije was wrong Nijin realized, he wasnt following in Gannei Ns path, he was to surpass Gannei and transcend in this life.

At the top of the stairs Nijin tripped and fell, landing face first at Kokije and the rest of the Higher Councils feet.  He looked up clumsily and pulled Kokije to the side, interrupting the meeting that was occurring.  Nijin didnt bother to apologize or explain to the other members what was going on.  He simply spoke to Kokije in between rushed grasps at air, You were wrong, he paused, The way of N, the ninja, again he caught his breath, Gannei, the grove, last  Nijin stopped as Kokije raised a hand to signal him to slow down.

What is it Nijin, what happened? Kokije tried desperately to calm his student.

Nijin took a deep breath and exhaled slowly, some of the Council members grunting in disgust as Nijin took his time interrupting their session.  Nijin looked Kokije in the eyes, not as a son, but as an equal, I am to follow the way of N, or we all will suffer the same horrible fate.

This boldness garnered snickers and chuckles from the Council, all but Kokije found it hard to not erupt into laughter.  It was preposterous to propose the idea of the Shrine being in danger, let alone its students.  And the idea of there being another path than Way was the most ludicrous idea the Council members had heard in decades.

Sourjiho, a senor member of the council for twenty years, spoke for all of them when he inquired, What in all of existence has possessed you boy?

His comments were echoed in various amounts of words by the rest of the council but Nijin ignored their jeering and stared intently and confidently into his fathers eyes.  Kokije stared back at Nijin, his sons eyes emblazoned with passion and confidence.  Kokije knew at once his worst fears had been realized.  He knew from when he first saw the boy that Nijin would be special, but he always hoped it would not come at the expense of grave circumstances.

Nijin, son, you must follow your fate where ever it may lead you.  I am no longer able to help you; this journey must be taken on your own.  Leave in the evening, and may Gannei shelter us all.

Kokijes faith in Nijin silenced the mockery coming from behind the two.  Nijin and Kokije stood, eyes locked, for a few moments longer before Kokije bowed deeply and walked past Nijin down the stairs and into the forest.  Nijin turned to look at the Council, fists clenched, looking down upon them in their stunned silence with consideration before turning to go to his room.  What lay ahead Nijin doubted even Gannei could foresee.
