The comparison to the word "retarted" "stupid" "crazy" and "queer" are not valid. All of them have an intrisic negative meaning.
If "retarded" can be viewed by some to be intrinsically negative, why can't "gay"? What makes you view "gay" as a neutral term and "retarded" as a negative one. There was a time when "retarded" was the acceptable term. Then it was chased down a euphemism railroad to newer terms, each eventually being deemed too offensive and moving onward to a softer-sounding word. Now, even if I utilize the hideously euphemised term "differently mentally abled", I could be perceived as a dick. What makes the term "retarded" any more detrimental to its origin social group than "gay"? Doesn't "gay" have euphemisms of its own that could be used to prevent it from connecting to its less-than-savory alternative usage? "Retarded" means mentally handicapped. Why being handicapped is intrinsically negative is beyond my comprehension. It's the same as being different. So, to call someone "retarded" or "gay" is to call them different, in a way that insults the mentally handicapped or homosexuals.

In sum, they both have neutral extrinsic meanings. Their intrinsic meaning is defined by how they're used. I think what you meant to say is that "gay" is still being used widespread to refer to homosexuals as a whole and that using as an insult might corrupt it. Because, when it is used as an insult, its intrinsic meaning becomes negative.

Secondly, the word "queer" doesn't need to be negative. In fact, it most often isn't. In academia, the study of gay and lesbian culture is termed "Queer Studies". No lie. The term "queer" is not viewed negatively by that group--they have commandeered the term to represent themselves in a positive light. The slogan "We're here, we're queer; get used to it" was not employed because it used a socially-charged word negatively. It was employed because it used the same word that was previously disparaging in a positive light. The word itself does not bear an intrinsic negative meaning. The meaning of the word "queer" is the same as the meaning of the word "homosexual". Again... the meaning of the word is neutral. The intrinsic meaning of the word is based on how it is used.

Thirdly, the same applies to the term "crazy". I have plenty of friends who would call me eccentric, manic, crazy, zany, and any slew of words which a person without such personality traits would deem offensive. But I identify myself with those words and I realize that my friends do not use them disparagingly. Therefore, the intrinsic meaning of those words as used by my friends in reference to me is positive, not negative.



Unfortunately, there's no way to make people stop using words that have intrinsically negative meanings. Why? Because we define whether a word is negative or positive. Language doesn't dictate when a person uses a bad word. People dictate when they use a word badly. And using gay as an insult is certainly using the term badly.

Honestly, I don't think there are any words which cannot be used appropriately if intended as such. And although I'm in the position where I often have to tell people which words to use and not use (particularly at the office), I'm not one to believe that a word's meaning cannot be salvaged from its connotation.
