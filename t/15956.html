#298817 2 2007-02-19T03:29:07Z Raigan
this is possibly the least well-written part of the code, aside from the editor; instead of trying to figure out the correct formula, i simply used all the conditionals i could think of until the results appeared to be correct.

you can see a diagram of the edge states here: http://www.harveycartel.org/metanet/tutori...B.html#section2

Basically, the problem is that for each edge in the grid, there are actually _two_ edges, because each cell stores the states of the 4 edges that bound the cell. Or, another way of looking at it is that if you placed a solid lineseg on the edge, you'd still need to define which side of the lineseg was "outside" and which was "inside".

The convention we established was for a cell's edges to have their surface normals pointing towards the center of the cell. For each pair of identical edges, the one that belongs to the cell whose tile is solid along the edge is turned off (because its normal points in towards the full/solid region), while the one that belongs to the cell that's empty along the edge is turned on (because its normal points out towards the empty space).

Doors are "double-sided" edges, both edges are solid.

This is one of the many reasons why we encourage people to take our ideas and implement them themselves -- our methods aren't the best.

A much better solution would be to store edges in a seperate grid, or to find a different way to represent the surface of the world. Unfortunately it'll take a while, but when we're done robotology we're going to write about our new method, which is much, much, much simpler, faster, and each to implement. Basically, the best way to represent the surface of the world is to use arbitrary linesegs and arcs, and only use "tiles" in the editor.
