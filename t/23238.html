#20855 1 2008-07-21T21:59:19Z Strife
To warn you, this is mostly just venting.
I'm a federali code-monkey, and the latest bit of annoyance I've had to deal with is creating an Acquisition Report template in LaTeX (the typesetting language, not the stable dispersion of polymer microparticles). My boss has recently been shown that LaTeX exists, and now he's going crazy and demanding that everything use it, and he's volunteered me to help. Thing is, I don't know jack about LaTeX. Or more accurately, I don't have any experience with LaTeX. So I've devoted pretty much the entirety of this work day so far to learning it.

SPOILER: It fucking blows.

First off, I am amazed to discover this new culture in which people get so excited about fonts and typesetting. If you skim through some usenet posts on the subject, you'll find people who dedicate their lives to arguing just how many pica of spacing is necessary after a wide variety of punctuation marks depending upon the context. And then it hit me that there is probably an entire department of losers working for Microsoft who have zero other reason to be on the payroll other than their work in maintaining and refining truetype fonts. To me, this seems about as useful as employing people to count the blades of grass on the front lawn, ensuring via a set of rules made as convoluted as possible that there is a prime number of blades at any given moment, and that their summed lengths exactly equal the number of tire treads in the senior staff parking lot.
If you are this fascinated by fonts, I highly advise that you kill yourself.

Secondly, I had no idea that people gave so much of a collective shit about spaces. Before LaTeX, I only knew of 'a space', like the kind I put after words and sentences and shit. But now I've been introduced to this wide collection of "word spaces" and "sentence spaces" and "thickspaces" and "thinspaces," and I realize that in the eyes of these people who care about these little tiny fucking nuances, my typesetting has been in flagrant violation of all that is holy in the typesetting bible. Did you know there are at least three kinds of dashes, too? See, I've only been using the one --- the single hyphen --- but apparently that's incorrigible. The rule is: a single hyphen for compound words such as in "a double-hyphen for number ranges," a double-hyphen for number ranges as in "Snape kills Dumbledore in pp. 348--349," and a triple-hyphen for references, as used above around "the single hyphen." And y'know that little thingy in the corner of your keyboard, just below the F1 key and on the same key as the squiggly key? That little guy that you press when you want to get to the console in many developer-friendly games? That little bastard, who is so far the fuck in the keyboard's Alaskan wilderness that it destroys the flow of your touch-typing every time you have to press him, is the preferred left quote character. This means that every time I've typed ["rapably soft"], I should've typed [``rapably soft"]. Yep yep - two characters as the left quote, one on the right. Seems unbalanced to me, but I didn't make the rule.

Thirdly, since typesetting is all there is to this bitch, that's all the manual talks about. But it has to be thorough and it has to make itself look comprehensive, so it draws out every single possible fucking variation in typesetting as much as it can. Reading this thing is like listening to your grandpa (the one with senile dementia) talk about every last one of his fishing stories, all that he's ever lived through, heard, or made up, in a single, unstoppable, slowly-advancing wall of pain. This thing is nothing short of pure, unrelenting agony stitched into the fibers of the parchment. No one but the most diehard fanatic of typesetting can read this without having to take a break every paragraph, and you'd have to be just fascinated, just ass-to-the-windshield fascinated with typesetting to read the entire thing in one go, which is what I'm being asked to do, and I quite simply lack that amount of enthusiasm.

And finally, the language itself has to be one of the most contrived and ungeneralized I've ever personally come into contact with. Every little change you want to make has its own name, its own fucking 'environment' to work in. For example --- in every language where the backslash is an escape character, how do you write an actual backslash? In every sane language, it's just two backslashes: '\\'. But not in LaTeX, it isn't. No siree-bob. You wanna know what it is in LaTeX? Do you really, really wanna know? It's '\textasciibackslash'. "Text ASCII motherfucking Backslash." But oh, that's just the beginning. For every little bit of whitespace you could possibly cram in before, after, and in between punctuation, or even indentation, there's a unique command.
\spaceafterleftmarginbutbeforebulletpoint{5pt}
\spacebetweenbulletpointandtextbutnotbetweenleftmarginandbulletpoint{4pt}
\spacebothbetweenbulletpointandtextaswellasleftmarginandbulletpoint[prettypleasewithsugarontop]{8pt}
These same shenanigans are exactly why I ended up turning my back on Perl. "I want Perl to pick up my daughter after school." "No problem! Just set the environmental variable called dollarsign-chinese-horse-symbol to reference a filehandle that points to a file named three backspace characters and you're good to go!" Every function, every single way of fixing a problem, has its own unique name and implementation, and that's just plain unacceptable for something that allegedly deserves to be called usable.

In complete honesty, I don't have any qualms about using MS Word, or open-source equivalent, for my typesetting needs, primarily because I don't exhibit such an excessive amount of caring, and I really don't think it'll be to my detriment unless the guy at my job interview is on the LaTeX dev team and has a stroke after seeing my resume, but then I probably wouldn't want to work with him anyway.

And now, back to work.
*sigh*
Shit sucks.
