This short story is entitled "The Four Days", for lack of a better name.
A baby was born to an aristocratic family on a bright spring morning. They were very happy, of course, and he was such a cute little baby, of course, and they said these things over and over again. Four days later, on another bright spring morning, is the Naming Ceremony. Friends and relations arrive from all over the country to see the baby at the ceremony, and with the family's moderate riches, they had had a wizard hired to analyze any magical powers he might have inherited from his mother, who was a fairly accomplished sorceress. The wizard just arrived, and the soothsayer too -- any family who can afford one would certainly hire a soothsayer to say something about their child.
While most of the guests have arrived and a only few more are trickling in, the wizard casts a few spells over the baby, and declares the results: "You're in luck, madam; If his magical powers come into focus, he will be very powerful. Why, he should be at least as powerful as the Archmages of legend. You should - well, if. It's a pity no one's found an accurate way to predict whether the powers will focus or not." His parents are fairly well-informed about this issue, but basically all it means is that you don't know beforehand whether your child could be a wizard or not. Their powers usually come into focus between the ages of eight and thirteen, but sometimes earlier and sometimes later, and often never. You never knew, so you could always keep hoping.
Then comes the Naming itself. The soothsayer has to wait, since it's bad luck to tell the future of an unnamed child. They name him Lirek, after his great-uncle Lirek who was the best wizard in family history.
After that, everyone is all excited about what the soothsayer is going to say. It's usually a interesting fact about the child's life, which may or may not be all that useful to know. Of course, sometimes the fact isn't interesting, but in those cases it is often significant to the life of the child, or to the world. And they are almost always right.
However, Lirek was an unusual child. After a bit of mumbo-jumbo and hand-waving, the soothsayer has only one thing to say:
"He will not die young."
The guests are a little disapointed. Everyone starts whispering or talking immediately after that pronouncement, and since all scheduled activities are over, it is indeed time to chat. The general consensus is that it was not interesting but was good to know, although the child's mother is heard to say "Well, to tell the truth, it's kind of relieving to know that, but it's not good to think about dying young even if you do know that it's not going to happen."
Gradually, conversations drift into other topics, and guests drift home. Before long, only the child, and his parents are left.
"Well, at least the ceremony is over," sighs the father.

The noonday sun blazes brightly on the two children relaxing in the field. One is Lirek; One is a childhood friend, Quiio. It is one of those happy days of youth when everything goes right and you have no worries... for Quiio, at least. After a long silence, Quiio speaks up. "I got accepted into the University!"
There is no schooling for most people; the University is where children with magical abilities are taught to be wizards.
"So was I," Lirek mutters.
"What? I thought they didn't let people in until they're thirteen. You're only eleven!"
"Yeah, only the wizard from the University said they'll have to make an exception. Something about 'powers coming into focus so early.' - I was only six years old. It's rare, he said. He said 'It wouldn't be right to leave powers like that seven years without an education.'"
"Huh! Lucky you. Why so morose?" Indeed, Lirek is not looking particularly happy about this turn of events.
"The wizard also said..." Lirek pauses, probably for effect. "'Live young, die young.' I don't think he meant me to hear that though."
Quiio does not flag. "That can't be right. Didn't your soothsayer say you wouldn't die young?"
"Who told you that?"
"I was at your Naming Ceremony." Quiio grins.
"Sure, when you were a child of two years. Probably your mother told you."
"Lirek, you're impossible." He turned away in mock disgust.
There is another long silence. This time it is broken by Lirek. "What kinds of magic do you think they teach?"
"Probably flying! And throwing fire and calling lightning, and, and messing with the balance of the world, and..."
After Quiio finishes demonstrating his ignorance of magical powers, Lirek speaks. "I think they teach spying, science, a bunch of random cantrips you'll never need, and when not to use magic. Probably they teach the last one the most."
"Now, why would you think that?" Quiio asks, his expression quizzical.
"It's just a feeling I have, like the one I had when I thought the Johnsons' cat was dead and it was. No one could have predicted that."
"Maybe you saw it first then. Or maybe you're just a lucky shmuck."
There is another silence.

It's evening, with the sun about to set over the horizon. The autumn leaves have begun to fall, filling the tower window with a colorful view of yellows, oranges, and reds under a sky of surreal violet. Presently a black-robed man ascends the stairway into the highest room, followed by one robed in yellow. They are engaged in friendly banter, about old times they were both at the University. They're now discussing the various skills of other former students. But shortly, the conversation becomes more serious:
"But you, Lirek! You were the best at everything. You could have done so much good with your powers! Been a maker of kings, you could have--" the man in yellow begins.
But Lirek cuts him off: "You remember as well as I do that the Old Ones were afraid of my power." His voice is stern and brooks no protest.
"Yes, but..."
"And you know the legends they taught us: 'Some mighty wizard villian took over the whole place but he was defeated by ancient powers.'" his voice is mocking, but does not lose its edge. "Who wrote the legends? Not the villians! King-making is all well and good, but what if the people don't like the king? Then it's the wizard's fault. Then they call up their Powers Of Good and wham! I do not want to end my life as a villian in a fairy tale." his face darkens. "Speaking of which, I do not want to end my life at all."
The man in yellow smiles at that. "Of course, no one wants that. But immortality is one of those things everyone wants and no one gets. It's one of those paradoxes."
Lirek smiles wryly. "I always said you have no ambition, Quiio." Quiio laughs. "But seriously - what's a body but a bunch of matter? Magic has always been good at influencing matter."
"You and your theories. Magister Vandlen said that life doesn't reside in the body, it's the magic that does it. There's some pretty good theories out there, after all, wizards do start losing their powers before they die..."
"Sure they do, that's why they die. I've done plenty of research into it myself. Most everything that kills a wizard is something they could have stopped if they had their powers. The rest, well... Probably some sort of spell could solve those too. Dying of old age only happens because you're weak then and vulnerable to every little thing that hits you. But magic can hold back those things! So all that's left is to find out why your magic decays, and stop that..."
Quiio, worried by the seriousness with which Lirek has been speaking but not interested in the subject, tries to end the conversation. "I think you're becoming just a wee bit obsessed with death, Lirek. It's getting late, though - I should be resting. There's a Council meeting tomorrow, and I wouldn't want to lose more sleep than I will anyway."
"That's just because you've never had a healthy obsession for anything before, silly," says Lirek as Quiio vanishes in a puff of smoke. Smiling but slightly annoyed, he continues anyway, "Fine - go play with your kings and empires."


A young man - barely more than a boy, actually - is riding up the steep incline to the ancient tower. The horse doesn't like it. Not only has he been riding hard for hours on end, but most of the time through snow. The horse would also like it if there was more light than the lantern the boy was carrying, but the sun set hours ago and the moon is nowhere to be found. Still, there's only a bit farther to go before they get to the tower. The boy jumps off the horse and runs up to the door. He raises his free hand to knock, but the door swings open of its own accord.
Cautiously, he steps in, his lantern raised. It's still too dark to see well. "Hello? I'm here to see the wizard!" the boy calls into the darkness. His voice echoes off the walls, much to his disconcertment. Another voice calls from the darkness: "Come up!" This voice does not echo; it is the echoes. The boy's eye falls on a stair that ascends into darkness and he takes it.
Around and around the tower the stair goes, but the boy is not tempted to explore the darkness of the the further rooms. He emerges into the topmost room and catches his breath. This room is brightly lit, though you can't see it from the outside. It must be magicked, the boy decides.
The boy bows deeply to the wizard who inhabits the room, and says with regality, "The Council of the Old Ones extends its respect for your endeavors and would like to offer you a position of ---" the boy stops when he realizes that the wizard has stopped listening.
"Ah..." sighs the wizard. "So they have finally made the decision. By majority vote, I trust. You may tell them that I 'appreciate their offer but regrettably will not be able to accept.' I don't want the position, whatever it is."
"May I ask why?"
"Because of Quiio Veranques. The best friend I ever had."
"I've read about him in the histories. But that was over six hundred years ago!"
"Yes, I suppose it was. He was on good terms with the Council, he was. Then there was a rebellion, killing lots of people. Someone had to put it down, they did. All those wizards and warriors and everyone acting like they're such heroes, and then wham! Lives start getting snuffed right and left. The stench of carnage fills the air, and you wonder whether any of these murderers are really heroes at all. But out here, there is no one. No one to rebel against my authority over this tower. No one who might mislay a blow and chop of my head, whether or not they're trying to. Just don't go telling the council that, just tell them... let's see, 'I appreciate their offer but regrettably will not be able to accept. I doubt that I would be of significant help to the Council, until the situation became serious enough that I would not be willing to remain, and I do not wish to act like a coward.' That should do."
He looks at the boy more carefully then, who feels as if the wizard is looking straight through his body to his soul itself. "No, you won't tell them that." He plucks a piece of paper with his statement from thin air. "You'll die first -- freeze to death in the mountain pass. A pity about that, a promising young man you look to be too. But you really shouldn't have ridden so hard. Your horse will be dead before dawn and you'll be stuck with walking, that's probably why. No point trying to avoid fate, though. Just take this --" a firework appears in his hand "-- and set it off when there's no hope, that way they'll find the message. Now go!"
The boy is startled by the wizard's proclamation, but decides that he is probably insane anyway. He hurries down the staircase.
"Poor devil," mutters Lirek. "Maybe Quiio was right. Maybe I am a bit obsessed with death."
