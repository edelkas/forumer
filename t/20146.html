#415536 43 2008-03-10T06:06:05Z Keron Cyst
Okay, my auditions are done. I want to summarize all of my experiences, just for my own notes, and for anyone half-interested in the process of music auditions. I'll detail the cities that the schools were in, because I've been told by several that major factors aside from the quality of the school and the teacher to be taught by are friends, perhaps weather, and city life.



Feb. 16 (15? don't remember):
Boston, MA - The coldest of all the four cities I visited. I walked by the Church of Scientology as I went to dine with a relative of a church friend of my parents'; her perspective was a very, very nice revelation of what the city life is like (a.k.a. excellent!): Because it is so cold (even when I arrived, I saw traces of ice from leftover snow some days before), people flock indoors to spend their free time doing little other than shopping and dining, and hanging out at pubs, like P.F. Chang's Bar & Bistro or The Cheesecake Factory, so a social life is totally robust here. The mall that she took me to was superb (though the Legal Sea Foods restaurant we ate at was an atrocious rip-off of crap "food" pretending to be in a high-class restaurant; I still remember my ~$20 seafood casserole's virtual 'puddle of garlic' with a few random shrimps, scallops, etc. tossed & melted inside Though their starting bread as well as famous New England Clam Chowder were indeed top-notch). Boston had a mind-blowing, sophisticated subway system; all of the cities did (except Philadelphia's, which I didn't experience, and only might shortly after this post, but probably not).

The New England Conservatory - audition was okay. Like Curtis, I messed up in the Liszt; this was the first, so I was still getting used to the audition process, so I didn't anticipate the cut that one of the four judges had me execute, which wrecked my confidence for pretty much the rest of the way, and provoked me to concentrate more on avoiding wrong notes than to focus on musical expression. I think I will get low scholarship... if I get accepted at all. I played (in this order) the Valle d'Obermann (I was cut from the beginning of the Henle-Urtext third page to the tremolo section, and then was stopped at the end of it), Bach's G-Major Prelude & Fugue from Book I (they cut me off before even the first page of the fugue), the exposition of the first movement of Beethoven's Waldstein Sonata (ugh, did horribly there), its the second movement (cut me RIGHT while I was about to enter the main right-hand melody), Debussy's Pour le Piano - III Toccata (that was good; they cut me off right before the development of the development, a bit after the middle. They listened to that and the Liszt the most, which were incidentally my best pieces), and Chopin's tude Op. 10 No. 8 (cut me right as I entered the minor part, after seemingly arguing over each other after the Debussy as to whether they should hear it at all).

The Bach was horrendous. I tell you, one of the most difficult composers, that guy... haha.
