I think our definition of "complexity" differs... what do you mean in referring to that? (Not just Atilla... anyone here) Perhaps I was too insisting on merely multiple overlapping melodies. I would at least like however a grabbing one, not one which keeps repeating every four measures.

Very few songs have a melody which repeats every four bars. The melody in a rock song typically consists of the vocals, which usually repeat a verse several times. Most verses are maybe six to twelve lines of vocals, with perhaps two bars per line. Granted, the lines are sometimes repeated (often in twos) but it's still far from every four bars. The chorus and an instrumental section (including the ever-popular guitar solo) are also commonly used to break things up.

Beside which, classical music often has a single motif which is repeated with variations throughout the piece. A degree of repetition is a natural part of music; it is one of the things which makes a piece a coherent whole rather than simply a sequence of notes.

My interpretation of complexity? I naturally tend to lean towards a somewhat mathematical interpretation. It's the relation of elements of music to one other on various levels, particularly pitch and duration. In general, the more information you need to describe something, the more complex it is. For example, a string of 8 C's is very simple, because, well, it can be described as just 8 C's. ACBDEFGA is also very simple - "Start at A and step up a note at a time for 8 notes". GECFDBflat is more complex - "Start at G, step down two notes, step down another two, then start at F and repeat". Having 100 instruments can allow more complexity, but you can also get instructions which are fairly simple, like "10 of the violinists should play C, 10 should play F, and 10 should play A." Having a bunch of instruments is no guarantee of complexity. It also needs to be understood that complexity is not always desirable. Under my complexity scheme, for example, the most complex sound would be a bunch of random notes, which just sounds discordant and horrible.
