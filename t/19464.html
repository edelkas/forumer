Bilingual Education

Although we recognize English as the prominent language in America it is not, and has not been declared as our national language. It would be surprising to create that kind of unity and nationalism by making English the official language. America is slowly becoming populated by a Hispanic people. It is estimated sometime within the next 50 years that Whites will no longer be the majority of people within the United States. This is a wake-up call that we should all recognize, that we are all part of a global community. If we cannot communicate with others in this community then we will lose in the long run. This is why it is necessary for children to learn a second language in schools starting at the elementary level.
During the 1980s an issue came to the forefront of American minds: Should English be the official language of America? In 1981 the 97th Congress was faced with a proposal to add an amendment to the constitution making English the official language of the United States. The amendment also made it so that federal and state laws and regulations requiring the use of other languages would have been prohibited (Eschholz 380). Although his English Language Amendment was struck down in the senate his cause to make English the official language was not.
U.S. English, an organization tied closely with Hayakawas cause, was founded in 1983 by Hayakawa and a man named John Tanton. Tanton was an ophthalmologist from Michigan who founded the Federation of American Immigration Control (FAIR). He not only favored English as the national language he also called for tighter control of immigration. As the chairman for English Only, Tanton handpicked its employees and set policy directions. In just five years from 1983 to 1988 English-only had 13 states that made English their official language, and all but two of the fifty states had proposed legislation for making English their official language.
Since the birth of English-only many critics said it had very racial undertones, but they were never able to come up with any evidence. In 1988 that changed when a private document written by Tanton claimed that America was going to be dominated by Hispanics through immigration and high birth rate, in one passage asking Will the present majority peaceably hand over its political power to a group that is simply more fertile?
As you can most likely see the racial bigotry of this statement I am not going to present the counter to that, or else my paper would be about racism through language. To counter this argument I present to you the facts of benefits from learning two languages.
One of the most interesting studies on language involves the Sapir-Whorf Hypothesis, formulated by two linguists, Edward Sapir and Benjamin Lee Whorf. Their idea of language as a structure of thought leads into many interesting discussions, one of which says language is a restriction to how people view the world and how they voice their thoughts and opinions. Ellen Bialystok shows that the ideas for spatial concepts among English speaking adults and Korean speaking adults differs in that English uses words for space with things that go together, such as rings and hats are put on, apples in bowls and pieces in puzzles are put in, and put together is what happens to Lego pieces. But for Koreans the language they use for these spatial terms differs among what is tight- and loose-fitting. Therefore putting together Legos, rings and puzzle pieces are all defined by one term, while putting on a hat and putting apples in a bowl are phrased by a different term. So how does this benefit children and bilingualism?
One of the key points of the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis is that people who know more than one language perceive the world in a much different view. They voice their thoughts and opinions differently because they have connected two languages that may have different ideas of space, time, objects, and even movement. Bialystok claims that this idea is correct in principle but wrong in order. She does not deny though that learning a second language is concept learning as well, that learning a new language in this case changes the way concepts are organized in our mind.
In California, especially Los Angeles, the bilingual education there is failing. Only 5 percent of children classified as not knowing English going into school come out of school with a working knowledge of the English language. This number is a dismal rate and lends a hand to the fact that many parents dont want bilingual education because of horrible success statistics. Many of the California schools claim that it takes a 5-year-old child seven years to learn a new language, based off of the results of their bilingual education. Seven years? I can probably correctly say parents dont want to pay extra for seven years so their child will learn English. Speaking of money, did you know that California schools receive extra funding for each child in their school that comes out not knowing English? It is now wonder the schools are failing in their bilingual programs. This is not only limiting to a childs education it is not fair. Why have bilingual programs that dont work? To have a successful bilingual program it would require benefits for students and schools who are proficient in both languages they are learning.
Generally humans fear what they dont understand. I believe the reason there is an English-only movement in America is because those people dont understand the languages brought into America. We are a diverse culture with diverse people. It is important that we become more accepting of others; to accept them we must understand them; to understand them starts with learning their languages. I would urge you to think about how a second language would benefit children: As China becomes a growing economic power that has many times more people than we do, why not have Chinese available in public schools? By providing a second language to students they will be prepared for the rest of the world. College already requires 2 years of second language; nursing programs require you be somewhat proficient in a second language. You can help prepare the next generation for the world ahead of them, and you can start with second language in schools.
