#441385 2 2008-06-02T22:02:41Z Strife
By and large the most useful math skills in programming are discrete things (no pesky decimals or fractions... most of the time). Off the top of my head, I'd recommend Set Theory most highly, as well as Linear Algebra (matrix operations). A smattering of Calculus, particularly multivariate, would help greatly if you plan on doing anything 3D, although a few off-hand topics covered in calculus like series and sequence calculations may end up being useful every now and again.
Other than that, you don't *really* need much complicated unless you're trying to make the game uber-realistic. Super Mario has its own very simplistic laws of physics, whereas Mare and Raigan put in some hardcore mechanical physics expertise to work in making N.
#441431 3 2008-06-03T01:04:03Z scythe33
Understand the algorithm before you write the program; it makes it that much easier. When I was first learning, I once tried to copy a merge sort into my program; I got nowhere. When I learned the algorithm, I came up with an implementation better than the one the instructor recommended. It goes the same way with anything bigger as well.
