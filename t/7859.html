Ingredients:
Cooked, mashed pulp of 1 cantaloupe
1/2 cup sugar
8 tablespoons corn flour
1/4 teaspoon salt
3 egg yolks
2 tablespoons water
1 tablespoon butter
1 baked pie shell


Method:
Combine dry ingredients and add to hot pulp. Stir until thick. Beat yolks and water. Add a cilantro cantaloupe mixture to yolks to heat gradually. Continue cooking, stirring until thick and creamy. Add butter and pour into baked, cooled pie shell. Cover with Meringue.


Mirangue:
3 egg whites
1/4 teaspoon cream of tartar
6 tablespoons granulated sugar

Beat all the ingredients until stiff. Top pie and bake in preheated 400 degrees F oven until delicately brown on top.

Candy cobs: A dessert made of a sugary clump of corns.

Makes 8 cobs

Ingredients:
3 Tablespoons of margarine or butter
4 cups tiny marshmallows
2 cups popped popcorn
2 cups puffed corn cereal
1 cup candy corn
Softened margarine or butter

utensils:
Waxed paper
Baking sheet
Measuring cups
Large saucepan
Wooden spoon
8 wooden sticks

Method:
1. Cover the baking sheet with waxed paper. Save until Step 5.

2. Put the 3 tablespoons margarine or butter in saucepan. Put it on a low heat stove. Melt until melted.

3. Add the marshmallows to the saucepan. Once again, melt them until melted, stirring with the wooden spoon all the while. Then turn it off when done.

4. Add the popcorn and puffed corn cereal to the pan. Quickly stir until the popcorn and cereal are coated with the marshmallow goop. Wait until it is cool enough to touch.

5. Rub the softened margarine or butter into 4-inch logs. Stick the stick into one end and out the other. Put the cobs on the baking sheet, then let them cool completely before ingesting.

Blizzy's chicken salad

1/4 head of lettuce.
1 - 2 Tomatoes.
3 cups chicken, cooked and cubed.
1 1/2 cup celery, sliced.
1/2 teaspoon salt.
1 cup mayonnaise.
2 Tablespoons lemon juice.
1/4 cup onion.
pepper to taste.

Serves 6.

1. Line a medium-sized baking pan with lettuce leaves, and line the edges with half-tomatoes.

2. Mix chicken, celery, salt, mayonnaise, lemon juice, and onion in a medium-sized mixing bowl until well-mixed.

3. Pour into baking pan, cover in tin foil. Chill for 30-45 minutes, serve on buns as sandwiches, or as a side dish.

The most amazingly simple recipe of all times, the thing I've been making after school every couple of days for the past year or so...

Sweet potato Mustard Chips. Llama. (Sorry, no clever Llama themed pun this time, folks!)

Ingrediants:
*Sweet Potatoes
*Olive Oil
*Mustard seeds (About 1.5 teaspoons per serving)
*Mustard powder
*Dried chilly/ perri perri/ cayene pepper
*Dried spices of your choice
*Dried herbs- of your own choice.
*salt + pepper to taste

Method:
*Slice the sweet potato finely.
*Dry- fry the mustard seeds in pan.
*Add in the oil and sweet potato slices
*Fry the sweet potato slices, stirring constantly to mix them around and get them properly cooked on both sides
*Sprinkle on mustard powder + chilly from time to time
*Take out of pan when golden brown
*Sprinkle with salt + pepper
*Can be served with tomato sauce (ketchup), or sweet chilly sauce, or whatever you jolly well feel like serving them with.

Takes me about 10 minutes to slice, but I'm totally paranoid about chopping off my fingers (I cut them quite badly a couple of times as a kid), so it should take you no more than 5 or 6.
cooking is very easy, and can take 5 minutes if you do them all at once and do them right... although, for your first few batches, it can be safer to cook them in batches and take slightly more time.
I strongly suggest you fry them in a pan and avoid the deep fryer- you get more of a sweet potato-y taste that way.
Modify the recipe to your taste- add in whatever dried herbs + spices you want/ have on you.
You don't even need to use the mustard seeds + powder if you don't like it, but I think it goes brilliantly with the sweet potato taste.
I prefer them dry, without sauces, but use them if you like.

It's a lot nicer than it sounds, and probably the easiest- and nicest- way of preparing sweet potato, my favourite vegetable.

BB's Italian soda tips
Yes, yes, these are very simple to make. But these are the tips that play in my head:
Use seltzer water, not tonic water. Tonic water has added sugar. Also, if you find flavored seltzer water, don't use that either. It'll make your soda taste like poopoo.
For the syrup, I suggest a berry flavor. They usually guarantee a good taste. Flavors like caramel and hazelnut are supposed to be used in other recipes, and flavors like mandarin orange are just gross in soda (to my taste, anyways).
Before putting syrup in, taste the water. This way, you have a starting point as to how much sweeter it needs to be.
Add as much syrup as you think you'll need, then add a touch more. If you taste a dab of the stuff, you'll know that the syrups have intense flavor, so don't dump the whole bottle or something weird.
Taste another sip. If it's too sweet, too bad. If it's not sweet enough, add a little more. If it's perfect, then, well, perfect.
Stir. (Duh!) Stir until the syrup is diluted.
Put whipped cream on the top. Add just a second or two's worth, pushing on the tip hard. This way you have a perfect glop of cream at the top.

Ian's Student Snack (1)

Ingredients: Oil, Bread, 2 Eggs, Cheese (Red Leicester for preferance)

Put a small amount of oil in a frying pan and put a slice of bread in on top of it. Pour a bit more oil over the top (the bread shouldn't be dripping, but a decent amount of oil is good) and turn on the heat so the bread is nicely fried (looks like pale toast, and has just aquired the crispy texture on the outside. If the oil is hot it shouldn't take long) Careful when turning the bread - hot oil hurts if it lands on you (voice of experience).
Put the bread on a plate, and using the remaining oil (add a bit more if you need to) fry your two eggs in whatever style you want, 'though personally I like sunny side up. Put the fried eggs on your fried bread.
Finally, grate as much cheese over it as you feel is necessary.

Consume. Feel your arteries clang shut with the pure greasyness of it all. Enjoy.

Atilla's Student Snack 1 ("I-Can't-Believe-It's-Not-Pizza")

Ingredients:
Bread
Cheese (Extremely-tasty-indeed cheddar is Atilla's cheese of choice. But tasty, extra-tasty, or extra-sharp will do.)
Tomato sauce
Ham and any other toppings you feel like.

Method:
1. Toast bread. It must be crispy enough not to go soggy when you put the tomato sauce on it.
2. If you have sliced ham, it should be put on first so the sauce doesn't make it slip off. Alternatively, apply extra cheese in Phase 4 to prevent slippage.
3. Pour tomato sauce on toast. Remember that it will expand when heated, so don't put too much on unless you enjoy removing sauce stains. Covering about two-thirds of the slice should be fine.
4. Put everything else on top of the sauce. The cheese must go on last so that when it melts, it will hold everything else down.
5. Microwave it until the cheese melts (probably around 30 sec + 15 for each extra slice). You can also use a griller if you really have to, but microwaves are so much quicker and easier.
6. Eat. Be careful not to burn tounge on red-hot (ahahaha) tomato sauce.

ANZAC Biscuits (Atilla's Improvised Version)

Ingredients:
Flour
Golden Syrup/Honey/Sugar (whichever happens to be lying around in sufficient quantity).
Butter
Oats
Any decent nuts you can lay hands on (peanuts and walnuts are good)
You can stick corn flakes and suchlike in too, if you want...

Method:
1. Grind the oats and nuts (and any other dry, chunky ingredients) into itty bitty pieces. Putting them in a freezer bag and beating them mercilessly with a rolling pin is generally the easiest way to do this, and is also good for relieving aggresion. DIE, UNRIGHTEOUS OATS! DIE! BWUHAHAHAHAHAHAAAAA!
2. Chuck the flour and the stuff you just beat to a pulp in a big bowl. Don't go too heavy on the flour (somewhere around 50/50 is generally all right, but I like more nuts...)
3. Melt the butter. If you have sugar, melt that too.
4. Pour the butter and syrup/honey/sugar in with the dry ingredients. Mix them into dough.
5. Make the dough into balls, put it on a baking tray, and stick it in the oven 'till they look golden-brown. Recommended temperature is about 200 C.
6. Scarf them all immediately. They're better when they're still warm. Alternatively, put them in an airtight container and they'll last for ages.

But, on the note of garlic bread,

The Daily Llama Bread
Ingrediants:
*French Stick
*Butter/ Margerine
*Garlic
*Mozerella cheese
*Parmensan cheese
*Chilly sauce- I reccommend hot perri perri or a habanero paste, and sweet chilly tends to taste horrid in here.
*Italian herb blend

The method is really, really simple. Cut up the french roll, butter both sides of the bread very well (to stop it from drying out in the oven), stick on many combinations of above ingrediants and lay each piece individually on a tray, which then goes in the oven for about 12- 15 minutes at a reasonably high temperature.

For more traditional garlic bread, just use margerine, garlic, and italian herbs, and reform the portion of the french stick as you make it- layer it back in on itself. Then, simply wrap the whole lot in aluminium foil (bread stick shaped again, but cut in pieces inside) and stick that in the oven for a while.
Tastes way, way, WAY better than anything you can buy at a shop.

Oh- and, a very brief overview of something worth experimenting with-
Pazza
Basically, make like a normal pizza- but use pasta sauce rather than tomato paste on the base, and layer with pasta prepared simultaneously as the rest of the pizza. Other than that, do the same things as normal with the ingrediants that you most like.
(Tastes the best when you make your own pizza base, but most people don't have the time. I've only done it twice (with my own base), and it was brilliant- I'll look up the recipe if anybody is interested.)

ALTERNATIVELY-
Put on a small layer of pasta sauce, some lasasgne sheets, then more pasta sauce of a different nature (A homemade cheese sauce, using butter, flour, milk and chese can work great on the top layer) and simply do normal pizza stuff on top of this.

It's way, way nicer than it sounds.

Beo's Onion Meat-Mush

Ingredients:
500 grams Mincemeat
1 Onion
3 Cloves of Garlic
2-3 tablespoons Olive Oil
(Optional) 1.2 Kiloes Tomatoes
(Optional) Bayleef

Equipment:
Frying Pan

Method:
1. Remove the Onion's brown skin and chop up into tiny pieces (about 5mm by 10mm.)
2. Cut the galric cloves into even tinier pieces, as small as you can make them.
3. Put frying pan on stove and turn on the gas.
4. Add the Olive Oil, and the chopped up garlic and onion.
5. Stir until the onions are lightly golden.
6. While waiting for the onions/garlic to cook, chops up the mincemeat into sizeable chunks.
7. When the onion/garlic mixture is golden, approaching brown, add the mincemeat.
8. Mix it all up, breaking the mincemeat into small chunks. Add salt, and a bayleef if you have one.

9. (Optional) If you want to end up with a Bolognese-type sauce, then slice up the Tomatoes and add them. Mix until the whole mixture is reddish.

Suggestions:

Perfect with pasta and parmesan, or as a complement to salad.

Cheese Pillows

Two slices of Bread
Two slices of American Cheese
Toaster that only toasts on one side at a time
Microwave
Knife
