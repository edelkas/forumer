It is quite interesting to read a poem on a topic from multiple viewpointsIt sparks ones imagination. By analyzing the similarities and differences of the two poems, one can gain an understanding of different viewpoints in poetry.
Firstly, The poems Chocolate by Catilina Rose and Chocolate by Myangel Twao are similar in many ways. The obvious similarity is the titleIt is the same for both poems. Both speakers love chocolate, they both like the sweet, sugary taste. Both speakers are talking to an indefinite audience, possibly to themselves. At the end of the first poem, the speaker would like to have another piece, similar to the beginning of the second poem. The limited amount of similarities in this poem shows how different 2 poems on the same general topic can be.
Next, poems differences are numerous. One speaker is actually eating the chocolate while the other is only looking at the chocolate. One speaker is experiencing the delight of the chocolate while the other is only imagining it. The speaker from the first poem actually knows what the chocolate tastes like while the second poems speaker is wondering about the taste of the chocolate. The first speaker concentrates on the actual taste of the chocolate while the second includes auditory and visual aspects of the experience of eating the chocolate.
In conclusion, The poems Chocolate by Catilina Rose and Chocolate by Myangel Twao may have the same title, but have many similarities and dissimilarities as well. This is a good example of viewpoints in poetry and other forms of literature.

Next are my 3 favorite poems and my ideas about them.

Poem #1
Broken Glass By Joshua Bonds

mad like a vulture
no prey

i am a soldier
wounded in duty

im angry, man
you hear me?
angry
like a second place loser

loser, man.

can you dig it?
can you feel me, winner?

i am not weird
i dont hold grudges
or sing in the shower
or chew rusty razors

do you?

i break glass
i cut it, man
i am able to cut glassy stares with sharper ones

believe me Ill do it
ill handle it

im crazy mad

you cant stop the rain
or the reign

[Grammatical mistakes as in poem]

Response:

This poem is very interesting. I read the poem 3 times in quick succession and interpreted it in 3 different ways. It can be understood in many different waysI think it is about a boy that is missing something from his life and is regretting it. He may be missing a person, a purpose, or maybe a resource. He talks to a winnerpossibly someone who has that something that he is missing, and tells him how he feels. The tone changes near the end (at believe me Ill handle it) from grievance to determination. He decides to move on without that something that he lacks. I like the specific metaphor I am a soldier wounded in duty because it tells us a lot about how he feels. He feels helpless, unable to do his job, achieve his goal, or feels restrictedjust as a wounded soldier would in battle. I am confused at you cant stop the rain or the reign. It seems to be telling us that there is nothing anyone/anything can do to stop him from achieving his goal, but I really cannot be sure.

Poem #2
All watched over by Machines of Loving grace, by Richard Brautigan

I like to think (and
The sooner the better)
Of a cybernetic meadow
Where mammals and computers
Live together in mutually
Programming harmony
Like pure water
Touching clear sky.

I like to think
(Right now, please!)
Of a cybernetic forest
Filled with pines and electronics
Where deer stroll peacefully
Past computers
As if they were flowers
With spinning blossoms.

I like to think
(It has to be!)
Of a cybernetic ecology
Where we are free of our labors
And joined back to nature,
Returned to our mammal
Brothers and sisters,
And all watched over
by machines of loving grace.


Response:

The poem creates an image of someone sitting down and thinking of a far away place where computers and nature can live together in harmony. I like this poem because poems on such topics are very rare and the idea portrayed by this poem is so imaginative. The speaker seems to be a young person, going the comments in parentheses. He/she is excited about her dream. The speaker goes on about describing the place and showing us around. I have lots of questions when I read this poem. What year is it in? Is it even slightly realistic? Can humans ever forget their labors and return to our mammal brothers and sisters? I really like the authors writing style. The words used go together like the pieces of a puzzlethe poem flows very well. They create a very detailed image, with each line adding on to the last.

Poem #3
What I am (In the Eyes of my Father) by Dwight Beavers.

I am nothing
In the eyes
Of my father.

When I get
Good grades
He doesnt say

Anything, not
One good word.
When I didnt

Get into a top
High school
He said I was

Nothing, never
Going to be
Nothing. I am

Beaten down
From heaven
By the shaft

Of my father.
It feels like
A bullwhip

Going across
My back
Every time

He puts me,
Beats me,
Down, down.

Response:

The speaker is obviously neglected at home by his father. He has no self-esteem because of his father. He tells us that his father doesnt care about him and provides examples in the following lines. I like the simile where the speaker compares how he is beaten down to a bullwhip going across his back. At the very end, the speaker suggests that he is beaten (he puts me, beats me, down, down.), but it may also be a figure of speech. That is one of the reasons I really like this poem. The poem is very similar to other poems I have read but it is one of the best-composed ones. The words bind together to make you feel the poem. They flow together very well. I do not understand how he could have gotten good grades and still not be admitted into a good high school. Of course, his grades might not have been good enough or he may have been talking about a different time altogether, but If his aim was to get into a good high school then his perception of good grades would have been the same as the good high schools.
