I'm posting this to help spread the word about the Steal This Film series and the movement it documents. In brief, there are two documentary films in the series, both are a bit more than half an hour in length. They're about BitTorrent, P2P file sharing, and the free culture movement in general.

If you've ever downloaded a copyrighted song or movie from the internet, you have an obligation to watch these. If you have never done this, you also have an obligation. Because I say so. You can download them (for free, of course) through BitTorrent from stealthisfilm.com, or you can watch them streaming on Google Video (#1 and #2).

You will hopefully find these films to be thought-provokingparticularly the second one (so, if you're only going to watch one, let it be that one). If you do watch these films and find yourself seeking more information, you're clearly an intelligent person, and so you can do research on your own. I'd suggest you start by reading Free Culture, a book that's freely available to read online (how fantastic is that!?).

#401153 6 2008-01-16T01:37:33Z wedgie
Very interesting videos.

One thing that gets me is the whole 'people are losing out from piracy' idea, be it the movie makers or the record labels. The way I see it is, if these people are really losing out from piracy, then why are they still producing?
Lets say a record label is losing out because potential sales of their music are being lost to people that are downloading this music instead. If these people were really losing out on so much of their income due to piracy, then surely these people would not be able to continue producing. But alas, they still do, which tells you that the problem doesn't lie with money. The artists would stop making music if they weren't getting any profit from it, unless they were a non-profit making group.

Quote: Second video
Making money is not the point with culture or media. Making something is the point with media.
So I totally agree with this. The majority of 'pirates' don't want to rip off the people that make this stuff by profiting from it themselves; they just want to share it.
