The car is doing nothing with its velocity. The velocity is indirectly causing the deer to disintegrate.

You can look at it two ways, they both work:
The convoluted way:
The velocity means that the car has to have a massive decelleration because it needs to come to a complete halt from a high speed in a very small timeframe. The decelleration, when factored in with the car's mass, gives you a force (F=ma). So basically, when the deer and the car hit, the car is slowed down extremely quickly, and that resulting slowing force is reflected onto the deer. For every force, there's an equal and opposite force, right? That's not always a push. The car's frame is pulled in towards itself, and the deer's carcass does the same thing, as an opposing force to the car's frame's deformative force.
The easy way:
Having a velocity means that the car has Kinetic energy. Energy is always conserved (unless there are some funky nuclear reactions going on, which there aren't in this incident). Let's take the car-road-deer system. However much energy those three have stored in them, that number stays constant (ignoring friction with the road and air). That means that when the car stops, it loses its kinetic energy... but the total energy of the system stays the same, so that energy has to go somewhere. In this case, it goes into the deformation of the car and the deer.

Velocity does not directly do anything besides say that the car is, in fact, moving. To change velocities, whether it be coming to a stop or speeding up, in any amount of time, you need an acceleration. And in this case, it's the acceleration (decelleration, to be exact) that's directly causing all of the damage.
