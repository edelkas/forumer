But there a few half-decent answers from the 'free will' point of view of things.

1. There is no absolute equation to predict the way things (on Heisenburg's small scale) will happen - you can only predict the chance that things will happen. Schrodingers Cat is a good example of this - you cannot tell whether the cat is alive or dead, only the probability that the cat is either alive or dead. At the quantum scale, matter is not predictable.

2. It is (as the Heisenburg Uncertainty Principle states) impossible to know the exact position and velocity of any subatomic particle. And this is not just because the photons 'bounce off' the particles and affect it - if you could (somehow) put an electron in a box, and then slowly make the box shrink (therefore narrowing the places where the electron could be - and therefore making you know where it is more accurately), the electron will move faster and change direction at random - unpredictably.

#108913 13 2005-10-17T12:11:47Z Atilla
I've heard this sort of thing before. However, I don't believe it covers all points. There appears to be an element of randomness in the universe, as iangb said; it is entirely possible that the fate of the universe is not predetermined by cause and effect. This doesn't negate the fact that nothing has any meaning whatsover, of course... in fact, it is worse - in means that nothing exists, only the probablity of something.

Another thing to think about is whether there is truly a "reality", or if the universe is only our perceptions. According to the Theory of Relativity, for example, an object becomes shorter in the direction of motion when it moves relative to your reference frame. Consider then a very fast car and a tunnel. This tunnel has a door over one end which opens instantly when the rear of the car passes through the start of the tunnel. When they are at rest relative to each other, it is just longer than the car, so you can drive through it at low speed safely. However, imagine now the car is travelling at nearly the speed of light, relative to the tunnel. From the tunnel's point of view, the car is rushing towards it, so the car becomes shorter and fits through easily. From the car's reference frame, the tunnel is moving near the speed of light, and gets shortened dramatically. Which means the whole car will no longer fit in the tunnel, so the door won't open and they car will crash... What happens? Does the universe split in two, depending on which reference frame you inhabit? Perhaps everything is created by the observer; there is no time or motion, it is merely our perception flicking through nearly-identical universes and stringing the sensations together, like the eye does with disparate frames of a movie. Whatever happens, we will never know.

This is, of course, why I am agnostic. We can never say with absolute certainty that what we percieve is real or true, or even that there can be the concept of reality. Our limited senses can always be deceived. We could be just brains in a tank somewhere, tricked into believing in the world. Our universe could merely be the latest virtual reality sensation, and we are all enjoying a bit of role-play after work. We may be having a long and complicated dream. We may, in fact (and against all the odds), be right about Life, the Universe, and Everything. But we cannot tell, nor will we ever be able to. It is our fate to be fascinated by the workings of the universe and its origins, if indeed it has any, because we are humans and that is a part of our humanity. We convince ourselves that things like truth, justice and mercy are real, because our genes dictate it, or god dictates it, or because it is something we have decided for ourselves. And who can say they are not? Reality may not be defined merely as molecules and atoms; things do not necessarily require physical form to exist. It matters not, because people will act as people regardless. All we can do is get on with our lives as best we can, whether they are dictated by chemical reactions, by the gods, the stars, or our own free will.
