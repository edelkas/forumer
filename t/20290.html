...whee. Emotions can be a funny thing. If a heroin addict is never deprived of heroin, he or she will never experience sadness. That's a weird idea, isn't it? Addicts are some of the most victimized people in soceity; we look at them unfailingly as people who have lost something. And without drifting too far into Brave New World, it's ironic that most people would be unsatisfied with eternal happiness. There is no key to happiness that can be given without changing something about yourself-often, giving up a part of who you are. I might well just be blathering here. There are two commonly portrayed types of happy people-the idea of "life in the fast lane", where life has so many changes you can't help but forget about things that are behind you, and the idea emphasized by such groups as patagonia, which is just the opposite of that-yet, many outside observers looks on either version as if that type of person is missing out on something essential to life. We drive ourselves away from these lifestyles, not towards them, and that's not easy to reconcile. Yet we do tend more towards the former near college years, and the latter as we age-and a common symptom of aging is wishing to recover that which we left behind, the midlife crisis. If I were Plato, I'd have to give up here. But I'm an idiot, so I can keep typing and hope I get somewhere even though I won't. And damned if I'm the first to say it, but we have at least in the past admired tragedy. Learn to look at the pitfalls in your life and take them as a reminder that you're still alive. Because you are. Eternal happiness is death.
