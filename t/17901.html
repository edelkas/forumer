#332602 2 2007-06-18T15:59:40Z Raigan
hey,
It's funny, everyone seems to act like good collision detection is simple, but in our experience it's much harder to do than physics!

For your last problem: I suggest moving away from tile-based collision and using geometry (lists of edges). You can still use tiles in the editor, but when you load the level you convert them into the corresponding surface edges -- so that in this case the dark grey tile wouldn't contribute any edges at all. Correctly generating the edges can be a bit tricky, but in the long run it pays off.

For your first problem: we also came across this. The basic problem is that (as usual) your code isn't looking at the "big picture", it's finding a local solution which is no good. You can actually detect when this is happening, see the attached picture -- in the lower case, the intersection point lies _outside_ of the edges that generated it. However, once you've detected this it's hard to say how to fix it -- you could use this as a hint to look at vertex-edge intersection instead of edge-edge perhaps.


Finally, our experience is that you're never going to get a system that is perfect -- you'll always be able to dream up a case which breaks it, and that can be frustrating. But, a better solution is to realize when you're doing this, and attack the problem from a different direction: try to figure out a way to prevent that case from ever happening.. such as clamping the max speed of objects, running collision detection several times per frame (so that things don't move very far in a single step), etc. For instance, in Robotology we were having problems getting very thin/acute corners to behave well; after about a week, we simply said: screw it, we just won't allow very thin/acute corners. Problem solved

We actually gave up on these analytical methods and used a different approach: we spawn a "proxy" circle at the circle's position, then iteratively push the proxy out of collision with all nearby edges, one-by-one. Then, the penetration vector is the vector from the circle's position to the proxy's position. This isn't an exact solution, and can fail, but it's simple.

Good luck!

raigan

Quote: mattk210, June 19, 2007 07:29 am
EDIT: what do you mean by that proxy circle thing? do you mean keep it the same (as the tutorials) but add up all the individual projection vectors? because I'm not adding bounce/friction impulses, i'm not interested in that.

The proxy collision is basically the same as the "iterate multiple times" collision, except that instead of moving your actual object with each collision, you move the ghost/proxy object. then, when the proxy is outside of the world/isn't colliding, you can use the object->proxy vector as a single penetration vector.

it's not any better than moving the object several times, it's just that sometimes you only want a single penetration vector, and this is a way of "combining" several penetration vectors together.
