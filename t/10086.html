#181654 5 2006-05-29T00:46:26Z iangb
Evolution, in a strictly biological sense, is when beneficial random mutations occur in an individual of a species, causing that individual to be better suited for survival and procreation in it's particular environment. Because that individual is 'better' at surviving, it is more likely that the next generation of the species will also contain the mutation.
Conversely, if the mutation is not beneficial, then the individual will not survive to pass on the mutation.

#181665 7 2006-05-29T01:13:52Z Atilla
"Evolution" in the general sense means that something changes gradually, usually to a superior state (otherwise a word such as "degeneration" or "decline" would be used).

In biology, the Theory of Evolution postulates that organisms may change from generation to generation due to pressures on reproduction. This is in turn based on several observations:

1. There is variation in organisms. Even within a species, not all members are identical.

2. Some of these variants are traits which may be beneficial/harmful to an organism's chance/rate of reproduction and/or survival.

3. These traits may be inherited by the offspring of an organism.

4. Current organisms are the result of this process.

The Theory of Evolution doesn't really include the spontaneous generation of life, since it deals with what happens when you already have organisms. However, the process of evolution may be used to describe the developement of self-replicating particles which could not yet be considered life. Note also that it is important to distinguish the general theory from other, more specific theories - for example, punctuated equilibrium.

The process of evolution is a fact (that is, it can be observed - as in the breeding of dogs, the appearance of new bacteria which are resistant to old antibiotics, etc.) and may also be used to describe more general cases where selective pressures effect the propagation of a design, idea or variant. For example, computer chips and artificial intelligence have been developed by producing random variations, selecting the ones which most resemble the behaviour you want, then generating another batch with minor variations and mixtures of the selected specimens and repeating the process until you get something that works well. You could also speak of cultural evolution and how ideas that have traits which will result in their being propagated are more likely to be enduring and widely known. From a purely sociological viewpoint, religions are a fine example.

And please, please remember that the words "fact" and "theory" have different meanings in science to everyday use.

In biology, evolution is a process by which divergent heritable traits arise in populations. these traits are passed on from generation to generation. Over time, the traits that help the organism reproduce in greater numbers than his peers gradually gain dominance in the genetic pool of that population.

the contexts in which evolution may happen are when one or more of these hereinafter conditions are met. the first condition is that thier must be genitic mutation in a population. The secound condition is that there must be no genetic flow, because with gentic flow the population's gene pool become more similar to the gene pool of the larger interbreding comunity. There must be non-random mating. individual in the population must selectively breed, ether by assortative mating or by sexual selection. And finaly thier must be gentic drift. gentic drift is when by chance a genotype is removed from the population.

natural selection is slightly diffferent. for natural selection to happen, all four factors must be met within a population. the population must have variation. In other words, individuals in the population must differ from one another. these variations, or traits must be heritable. of the heritable traits, the trait must affect how the individual with the trait is adapted to the environment. the adaptation to the environment must affect how the individual that poseses the trait reproduces.
