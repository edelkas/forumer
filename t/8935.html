I'm going to post various music-based top tens. One at a time. Whenever I feel like it. Please agree/disagree, contribute, do whatever you want. I want to hear what you think, and most of all, if you're inspired by anything I write about a piece of music, go out and listen to it. I have an impeccable taste, honest.


Top Ten Songs With "Time" In The Title

10) Ben Scriven - Quality Time
This song is a bit silly. But it's number ten because it's silly.
/hindsight edit: And it has a kazoo solo. 'nuff said.

9) Hot Hot Heat - Running Out of Time
Vintage Hot Hot Heat; full of hooks, and over in the blink of an eye.

8) The Delays - Long Time Coming
What a wonderful little slice of music this is. It makes me all warm and fuzzy inside - it makes me think of family; I'm not sure why, but I like it!

7) U2 - Sometimes You Can't Make It On Your Own
It's remarkable that U2 are still a quality band after all these years. Sure, they haven't really done anything different, but their consistency is outstanding. This song is proof that they can still write brilliant songs.

6) Muse - Time Is Running Out
This is the best single Muse have ever written. It feels so electric and alive, and every time I hear it I am surprised about how I am yet to become bored of it.

5) Ben Scriven - Turn Back Time
Oh my. I just love the oriental instrumentation in this song coupled with the shamelessly unsubtle lyrics - "I can't comprehend how much I wish you were dead" is hardly poetry, but it works dammit. This song has groove, this song has feeling, and it has a wonderfully shameless instrumental climax.

4) Radiohead - We Suck Young Blood (Your Time Is Up)
This song is dark, dank and achingly gorgeous. The production is wonderful.

3) Semisonic - Closing Time
This song makes me happy. Very happy. I don't know whether it's because it evokes good memories, or whether it's darn good, but this song just makes me really happy.

2) Pink Floyd - Time
Unfortunately I'm a little too young to really be completely engrossed by the sound and feel of Pink Floyd, but this track is genius. Pure genius.

1) Green Day - Good Riddance (Time of Your Life)
This song is truly beautiful & honest; really pure songwriting.

That's the first top 10. As I said, comment as you wish. I want to hear what you think.

As promised, here is my Franz Ferdinand Top 10. It was far easier for me to write comments for this one than on my Muse Top 10.

1. Darts of Pleasure - This is the song that got me hooked on Franz Ferdinand. I can get caught up in the beat so easily, and the whole song just bounces on it. The lyrics are also quite distinctive: you've got "you can feel my lips undress your eyes", and the song ends with three repetitions of a verse in German.
2. You Could Have It So Much Better - A song that is both bright and cynical. I love the lyrics, an inspecific rejection of the accepted order and an equally inspecific call to arms .
3. The Fallen - Lots of biblical allusions in the lyrics that I don't quite get, especially in the breathtaking final chorus.
4. Your Diary - This song doesn't deserve to be a a B-side. The chorus is great (musically, not so much lyrically), and the guitar riffage throughout this song is also excellent.
5. This Fire - Can't really say much about this one. It's... good?
6. Auf Achse - This song is a combination of many different musical stylessort-of-romantic background notes, a disco-y beat, a dance-y beat, and the great choruswhich just manage to form a cohesive song.
This starts off with a romantic sounding intro, and then an almost disco-y beat comes in, giving it a strange vibe. Combined with the chorus, this makes a great song.
7. Take Me Out - A tragically and ridiculously overplayed song, but a fairly good one nevertheless.
8. 40' - It would appear that Franz Ferdinand got writer's block on this song, explaining the two dozen repetitions of "la la la la". A good song nevertheless.
9. Outsiders - Twangy guitars, something that sounds a lot like those weird plastic toy tubes that make a whiny, warbling sound when you turn them upside down, and a drum beat that doesn't change for the entire song.
10. Jacqueline / Eleanor Put Your Boots On - I can't decide. Jacequeline is a more typical song, while Eleanor Put Your Boots On is a much softer song. Call this a tie.

iangb's top 16 songs over 7 minutes long

I limited myself to one song per band, or there would be many more Yes/Propellerheads songs in here. The only band I have missed out on is Pink Ffloyd, merely because I don't appear to have any of their longer songs on my laptop. My loss.

Not quite make-its (in no particular order): Jesus of Suburbia (Greenday), Calenders and Clocks (The Coral), Silverf*ck (The Smashing Pumpkins), Citizen Erased (Muse), Sway (lostprophets), First Circle (Pat Metheny), Smash (The Offspring) and quite a few others by Coldplay, Jimmy Eat World, Pendulum, Dragonforce and Idlewild, to name a few.



16.) The Stars Are Projecters, by Modest Mouse (8:46)
A trippy, lilting example of Modest Mouse's particular brand of genius. Dreamy, but sharp.

15.) Catherine Parr, by Rick Wakeman (7:55)
Flowing keyboard melodies from a member of Yes, this song sounds good and is aproximately impossible to play.

14.) Insomnia, by Faithless (8:41)
One of the ultimate dance tunes, I felt obliged to my student nature to include this one. Move to the beat.

13.) Freebird, by Lynard Skynard (9:07)
Better than 'Sweet Home Alabama', and just as styled. Takes some getting used to, but then you won't want to stop.

12.) Frozen, by Celldweller (7:00)
If you saw a trailer for the film Constantine a while ago, you will have heard a slight remix of this song. Rubbish lyrics, but a tune that you can't ignore.

11.) 21st Centuary Schitziod Man, by King Crimson (7:24)
Any song that includes the line "innocents raped with napalm fire" has to be good. Maybe. This is old, experimental, angry prog rock, and I love it.

10.) Crusader, by Chris De Burgh (8:47)
Old but not forgotten, this sounds fantasitic in spite of the slightly pretentious lyrics - and the fact that I recorded it off vinyl.

9.) Suppers Ready, by Genesis (24:33)
The cleverest lyrics in the list. And a fine, fine tune. A flower?

8.) Come Back, by the Foo Fighters (7:51)
This song varies between the melodic and the rocking, with a classic Foo guitar anthem supporting Dave Grohl's gravely voice. A song worthy of the Foo Fighters

7.) California One Youth and Beauty Brigade, by The Decemberists (9:50)
Inspiring a forum-wide conspiracy, this song sounds... unique. It champions the weak, and wins. Listen to the lyrics. Nothing will stand in our way.

6.) The Revealing Science of God, by Yes (20:26)
It's a Yes song. Need I say more? It has clever, flowing lyrics and creates, well, a topographic ocean of soundscape in your head. There were many many other potential Yes songs (Starship Trooper, The Gates Of Delerium, etc), but this was my favourite. It was a darn hard call, though.

5.) Art of Life, by X Japan (28:56)
There was no way I could miss this out. The longest song I know, it includes about 5 minutes of high-pitched fast-paced 'air guitar', followed by an equally long piano solo. The only reason it isn't higher is that the piano gets a little too experimental for my tastes.

4.) On Her Majesty's Secret Service, by The Propellerheads (9:20)
The classic James Bond theme, reworked with a beat. Like Clubbed to Death, you can just lean back, close your eyes, and listen.

3.) American Pie, by Don McLain (8:32)
Definitely Don's greatest piece (allthough others come close), this song is triumphant in its tragedy. Plus it has some of the most metaphorical lyrics there are.

2.) Clubbed To Death (Kurayamino Variation), by Rob Dougan (7:29)
The best chilled-out pure instrumental from Rob D, this song also includes the piano solo that is inspiring, soaring, and simple. The video for it ain't bad, either.

1.) Tubular Bells (Part 1) - Mike Oldfield (25:29)
This is probably one of the best know 'long songs' that there are. From the haunting initial melody that was used in The Excorcist to the triumphant finish ("Tubular Bells!"), this is a masterpiece.
