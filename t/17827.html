#332418 7 2007-06-18T02:25:45Z Daddaluma
I can agree that there's a subset of American/Western culture that suffers from the same "cool pose culture" as described in the article. However, that's simply a tiny minority, and I'd argue that it's been with us forever and isn't exactly reaching epidemic proportions today.

Anyhow, that still misses the point of the article. In terms of the overall American culture, that group that feels the need to devolve into a badass, or however you want to describe it, doesn't set the tone for all Americans. You and I don't feel pressure to "be true to our identity" by conforming to that group.

Whereas there's a great deal of pressure for an American black person to resist things that are considered "white". It takes tremendous strength of character for someone to rise above that pressure and actually go on to achieve.

Here's another great relevant article: http://www.eastbayexpress.com/2003-05-21/n...black-flunking/

You might want to read it before continuing.

This trend doesn't simply affect the impoverished, either. Black children from relatively wealthy families feel the same pressures to not act white. It also brings up some interesting ideas about voluntary immigrants versus involuntary immigrants. Native Americans and Black people born in this country still retain this idea that they're their own culture, and that anything they do that resembles conforming to society at large (such as speaking standard English and doing well in school) is a self betrayal.

Voluntary immigrants don't share these attitudes. They come here fully recognizing that they will have to learn the language to succeed, and that they'll have to work harder than the average person to overcome the resulting challenges. They also recognize that learning to speak proper english, and learning to conform to cultural norms in the new society does not in any way betray their self identity.

So what's the main point of all this?

It seems quite clear to me that "racist" institutions are rapidly becoming a thing of the past (in most areas at least), and the places you can still reasonably place the blame for the economic and academic disparities that exist between white America and black America are similarly rapidly dwindling.

Even when you remove poverty from the equation, the fact that black people are still doing worse in school speaks volumes about where the real contributing factors lie. The attitudes that are pervasive throughout American blacks are the real culprit, and until more people are willing to publicly stand up and admit it, there will be no significant progress toward righting the very real wealth imbalance.
