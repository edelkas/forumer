#426807 11 2008-04-10T14:23:51Z iangb
Unfortunately though, scythe, it does come back to nuclear. 'Cos although hydrogen burns cleanly, getting the hydrogen is a whole 'nother matter.

Of the three main methods of hydrogen production, two (hydrocarbons and algae) both produce CO2, so in order to make the production method greenhouse-friendly, you need to use carbon capture - something which
a) Could also be used with the burning of fossil fuels (though more specifically at power stations that at-the-car, currently)
b) I'm highly dubious about. Carbon capture is essentially taking CO2 that is released and storing it underground... for ever. It's the greenhouse gas equivalent of a landfill site, and look how successful those have been.

The third method of production is electrolysis of water: something which doesn't cause any pollutants in itself (much like the combustion of hydrogen), but which requires electricity to do so - essentially the energy from the electricity is being used to power the car, just via the medium of hydrogen. In order for that electricity to be 'clean', we need to address the problem of power stations. Enter conventional nuclear (a short-term stopgap), solar/wave/tidal/geothermal power (our best current option, IMO), and fast breeder reactors (th3 f00tur3, as Keron would put it)
