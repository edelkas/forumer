I thought Starfox Adventures turned out pretty well actually. = \

The graphics and music were fine, and the gameplay was... slightly poor, but not terrible. The horrendous thing was the plot, which made no sense whatsoever. The actions of the characters were bizarre and contrived*, and the final boss was just completely absurd and horribly disappointing. Andross again? He hadn't been mentioned all game, and he was killed exactly the same way as the last two games. Not to mention that Arwing-flying wasn't even a major component of SFA, and invoking it for the final battle is particularly silly given Fox's previous insistence on walking and using the staff instead of actually using his normal equipment. Argh.

The whole thing reeks of a promising game which had the Star Fox paint slapped on at the last minute, when there should have been final testing instead. Particularly since it was announced that Rare were being bought out by Microsoft shortly before the game was released. If Rare had had time to fine-tune the gameplay, rather than being forced to waste their time shoving Slippy's ugly face where it doesn't belong, it could have been a good game. It may even have started a successful franchise of its own - and perhaps Nintendo felt they couldn't let Microsoft acquire an asset like that. By insisting on the Star Fox licence, they harmed the consumer, but coincidentally deprived their competitor of a potentially valuable IP. It's certainly possible to make the case that Nintendo shafted consumers just to spite Microsoft. The alternative is that Nintendo callously slapped an irrelevant licence on because it would increase sales, even though they knew it would decrease the game's quality. I find both distasteful.

And, of course, SFA was sexist. Krystal was originally going to be a strong, competent character (and fully playable, might I add). But no, the female must be relegated to the "damsel in distress" role. And have that cretin Fox ogle her, too. Ridiculous.

And now I'm going to fight this huge dinosaur on foot, despite the fact that it's standing the the middle of a largely empty plain and could easily be taken down with an Arwing."
