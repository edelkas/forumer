#195650 28 2006-06-28T04:27:09Z Teh_Skyzor
A few things:

1. STDs as we know them definitely did not exist in the early days of humanity. There were no doubt viruses and bacteria, but they were radically different from the ones we know today. Keep in mind that evolution is an ongoing process, and viruses aren't exempt from it (though they are non-living).

2. Don't discuss personal views in here. Saying "sex is the meaning of life" offends me in ways you cannot possibly fathom from your shallow viewpoint.

3. Diseases in general do not mutate as a result of an immunity. This is a common misconception. Bacteria and virus genetic material mutates randomly, and then the non-beneficial (to the infectious agent) mutations are "weeded out" through natural selection (e.g. if there's a mutation that kills the bacteria, it won't reproduce, so thus there will be no future bacteria carrying that mutation). These mutations usually happen through errors in the cell, where DNA is mutated by chance, due to errors in copying or other mechanisms (such as uptake of DNA from the environment, or the "wrong" genetic material being encoded into a virus when it is being manufactured in the cells it is infecting). Forgive me if you were oversimplifying, obby.


There's a lesson in biology (and philosophy) for you.
