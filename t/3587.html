#73676 12 2005-06-10T06:16:31Z Daddaluma
A lot of you seem to be misunderstanding the idea. This phrase is only used when you do some bad stuff to achieve a greater good.

So Brickman's example "If you have to kill somebody to get home in time for your aniversary" is ridiculous. This is not what the phrase applies to.

LV's example is a good one (albeit quite an extreme one). Would you personally push the button that would instantly slaughter 2 billion people if you knew it would save the rest of the people and the earth?

More examples. The police strongly suspect that they have captured a terrorist involved in plot to launch a nuclear missile at the US. THey torture their captive for hours until he gives up and reveals what he knows and it leads to the successful thwarting of the attack. Do the ends justify the means? (thank you 24)

Another one: research suggests that violent tendencies may be genetically linked. The government institutes a policy of sterilizing all humans who are convicted of violent crimes. In a few generations we see a large decrease in the number of such violent crimes. Do the ends justify the means? What if we changed "sterilized" to "executing"? (thank you eugenics)


These are the kinds of questions you should be thinking about. Any idiot can come up with a scenario where obviously the ends don't justify the means, that's not what you should be thinking about.

How about one last one. The human race is seperated into alphas and betas. Alphas live their lives however they want. They can choose their jobs and pursue whatever liveliehood they'd like. Betas are like slaves. They do all the dirty work. Manual labor, janitorial all the tough crappy jobs that have to be done but most people don't really desire to do.

The catch is that the betas are fed a drug called "soma" which makes them extremely happy and satisfied with their lives.

So everyone is happy with the arrangement. Normal people (alphas) live in a much easier and cleaner environment and the betas live long and fulfilled lives due to the soma.

Do the ends justify the means? (thank you Brave New World)

Edit: I just reread Keron's post, and it seems that he specifically said that he wasn't talking about the kind of huge issues I just brought up. But the general point still applies. The ends can only justify the means if the ends do a greater good than the means do harm, but even then you'll run into a bunch of ethical questions which are fun and important to think about.

Edit 2: added credit to where it's due in my examples
