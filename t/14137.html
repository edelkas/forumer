#262606 18 2006-12-01T15:35:01Z Tsukatu
I take Boston's famous T-line to some of my classes. I could really just take a 20-30 minute walk instead of taking a 15-20 minute T-ride, but I ride the T anyway. And the only reason I do this is because the one part of the T's map that goes above ground is right where my university is, and outbound rides on the T from the very first stop that's above the ground is completely free. Going back inbound, though, costs money, so I usually walk back.

Problems with it:
-There are no consistent patterns to any given train's arrival. Sometimes a train comes four minutes after the one before it, and sometimes it takes twenty minutes. And best of all, there's no sign telling you when the next one is coming.
-There are very frequent delays because the above-ground track crosses a whole slew of intersections, and it is obligated to wait for every last car and pedestrian before it starts going again. Each one of these delays is a few minutes long, and it happens a couple of times in each one of my trips.
-The people they hire to drive the cars are a bunch of shit-eating, monkey-fucking motherfuckers. Every single one of 'em. I have not to this day met a polite or patient T-driver. If you're hurrying to get into the car and end up making it so that you even have to wait for the person in front of you to get in, they'll see that you came later than everyone else and close the doors right in front of your face. Then they'll sit there for a minute or two with the doors closed, train motionless, as they wait for traffic to finish flowing (when they could just open the doors for two seconds to let you in). If you come to the front of the car and knock politely, the driver just looks over, smirks, and ignores you. And this happens all the goddamned time. I see it happen every couple of stops to every age group, race, and gender under the sun. The driver usually ignores me when I tell them there's someone outside, too. Fuckin' douchebags, the whole lot of 'em.

Other than that, the T is excellent. For the most part, it's dependable, fast, and inexpensive.
