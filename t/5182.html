I was assigned to write an evaluation essay recently in English class, and I decided to do it about N. I just got the paper back today, and it got a 100% grade so I decided it was worth posting here.

Quote
The computer video game, N, is the first release from Metanet software. Available for free download at the developer's website (www.harveycartel.org/metanet), the game is easily accessible to anyone interested. The style of N is minimalistic, using light gray against dark gray to fill most of the screen. The game only uses three controls: left, right, and jump. Levels in the game are meant to be beaten quickly too. Each set of five levels has a 90 second timer, and that's usually more than enough time to finish them. Yet despite its simple appearance, N can be much more enjoyable and offer more hours of play than any of today's best-selling titles.
In N, you control a small ninja jumping and running around each level. You can't attack your enemies here though. You're only allowed to avoid them, but this part of the gameplay doesn't take away from the fun. It actually adds to it very much. A total of 500 levels make up the game, which easily provides over 100 hours of play. Every five levels should take a total of under 90 seconds, but you're expected to die and repeat each level many times before completing it. Inspired by games like Lode Runner, you are presented with a whole game board at once and can try to plan how to solve it without getting killed. In this way, it has some elements of a puzzle-type game. N always keeps players thinking, planning ahead, and learning from their mistakes.
The part of N that makes it stand out on top of all other 2D platformer games is the perfection of movement. Perfectly fluid player control keeps people coming back. Watching the ninja move, it really feels like it knows what it's doing. Animation is fluid, dynamic, and realistic. The precision of control allows players to improve their technique endlessly. The game's best players will easily spend hundreds, or even thousands, of hours playing levels to perfection. Also present to keep the game fun is the ragdoll engine that activates after death. The ninja will flop onto the floor, or break into pieces and be flung around the level. Since death is such a common occurrence, it's nice to have something fun to watch between plays, plus it helps stave off frustration.
There is a way for those people to show off the skills they develop. Online highscore tracking is built into the game. The 20 fastest times for every level are viewable in-game or on the developer's website. Even after finishing all 500 levels included, people will often go back to them over and over to get onto the top 20 list. (Teacher's Comment: Obsessive compulsive people?) Players here can get highly competitive, playing the same level for hours at a time in hope of cutting a tenth-of-a-second off their time. Despite how fast players get however, others can always find ways to improve highscores. Since the movement in the game is so precise, holding a key for an extra 1/40th of a second can mean the difference between first place and second.
More casual community can be found in the map-making element of the game. Every copy of N has the N-Editor, Ned, built-in. This tool gives every player the same capability to make levels that the developers had. People can use the tools given to create and test levels they make themselves. Once a person is satisfied with their creation, the level can then be saved into a small file and transferred to others to play. The online N User Map Archive (numa.notdot.net) houses over ten-thousand different user-created levels, which can easily be imported into the game using Ned.
For even more interaction with other players, players can visit the game's forum (metanet.forumer.com). Players can chat casually about the game, complain about it, ask for help, or talk any other subject unrelated to N that comes up for discussion. People are encouraged to bring attention to especially good maps at the Map Archive. Competitions are constantly running to challenge the best map-makers' creativity. Each competition has a theme, such as building symmetrical maps or only using a specific few objects.
Like every game, though, N has its problems. One is the technology required to play. Even though its appearance is simple, it tends to run slowly on older computers. Gameplay can become choppy and make it difficult to perform the precise movements required to play. This is a problem because the difficulty of the included levels also increases very quickly. Relatively few people ever finish even 100 levels, let alone all 500.
Despite its few drawbacks, all of the different features of N add infinitely to the amount of time you can spend playing the game. N is a game that every gamer should take a chance to play. On top of everything else, it's offered for free on the internet. The community is friendly and at the same time competitive. If that's still not enough to convince you, N has the one key element that every game hopes to achieve: it's fun.
