You don't need it, and you can't use it. For simplicity's sake, you round off to a significant number of figures and work with that.

In pure math, you can use infinite precision. If you couldn't, we wouldn't have concepts like pi. We wouldn't have limits and asymptotes. Infinite precision is what allows us to say that a curve approaches its limit without ever touching it. Otherwise, you'd have to say, "Okay, after 50 miles, these lines converge," or "After 50 miles, these lines become parallel." In applied math, you can do that - in fact, you'd probably do it a lot sooner than 50 miles - but in pure math, you can't just change a line because you're tired of handling its quirks.

0.999... = 1 is an axiom. It's just a concept that you accept and add to your available math tools. There are special cases where the difference is important. For example, 1 / (1 - 0.999...) is solvable. It calculates to be infinity, actually. 1 / (1 - 1) is unsolvable because you have divide by 0. But besides these special cases, you can usually take 0.999... to be the same as 1.
