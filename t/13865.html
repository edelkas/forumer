N[/u]

Can this game live up to its community following? I will review this and find out

Going back many years, there was a game called Lode Runner, a few may have heard of it, some may not, but it was a brilliant game in its own right, being addicting and challenging at the same time. Then there is now, 2006, and we have N, it is based off Lode Runner and you can see the similarities, but N is also a lot different to its ancestor, there are multiple tile shapes, making levels more diverse and different, there are many enemy types and objects, which also provide a greater challenge. This game is easily addictive and it is hard at the same time, providing a challenge to pretty much everyone. Its current version, 1.4 is the best yet, with refined physics, a level editor and less glitches, it is almost unbeatable, but obviously is, due to version 1.5 being released in the near future.

Graphics & Sound

Ns graphics arent much to shout about, it is a two-dimensional game set in a grey world, but look closely and everything seems crisp and clear, there are no blurs, no loose pixels and no sharp pixelated edges, making this a beautiful game to look at too. Sounds in the game seem in depth, they are sharp, like the graphics, but also provide feeling to the game, when your character dies, there is a sharp, crunching sound of the bones breaking in the players body, and everything literally falling to pieces, due to the physics in the game, which I will come to later.

Playability

N is a vast game, with a good 500 levels in the Main Game alone, included with the game upon download are approximately 200 User Made levels in one file, which are loaded into the game, this makes 700 levels bundled with the game already, but you can expand this, to this very point, there are another 68334 maps available from NUMA, The N User Map Archive, to which people submit maps for the world to see, play and rate. But that isnt all, on the forums, people have started to make level packs, which provide another few hundred levels to play, so you can never get bored of it.

Physics

Now here is where N really starts to show its strengths, it has rag doll physics used for the player, meaning when the player dies, the body becomes limp and flops around like, as the name suggests, a rag doll, this provides reality to the game, with the fun of watching the dead body being flung around the level from coming into contact with enemies.

Verdict

N is a great game in its own right, but a few things are yet to be improved, as version 1.5 is to be released, that should clear up any problems this current version has. For a game made in Flash, it is huge, and must have required a vast amount of ActionScript and coding, as well as a long period of time. Credit from this goes to Mare and Raigan, who made this game; it is a truly brilliant game, and best of luck with version 1.5!

+ Amazing physics
+ Number of levels
+ Level of difficulty is not too hard, but provides a challenge
+ The number of tricks to learn also spans miles
- Slight stutter on slopes and chances of survival in certain death situations

Final Verdict: 93%

Very good, still slight room for improvement, but overall a brilliant game that lasts hours upon hours.

The Snowball Fighter, by the Fluidmechanics Laboratory





The Game
You are a rubber duck. Believe it or not, you're a freakin' yellow duck which squeaks upon taking damage. Your tasks are many. They can range from defeating other ducks by throwing snowballs at them to solving puzzles in order to recapture treasure on high pillars, snatching away the love of a girl-duck, or any combination of these tasks combined... possibly under a time limit. You are The Snowball Fighter.



Yep, that's some treasure right there, and also shows the field; The Snowball Fighter always takes place in a square playing field. Maps are tiled, but movement is super-smooth and awesome. All the game has are the arrow keys to move, and Space Bar to throw a snowball. There is a key (I think V or B; it's been months since I played the game itself) are some optional alternate camera views to take advantage of the 3D (an overview and first-person view), but naturally the tilted diagonal angle is the best.

Aside from the primary objectives mentioned above, you must also sometimes push blocks Sokoban-style to get out of cramped sections and/or to rest upon blue switches. There are also red switches which can be triggered by throwing snowballs onto them, but the pillars they lower or raise revert to their original positions after a short time.

There are also small power-ups in various missions throughout the game. These can range to health boosts if you've taken a lot of damage, to temporary abilities (such as super-enlarged snowballs, which are actually required for some levels), etc.

Graphics & Sound
The graphics in The Snowball Fighter are incredible, especially for being a freeware game, but the real love lies in the sound. The innocent cries of enemy ducks taking damage or dying are freakishly cute, and the music is very grabbing, ambient yet bold in its composition, and fitting perfectly to the mood of the mission objectives at hand.

But the graphics are not to be ignored, either; the ducks' shadows are generated in real time as they rest on snow or blocks, or even other ducks. And don't forget the creation of those shadows (if they show very darkly in the first place, for there are also evening and night missions); surrounding the playfield is a wonderful random background, which only enhances the grandeur of The Snowball Fighter.



Playabity
The game is extremely simple and will get you addicted in no time. All there is to it is the arrow keys for movement and the Space Bar for your action. Aside from moving your own duck, you may also have to push blocks (simply by pushing against them with the arrow keys) - the camera assumes a straight top-down view if your camera is in the default position. The opposition in beginning levels may seem a tad simple, but just wait 'til you reach the meatier chunks near the middle and the end of the game, where the AI becomes extremely competent.

Physics
The physics in this game are stunning. Although there is no crazy ragdoll action like N, snowballs and your 3D depth-perception of them play a major role in attaining particular heights from climbing pillars in order to hit an otherwise unreachable switch. Many levels take advantage of the game's physics in combination with the sometimes obscure camera angle to formulate their puzzles. The snow on the "battlefield" even crushes as ducks navigate across them, and your duck slows in movement as it pushes blocks. This Lab has really done its research.



Verdict
The game is freakishly smooth, something that almost all games nowadays lack, and provides kiddy-level, totally blood-free violence great for all members of the family interested in working their brains in solving puzzles or raising a bit of adrenaline in tackling enemies. Unfortunately the game lacks a map editor, but the 25+ missions are more than enough to keep casual gamers busy. The graphics and sound are also extremely well-done and more than sufficeheck, morons nowadays try to release their crap as commercial when brilliant, underground coders like these guys publicize their work free of charge. Get it now. This game definitely lives up to its creators' name and indeed sports quite fluid mechanics. 98/100 (for lack of map editor, and relatively low repeating of game overall ;p Although it is nice to play again after a few months, once you forget all the tricks and turns, heheh).

Need For Speed: Most Wanted - review by BritanniaWD


Lets start with the game's career mode, which as any player will tell you begins with an awesomely good bang. You are a nameless, faceless new racer hoping to get in the street racing scene, in the city of Rockport. That street racing scene is based on an underground ranking known as the Blacklist. The Blacklist governs who takes on who, and when. After you accidentally bump into the local top cop, Sgt. Cross, you run into a punk named Razor. Hes unquestionably the sort of guy that lives his existence hanging with people of the in crowd. He's at the bottom of the Blacklist, but after a few movie-style races later, he's messed your ride and has won it from you in a race. Welcome to the Blacklist, people. In the meantime, you're carted off to jail by ol Cross who just happened to be passing by. Left with nothing but some puzzling assistance from a stranger named Mia, who managed to save you from a life behind bars, your mission is to get back in the racing world, and drag, slam and slide your way to the top of the Blacklist. This now happens to be topped by your old mate Razor, whos using your old car to wipe out the opposition.

Mia and Razor

Incredibly, for once a game has a great story hook at the beginning that makes you crave to see the career mode right through to the end. The early story sections are told through an odd mix of high-definition computer-generated cars and full-motion video actors. The acting in these early chapters is terrible... terrible good, that is. You'll be scratching your head and questioning if these clips are deliberately bad and meant to be seen for laughs or if they're just inadvertently amusing. Either way, they're great fun to watch. Its a real shame, after a short prologue, you stop seeing these entertaining video sequences, and the story is conveyed via voice-mails or test messages from various characters in-game. Theyll be saying they will kick your ass as Ronnie (#3) would put it, or itll be the cops saying they want a bit of you or even some people asking if you are a cop. Will you get to utter the magic underground racing words, "Mia, I am a cop"? Or are the plot twists just devastatingly clear? Trust me; youll have to see the story through to find out where everyone's allegiances lie. And Im sure you will be in for some shocking outcomes.

Sgt. Cross gives you a good old talking to

Anyway, lets move on to playing through the game. Youll work your way up the Blacklist in a multistep progress. Before you can take on the next Blacklist racer, you have to satisfy a list of requirements to convince them to race. You'll have to win a set number of race events for starters, then you'll have to reach a set number of pursuit milestones (for example, immobilizing a specific number of vehicles) and earn enough bounty (the Blacklists way of working out how good you are but crediting you kudos for the amount of damage and terror you cause) by riling up the police. The cops simply hate street racers and will give chase when they see you rolling around the open city in your pimped out ride.

Cops on patrol

From my view, running from the cops is the best action the game has to offer. Chases usually start while you are cruising the free-roam city, with just one car on your tail. But as you resist being busted, you might find 20 cars giving chase, in addition to a chopper flying overhead in later in the game. Personally, the most Ive had was 37 cars, 4 trucks and one chopper in one chase that lasted a full 33 minutes, and trust me, there is nothing in the gaming world quite like it. Losing the cops gets tougher, as your heat level (how dangerous you are, so how much the cops will throw at you) rises. Level one heat is just a few of your standard squad cruisers. But by the time you get up to one like level five, you'll be dealing with heavy roadblocks, spike strips, helicopters, ram trucks and federal-owned well-driven Corvettes headed by Cross. A meter at the bottom of the screen designates how close you are to losing the cops or getting busted. Having your car stopped - by spike strips or getting completely boxed in by cops - is how you'll get busted. To actually get away, you'll need to get out of visual range and stay there. If you have to many cops on your tail, use pursuit-breakers (hazards like scaffolding that can be used to take out cops) to ease some off. A nifty 'bullet-time' like function for use in situations, for example, when you suddenly spot spike strips. It slows the whole world down, giving you more time to change direction or check what is going on. Thus, meaning you can escape from some of the most dangerous situations. However, it is limited just the same as nitrous and refills when you travel at speed. The initial evasion changes the meter over to a cooldown meter. You'll have to lie low and wait for that meter to fill up and the chase to end. This is almost certainly the tensest part of the entire chase, since you never know when two cops might come flying around the corner and spot you, starting the whole chase up again. It all kind of works like some sort of uncanny, wonderful cross between Grand Theft Auto's open city and Metal Gear Solid's stealth and skill. All the while, you'll be acquiring heat on your car as the cops start to want you in the nick, and out the publics way more. Plus, as your heat increases, so does the time you take to cooldown, so there are handy cooldown spots to help you fill the meter all the faster. This means that you'll have to keep a couple of cars around, because acquiring heat on one car lowers the heat on your other ones. Redoing the look of your ride also helps lower the heat instantly, but this means you have to spend money, making it a tough choice between the swapping rides or changing looks. Also, getting busted too many times can result in your car getting impounded, meaning you have to wait for the cops to say that you can have it back, but you still have to pay them a release cost. Having all your cars impounded means you have no ride and thus you go to jail. This also means that the game abruptly ends and you cant continue to become the most wanted.

The cops give chase

There is also a lot of racing to cover in Most Wanted's career mode edging on too much, maybe. You'll engage in point-A-to-point-B sprint races, multi-lap circuit races, drag racing, checkpoint-driven tollbooth races, and speed trap (where the winner is the player that accumulates the most speed while passing by a handful of radar cameras spread throughout the track). The races are solid, and fun but not amazingly good. The artificial intelligence doesn't really help things along, because most of the game is rubber-banded like mad, meaning that its designed so you quickly catch back up with them. Ive put my controller down for about half a minute, then picked it back up and caught my rivals back up on the final lap. And though the AI will occasionally crash and come to a complete halt to make it seem like its not set to do the same thing every time, but it'll still catch up very, very quickly just the same as you do.

A typical racing screen, showing all the menus and gadgets you'll be using

Later on in the career, you get a voice-mail message from Mia saying you that things are going to get harder. From then on, the computer drivers magically begin taking all the shortcuts, and the rubber banding only seems to work against you. As a result, catching up after a mistake is also made much tougher. If this difficulty had gradually sloped up, it wouldn't be a big deal. But flipping the switch from drive like an idiot to drive like a professional is quite annoying. Fortunately, the racing action in itself is satisfying enough to keep you going. And of course, you'll be so dying to find out what happens next in the story, it doesnt really sink in.

The free world of Rockport is very involving, thanks to it's realism

Now on to what you will be using to get there. The game has more than 30 licensed cars that you'll be able to purchase or attain from other racers by his pinkslip. Pinkslips are the key to unlocking advantages in the underworld. From stealing the rivals ride to extra bounty or more impound strikes (the amount of times a car can busted), when you beat a Blacklister, his pinkslip is yours. You start out with pretty weak vehicles like the Fiat Punto, but eventually youll pick up a much faster cars like Supras and Mustang's, then move on to bigger guns in the food chain akin to Corvettes and Lamborghinis. You can also find the Carrera GT (along with many other Porsches), a few Fords, and Lotuss, Lexuss, my DB9 and many more. Buying them stock means you'll have to apply improvements yourself, where as winning them from rivals you get all the upgrades already. You can buy a lot of different performance gear, for example nitrous and new tires, plus a load of visual stuff, like body kits, spoilers, vinyls, window tint and much more. Applying visual upgrades also lowers your heat level as I said before, meaning they are pretty useful once the cops take note of your faster cars.

Changing the colour is just one of many things you can do to personalise your ride

Aside from the awesome career mode, there's also a challenge series that gives you a car and a specific goal. Race goals are fairly easy to recognize, but the pursuit challenges ask you to achieve specific milestones in a chase, like blasting through five roadblocks or racking up a specific amount of property damage. You can also just dive into quick races with mates or one your own, or take it online. Online, the game is focused strictly on racing, which is a tad second-rate. Teaming up to avoid the cops or letting some players drive police cars probably would have been more interesting, but then again, thats what Carbon went on to do. Still, the game offers sprint, circuit, and drag races for up to four players, and it keeps an awesome online version of the Blacklist going so you can see who the most dangerous online opponent. On the online game-designing side, you can play in ranked or unranked games, and you can state a disconnection or did not finish percentage, letting weed out ones with bad connections and all the punks no self respecting Blacklister needs to deal with. You can also turn off collision detection between players if you want to thwart people from just crashing into one another over the entire race. The problem is, this only allowed in unranked games. All things considered, going online is efficient, but with no sort of pursuit mode or other police-orientated races, it's awfully common.

The challenge series is instant excitement, and is a great way to learn which cars are best for different situations

Graphically, the game looks great in general. But when you look at the details, some parts of it look superior to others. For the most part, the game does the large, homely city environment very well. The different parts of the city give a nice sense of variety, and the car models are sharp and very detailed, especially when you start painting them with extreme triple-coloured paint. The game delivers a pretty good sensation of speed and seems to be able to be customized reasonably well to fit different PCs. There's a level of detail setting that gets the image quality up to around the Xbox 360 version's graphics, but when that and the resolution are turned all the way up, you're going to require a really powerful machine to get a playable frame rate out of it. However, the game doesn't have much real car damage at all. You'll see your rear window crack up after a few good wrecks and a few scrapes on the wings, but there's never any real damage to your vehicle like in Project Gotham 2. On the sound side, the game has outstanding engine noises that change depending on which car you're in and which upgrades you have. The rest of the sound effects are also of excellent quality. The game uses quite a bit of voice acting in the story, which is good. But the best voices come from the police. When you're being chased, you'll pick up the police wave-band and hear them communicating and cooperating as they try to track you down. The 'cop talk' sounds awfully authentic, as there are more than 100 different items they will say, thats not including the description of your car. On description, they'll say what colour and model you have, plus it's done in three different voices. Thats 30 cars, and more that 50 colours, making it all sound very real. As you are being chased, you'll eventually decipher the police 10 codes and work out when they're going to lay out spike strips, where they are going to set up roadblocks, that they've caught another street racer and so on. While the 10 codes used don't seem to be the genuine ones the real police use (at least that's what a modest basic searching on Google told me), they sound good enough to be realistic. Then there is the music. Oh the music. Included is the standard mix of rock, R&B and hip-hop you've come to expect from EA's games, including a few songs from Styles of Beyond, that all add so much to the street racing theme and excitement. Plus, you can import your own tunes for playing in-game, on all systems and the pursuit theme is looped very well when you are being chased down.

The graphic options menu

So to sum up, while the actual racing in Need for Speed: Most Wanted is probably the weak link in the chain, but it's still good enough to keep you involved as you move from racer to racer, working your way up the Blacklist as you go. But the deal-breaker and most stunning aspect of the game are the cops. If getting away from the law in your own custom ride sounds like your idea of a good time, you'll have great fun here in Rockport.

Overall rating: 8/10 [Amazing]
+ Amazing intense cop chases, large customisation, great story - Just above average online, racing AI

Soldat
Sometimes shooting games are much more fun in a simple 2D format, and dont need to look realistic to be good. Soldat fulfils my expectations for an excellent shooter that gives each round capabilities for tactics, skill, or just brainless murdering sprees. With equally enthralling single player and multiplayer games, both of which contain several styles of gameplay, Soldat should occupy the even the gamers with the shortest of attention spans.

Select your weapon, soldier.

In any game, you must pick a gun, and a secondary weapon (you have grenades as well). Then you set off and try to achieve your objective, which involves killing as many enemies as possible, or running around chasing flags. When you die, you respawn again and repeat, until the end of the round, just like any decent shooter game. Its very simple stuff. But heres where the fun begins. There are 7 different styles of game, each of which can be tweaked for maximum fun, with different maps, modes, bots, and even the delightfully fun Weapons Mod.

The flamethrower is one of the powerups available in the boxes that get scattered about the map.

The game comes with 51 maps, designed for the different Game Styles, and 3 modes. Personally, I dont use any of them unless Im in multiplayer, but many people prefer Soldat with Realistic mode. Theres also Survival mode, where everybody respawns at once, and Advance mode, where you start with secondary weapons and fight for random primary guns. The bots, which are only really used in single player, all have their own favourite weapons, for example Boogie Man is a chainsaw-wielding maniac who fares surprisingly well for somebody who doesnt use missiles. For advanced users, there is Weapons Mod, a text file that can be edited to create crazy and powerful weapons, that are there to mess about with unless you turn into a real Soldat nut! Ive personally only used them to create ridiculously destructive miniguns

Weapons mod is a hoot!

The only way to go about describing the gameplay is to describe each Game Style in turn each is a new type of tactics, but the same manipulation.
Deathmatch is the standard brainless killing spree. Kill anything that moves. Some will find this the most fun, a simple distraction, but I prefer tactics, and a proper goal.
Capture the Flag, the most popular style, is a variation on the classic tag game with guns. Steal the enemy flag and return it to your own.
Hold the Flag is rarely used; your goal is to keep the flag with your team.
Pointmatch is also rare, and uses the flag again, its like Deathmatch but with a point system for different types of kill.
Teammatch is Deathmatch with teams.
Rambomatch is again similar to Deathmatch, but players seek a magic bow, and only when using this can they score points.
Infiltration is the most tactical, Alpha Team must captue Bravo Teams flag, but Bravos only goal is to defend it for as long as possible.

Customise your character, and watch his head get chainsawed off.

Your soldier moves like a dream. The mouse and keyboard-based combat works really well, if after a bit of practice. Youre treated to a jetpack, which makes moving around that much more fun. And maybe the best part of all: all guns are equal. This ensures true variety in weapons, Ive never been in a server where everyone is using the same gun. They help more in different situations, which is why I treasure my Barret, Desert Eagles, and AK47 for their differences.
The graphics arent a masterpiece, but why should they be? In 2D, the blood and guts arent thrilling, but they do the job. Everything is small but compact, to maximise gameplay. And they have the sound effects roundabout spot on!

Soldat can automatically take end-of-game screenshots, making bragging on forums much easier.

Multiplayer is the reason I have been playing Soldat so much recently. There are some servers that take gameplay to whole new levels. There is one called dodgeball, in which teams throw knives across the arena at each other, separated by a wall. Last team standing wins. Or decaying zombie, in which legions of zombies with bad AI swarm around their flag your mission is to clear them and take it. Or how about Snipe & Slice, a server where the only weapons available are the Barret, chainsaw, and knife. Multiplayer for me is the reason Soldat is lastable, the AI gets old and predictable (after a long while), but multiplayer lives on!

Why not try sticking 20 bots in a tiny arena to see what happens?

Overall Soldat is amazingly compact, with so much different gameplay its unbelievable. Considering the fact that its freeware (technically shareware, but the shareware doesnt really add a lot to the game), the game is definitely worth checking out. And give it a chance, check out all the stuff Ive mentioned if it takes your fancy. Im sure there are a heck of a lot of details that Ive not even looked into. Get some headshots for me, young soldier.


There, I realised I'd said I'd do one at about 7:30, it's 11:00 now (although I had other things to do), and here it is. Hope you enjoy it.

Counter Strike Source


This game is only Counter Strike 1.6 on top of the Source engine, do the fancy graphics make it a better game overall, lets see

This game is based on multiplayer warfare, between two teams, the Counter Terrorists (CTs) and the Terrorists (Ts). You may think that you will only be playing Online, yes, you are, but you can play against bots if you want to practice.


A terrorist running away, from what I wonder?

At the start of any match, you have a small pistol and a knife, you have to buy weapons with the cash you have. You earn cash by winning games, even if you die, if the team wins, you get more cash. There is a special pricing system in the game, so that the prices of weapons will change from week to week, and from server to server, so you may pay $1000 for something, and then pay $2000 the next week. The CTs get different weapons from the Ts, so if you want a weapon the other team gets, you have to drop your weapon and pick up theirs, once you have killed them of course. You can only buy weapons from buy zones, you find out if you are in one or not by the green trolley symbol tha appears on the left of your monitor.


The buy menu in the rifles section, what to choose...

The maps are all different, you get realistic maps, and you get VR maps, you get surf maps, which I will explain later on, and some user created maps, each providing different scenery and different sniping points. Also you can get different tasks on each map, some maps have bombsites which the Ts aim to plant their bomb at, if they are successful, the CTs have to defuse it before it explodes. Or there is the basic eliminate all enemies deathmatch mode, or you can try to rescue the hostages, where CTs have to make it to the hostages, and return them to the hostage rescue point, without losing any en-route. The surf maps are deathmatches, but they usually consist of long prism shaped ramps, which you have to slide along by holding either strafe key, so you stay on, for example, if A and D are your strafe keys, left and right respectively, you would move like so.
D /\ A. Here, you hold the left strafe to keep on the slope on the right side.


One of the more realistic maps in the game.

Weapons come under 5 categories and an equipment category; there are Pistols, Shotguns, SMGs, Rifles and Machine Guns, the equipment category contains grenades, armour, defusal kits and night vision. So you have plenty to buy. The combination of weapons you carry can also be chosen tactically, choose a sniper and a strong pistol, choose a strong SMG and a weak pistol, there are many combinations, each having it's advantages and disadvantages in maps. Also in the game, when you, or any opponent dies, the body goes limp, using ragdoll physics, similar to the ones N uses. This means that you can shoot the body, and it will flop around everywhere, creating many opportunities for mild humour.


An example of the physics, it seems the character was shot from the front and fell back

Each side has 4 custom characters to choose from, the CTs have people from different armies across the world, the British SAS, the SEAL Team 6 from America, The GSG 9 from Germany and the GIGN from France. The Terrorists have 4 people from different terrorist groups, each looking somewhat stupid in their own right. Another point of the game is the spray option, you can graffiti the map you are on in game, but the graffiti gets removed over time, or when you spraypaint somewhere else. You can use a pre-set spray, or upload a custom one, meaning all sorts of pictures get used (Dont get any ideas!).


One of the standard spays, which has a clear outline so it blends into the terrain behind it.

Most servers in the game use VAC, which stands for VALVe Anti-Cheat, this prevents cheaters getting away with what they do and banning them, some hacking programs can not be detected by this, but the system keeps getting updated to block the newer hacks. The servers can be filtered so you can find exactly what you want, for example, you may want to play on the map de_dust2, so you type in the map box the map you want, and it finds all servers currently playing on that map, making the game overall much more enjoyable.

Just another recent addition are the mods, these are twists on the main gameplay modes that opens up new ways to play, the only mod I have noticed so far is the ZombieMod, where there are two teams, one team has all the humans on it, while the other has one zombie. The zombies have different character designs too, making them unique. But the main difference is the overall gameplay and stats, if a zombie kills a human, they turn into a zombie and their screen becomes green, they move quicker and have a absurd amount of Hp, while the humans have little Hp, they have infinite ammunition, so they shouldn't worry about being killed so easily.


A screen of what it's like to be a zombie...

Counter Strike Source is all about the multiplayer, if you want a game with a strong single player campaign, buy Call of Duty, but if you want fun, up to date warfare, where you can play against your friends, this is the game to buy!

+ Multiplayer is addictive.
+ Source engine creates realistic graphics.
+ Choice of weapons gives greater enjoyment.
+ Some mods change the overall gameplay.
- Some textures don't load and become ugly purple and black check patterns.

Final Verdict: 87%

Good game, fun for a while, but eventually the lack of single player campaign will deter buyers and reduce replay factor. This game is replayable, but good for the odd while once you have become bored with it.

Wii Play

For an extra 5 you can get a copy of Wii Play with a Wii Remote, is it worth the added cost?

Wii Play is made up of 9 smaller games which all use the Wii Remote in a different way. When you start you only have access to one game, but once you have played the first game you get access to the next game and so on (regardless of success) until you have unlocked all nine. I will go into more detail with the games later on, but for now I will explain the main features of Wii Play itself. Wii Play is for up to two players, meaning that you are more likely to pass the remote around between a group of three or more. The compatibility with your Miis is also a fun addition to the game, meaning you can play as yourself (or as close as you can get to make the Mii look like you) in the games.



The first game available is shooting. In a duck hunt esque style, you have to shoot all the targets, or as many as possible. These targets range from clay pigeons to UFOs that try to abduct your Miis. During multiplayer, the UFO stage has 6 Miis, which normally are all yours, but they are split 3:3 between the players. If you try to save yours and let theirs get abducted, you will gain more bonus points at the end. The ducks from Duck Hunt make an appearance each round giving the opportunity for bonus points, if you can shoot them.



Upon completing a round of shooting you unlock Find Mii, this game does as it says on the tin. You have to find the Miis that the game tells you to. The Miis will be doing a set activity and you have to find the ones specified by the game. Sometimes it will be the one you are using, sometimes it will be the look-alikes. In later stages of the game you have to use the Wii Remote like a flashlight to find your specified Miis in the crowd, adding enough variety to last a good 20 minutes.



Next is Table Tennis, you are required to move the remote to move the paddle on screen so it hits the ball. All the rules of normal table tennis apply here, from serving to obstructions. In multiplayer it is the first player to obtain 11 points, while in single player mode you have to rally for as long as possible, but the game stops at 100, so you will eventually have 5 high scores of 100.



The game after this is probably the most difficult and least entertaining here. Pose Mii requires the player to move the Mii on screen and change the pose it sits in to match the one in the bubble. In the later stages of the game you can find yourself missing bubbles and it becomes frantic. If you miss a bubble and it falls to the bottom, you get a miss, after three misses your game is over. In multiplayer, the colour of the pose is essential, if you see a red bubble falling to the bottom and you are P1, you can leave that as P2 is red in the multiplayer mode of the game.



The fifth game you unlock is laser hockey, which is similar to pong. You have to score as many points as you can in single player mode, and the lead you have over the CPU player is your final score. In multiplayer mode the first player to reach eight points is the winner. If you twist the remote upon impact with the puck, you can send it flying a different direction across the table.



Next in line is one of the best games in Wii Play. Pool is fairly difficult to start with, but once you have grasped the controls it is good fun for everyone. The objective is to pot the balls in order, you have to hit the target ball first; this means potting other balls afterwards is not classed as a foul, but fouls deduct points from your final score. To hit the ball, you have to pull the Wii Remote back while holding B, then push the Wii Remote forward to hit the ball; still holding B until the ball has been hit.



Fishing is fun, but it requires a lot of patience. You have to catch the fish by swinging the Wii Remote back once a fish has bitten (The Wii Remotes speaker will let you know if one has). You can gain bonus points by catching the specific fish shown at the top of the screen. Some fish subtract points from your total, so be careful.



Charge is a game where your Mii rides a cow and tries to knock down scarecrows. Holding the Wii Remote sideways, you tilt it forward to make it move faster and tilt it back to make the cow slow down. To jump fences you have to quickly lift the Wii Remote up.



The last game in Wii Play is Tanks. Here you control a small tank (Blue for P1 and Red for P2) with the D-Pad or the Nunchuk attachment. Pressing the B trigger fires shells that bounce of walls once. The aim is to destroy all tanks on the board to progress to the next level. The enemy tanks vary in capabilities that are shown by the colour of their tanks. There is little depth to this game, but it is fun while it lasts.

Looking at the whole game, it does what it intends to do, it teaches how to use the Wii Remote. The graphics style looks good and fresh and the game lasts forever. It lacks in single player, but multiplayer is where this game truly shines; for a fiver extra you cant go far wrong!

+Variety of games is superb
+Controls are simple to master
+Multiplayer creates longetivity
+Value for money
-Table tennis is a letdown.

Final Score: 91%
