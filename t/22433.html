#20086 1 2008-04-26T21:40:11Z Strife
As you may have guessed from the title of this thread, I work in an IT department, specifically for a university. It's a paid job, but the service we provide is free for students and faculty. We take phone calls as well as walk-in cases, and they're handled at our desks.
One of my coworkers received a particularly difficult call - the caller was very impatient, insulting, threatening, and even screamed a bit, too. This coworker remained polite and cheery as ever on the call, and then commented out loud about the call she had received. I, being the sort of person to bring up my opinion of the subject in casual conversation, brought up my opinion of the subject in casual conversation. A customer overheard me and politely objected, and then we discussed it a bit.
...in case you're wondering what inspired this thread.

I define [being paid to take your crap] as receiving monetary compensation from you for performing a service for you. If you're not paying me, and no amount of your money is in my paycheck, then I technically don't owe you a thing.
I also define [being paid [i]enough[/i] to take your crap] as receiving such an amount of monetary compensation from you, directly or indirectly (e.g. through my employer), that I, personally, deem is worth the trouble you give me.

Let's say I start up a service where I hire people to help you when you request it. I pay them out of my own pocket, and I don't charge for the help in the slightest. In this situation, there is absolutely no difference in the benefit I receive whether I help you or not, because you're not going to recompensate me whether I assist you or not. In other words, doing business with you, no matter who you are, is exactly as beneficial as not doing business at all. This means that I'm not obligated to help you do anything, and that the service I'm paying people to provide for you is me being benevolent, me acting out of the goodness of my heart. If you come and request the service, I said I would perform it, so I probably will. I make no promises, and you should understand that a free service means that I'm completely free from liability, but I'll probably do it because I said I would. But if you're being exceedingly difficult, then I have no incentive whatsoever to put up with you, and my associates and I are not going to be affected in the slightest if I refuse to provide you the service I said I'd provide. In fact, in being difficult, you may be taking up valuable time I could be spending on customers who will be grateful, so it may even be a net benefit to ignore you.

Let's reduce this even further:
Let's say I offer a service wherein I, if asked, will pick up something you have dropped, and maybe even help you carry it. I don't charge for this service, and it in fact probably inconveniences me, so I'm performing it out of the goodness of my heart.
If you drop your book and ask me to pick it up for you, I will probably do it if I'm legitimately making the offer. But if you drop your book and say, "hey asshole, pick up my book," then you can go to hell as far as I'm concerned. If you want to pay me $20 and say "hey asshole, pick up my book," then that's fine - you're effectively making the offer, "can I pay you $20 to remain unaffected by my calling you an asshole," which is an entirely different offer (one I'd probably take), in addition to the service I provide for free. But otherwise, there is no incentive whatsoever for me to help you if you're consciously being difficult.

I think it's fairly safe to say that if you take advantage of a free service - even if the workers are being paid - and you're consciously being difficult to deal with, then you should expect to be treated in kind. In some cases, such as if your opinion is influential and those providing the service would somehow lose money if you had a bad experience, or even if the workers are being paid enough, then they will still be polite to you regardless, but this is not something you should expect. Otherwise, what am I, as the proprietor of the service, supposed to be afraid of? That you'll call my supervisor and have him explain to you how your business means nothing to us? That you'll go off and tell your friends to not use my free service so that I can't get their $0 as well? Even if you cut the customer base down to 10%, our revenue is still $0. Give me a break.

In my case, I'm being paid by the IT Department, which actually makes most of its money from sales, and only a very small fraction of your tuition as a student comes to the IT Department at all. As in the previous example ($20, "asshole," etc.), I may decide that the amount you're offering to pay me to put up with you just isn't enough. So if you pay your tuition, a fraction of a cent of which probably comes to me in my entire duration of employment, I may very well decide that that isn't enough for me to put up with you.

Obviously, an employer can be dissatisfied with employees who give the minimum expected (no service whatsoever), but such employees would be fired because they're wasting money, not because they're making the employer lose any money (because they aren't). The employer-employee relationship is that the employee is an extension of the employer, so if the employer feels that an employee isn't carrying out the employer's intentions, the employee is going to be fired because he's vestigial, but not because he's bringing harm to the organization as a whole (because he isn't).

Clearly, being a liability as an employee is bad. Don't misunderstand and think I'm saying that employees should feel free to be a liability for the employer or the customer, because the service is no longer free in such a case ("we fix your computer, and all you have to do is take a lot of crap from us"). If you're being paid to sit on your hands, then you are a liability for your employer (he is giving you money for absolutely nothing in return) and you should expect to be fired.

As an IT guy who offers tech support that you hardly pay a penny for, I try to help everyone, but if you're being exceedingly difficult (and I stress "exceedingly," because there are naturally more patient guys than me who would like my job), then I won't give you the cheery/preppy/polite treatment I give to the grateful customers.
