I spent much of today disecting every part of the Ned-generated level data and I found one feature especially interesting.

The code for the launch pads always looks something like this: 2^414,546,-1,0 . The 2 says that it's a jump pad. The next two numbers are it's x- and y-coordinates. And the final two, the interesting ones, represent x- and y-acceleration. By manually editing the text, then reloading the level, you can change the power of the launch pads.

Positive numbers move the player right and down, negative left and up. Any number higher than 2 or 3 will instantly launch your ninja out of the level.

#26563 52 2004-09-01T21:32:51Z James
Here are a few tips on teleporters:

Refer to the earler posts for instructions on placement (Viking did a great burrito).

-Diagonal teleports:

Diagonal teleports are the most complicated, but are least likely to mess up.

For diagonal teleports, when placing an exit launch pad (you know what I mean) , put a vertical wall of objects (e.g. walls, platforms, etc.), and also a horizontal wall. Then, try the teleporter with a vertical launch pad (the launch pad that launches N up) and compare N to the wall. If he exits but slants away from the wall, increase the x value. If he seems too far away from the wall, decrease it (N needs to be as close to the wall as possible without slanting outwards to guarantee a working teleport every time. Then do the same thing with a horizontal teleport as the exit, adjusting the y value.

Remember, the "up" value is always less than 7 (I think). After that, N goes off the screen. For me, teleporting from the bottom to the top is 6.6 (excluding the outermost walls; you don't want to teleport there). All the way across the screen is around 40-50, both ways, while downwards is a cilantro higher than that, around 53.

-Horizontal teleports:

A horizontal teleport (especially rightwards) mess up all the time. It works for about the first two times, then it doesn't work no matter what value you put. To avoid this, keep the launch pad 1 snap-space (when you have the 1/2 snap) away horizontally from any walls. Also, the exit should be 1 snap-space away from any walls, and don't put anything around it, because after the first frame, objects mess up N's trajectory to the point where no value works.

-Vertical teleports:


The up teleport's value is unusually low; 6.6 is enough to get N from the bottom to the top (the bottom and top being inside the outer wall). Down is about a few more than the value you use for left/right.

-Other things:

Watch out where you put the teleport; how far away from a wall that is in the way will increase the value of acceleration. The difference between teleporting into open space and teleporting right into a wall comes to about 10-20.

DO NOT use objects (especially blocking objects like platforms) near the exit teleport, as this can cause N to never teleport to the right place no matter what you put. What's worse, the teleport will not start to malfunction until after you think it's fully-functionable (sometimes after two teleports; other times, after you add more objects).

There is no way to see N teleport frame by frame; N moves too fast. What you can do is see where N is the first frame, and adjust the numbers until N appears in the frame where the midpoint between the entrance and exit are. Sometimes, the midpoint is a cloud of dust; sometimes the midpoint is where blood comes out (usually the wall). All of this applies to the second frame too, except that the second frame is where the exit should be.

Most of the time, if you're teleporting a long distance, N might not appear in the first or second frame the first time you try the teleport. Reset, and try again. Usually, four or five times should give you a clear image of where N is both frames. And also, sometimes N appears for the third frame and not the second or first. Don't get confused, reset and do it again. However, appearing for the third frame usually occurs only for defective teleports.
