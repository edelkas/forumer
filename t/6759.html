#131790 8 2006-01-12T01:59:37Z Atilla
Face-to-face and internet discussions both have advantages and disadvantages. In an internet discussion, you can nip off to Google a stack of evidence, you can also take more time with the phrasing and construction of your argument, and people can't shout you down or talk over the top of you. However, because you cannot interject or clarify the way you can in a face-to-face discussion, and because writing often lacks the nuances of expression and tone present in speech, people are often misinterpreted. I suspect I am particularly prone to this problem, because I tend to write long and complex responses. Then again, I do that when I speak, too.

When I debate in person, I usually make an effort to slow myself down and take time over my answers. If the person I'm debating with is the sort to just go "Hah, you don't have an answer" instead of waiting for me to think, then there's not really much point in trying to argue with them.
