As of recently, i have run out of books to read. I have just finished "Six Sacred Stones" by Matthew Reilly (awesome!), and as i have already finished almost every single one of my books three times or more, i need some more stuff. I will be asking you to give me suggestions as to what books you have read that you think are really exceptional. I will make a list of my favourites, and will add others that people enjoyed.

***Please be serious. Don't suggest porn or toddlers books, say "i can't read" or "your a nerd because you read". There is a time and a place and that is not now nor here.***

So without further ado, here is the list.

Books/Authors Worth Reading

Matthew Reilly - all of his, but i recommend "Contest", "Seven Ancient Wonders" and "Six Sacred Stones". Full of non-stop action.
Phillip Pullman - "His Dark Material" Trilogy, probably the best i have ever read. Fantasy.
Douglas Adams - The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy, The trilogy of five. (that's right, five). Really wierd, but hilarious. Science Fiction.
Garth Nix - The Old Kingdom Trilogy, fantasy, fantasticly written.
Steve Berry - "The Romanov Prophecy", "The Templar Legacy" and "The Alexandria Link". Has a unique style, revolves around historical events. Similar to Matthew Reilly, but more realistic.
Garth Nix (again) - The Keys to the Kingdom. For a yonger age group, but still enjoyable. Fantasy.
Paul Stewart and Chris Riddell - The Edge Chronicles. Really fantastic fantasy series. Also younger age group, but very well written.
Jonathan Stroud - The Bartimaeus Trilogy. Alternate Universe type series, but cool. Fantasy (sort of)
Michael Gerber - Parady books. Very funny. Did harry potter parodies, lord of the rings parodies, narnia, etc.
Eion Colfer - The Artimes Fowl Series. How did i forget? Science fiction, funny, actiony.

Any more i come up with, i will add to the list. Also, if you have a fav book, don't hesitate to ask.

#381835 3 2007-11-22T13:42:13Z gloomp
Well, anything by Nancy Kress, for starters, but I'd also recommend:


The Wonderful O, by James Thurber. Delightful little fable. The version I read was wonderfully illustrated.

If you want something a little heavier, i am no one you know (yes, uncapitalized,) by Joyce Carol Oates. For me, the crowning glory of this collection filled to the gullet with gems, would be Aiding and Abetting, a tension-filled, ferocious little story. The book is not perfect, though. Steer clear of Me & Wolfie, 1979, an indecipherable mess of a story.

For something between the two extremes, try Life of Pi, by Yann Martel. You may find it a little slow to start, but you soon grow to enjoy the long, winding narrative, told by a fascinating narrator, who follows three different religions.

Philip Reeve- Larklight. Fantasy. It's more of a younger age group book, I believe, but still very good. The alternate-existance setting is amazing, and the book combines humor and oddity with a save-the-world-story to create an extremely interesting storyline.

Angie Sage- The Septimus Heap Trilogy. Fantasy. Again, I think it's aimed toward a slightly younger age group, but the fantasy world inside it is very entertaining.

DJ MacHale- The Pendragon Series. Fantasy. A dramatic and exciting world-to-world series.
