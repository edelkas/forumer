I think there's a problem with hydrogen powered cars. Won't they blow up easily? I mean, seeing how explosive hydrogen is...

Assuming storage as pressurized, non-cooled hydrogen, there will be an explosion risk if oxygen enters the storage tank. With storage as pressurized liquid hydrogen, this risk is much less problematic. With storage as lithium borohydride or ammonia borane (the two best storage materials by a long shot), the risk of explosion is nonexistant. Unfortunately, both lithium borohydride and ammonia borane are solids, which explains why hydrogen cars have yet to take off. I love how the article ignores catalytic converters, which have been proven reliable and effective beyond expectation. Do you see a brown haze above your city? You have platinum tubes to thank.
Plus, many of the problems of catalytic converters go out the window when you are burning hydrogen rather than gasoline. Nickel can be used for converters quite effectively; however, it reacts with carbon monoxide to form the extremely poisonous nickel tetracarbonyl. Copper can also be used, but it catalyzes the formation of dioxins from unburnt hydrocarbons. The exhaust from a hydrogen burning engine would obviously contain neither carbon monoxide nor unspent hydrocarbons, and neither copper nor nickel will cause problems. See here.
