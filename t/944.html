Ok, here is the basic algorithm I used to get my rope working. It works pretty well, but there are still some points where it gets stuck. They could probably be overcome with some tweaking.

The rope is represented by a linked list of Vectors. The two Vectors at the beginning of the list is the portion of the rope attached to the player. Only this section needs to be updated, as the rest of the segments are wrapped around terrain and are static.

There are basically two seperate algorithms, one for wrapping one for unwrapping.

here is the wrapping algorithm:

calculate if the rope is moving clockwise or counterclockwise

interpolate through the end of the rope connected to the player one pixel at a time from its previous position to its current position (so the rope doesn't skip over terrain when moving too fast)

while the rope intersects terrain
split the rope at the point of intersection that would have been encountered first(determined by the previous clockwise or counterclockwise calculation)


to unwrap the rope:

while the line from the player's position to the 3rd vector in the list does not intersect terrain
remove the 2nd vector in the list

I'm not sure how well this will work in N. In Mr. YoYo, each block in the grid of the physical representation of the level is the size of four graphical pixels, so in N this algorithm will take up about 4X as long to execute unless you create a seperate physical representation of the level just for ninja roping.

Sorry if i was a bit confusing

hey,
that's OUR algorithm as well, almost exactly!! that's really weird.

one thing we did though, is to preprocess the level to find convex corners -- the rope can't collide with concave corners so this really helps avoid a lot of collision tests. also, we do use seperate rope-only collision data.

it seemed that you may have been using some fancy circle-arc length type of stuff instead for the rounded corners, since the rope really wrapped smoothly around. but if you're doing it at a 4x4pix level, linesegs are just as good -- sadly, in N the tiles are ~24x24 so faking curves using linesegs looks awful.

another difference is the "un-wrap" test; what we do is store the signed area of the triangle formed by the "top" (closest to the player/end point) 3 points in the list. then, each frame you just need to test to see if the signed area has a different sign (i.e if it WAS positive when you collided, and now it's negative, you're un-wrapped.. or vice-versa). i hope that makes sense it saves another round of collision testing, which in our case was a big slowdown.

one thing we tried that was a lot of fun was having two delicious ends -- it's pretty easy to change the algo so that you treat both ends of the rope list as "moving".. that way you can drag things/etc...

anyway.. exciting! we've got 2-3 non-rope-based games in the works, but eventually we really do want to return to the idea.
