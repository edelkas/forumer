#436857 2 2008-05-17T12:50:19Z George
Hello, jono123, and welcome to the forums.

Users often have problems with drones because they haven't used the correct algorithm (or perhaps that don't know how they function). When you're about to add a drone in NED, you'll see some options correlating to various keys (e.g. alternating, dumb, surface follow, and quasi-random). Each algorithm has a specific function:

Alternating (W) drones change according to the opposite direction, so it's not always the same.

Dumb (A, D) drones will only ever move in a clockwise or anticlockwise direction, depending on which key you use.

Surface follow (Q, E) drones are fairly self explanatory. They will always follow the surface of the tile, circling them indefinitely. However, if it does not touch a surface (according to the direction you choose), it will just move around in a circle.

Lastly, quasi-random (S) drones change their direction depending on a timer. At certain times, it will select one direction, at others, a different direction. Some people have found the exact times of the selection, but it's not really that important.

You may think that the algorithms available are insufficient (well, they are), so you'll have to make do with what you have. Remember that you can always place your tiles in certain positions to manipulate the drones to do what you want. The same goes for the different types of doors.

I hope I've covered the basics about drones. Other members should feel free to elaborate on what I've just said.

Also, if a switch is not going in the right place, it may be because of a NaN. A NaN (or, not a number) occurs when a letter character is input into the code. The most common way for this to happen, is if you do not click out of the data box when you press 'L' to test your map. The easy solution to this problem is to always remember to click around outside of those text boxes a bit.
