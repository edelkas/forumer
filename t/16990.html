The reason that I ask this is because omnipotence seems impossible on many levels when criticized logically. God is described in the Bible as omniscient and omnipotent. Well, for starters, this statement is redundant. To know something is a type of power, and a god with all powers would be able to have such a power. Obviously. But wait! It gets wackier!

If god is omniscient, then he obviously knows what he will do next. He is therefore unable to do anything else. There is something that he is then unable to do, and he is therefore not omniscient or not omnipotent. That would work, except that omnipotence implies omniscience. Omnipotence therefore does not exist.

Another side paradox:

Could god make an object that he can't move?
Could god make a taco so spicy that he can't eat it?
Could god overpower himself at his most powerful?

If god can make the object, then he can't move it, therefore he is not omnipotent. If god can't make the object, then there is something that he cannot do, and he is therefore not omnipotent. The same logic applies to the taco, or the overpowering.

What do you think? Does this logic imply that omnipotence is impossible?

Assuming that Omnipotence A exists and that it can influence the universe, consider this:
An entity with Omnipotence A can exist in such a way that allows us humans in the natural universe to say that he exists, and be correct.
If an entity with Omnipotence A doesn't exist, it is his option to come into existence.
This means that there are potentially an infinite amount of entities with Omnipotence A (well, a fraction of infinity, because how many of those are going to decide to exist in a way that would allow us to say that they exist and be correct?). Of that fraction of infinity, yet another fraction decide to adopt personality traits that we humans in the natural universe know of. Of that fraction of the fraction of infinity, yet another fraction is mercilessly hostile and wishes to destroy the universe an infinite number of times per second, destroying all the contents of the natural universe and preventing them from ever coming into remotely similar configuration ever again.
Such a tiny fraction of infinity is, of course, infinity. There are an infinite number of entities that constantly lay waste to the natural universe, even if there is also an infinite number of entities with Omnipotence A who are restoring it again. But you'd also have to consider that there are an infinite number of entities with Omnipotence A who choose to exist only so that they could put all of their efforts into making all of the matter in the universe continuous. There are also an infinite number of entities with Omnipotence A who want to completely fill the void of expanding space with sea otters.

As I may have said before, "I exist, therefore God doesn't."
As we can clearly see, supernatural things do not have any influence whatsoever over the natural universe. That whole domain is a waste of time because it's exactly what its name implies - these beings exist outside of reality, so they're not in the real world, so they're not real; they're made up.
I suppose it's fine if you think about made-up things as a hobby, with the understanding that they are made up and will never be able to influence reality, but some people pray to these made-up things and hope that they actually will change reality, and that's just not healthy.

If you hadn't noticed, McP, I've been talking from our perspective, about what we actually can know.
Y'know, that's what I was talking about when I said this:
If someone is talking about an entity that exists outside of the natural universe, one not only has absolutely nothing to say about this entity (including that it exists), but you're also demonstrating that the being you're talking about couldn't exist because you possess understanding of it (which basically means that the entity you're talking about exists in your mind, and nowhere else).

You can't actually know, or even think that God exists. When I say "God," you're thinking of God. That conception of God that you have proves that it can be grasped by a human mind, which means that if God does indeed exist, He is nothing at all like what you currently think He is. If God does exist, then you won't ever be able to grasp anything about Him. You wouldn't even be able to come up with the idea, or understand the idea that He exists.

It's like this...
A child who has never heard of language or mathematics is sitting on one side of a brick wall. On the other side is a book about quantum physics. The child can invent all manner of silly things that exist beyond the wall, but they'll all be things that he can imagine - and since he flat-out cannot, ever, have anything even remotely close to language or mathematics enter his head, it is impossible for him to think that a book about quantum physics exists on the other side of the brick wall. And no matter what the book about quantum physics does, it cannot make itself known to the child, because it is permanently unintelligible to him.
The book on quantum physics plays absolutely no role in the development of that child, and the child is incapable of understanding that language and mathematics exist. Neither plays any role in the other's life.
This is the relationship between things within the natural universe and things outside of the natural universe (or "supernatural" things).

Is this really that hard to understand?
