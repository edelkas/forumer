#313176 7 2007-04-14T22:14:21Z Tsukatu
OP makes me want to cry.

Infinite causation is nothing but speculation. Not only is there no shortage of ways that the universe could have spontaneously generated (for example, in this nifty concept called a quantum flux, there appears to be a result without a consequence), but it also can't be anything other than inconclusive. All our lack of knowledge shows is nothing beyond just that - we don't know enough. It is foolish to make conclusions when we know next to nothing.
You are making the "God of the Gaps" fallacy, even though you're not talking about God. The "God of the Gaps" fallacy is when you see something you don't understand and declare that it is caused by something supernatural. People did this all the time before humans knew much about science. They were convinced that things we can explain easily today, such as rain and nighttime, were the results of magic, or animal spirits, or angry gods. They made up an answer to a question they didn't have any means of figuring out, and that's exactly what you're doing - you're making up an answer because you don't know nearly enough to try any other approach.
In this instance, it's much, much more rational to remain content with your ignorance if you aren't willing to scientifically investigate the matter.
I, for one, don't give two shakes of a lamb's tail about the origin of the universe. I'm not concerned about it in the slightest.
