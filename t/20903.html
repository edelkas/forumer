It's kinda cheesy in some parts, downright bad in others, but I'm pretty proud of it. Please read it and tell me what you think.

Oh yeah, two more things:
-I might change some details. Feel free to suggest things.
-I might continue the story later. I'm open to suggestions here, too.


Okay, without further ado, here it is:



One thing we know, but refuse to accept, is that there are things we will never be able to do- things that we read in science fiction stories, things that we think up and wish we had. But the one thing we, as a people, are really set on creating, is a time machine...

TEMPUS
By *me*

The year was 2029. Many had predicted the earth to be in chaos and ruin by then. Some believed it was. Some believed it wasn't. It depended on point of view, like the refraction of light through a glass of water. Numerous technological advances had been made; many were insignificant and failed miserably, such as the digital toaster(advertised as, "An appliance that can make toast, tell the time, AND work as a 500mb flash drive!").
However, many inventions had immensely benefited mankind, such as the hologram. Holograms were used for many things- communication, security, et cetera. This paled in comparison, however, to what one man felt he was close to inventing. His name was, and is, Lucas Miller...
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Lucas sat alone in his small apartment. He could have had a house of his own, if he wanted to, but the apartment suited him fine. His eyes were closed, and others might assume he was sleeping, had they been there, but he was actually thinking. Thinking about things that scientists had tried to do, but could not. Thinking about what he was trying to do. Lucas was a scientist himself. He had graduated from Sercan University at age nineteen, and ever since he had been working on the same project. Now, at thirty, he believed he was close to finishing it.
His alarm sounded, and he sighed. It was 6 a.m. Time to get back to work. As he left his apartment building, he contemplated what the completion of his project would mean to the world. He did not dwell on this long, though, because he felt that he should focus on completing the machine first. He walked down the New York streets to his workplace, a lab called Jarell Mechanics. Several people stared at the sight of a tall, thin man in a white lab coat, but he was unnoticed for the most part. He was simply another generic person.
The lab was a large building that looked to be constructed entirely of silver and glass. He entered the lobby, and felt the heating system take effect immediately. As he passed the reception desk and approached the lab entrance, the security hologram appeared. It was a floating head.
"ID, Miller?" it queried.
"Why do you need to see my ID if you already know who I am?" asked Lucas.
"Sorry, it's protocol.," Said the head. "Hey, I don't like it either."
Lucas produced his identification card, and the head at it. "All right, everything checks out. G'wan in." The head vanished, and the doors slid open. The inside of the lab was not bustling with people as one might expect, but nor was it empty- there were several people milling about, as if they were unsure what to do. Among them, Lucas noticed Thomas Blaine, a twenty-eight year-old, short, forgetful man, as well as his assistant. As soon as Blaine spotted him, he hurried over.
"Come on, Lucas, we're running late already!" He said.
"What? Carson doesn't have a due date set on my project," Lucas said, confused. Travis Carson was Jarrell Mechanics' head scientist, in charge of coordinating all of the Lab's individual projects.
"Well, yeah, he didn't, but now he does."
"Oh, really? And what is it?"
"Ummm..." Blaine scratched his head, thought for a moment, then took out his PDA and began scrolling through his notes. Lucas sighed and glanced around the room.
"Okay, here it is..." Mumbled Blaine. "The deadline is April 26."
"And?"
"What?"
"The year?"
"Oh! This one."
"WHAT?" Lucas exploded. "That's in four days!" He began to run toward his own work area, in the gamma wing, while his assistant struggled to keep up.
"But," Blaine huffed, "It's almost done, isn't it?"
"Come on, Tom, you know very well that it could take longer than four days!" Lucas said. "What possible reason could Carson have for ordering the machine's completion so soon?"
He slid to a stop in front of Carson's office(which was on the way to the gamma wing). Blaine, who had been under the impression that they were heading straight to the work area, bumped into him. As soon as he noticed where they were, he said, "Aw, jeez, Lucas, don't argue with the boss. You know you won't win."
Lucas grunted in response.
"Whatever," Sighed Blaine. "I'll wait out here." He sat on the nearest bench as Lucas entered the office.
Carson looked up from his paperwork as soon as Lucas entered. "Hello, Miller," He said. "I think I can guess why you're here."
"I'm here to protest against your unreasonable deadline for my project."
"It won't do any good. I'm not changing my mind."
"I can't finish it in four days."
"Yes, you can."
"Sir, what reason could you possibly have for-"
"Just DO it, Miller!" Carson roared.
Lucas paused, startled. Then he said, "All right. Check back in four days. It'll be done." He left before Carson could reply.
"How'd it go?" Blaine pressed as he came out.
"Not good." They hurried toward the lab. After walking the route so many times, he knew it by heart- but it seemed like he kept getting lost this time, even though he was on the correct path. It felt like an eternity before he and Blaine arrived at their destination. Blaine opened the door and hurried in, but Lucas paused and gazed upon the machine within. A time machine. It was pod-shaped, and many circuit boards were visible through the gaps in its plating. It was attached to many cords running to various generators and devices around the room. Blaine was already activating a computer near the machine. Lucas entered and began to work.
* * * * *
10:30, Sixteen hours later, Lucas and Blaine were still in the lab, working. Everyone else had gone home. Blaine was almost asleep on his feet, but Lucas was still quite awake, hurrying to his blueprints on the table, then to the time machine and adjusting something, then back to his computer to punch in a few figures.
Suddenly, Travis Carson's voice blared into the room through the speaker system. "Miller. Blaine."
Lucas stood up, and Blaine, jolting awake, jumped to his feet, saying, "Huh? Whazzat?
What is it, sir? asked Lucas.
I cant let you finish this project, Miller. said the voice.
Huh? said Lucas and his assistant in unison. What are you talking about, boss? asked Blaine.
Do you know what the invention of a time machine would mean to the world? asked the voice. It would change everything.
Exactly! said Lucas, confused. So what-
I once read a book, the voice continued, In which a man travels back in time, using a time machine such as yours, and he changes something then. When he returns to his own time, however, everything is changed. I cant let this happen to our world.
Sometimes change is good, Travis! exclaimed Lucas.
NO! roared the voice. Everything is fine as it is now! Nothing needs to be changed!
Nothing never needs changing, Travis. Lucas said.
Yeah, boss, Blaine chimed in. Everything changes sometime!
ENOUGH! boomed the voice. Miller, I know you designed those blueprints in college. You are a genius, I grant you that. But when a friend of mine at Sercan told me of this, I knew I couldnt let it happen!
Why did you wait until the machine was almost complete to tell me this? asked Lucas.
You made some changes to the blueprints throughout the time you were given, did you not? queried the voice.
Lucas was silent.
You did do that, Lucas. whispered Blaine.
Lucas paused.
Regardless of what you say, Carson, I cant abandon this project. said Lucas.
I cant let you continue, Miller! said the voice. I have planted a bomb in the buildings framework. When I detonate it, you will be destroyed, and your precious machine and blueprints with you!
WHAT?
What about me, boss? wailed Blaine.
I cant risk letting you live- not when youve seen the blueprints! said the voice. No one will ever build the time machine!
Youre mad! exclaimed Lucas, but the voice was gone. Suddenly, a thunderous boom shook the building. The ceiling began to collapse. Blaine ran to the door and tried to open it.
Its locked!
Blast! exclaimed Lucas. A steel girder fell to the floor behind him. Aaugh!
What do we do? wailed Blaine.
Lucas ran to the time machine. Tom! Help me drag this to the door!
Wh-
Just do it!
Blaine and Lucas shoved the contraption as close to the locked door as possible. Lucas ran to his computer. If I can increase the flow of energy... There we go! Tom! Get back! He pressed a single key- and the time machine exploded. When he looked up, both the time machine and the door were gone, and Blaine was huddled in a corner, bruised but alive. At first he thought everything was okay, but a falling chunk of plaster brought him to his senses. He grabbed the blueprints off the table next to him, stuffed them in his pocket, then ran over to Blaine and grabbed his arm.
Come on, lets go! he yelled.
They ran through the corridors of the lab, debris falling around them. The time machines explosion had quickened the buildings collapse. After what seemed like forever, they made it to the lobby. They ran to the main entrance.
The door wont open! yelled Blaine frantically.
Rrrgh, not again! growled Lucas. He looked around. There! he said, pointing to a damaged console. That must be the problem! We need to fix it! Tom, youre good at that kind of thing, so you work on that while I reconnect the circuits to the door!
Gotcha said Blaine, and he hurried over to the panel.
Lucas dodged a falling beam and ran to the power cables. He put on a pair of rubber gloves from his lab coat pocket, quickly identified which cables were unnecessary, and removed them from the wall. He fixed one end of that cable to the corresponding end of the damaged one, and was about to attach the other end when he was tacked.
I wont let you leave with those blueprints! howled Travis Carson.
Travis, Ive read some books about time machines, too! said Lucas. And those time machines created good changes! Thats what my machines gonna do! And he threw an uppercut to Carsons chin. Carson staggered back, stunned. Lucas leapt to the power cables and attached the remaining end.
Its fixed! cried Blaine. He flipped the switch on the console and the door slid open. Carson recovered and dove at the two men as they raced for the door... And then let out a cry as he was crushed by a falling beam.
Lucas and Blaine ran into the night as the lab collapsed behind them.
