'Spontaneous combustion' in hospitals is usually the result of an electrical discharge, or heating due to an electrical field. People with breathing difficulties are often placed in oxygen-rich chambers, and static buildup on their clothing, skin and blankets can sometimes create a large enough spark to start a fire. In scans that use strong magnetic or electrical fields, if a cord is touching the patient's arm, for example, the field can induce a current around the the arm and wire (which form a closed circuit). Because of the the high resistance of the inulating materials, and the resistance of the person's flesh, much of the electrical energy is converted to heat and the patient can suffer severe burns. Or, indeed, catch fire. In fact, there are many, many instances of 'spontaneous' combustion which can be attributed to a discharge of static electricity.
