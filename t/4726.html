#97989 12 2005-09-06T14:51:03Z Guest
oh, i see the problem -- you're trying to DRAW the results!

if you think about it, the concept of "translating a vector" is meaningless since vectors are just a direction and a length; points and linesegs have start/end positions.. for instance, the dotprod of two vectors will stay the same no matter how you "translate" the vectors.

anyway.. as we learnt when making the tutorials, it's actually much harder to draw the results than to calculate them, for the exact reason you found: the results of the collision detection are a vector, and it's easy to move something by a vector.. but it's hard to automatically calculate where to draw the vector.

i'd say don't worry about it -- just draw it anywhere nearby so that you can see if the length and direction are correct; that's the important part after all. making things look "pretty" like in the tutorials is probably a waste of time. i think when we were writing the tile collision stuff we just had all the vectors start at the centerpoint of the object.

to answer your other question, yes, there _are_ in fact lots of hacky conditionals/etc. in order to get it lined up correctly, but only in the source for the tutorial _diagrams_, and not the real source. so you're probably on the right track.

and to answer your final question: the way we did the diagrams is horribly inefficient, basically we just handled each case seperately (each different axis) since we couldn't find a single formula that would work for every case. basically it's a big mess. but the good news is that you probably don't need to draw the solution nicely.. unless you're making a diagram yourself.

raigan
p.s - this is exactly the reason why we didn't release the source for the diagrams -- it's just way too confusing and useless anyway, since over 1/2 of the calculations have nothing to do with detecting the collision, and are just for getting everything positioned nicely. i suppose we could release the source, but.. i'm afraid it would just confuse people even more.
