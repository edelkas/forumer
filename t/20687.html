#390959 46 2007-12-19T03:02:19Z iangb
In that thread, my argument that got shot down is now strangely relevant again, because we are starting with the assumption that free will exists in life. To recap it here:

Global premise 0: God exists, is omnibenevolent and omnipotent (but constrained, for the moment, by logic and free will).
Global premise 1: Heaven exists, and is a perfect place free from suffering.
Global premise 2: Free will exists in (at least) life
Argument 1: Free will is given by God because He loves us. Therefore free will is a good thing - life with free will is preferable to life without free will. Therefore free will exists in heaven, as life in heaven without free will is less preferable to life with free will, and heaven is perfect.
Argument 2: Free will directly causes suffering. This is proved by contradiction: if it did not, then God could use his omnipotency to ensure everyone lived suffering-free lives without breaking His constraint under the soverignty of free will. Therefore there is no free will in heaven, as heaven is free from suffering.
Obviously, arguments one and two are contradictory. This means (assuming both arguments are watertight) that one or more of the three premises is incorrect. Looking at them, in reverse order:
2. Heaven does not exist. This causes problems in that Heaven is one of the core beleifs of Christianity.
1. Free will does not exist in life. This raises the old problem of suffering in life vs an omnibenevolent god.
0. God does not exist. Crap.

The other solution, of course, is that God is omnipotent about the whole thing: He may agree to be bound by free will and allow free will in heaven, but as He is not constrained by logic then He can solve all problems without breaking his self-imposed boundaries, even though this is logically impossible. However, there are related issues with this: as this solution is arrived at through nothing but logic, but it concerns an illogical God and is therefore inherantly flawed. (see the 'Logic and ineffability' thread).
