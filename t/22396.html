Beyond the Myth of ADD/ADHD

........When a man in a lab coat tells you something is wrong with you, to what extent do you believe him? A rapidly spreading disorder, Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder, has recently swept the nation. ADD (Or ADHD, depending on the diagnosis) is usually diagnosed in young children and teens, but has even grown to young adults, who have trouble paying attention and keeping still. Recently, the rate of diagnosis has exploded, causing many young children to be on prescription drugs and creating an addiction to these substances to retain normal behaviors. It has been debated, however, whether or not ADD/ADHD is a problem.

........There are a good number of people who feel that ADD/ADHD is a legitimate disorder. In this day and age, it is very important for the youth to learn all of the information mankind has uncovered. ...without appropriate identification and treatment, AD/HD can have serious consequences. These consequences may include school failure... (CHADD, 1) ADD/ADHD and its effects can hinder learning in children if nothing is done to help. Without a good start, it will be hard to become successful in the futures of those affected by ADD/ADHD. Recognizing and treating those whom have ADD/ADHD will do a great deal to help them in their education, their career, and their life in general.

........ADD/ADHD can also have effects on those who have already completed their education, as it has been more and more diagnosed in young adults. Adults with ADHD may have difficulty following directions, remembering information, concentrating, organizing tasks or completing work within time limits. (WebMD 1) Trouble paying attention and completing tasks in adulthood can lead to serious consequences in jobs and life. If ADD/ADHD is such a big problem, then something needs to be done to help these people. It is important for everyone, including those with disorders, to have a chance to learn and be successful.

........Hyperactivity and lack of attention aren't the only problems ADD/ADHD has towards learning. About 20% to 25% of children with ADHD meet criteria for a learning disorder... (NCBI.com 1) With up to one fourth of those affected by ADD/ADHD also suffering from a learning disorder, it is important to help the patient learn as easily as possible to help ensure a productive future. If, at the very least, it is possible to assist with ADD/ADHD, then the person will be more able to cope with any learning disorders they may have. This way, they have the greatest chance at becoming successful rather than falling behind their peers.

........It is good to be concerned about youth, be it teenager or young adult. They are the future of our society and, if unable to learn to lead and teach, will be unable to further the human race. If ADD/ADHD really is a concern, and this big of one, then steps need to be taken to help children learn effectively. It is also important for the young employees of the world to be able to handle situations properly and efficiently in the world of business. Regardless of the age, a person needs to be able to handle learning and life situations properly, and without interference from a disorder.

........There is another side to all this, however. A great number of children have been diagnosed and are taking drugs for ADD/ADHD, and these numbers are still growing. In a survey taken, out of 42 participants, 10 (~24%) were diagnosed with ADD/ADHD. (Metanet.com 1) This is a large percentage and, although possibly inflated due to participant quantity, is quite a big number of people affected by ADD/ADHD within such a small group. It does not seem probable that so many people, so recently, would be diagnosed with this disorder. It is a lot of drugs being passed around.

........Drugs such as Adderall and Ritalin are the most popular method for treating ADD/ADHD, but it is not particularly healthy for our children to be exposed to this many chemicals. Children who used central nervous system stimulants were 20 percent more likely to visit an emergency clinic or doctors office with cardiac-related symptoms... (news.ufl.edu 1) These drugs, when taken regularly, can cause heart problems along with additional complications. It is not necessary to risk the health of those with ADD/ADHD for a disorder that is not a huge problem to begin with.

........The biggest reason why ADD/ADHD has become such a huge issue is because any form of lack of attention, hyperactivity, or even trouble sitting still is viewed by a lot of people as the direct result of a mental disorder. The way a child is considered to be victim to a disorder relies heavily on the way society views certain behavior, and this leads to a constant changing in how doctors and general people think of ADD/ADHD. (Danforth, 167-190) If a parent believes their child to have ADD/ADHD because of their behavior, then they will take action to get medication for the issue. What actually qualifies as ADD/ADHD may be a different set of symptoms than what the parents and doctors are immediately witnessing. If what is considered to be this disorder differs from place to place, time to time, and person to person, then it is not practical to provide dangerous medication to such a large number of patients.

........Too many kids are being diagnosed with something that does not require the amount of treatment it receives. Too many drugs being handed to our kids means there is a lot of promotion towards drug abuse (Both for those who are prescribed the drugs and those who happen to get their hands on it), as well as having a detrimental affect on those who take the drug. It is also a problem that there is such a large number of people have been diagnosed so recently in such a large age group. Something does not seem right here.

........But there does seem to be a practical solution. It is safe to assume that some people do, in fact, have issues with paying attention and maintaining control of themselves, but not such an incredibly large amount. Kids will be kids, and with age and maturation, they will calm down and be able to lead productive, successful lives. There is no need for so many kids running around with drugs to calm them, nor are these drugs safe in the first place. As a solution, there needs to be more research done to potential ADD/ADHD patients to see if they actually qualify to more refined and specific requirements, and less reliance on drugs as the cure. With psychiatric research being done directly to those who do or may have ADD/ADHD, doctors can be more sure if there really is a problem and can also learn both more about ADD/ADHD and what treatment, be it drugs or therapy or both, is truly necessary. Those who do have a problem can get the proper, safe treatment for their disorder and those who only have a little trouble paying attention can learn to cope with their issues or work, either by themselves or with help from others (specifically parents), to become successful in schooling.

........It takes a lot of work to see if there really is an issue with a child's learning, but it is better to be safe than sorry there is no need for a large amount of the population to suffer from ADD/ADHD if they don't actually have it. It is very possible for a small portion of the population to have trouble learning because of ADD/ADHD, and it is important to cater to the needs of this group. However, there is no need for medication and treatment for a completely normal behavior of our youth. Brttrx, when asked about having been diagnosed with ADD/ADHD, had this to say:
I was told I had ADHD when I had bad grades and I got some Adderall for it but I never took it unless my mom made sure I did. It made me lose my appetite and made me have bad anxiety, and barely helped me focus. I found out that I should just do my school work and my doctor told me I grew out of it and they stopped giving me prescription medicine. I gained 20 pounds within a week of getting off them.

Ask yourself this: Is it all really necessary?
