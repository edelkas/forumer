#94808 17 2005-08-28T03:05:49Z brickman
Alright, after playing Maple Story, I have officially decided that nothing can be worth the time it takes to get to level 10 in order to get an actual class, with actual abilities who can actually DO something interesting/that makes the game interesting. I can deal with a half hour or even a full hour of "training" before the real game starts just because its hard to find ones where it isn't there, but this is ridiculous. However, it took me until I had reached level ten to realize it wasn't worth it, so I could continue anyways. But when I think about it, the lack of attention to fun factor displayed there is very ominous and so I think I'll leave.
With that, I'm going to make the top ten list of things NOT to do in an rpg:
1) Make the player have to level up to get a class. I don't care if you do a locked in class system or picking and choosing skills. I don't care if there's a ten minute training area to get you used to the controls, especially a skippable one. But when you require the player to reach level x before leaving the starting island and choosing a skill (ironically both games in this thread do this), and x is more than 2, you're basically weeding out any players who actually care about playing a game. Which leaves the powergamers and idiots.
2) Concentrate more on the character appearance than the characters. I'm sorry, but I'm not playing an MMO to design a cool looking mage or play dressup. Quite frankly, if I had to choose between having a dozen different choices for every body part plus choice of colors on all items vs only one 16 bit character sprite per class and having a 10% increase in the number of abilities and SKILL variation within each class, I'll take the second any day.
3) Making us do the same thing repeatedly. Now, I know that a bit of repitition is inherent in the genre. But it doesn't hav to be as much as it usually is. Give us three or four areas to explore at any given level instead of one or two, and don't make us kill the same enemy more than 50 times in the same place. And I don't want to associate the word "grind" with your game.
4) Ignoring the advantages of multiplayer. Also known by me as "lite" multiplayer, this basically means neither requiring nor strongly encouraging cooperation. Every time someone accuses someone else of stealing a kill or item, your game loses points. Players should tag team out of necessity, not yell at anyone who tries to fight the same monster they are because the area's baddies spawn slow or they need to get to the area on the other side of it faster or they simply can't handle them on their own. Party systems that give a minor bonus for kills by party members doesn't cut it. You need to make it so the player NEEDS to and WANTS to have a party, even if they're training. A way to help with this is to make sure that no single character can master every skill, or even every skill allowed by his class, so that he needs someone to fill in his weaknesses.
5) Generally speaking, forget that the focus of a game is to have fun.

I think if all or even a good handful of MMOs followed these rules the genre would be so much better. As it is, I can think of one.
