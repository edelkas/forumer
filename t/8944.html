#164724 6 2006-04-14T15:23:36Z blue_tetris
http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swastika

"The swastika (from Sanskrit) is an equilateral cross with its arms bent at right angles in either left-facing or right-facing direction. The swastika is a holy symbol in Hinduism, Jainism and Buddhism. In the West, it is most widely known and used as a symbol of Nazism. It is traditionally oriented so that a main line is horizontal, though it is occasionally rotated at forty-five degrees. The Hindu version is often decorated with a dot in each quadrant."

Swastikas (even when called swastikas) represent many more things than just the Nazi party. All those Eastern religions used them far before Hitler and his cool cats picked up on the idea. Even Native Americans use the symbol frequently in their designs. The shape appears in Icelandic ancient literature bearing the name Thorshamar (Thor's Hammer). It has even been called the tetraskelion, in the same genre as triskelion. There's even an "evil" version of the symbol called a sauwastika, which is left-facing instead of right-facing. Contrary to what you may have heard, the Nazi party used the ordinary right-facing swastika, which was not the "evil" sauwastika.

On maps in Taipei, the swastika is used to indicate a temple (with a cross being used to indicate a Christian church). Rudyard Kipling used the symbol as his personal insignia prior to WWII. Swastika, Ontario is a fine place to live, even--they refused to change their name when the symbol became co-opted in the Western eye. Indian, Polish, Russian, and Swedish military and social groups used the symbol, also. The Raelian philosophy uses a unique symbol that incorporates the swastika with a star of David; the symbol has nothing to do with Nazism.

*takes a breath*

So, as you can see, that's not a "Nazi sign". It's a swastika. And it has other purposes. It's a very common symbol and it's often difficult to avoid when designing things. It's quite possible that this map maker ended up with this common shape in his map without aiming to do so. Then, when it came time to think up a name, all he could think to do was disclaim the symbol he used (which he could have done in the description).

Also, technically, the swastika in that guy's map is facing the wrong way to be associated with Nazism. Any objections to this map would be purely subjective, since it doesn't confide in Nazism or present any content that directs hate towards a specific group. All it does is use a certain symbol. A certain common symbol that carries a stigma that would prevent most people from using it.
