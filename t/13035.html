#11624 1 2006-10-08T03:32:19Z Black Sheep Boy
So here it is, my definitive, semi- accurate blow- by- blow account of last night's Panic! At The Disco Concert. Read it someday, when you're really bored. Any replies of an equal or greater length will recieve cookies.

12.00 Arrive to line up at 12. Notice that there is very little cue, but what cue there is is made of harcore, black- clad scenesters. And nothing but. Festival Hall is surrounded by them, with eyeliner and black fingernails and black jackets over black t-shirts over black pants on people with black hair and sometimes black fedoras and black shoes. I begin to wish I had worn something other than my stylish, abstract light- blue- on white shirt.

Stand around. Realise we have another 6 hours untill the doors open. Leave.

12.30 Spot a Krispy Kreme store near Spencer Street Station, still celebrating its opening. We join in the celebration with some overpriced, kinda average doughnuts. Take some helium baloons and cheap party hats anyway, and wear them for a bit until we decide that Krispy Kreme is actually pretty overrated, so throw them out.

1.00 Enter Minotaurs, the shop which calls each and every nerd to it in Melbourne. No true nerd needs directions. The gentle call of Comics, DVDs, Script Books, and huge amounts of Buffy merchandise call stongly enough for even foreign nerds in Melbourne to find it without even knowing it exists. It calls to the heart of every person with no life.
Once inside, Sam salivates infront of Little Briton scripts (Actually, that's a lie. I say that to make myself feel better about dragging him in there, in the Joss Whedon section, where I spent way too much time. There WERE Little Britain scripts nearby, but I reckon Sam doesn't get as enthused by script books as I do.), whilst I while away the time staring longingly at the original sheet music and script for Joss Whedon's "Once More With Feeling". Sam absentmindedly lets go of the balloon, which floats to the ceiling and pops, loudly, attracting the attention of the store to the two of us, standing infront of shelves of Buffy merchandise. We retreat from the store in shame, but not before buying a comic with no plot, no grammar, and very little grammar for a measly $4, which we laugh at for a long time. "Professor Your An Idiot, Do You Now That?". It was classic.

1.30 Enter Dirt Cheap CDs out of boredom. I buy "Acoustic 05" compilation, desperate to hear the acoustic versions of songs from my beloved Snow Patrol, Bloc Party, Embrace, Black Rebel Motorcycle Bluc, Jose Gonzales, Oasis, Damien Rice, and Rufus Wainwright. Sam buys the Spongebob Squarepants Soundtrack on a whim- which I fully endorse, if only because it has a track by The Shins on it. It's only much, much later that I realise that my CD does not, as the cover suggests, contain many acoustic versions of songs I love. It just contains the album versions of songs which are heavily acoustic guitar based.

2.00 Grab some sushi, buy some cheap Jumbo cards at the Reject Shop for $2, Sam gives directions to Cat and Jo. Start heading back to Festival Hall, but via some op- shops, where I search for white pants to make myself even more conspicious once in the mosh pit. Shame they were closed. I would have worn them.

3.00 Line up, and sit down with the scenesters, and play cards. A bored, kinda hot random (Lucy) asks to join. Later on, I get asked my phone number by her, and start feeling very manly, if a little crap at cards.

4.00 Cloak my bag for $2, along with a smaller bag inside, belonging to the broke Lucy. FIGHT THE POWER. FIGHT THE SYSTEM.
While in the cloaking cue, I make friends with a scene kid. And feel scene by association. I also meet a girl who begins to call me "The Tall Guy", and who later asks me for an e-mail adress, as does her friend. I feel yet more masculine and manly. It's kinda awesome.
I jokingly call her "The Observant Girl" after she bursts out with "Hey, you're The Tall Guy!", then apologise for paying her out, then apologise for apologising, and run away. Our short- lived romance looks, at this moment, impossible.

5.00 Jo arrives. A person stands up infront of the doors to tell us that the doors won't open yet, so we don't have to jostle in line or get to our feet yet. So everybody gets to their feet and jostles in something which could be called a line, if you have a pretty liberal definition of one.

5.30 Somebody who's been lining up for a while passes out. Jo, Sam and I emerge from our place in the line to offer water and sugar- filled treats. Her friends just stand nearby, blowing smoke into her face. When she's feeling slightly better, we try to return to the line which we edged less than a metre out of. The gap has closed, by people wanting to get in ahead of the stupid people who tried to help a young girl who passed out.

6.00 Doors open. I wait around outside for Cat to arrive while Sam and Jo go in, wishing I hadn't lined up for so long if I'm going to enter the building that late in the que anyway.

6.20 Some girl sees a tech guy on stage, and the crowd screams and surges. Everybody is hyperventilating and trying to get oxygen from the mush of people. Some fail. 40 minutes before the first support act, a few people are carried out, unconscious or merely kinda out of it, one or two trampled on, to the paramedics.

Mob Mentality is an extremely stupid thing. People rush at the stage, everybody's centre of gravity goes screwy, then we fall back again. And try again. At one point I go down, but scramble to my feet again before the gap above me closes entirely. Man are people stupid, struggling so to get nearer to the empty stage and tech guys, not realising that not actually achieving anything the first 50 times means that they'll probably not achieve anything the 51st.

7.00 Something with Numbers stand onstage and do their thing. And do it really badly. The heavy punk/ pop doesn't fit in at all with what one would expect at a Panic! At The Disco concert. People love it anyway, and more people get trampled, pass out, and carried out over the front of the barrier to the paramedics. Nobody cares that the band is crap. Jo and I retreat after the act finished from the moshy-est part of the mosh pit, and grab some water- $4 for bottles the same size as the ones we got at The Reject Shop for 50 cents. All of us (Here meaning ME) have headaches, are thirsty as hell, and feel dizzy.

During the interval between Numbers and Kiss Chasey, I meet up with Observant Girl and Friend again, who ask me first for my myspace (which I don't have). I feel kinda awesome.

8.20 Kiss Chasey step on stage. They're as bad as we feared. I wish we had screamed "We Love Your, Kiss Tackle!!!" after one of the songs, even if we would have gotten mauled by little kiddy scenesters. Sam and Jo head back into the mosh. People have been getting kinda abusive at me whenever I'm anywhere except for the back, because I'm the tallest guy there. I stand around near the drinks booth, feeling kinda sorry for myself and trying to enjoy a truly terrible band. In the next interim, I meet with Sam, Jo and Cat again. We buy more water. People are constantly throwing bottles around, and glow sticks on stage, and it's kinda bizarre.

9.00 Panic! At The Disco finally step on stage. This is it. The high point of the night. I just find myself wishing I actually really liked them to any large extent. I get seperate from the other three again, and hang around near the back, where I have a very good view over the sea of adolescents. Turns out that Panic! Didn't bring their semi- victorian set with them. Nor their victorian/ modern hybrid kinda burlesque costumes. Nor did they do any of the stage show they do in the states, with the dance routines and the like.
(Damn you, Jo, for telling me exactly what I was missing.)
The lead singer (Whoever he is) engages with the audience, frequently, with original lines like "Wow! You're the BEST audience we've ever had!" and "Wow, does Melbourne have some sexy ladies!" and "Melbourne is the BEST city we've ever been to!" and many other variations on the theme.
Panic! were pretty competent live, which was a welcome change to Kiss Chasey and Something With Numbers. They were tight, and sounded good- even if it wasn't much more exciting than listening to their album, except a lot louder and with large amounts of teenybopperish girls singing along and squeling.
Since Panic! only have 13 songs, what they play is- on the whole- very, very predictable. There is one up side, though, to only having 13 songs- they were forced to play 2 covers to fill in time. They managed a great cover of the Smashing Pumpkin's "Tonight Tonight", which actually worked really well- if only because they had strings in the band, and Radiohead's Karma Police. Which was the highlight of the night. Somehow, Panic! At The Disco managed an absolutely amazing cover of a Radiohead song. It was bizarre. And as far as I could tell, not a single person in the audience, save myself and Jo, knew who Radiohead where, or the lyrics to the song. It was brilliant. And they performed it far better than anything else they performed that night. And nobody had a clue what it was they were singing.

Sam, who hadn't drank untill that moment, kept scabbing some from everyone he saw with water bottles. As did everyone. Everybody who shared is probably going to end up with some wierd, obscure contagious disease. Which would amuse me.

At any given moment, there were a handful of people being dragged unconcious out of the pit. Some of whom still weren't awake when I finally exited the building, kinda dazed and out of it and slightly tramped on.

If nothing else, though, the lighting was really good. And that's something.

10.00 Exit. Find Lucy, return large amounts of bags from within mine from the cloakroom. Get yelled at by security. Decide to head home with Jo instead of staying over at Sam's place. Security yell at Jo and I some more. We eventually leave, drink large amounts of soft drink and fries, and I get dropped off at my house near midnight, staggering around drunkenly.

Realise I still have large amounts of stuff in my bag which belong to Sam. Before going to bed, shower, brush my teeth, shave, scrub, change clothes, eat, drink, use the toilet, and try to purge out as much of the night's concert from my system as I can.

It was either an extremely crap night, or an absolutely amazing one. I'm still not which. But either way, it had good lighting. And that's worth something.
