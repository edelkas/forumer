I found an interesting rpg recently called The Spirit Engine, and have been playing it a lot for the past few days. In it, you choose three heros from a cast of nine (with riflemen, priests and mages) and set out on a quest guided (if you can call it guiding) by a mute, defenseless fairy/spirit. There's an intricate, although not necessarily too groundbreaking, plot, and te characters are developed quite a bit (but I think they took shortcuts by making the guys in each row have a set "personality" so that they only had to make different dialogue for each character in about half of the cutscenes). But the main thing here is the combat. It uses real time combat where you have to quickly rotate the order of and determine the next attack of each character, with no fewer than 18 attacks for each character type-but you have to specialize eventually among three or four skills in order to stand a fighting chance, and you have to make sure you have attacks with multiple types for each one or else you'll be buried once you start reaching battles involving monsters which may have three different sets of resistances, immunities and (later on) weaknesses. Its a lot deeper than it looks at first since the various attacks have different speeds and damages and other inherent advantages (for instance, speed shot and chain magic get royally screwed by anyone with a partial resistance but are more effective against those with weaknesses and a certain boss) and each character has two skills to which he has a bonus and two with which he has a penalty (for instance, my priest is the best at partyheal and bless spells but is penalized with the hand of god and holy bolt spells). But it lets you play around and learn the system without fear of screwing yourself for later because there's a guy halfway through who lets you reassign your skill points. And. . . you know what? Talking about it doesn't really give you an idea of how well the thing plays. If you're looking for a new game, try it, and if you're not having fun by the end of the second world (the system stays pretty basic on world one, with only the last boss on the entire first island having a resistance) then go look for something else. But I think you will enjoy it.

Oh, and if you want a hint for your starting party, the three I picked seem to be good: peter (the fighter on the first row), zephyr (the priest on row 2) and eisford (the mage on the last row). The strengths of the first two are exactly where I would have expected and wanted strengths to be for that class (although if you think of fighters as meatsheilds you may think differently) and the same goes for one (but sadly not both) of the mage's. You won't be disappointed (until you get to the point where you're stuck, and you go to the forums to search for the boss you're on and everybody assumes that your priest has 10 levels of hand of the gods which is pathetically slow for zephyr and you never leveled up because you assumed you didn't want to invest points in a skill that was one of his weaknesses)
