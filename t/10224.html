Joke 1:
When Timmy was four years old, he saw his father play ping pong for the first time, and he found great joy in playing with the little white ball. When it came time for his fifth birthday, the only thing Timmy asked for was a ping pong ball. When it came time to open his presents, Timmy rushed through them all until he found the ping pong ball, with which he immediately ran up into his room. His parents didn't want to disturb Timmy's delight, so they let him play. They heard the periodic bouncing of the ball on the hardwood floor for two hours, and then it stopped. Timmy walked downstairs out of his room and began to play with the other presents. He didn't respond when he was asked about the ping pong ball, and he was disinterested in playing with it again. His mother couldn't find it when she cleaned his room.
For his sixth birthday, out of the blue, Timmy asked for two ping pong balls. His parents thought that this was a little odd, but since he seemed to enjoy the ping pong ball so much last year, they obliged. Timmy again raced through his presents and ignored most of them, and immediately ran upstairs with his ping pong balls when he unwrapped them. Timmy's parents heard bouncing and ecstatic laughter for a few hours, after which the noise suddenly stopped, and Timmy came downstairs completely serene and didn't mention the ping pong balls. His mother snuck upstairs when Timmy was distracted, and she couldn't find the ping pong balls anywhere. When she brought them up casually, Timmy didn't answer.
On his seventh birthday, Timmy asked for five ping pong balls. Just like last year, Timmy ran upstairs with his ping pong balls, and there was bouncing and giggling for a few hours, after which Timmy came down entirely disinterested in ping pong balls. Even when she searched outside of his window in the front yard, Timmy's mother couldn't find any trace of the ping pong balls.
On his eighth birthday, Timmy's parents decided to sneak into Timmy's room when he was playing with them to see what he was doing. When they opened the door, Timmy was happily bouncing his ten ping pong balls on the floor, walls, and ceiling, and having a grand old time. But when he saw his parents come in, Timmy was extremely upset. He demanded that they leave immediately and not bother him. They heard the bouncing noises gradually increase to their original frequency, and more laughter. They didn't bother him after that. Timmy was completely indifferent when he came down from his room after the bouncing noises suddenly stopped.
This continued for a year short of a dozen, with Timmy asking for more and more ping pong balls with each coming year. Every time, he went to his room and his parents would hear the hundreds of ping pong balls he got bouncing around the room. And every time, they would suddenly come to a halt, and a few seconds later Timmy would walk out of his room and refuse to talk about them.
On his 18th birthday, Timmy had received more than ten thousand ping pong balls. He went immediately up to his room to play with them, and his parents gave each other a knowing look. When four hours had passed, the droning noise of thousands of bouncing ping pong balls came to a sudden stop. Timmy jogged downstairs holding his car keys and putting on his jacket and told his parents that he'd be going to a friend's house. When he left, his parents ran up to his room as they did every year, but they still couldn't find a single ping pong ball.
Later that night, Timmy's parents received a phone call from the police. The police told them that there had been an accident, that Timmy was involved, and that he was barely clinging to his life at the scene. Timmy's parents got to the scene of the accident as soon as they could and found Timmy lying on a stretcher surrounded by paramedics. The paramedics let his parents talk to him.
After exchanging some very heartfelt words, Timmy's father couldn't resist.
"Say, Timmy, your mother and I have been curious about something for the last thirteen years," he said. "You know those ping pong balls you always ask for? What do you do with them when you're done playing with them?"
Timmy got a smirk on his face and looked up at his father. His heart rate was increasing rapidly.
"The... the ping pong balls?" he wheezed. "Oh, I... I..."
Timmy's heart rate suddenly went flat, and he died.

Joke 2:
A different, unrelated Timmy went to the circus for the first time when he was seven years old. His father knew that at one point in the show the clowns would come up and give all of the children in the aisle seats balloons. Timmy was very entertained at this when he heard it announced and saw the clowns coming up the aisles with balloons.
When the clown with Timmy's balloon reached his seat, he knelt down and started tying the balloon string around Timmy's wrist. "Hello, little boy," the clown said very politely. "What's your name?"
"Timmy!"
The clown frowned at him and shook his head. "No, it's not," the clown said slowly. "It's Asshole."
Timmy was very distraught by this. He convinced his father that he wanted to leave the show early.
On the same day the next year, Timmy wanted to see the circus again. His father humored him. Timmy sought out the same seat he had last year, determined that this time everything would be just right. The same clown came up to his seat in the aisle. Timmy wondered if the clown would remember him.
"Hello, little boy! What's your name?"
"Timmy!"
Again, the clown shook his head and said in that same polite voice, "no it's not. It's Asshole."
This time, Timmy stayed for the whole show. He wasn't going to let a stupid clown ruin his mood.
But over the next year, Timmy became obsessed with that stupid clown. If only he would say what he was supposed to say, Timmy's circus experience would be complete. He just wanted one correctly-performed routine to set things right. So on the same month and day in the next year, he went again. At nine years old, Timmy was very set on making things right. He sat in the very same seat as the year before, and the year before that. The very same clown came up to him.
"Hello, little boy! What's your name?"
"My name is Timmy," he declared.
But Timmy's expectant gaze turned into a glare when the clown shook his head again. "No, it's not. It's Asshole."
Timmy didn't even bother to stay through the end of the show.
When he was ten years old, on the same day of the same month of the year, Timmy was still obsessed with having a complete circus-going experience. He decided that it wasn't important that that clown said his name correctly. He would be content if any clown did so.
So he chose a completely different aisle seat, on the other side of the stage.
But the clown remembered him, and sought him out. Again Timmy found himself staring in the face of that bitter old clown with the forced smile and the unmistakable glint of recognition in his eye.
"What's your name, little boy," the clown said more forcefully than the last three times.
"Timmy," Timmy challenged back.
"No, it's not." The clown tilted his head and said through the gritted teeth of a malevolent smile. "It's Asshole."
For the next two years, no matter which seat he took, the same goddamned clown came up to his seat and called him an asshole. Timmy saw the other children with different clowns, and they were all happy because the clowns were nice.
After that, Timmy gave up on hiding from the clown. He used the same seat he had when he came for the first time. And every single time, the same clown came up to him and asked him the same question.
"What's your name, little boy?"
"Timmy," Timmy sighed.
"No, it's not. It's Asshole."
Timmy was intent upon getting the clown to be nice. He had never had a complete experience from the circus. Not only did he want the clown to say his name, he wanted an appology from the clown, too. He decided to demand his appology when he was fourteen.
"What's your name, little boy," asked the clown as he tied the balloon around Timmy's wrist.
"I want an appology."
"What's your name, little boy," he asked more forcefully.
"Say you're sorry."
"What's your name, little boy," the clown said through his teeth.
Timmy gave up. "Timmy. My name is Timmy."
The clown's attitude immediately shifted back to his artificially gleeful clown mood. "No, it's not," he said with a smile. "It's Asshole."
Timmy didn't give up. He kept coming back to the circus every year, and every year the clown would tie a balloon to Timmy's wrist, ask his name, and then call him an asshole.
But when he was 18, Timmy decided to get his revenge. From the moment he stepped out into the evening air and tore the balloon from his wrist the previous year, he started coming up with dozens of ways to get back at the clown. When he walked into the tent, 18 years old, he was set on his master plan. He sat down his usual seat and watched the show. Forty-four minutes into the show, just as Timmy had painstakingly calculated, the clowns started coming up the aisles. Timmy's heart was pounding as he saw his clown grinning malevolently at him as he climbed the stairs.
When he arrived at Timmy's seat, the clown started to tie the balloon around Timmy's wrist.
"What's your name, little boy?"
"Tim," Timmy replied confidently.
"No, it's not." Timmy was gripping his seat in anticipation for those last two words. "It's Asshole."
As soon as those words came out of the clown's mouth, Timmy launched into action. He leapt to his feet in his row, raised his middle fingers, and yelled, "fuck you, clown!"
