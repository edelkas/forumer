#116807 2 2005-11-21T15:33:36Z coffeefreak
I don't have a tutorial, but here are some pieces of advice:

1. Don't make too short DDAs. Nobody likes them.
2. Make sure it has many close calls. At least I think they're cool
3. Put in some more objects than just launch pads. A good DDS has at least some enemies, tiles, trap doors, normal doors, gold. AND the some oh-so-famous launch pads.
4. An impressive DDS also uses some game glitches and bugs, for example - you can use gold delay and/or thwump launches.
5. As much as people hate too simple DDAs, it shouldn't have too many objects. If it has, prepare to see some serious lag.

#116831 3 2005-11-21T19:52:33Z Ixi
One of my friends who's making a DDA is trying to stay away from glitches (gold delay, etc) and I agree with him. I think it's a little more impressive if you can make a DDA with standard game mechanics. Just an opinion, though.

Close calls are definitely good.

As for making DDAs, I suggest doing them a step at a time. Start from the very beginning, make something happen (usually this involves something like sliding down a slope or falling onto a launchpad), and see where that goes. Add an enemy and place objects to make the ninja avoid it, put more objects where he lands, etc. And keep testing.

Speaking of which, a "fast forward" would be very nice for testing DDAs. Has there been any discussion on such a feature?

#116930 5 2005-11-22T02:30:37Z nevermore
I'm going to give you the one rule that will make your DDA days much easier.

Place your enemies before you place your objects!

This means decide on what the tiles will be like (whether a few tiles in an open arena, no tiles at all, or a designed tileset), place your enemies (all of them) place the ninja, and place the objects. That's it.
