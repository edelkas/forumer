The best fit curve for that data according to this tool (plotting pixels vs. frames) is y = -0.06719496107154829 + 0.1245478033597809t + 0.06821845481550066t. Acceleration would be the second derivative of this position function, which turns out to be about a = 0.1364 pixels/frame/frame. Edit: Although, that best fit curve has initial position and initial time values, which realistically should be 0... hmm. It is pretty close though, over short times. Looking at frame 19, y = (1/2)(a)(t) = (1/2)(0.1346)(19) = 24.2953. Off by 2 pixels. Edit again: Although, using 0.15 as acceleration, you get 27.075 at frame 19. Even closer. I think I'd just use 0.15.

A brief how-to and a just-as-brief explanation of why these mechanisms work would be very much appreciated.

To get the ninja to stop at a oneway pad, you need to make sure that the oneway is not facing upwards and that it is in the right position.

The first part is simple: oneway pads facing right, left, or down will stop the ninja from any speed (assuming he hits it).

The second part is the tricky part. Use FBF mode to get a general sense of where the ninja is two frames after hitting the teleporter, then put oneway pads around there until one stops him.
