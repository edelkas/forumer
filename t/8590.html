I voted "Other", due to a lack of knowledge of immigrantion in America. Things are obviously a bit different in Australia, since we're girt by sea, and I don't know exactly what constitutes illegal immigration in the USA.

That said, I think immigration should be open to everyone as long as:

1. They would not be considered criminals by the standards of the country they're entering, or otherwise be considered a risk to public safety (eg. carrying a virulent disease).
2. They agree to abide by the laws of their new country.
3. The country has sufficient resources and infrastructure to support them.*

*No, not as in "give them benefits". Contrary to the opinions of many, legal immigrants recieve little in the way of special benefits, and aside from that are no more likely to use government money than anyone else - at least in Australia. I mean resources such as food, clean water, land, and so on. Obviously, a country which is short of such basic requirements can't be expected to let everyone in.
