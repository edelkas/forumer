#102349 10 2005-09-20T12:24:56Z blue_tetris
Lucidium's logic is partially correct. However, seeing the rating of a map influences the way someone votes. If the rating someone sees is high, they are likely to vote high (so as not to have to actually evaluate the map thoroughly for themselves) or low (so as to cancel out the high votes), depending on what type of voters they are. This holds true, also, if someone sees that a low value comes up. They may vote with the trend or against it, but are definitely influenced by the trend in relation to their voting style.

I think requiring a lower number of necessary votes would almost definitely create a trend toward the negative, no matter how the first few folks vote, because of how people tend to vote lower once they've seen the decided rating of a map. (Many NUMA members will pick out flaws to find out why something should be less than 4, without noticing the merits that would earn it a 4.) Raising your number of necessary votes would postpone (perhaps indefinitely) the revelation of the current votes. This would lead for a more unbiased rating of the map, because snipers will not think to snipe it (because they will not know if it is doing well or doing poorly).

I like this idea. The poster of the map will have complete control as to when everybody else gets to see the rating of the map.

From vote one, the creator would have a running average of all the votes and, when the creator so chooses (or at 12 votes, if the creator does not choose to reveal at his discretion), this rating would be revealed to the public.
