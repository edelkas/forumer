Welcome, pupils! Since N-Vision is suffering from terminal server problems I've put together a small large map pack which demonstrates many aspects of play, including some basic things for beginners and more advanced tricks for experts. The file is formatted so that you can simply paste a level's entry into that big box on the left of the userlevels menu to watch a replay. Note that it now has "&userdata=" at the top - rename it "userlevels.txt" and put it in the same folder as N (you'll want to move or rename your existing userlevels.txt, of course) and all the levels will be available through the userlevels menu. Alternately, copy everything below the "&userlevels=" line and paste into your normal userlevels.txt. If you want to play an individual level, you should enter debug mode (also known as Ned), copy the whole level entry (You know, $levelname#author#type#leveldata#demodata#), paste it into the "level data" box, click outside the box, and hit L. N will automatically prune out the unneccesary stuff and load the level for you.

For the most part you don't have to do the levels in order. Some levels do require a skill or trick from previous levels - this will be mentioned in the description.

The teaching staff now includes myself, Mr Ngaged, Mr Sketch, Mr thecalcgenius, Mr Zres, Mr jeca, Mr Borealis , Mr Qix, Mr Dark Ball Y-E, Mr Bufar, Mr jtbandes, Mr Scythe33 and Mr Zapkt. Say "good morning," class! Also, thanks to the people who posted levels which were not included, or who made other suggestions. I'm not going to list them all, because I doubt people want to read through dozens and dozens of names, but rest assured that I appreciate your effort.

On the curriculum (Updated 1:26 PM (GMT+10) 21/9/2008 (9/21/2008 for Americans)):

The Inclined Plane
Demonstrates several handy tricks using slopes - the perpendicular jump (release the arrows and jump whilst on a slope), the triple jump (jump onto a slope, then into the wall, then off the wall in quick succession), and using them to avoid splattering all over the pavement (just land on them, preferably moving downhill). The little pit on the left of the exit isn't required, but if you want you can practice a more difficult triple jump - you only get a small slope to aim for, unlike the jump to the exit. You'll need to time the first jump better, and get the timing of the next two jumps more accurate as well.

The Double Reverse Jump (zapkt and Atilla)
Another slope trick. Run towards a slope and jump just before you get there, exactly as though you were doing a triple jump. However, when you hit the slope you should release left/right and press jump, performing a higher perpendicular jump than normal.

Dangerous Tunnelling (Dark Ball Y-E)
Downhill slope jumps. Press and hold right (ie downhill) just before you hit the first slope, and jump pretty much as soon as you land. You should fly through the gap of mines, with a fairly flat trajectory (note: if you jump while running downhill, your trajectory will be lower, and if you jump whilst running uphill, it will be higher). Still holding right, jump from the next slope as soon as you land. Note that you go a little higher because this slope isn't as steep. Then simply jump onto the one-ways and run into the exit - or try a perpendicular jump from the steep slope if you're feeling skilled.

The Slope of Skill (Dark Ball Y-E)
The compainion to Dangerous Tunnelling, this one demonstrates uphill slope jumps. Jump from slope to slope to build up speed, then do a perpendicular jump from the last platform to reach the exit.

Bite the Curb (Sketch)
Just a short level for practicing slope jumps. You'll need to use the perpendicular jump for this one (release left/right and hit jump whilst on a slope). Remember, the more speed you have, the higher you'll go, and hopping from one slope to the other will prevent you losing speed in the corner. Although only one way is shown on the demo, it's possible to reach the exit from either slope. Also, note that if you stand where two slopes meet you'll be able to jump straight up, like you would on a flat surface.

And away!
Your basic launchpad high jump. Just hit the jump button straight after bouncing off the pad while you're against the wall.

Knees of Steel!
Surely you can't survive that... or can you? Clipping the edge of a block can save you from even the hardest of falls, as long as you hit it in the right spot. Incidentally, this trick will occasionally let you skip quite a large portion of some levels.

What momentum?
Strangely, the deceleration from a wall jump doesn't kill you, but hitting the ground does. This means hopping off the wall is a great way to stop fatal falls. It doesn't need as much wall space as sliding, so you can use it when you don't have much to aim for.

Floating in the Breeze
This level shows the odd floating effect when you jump into a roof. Float over the first bunch of mines just by taking a running jump (make sure you hold the jump key all the way). Get past the next two by jumping into the roof just in front of them and releasing the button, allowing you to fall between them. This might take a bit of practice, since you have to be fairly accurate.

Ding! (Sketch)
You walljump much higher than usual if you jump whilst running up a slope or curve, then quickly jump off the wall. This will be used a lot, so it's important to be able to do it consistently. Run up the slope, press jump when you're about halfway up (still holding towards the wall) and walljump.

Feels Like No Grip (Zres)
I'm sure everyone has discovered (much to their annoyance) that walljumping on doors, one-way platforms and thwumps is different to tiles. They lack tiles' "toggle-stick" function, so you must hold towards the object when you jump, or you'll just fall.

Feeling Tipsy
Avoid the explosive roof! In this situation, you will often be able slide down the edge of your platform and walljump across, as demonstrated in the first jump. Otherwise, you'll have to lean over the edge. To do this, move N *very* close to the corner (until one foot is hanging off, basically) and stand still. Don't move towards the edge too fast or you'll fall off. Pressing towards solid ground will make you tip slower, and if you do it while you're almost upright you can walk back onto the platform. Note that this trick only works on tiles, locked doors and trapdoors - you'll just fall straight off one-ways, bounceblocks, and other objects.

Bent
Argh! Those loathsome curved tiles often cause problems for beginners. You get used to them after a while, though. For the convex (outward-curving) ones, you should avoid walking towards the outside - you'll often fall off if you try it. Jump, then start moving sideways if it's at all possible. Jumping as soon as you land can also help you avoid slipping off. Note that if you're struggling to walk up the steep side, jumping will help. With the concave (inward curving) tiles, either hold towards the centre or jump to avoid slipping. If you want to keep moving, they're quite similar to slopes - jumping off one will send you higher, and you can use them to change your direction quickly.

Overhung (Zres)
Jumping into tiny gaps is also irritating. To get into the first gap, hop off the wall (not too high!) and try to fall in. If you hit the corner, a very small (and very quick) jump should get you in. To get out, you should lean over the edge a little, as in Feeling Tipsy, and jump up to the wall. For the second one, jump to the wall beneath the gap (remember, a walljump will stop you sliding off) and just climb into it.

Do the Double (lord_day)
It's possible, though difficult, to jump from a tile or object which is only one block high, and you'll have to do it in a variety of circumstances to finish this level. There's no real trick to it - you just have to hit the jump button fast enough. If you like, you can try "double shifting" - set the the shift key to jump, and press left shift while releasing right shift. This technique can also be used to jump rapidly up chimneys (one-tile wide shafts). Note, however, that it's entirely possible to finish this level without double-shifting.

Much Ado About Thwumps
Thwumps aren't always your enemy. Hitching a ride on them is going to be necessary at some stage. The first bit it fairly easy - just jump onto the thwump when it comes and inch along on top of it. When Thwumps move sideways, they don't take you with them, so you'll have to keep tapping forwards.

The second part is the most difficult. You must run towards the slope. Jump off the slope, then quickly off of the Thwump (like the triple jump), still holding right. If you time your jumps correctly, you can keep jumping off the Thwump all the way up. Two jumps should be enough here, though - and remember, if you release the jump button you'll lose your upward momentum faster.

The third part isn't too hard. Just jump into the back of the Thwump, hold towards it, and keep doing small hops to stay on. The forth is also fairly straightforward - just like the first, but much faster.

Finally, the Thwump jump. If you leap from the top of a thwump while it's moving upwards, the momentum will carry you higher than a normal jump.

More Ado About Thwumps (Borealis + Atilla)
Almost getting squashed by a Thump can give you a speed boost. It's much easier with a jump pad, but a similar trick is possible without one, though your trajectory will be different. To do it, stand underneath the Thwump, near the jump pad, and hold left when you're about to become ninja-pancake. It's not likely to be terribly useful in the main game, but it crops up occasionally in user levels, and if you don't know about it it can sometimes be used to cheat your own levels.

"But how do I get to the exit?!" I hear you cry. Well, Thwumps will also push you through one-way platforms (bounceblocks, too, for that matter). Just lure the Thwump up and stand beneath it. Once you're safely down, waltz over to the Thwumps facing the wall on the right. There's a glitch with Thwumps that makes them move slowly backwards when you approach if you place them one pixel away from halfway through a wall. In this state, they'll go right through walls, but you can still stand and walljump on them. You should set them off when you open the door - get through the curved corridor before they squish you, then wallslide and jump on them to get through the mines.

Thwumper Cars (Qix)
A couple more tricks with thwumps. In the first section, you should run toward the twump and jump so that you hit the roof just before it touches you. You should be able to squeeze past with the right timing. The second is another variant of the thwump-launching shown in the last level - this time you get sqeezed from between two thwumps rather than a thwump and the floor. Just jump in between the thwumps when they're moving backwards to do it - the later you leave it, the faster you'll fly out.

When Blue Octagons Attack (Atilla + Sketch)
This one just shows how drones move, and the seeking and passive variants. You shouldn't have a problem with the first three - just remember they don't go into partially solid tiles (like slopes and half-tiles), and time it right. Get past the next two by luring one down and jumping through the gap (note: they won't chase you into the side corridors because they're made of half-tiles stuck together). Then, reach the exit switch by luring the seeker drones into the trap on the left. Also, note the Zap Drone following the surface of the block in this area - there are several ways drones can move.

Tall People Need Not Apply
You can make it past zap drones by standing on some slopes (the 1, 2 and 3 tiles, namely) and the concave curves - just. The start is simple - just let yourself slide into the little pothole. For the shallowly sloped section, the gold marks the tiles you can use to slip under a drone. It just needs a little timing. For the final part, I recommend running under the drone at the first slope, as depicted in the demo, but it's also possible to slide under on the other side. Steep slopes are quite similar to the 45 degree ones when it comes to drone dodging, except that you have slightly more room and slip off much faster.

Trampoline
Shows four types of bounceblock jump. The first and simplest requires you to push the block down, then jump when it's moving upwards. Note that if you aren't moving sideways (however slightly), the block won't sink properly - just tap back and forth if you wish to stay in the middle. The second (the double jump) starts like the first, but rather than waiting for the block to spring up, you should tap jump at the lowest point, then quickly press and hold. You can go even higher with a triple jump (rap jump twice, then press and hold) if you're fast enough. The third is the sideways double jump. Run towards the block and jump into it. Still holding towards it, tap jump. Quickly press and hold jump and press away from the block. Finally, you'll need to do a sideways triple jump, which will send you much higher. Like with the vertical triple jump, you just need to tap jump twice, quickly, then press and hold.

Touchy (bufar)
Sometimes, being able to push bounceblocks around is dangerous rather than helpful - usually when there's something unpleasant beehind them. In this level, you have to avoid pushing the bounceblocks too far. This means reducing your momentum as much as possible before hitting them (try not to fall onto them from too high if you're coming from above, don't come in too fast from the side) and jumping off as quickly as possible. You can also avoid the mines by standing on the very edge of a bounceblock. This is difficult and unreliable, so I don't reccomend trying to stand still, but landing near the corners can give you a bit more leeway with your jumps.

This level is by bufar, but I edited it slightly to show that you can avoid mines even when they're right inside the bounceblock you're trying to jump off.

Wading Pool (Qix)
Shows how to push bounceblocks out of the way. The first one is the hardest. I reccomend running back and forth between the bounceblocks (remember to jump over the gap or you'll lose speed) until you've got some speed up. Then run into the right bounceblock, and just before it starts pushing you back jump furiously (still holding right). You should be able to squeeze past. Next, head up to the exit switch. You'll need to force your way past by performing rapid walljumps. To get back down, fall on the block from a great height, and keep moving sideways slightly while you're on it. You should fall through (note that you'll sometimes do this by accident in other levels). Since bounceblocks don't always return perfectly to their original position, you may also be able to push the block aside by running along the ground. For the next block, just drop onto it from a decent height and run out. Now, for the final section, there are many close packed blocks. The trick is to try and force yourself into the gaps between them, rather than straight into the blocks. Jumping furiously may also help, but for the thinner barriers timing and good aim will probably be more useful.

Linear Algebra
Lasers are fairly simple. They take a little while to charge up, they fire in a straight line, and they don't change their aim once they've locked on. Note that they have to rest for a brief period after firing in addition to charging, which will give you a little more time to get where you're going. And remember that the best way to get around them is generally to make them fire the wrong way then nip past while they're torching empty space.

Laser Pointer (Qix)
Shows how to get past a laser in a long, narrow shaft. Stand just close enough to one edge for the laser to fire, then slide down the opposite side of the shaft. As soon as it stops, drop from the wall to make it past. Note that most shafts are short enough for you to just drop past.

Target Acquired
A brief introduction to homing missiles, which will detenonate on tiles and doors, but not bounceblocks, one-ways, etc. Remember, they have limited speed and turning radii. Moving quickly will let you avoid them, particularly if you pass them while close - they'll lose time turning around. This is also a good way to guide them into walls and floors. Note that trying to turn around while a missle is near is often a bad idea, since it takes you a while to accelerate, but remember that walljumping can help you change direction quickly if you need to. Also, remember that there are times when it's advantageous to prevent a rocket hitting the walls - sometimes it's easier to avoid a rocket trailing behind you (after all, you can just run away from it...) and sometimes the laucher is in the way and you need to stop it firing (each launcher will only have one rocket in existence at once).

These Puppies Bite (Qix)
Floorguards for all! They're fairly simple - they just charge towards you when you're at the same height and there are no tiles in the way. Note that they can't change direction until they reach the edge of the platform or any non-flat tile - you can sometimes use this to give you a bit of extra time. Note also that they don't particularly care about doors, and will come straight through them. To avoid them, either run very quickly away from them (walljumping or deflection from slopes/curves will give you a bit of momentum if you don't have room to accelerate) or just jump over them - if there's room.

I Need More Space (Zres)
Introduction to Chaingun Drones. Unlike lasers, they'll still aim after they start charging. To avoid being shot, you need to move out of the way *just* before they fire. Or find something to lurk behind. Also, remember that their aim drifts slightly while firing. It's actually possible to slip between bullets, but I don't recommend trying to do it deliberately.

Tiptoe
Yes, you can stand on the points of those sloped tiles. It's quite tricky, however. Try to land with the ninja's centre right above the point, not too much momentum, and jump again as soon as possible so you don't tip off. This one's probably going to take some practice, I'm afraid.

That's Not Normal (thecalcgenius)
Somewhat similar to Tiptoe - you can stand on top of doors, even the ones that open when you approach. Keep the momentum as low as you can, but make sure you go high enough for them to close.

Precision (Sketch)
Beginners often have trouble walljumping off a half tile. Timing and practice are necessary, but many also find it easier to get some speed up and jump towards the tile, as is shown at the beginning of the demo.

Gripping the Ungrippable (Qix)
If you land on the exact top of a curve, it will be considered flat and you won't slip down. In a similar fashion, you can do a normal walljump off the side of a curve. It's fairly straightforward, but requires precise aim. Just treat it as a normal walljump, but make sure you hit jump when you're next to the vertical side of the curve. I find a little sideways momentum makes the timing easier.

Slippery Sides (Sketch)
Shows how to climb a wall with overhangs - this feature is common in jumping puzzles, but the technique is also useful in other maps. When you're trying to jump around an overhang, make sure you don't hold toward the wall when jumping - you'll go higher and wider.

All That Glitters
As requested, how to jump on diamond-shaped blocks. For shallow and middle slopes, don't aim for the centre - it's hard to maintain control on the point. Land on the sides instead. As with circular blocks, hold towards the middle to stay on, or jump quickly. Remember the slope jumps you've learned - it will make it easier to get where you're going, and you can often skip some areas. For the steep slopes, your best bet is to hop from point to point, as in Tiptoe, unless you have a little upslope momentum, which will stop you sliding off too quickly. However, if you don't already have upwards momentum, you won't be able to jump very high from the slopes and you'll fall off very quickly.

Clear Shot (Jeca)
Gauss turrets can take a bit of practice to get used to. Even if they can hit you, they won't fire unless their crosshair is within a certain distance (NOT exactly on you) and they have had time to focus. The crosshair will focus while it's pointed in your general direction, and will lose focus (or focus more slowly) if you're moving very quickly, hidden behind something solid (tiles, doors), or if the angle between you and the turret changes radically. Note that it stays focused after a shot, so if you're still nearby, the turret will fire again quite quickly. Their aim is a little slow, so jumping out of the way at the last minute or falling/running quickly past the crosshair is generally OK. The exception is when moving directly towards or away from the gauss, since even though the shot is aimed at a point behind (or in front of) you, you'll still be in the line of fire. Note also that, since jumping back and forth in a narrow space won't alter the angle between you and the turret much, it will still focus on you. Remember that the crosshair changes slightly and there is a charging noise before firing, which will give you some warning. Eventually, you'll be able to predict when a turret will fire without these signs, which is just as well because you need to watch where you're going.

Oh, and the section with the mines is a little tricky. If you're a beginner, don't be bothered too much if you can't finish it. And you'll probably find the bounceblock jumps from Trampoline useful in the second section.

One-and-a-Half Ways (Ngaged)
This shows how to exploit a problem with the collision detection for one-way platforms. If you walljump while you're just below a downward-facing one-way, you can jump right through it. It takes a bit of practice to get the timing right, though.

I'm A Mime! (Dark Ball Y-E + Atilla)
More collision bugs with one-ways - they don't work properly on some slopes, and and you can collide with them even when it looks like you shouldn't, forming an invisible wall. Putting one-ways inside walls like this is generally considered poor design, precisely because it messes up the collision detection.

In the first corridor, the one-ways in the floor will stop you just walking through. Jump over them to get past. Then, jump up the shaft whilst holding towards the left wall. As long as you keep holding left, you'll get caught on the one-ways inside the wall - allowing you to climb up without hitting the mines. Next, just fall down the other shaft and saunter casually through the one-way on the slope - it won't effect you unless you jump into it. Finally, inch along (and, indeed, right off the edge) of the last one-way so that you can jump into the shaft. This is one of the more irritating bugs - if you try to drop past a one-way, you'll occasionally find yourself standing on it instead.

Porous (jtbandes)
Another bug with one-ways - it's possible to tip through them if they're placed at the edge of a tile, which can be used to cheat some levels. The first one should be fairly easy - just walk towards the gap slowly enough for you to tip over the edge, as in Feeling Tipsy. Repeat to pass the second section. If you want to get the gold on the left, stand on the slope above the entrance, and slide down slowly whilst holding towards it. You should fall through the one-way. For the third section, you can get the gold on the right by jumping so that you just catch the edge of the tile. You should hold towards the tile, of course, and you should also try to keep your momentum low (scraping the underside of the tile is a good way to do this). It's quite difficult, so don't expect to get it first try. The fourth section should be handled the same way as the others, but it's more difficult because of the steep slope. You'll have to jump onto the slope. Mind you don't go too high, or you'll land on the one-way instead (and remember the collision bugs from "I'm a Mime!" - even if it doesn't look like you're going to hit the one-way, you'll still land on it) . The final section is similar to the area with the extra gold in the third - try and land right on the corner of the tile, and keep the upward momentum low. Make sure you come from beneath, not above - you'll just land on the one-way otherwise. Finally, slip through the last two one-ways, just like you did for the gold in the second and fourth sections.

Tunnelling Phenomena
It's possible to squeeze through some small gaps - if you can get the right angle and enough speed. The first one should be easy - just fall onto the slope and run down. For the second, take a running jump into the gap, then hit jump again when you're partway through (jumping upslope will give you more speed, remember). The third is similar, but you're going to have to walljump into it, and since you'll probably have less momentum, you'll need to get your aim and timing better. For the fourth, jump to the top and slightly to the left of the gap, then fall into it moving slightly downhill. It will probably take a few tries to get the angle right, and watching the demo will be helpful at this point. Anyway, the last one should be fairly simple - jump into the gap with a bit of horizontal momentum and just run through.

'Gainst Whom No Lock May Stand (Ngaged)
It's difficult. Very difficult. But it's possible to jump through the locked doors. I recommend rapping jump at an incredible rate, although some might prefer good timing. When you watch the demo, maximize N so you can see me shoot through the roof at a ridiculous speed.

Unlocking the Unlockable (Dark Ball Y-E)
You've probably seen gold delay used in DDAs. Well, due to the same quirk of collision detection, you hit only one switch when there are two on top of each other, though it will require very good timing. I find the easiest way is to wall jump just as you touch the switch, but you can also clip it as you fall or jump past. Note that this also applies to gold and mines, and that it works when there's more than two objects on top of each other as well.

I Retract That Statement
That is, the one where I said I couldn't do corner kicks, let alone instuct others...

Ahem. For those who don't know, it's possible to jump further and higher than normal if you take a flying leap into the corner of a tile and hit jump just as you hit it. As you can imagine, this requires near perfect timing, and even skilled players have trouble with it. The one in this level is set up at a convenient distance and height, so it should be a little easier than normal. As usual, they key is practice. Loads of it.

You can also kick off the convex curves in much the same way. This is often easier than a corner kick (though not so much in this map, because the height and distance aren't quite so nicely marked for you).

I Reverse That Statement (Qix)
It's also possible to do a reverse corner kick, which will send you upwards and slightly backwards. It's done pretty much the same way as a forwards corner kick, but you should release left/right (or press the opposite direction, if you prefer) just before you kick.

Revenge of the Corner Kick (Qix)
Next week, we'll be showing the Return of the Revenge of the Son of the Corner Kick, Part II.

Anyway, it's also possible to do a falling corner kick, for extra distance and little height. Fall away from the corner, then just before you hit it press towards it and jump. If you get it, make sure you press left again straight away or you won't make the distance.

Hello, my naem is... (scythe33)

It is possible to launch yourself a long way horizontally when doing a super jump from a launchpad. This definitely one of the hardest tricks in the book, if not the hardest. It's almost impossible to do it every time, and there are different "degrees" of the jump, with one of the most extreme being necessary to complete this level. The easiest way is to hit the launchpad diagonally, as shown in the demo. You need to be a little bit away from the wall, or it'll just be a normal launchpad superjump, and you've gotta hit jump quickly, because otherwise he'll be too far away from the wall. I can't stress this "a hair away from the wall" part too much - you can't be falling next to it.

#285033 244 2007-01-13T02:30:27Z Atilla
Did you watch the demo?

Get a good run-up. Make sure you're going as fast as possible. Tap jump *just* before you hit the slope, as shown in the picture. If you jump too early, you will be falling as you hit the slope and lose momentum; ideally, you should still be rising as you hit the slope, but you should be still be able to make it if you reach it at the peak of your jump. As soon as you land on the slope, hit jump again (still holding right), then again as soon as you hit the wall (also, release the right key now). Don't try and wait until you can clearly see the ninja touching the wall or you'll probably be too slow; anticipate when you're going to hit the wall.
