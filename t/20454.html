#384708 11 2007-11-30T18:57:15Z southpaw
I took Mo Duk Pai Kung Fu from the time I was 9 until October 2006. I had to go through kids ranks first, which went: white (you start out at white as soon as you start training, but you don't get a sash for it), orange sash, orange-purple, purple, purple-blue, blue, blue-green, green, green-brown, brown, brown-black. That's as far as you go as a kid.

then as an adult you go: white, orange, purple, blue, green, brown, brown-black, black. Then it's just degrees of black belt from there on out. Once you have achieved your second degree black belt you get the title of Sifu.

Quote: SoY, 2007-11-30T15:16:06Z
Karate. Shotokan, I think it was... I'm a black belt. I remember after I did the test and got my belt, the sensei said to me, "You have to remember that this may seem like the end... but it is only the beginning."
I have always had a problem with other styles of martial arts. And I think this finally gives me the reason why. For me it always seemed like other styles quickly advanced you to black belt, and then you started your training for real. In Mo Duk Pai, the average person takes years, I think usually around 5-10 years of training, often around 7 years though, and it felt like you weren't advancing too quickly. I took Tae Kwon Do as well and within 6 months I had advanced 2 ranks. So Mo Duk Pai was a better fit and it allowed me to feel like I was actually advancing to a level that was consistent with how advanced I was in my training.

I think overall martial arts is a good thing to know. Some styles just seem weird at how fast their students advance.
