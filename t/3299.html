#69021 2 2005-05-23T22:17:00Z nevermore
Ok, try playing around with just ONE tile. Play with symmetry. Rotational, vertical, horizontal, inverted. Once you get something pleasing to the eye, figure out what you want the challenges in the level to be. Should it be a race? A puzzle? An action map?
Once you've gotten that, you must play through the map and actually see what kinds of jumps and moves you can make on the tileset. Modify it to improve the flow.
Figure out what would work best as challenges. Should it be rocket based? Thwump based? A mixture?
Then proceed, and place gold to please the eye.

#69058 3 2005-05-24T02:55:39Z wilderness
mostly, i work almost exactlly as never said:

i play around with tiles making different shapes. when i find something i like, i expand on it. repeat it to make something symmetrical for example.

or

i'm inspired by something completely un-N-related. for instance my map, sleepy hollow: for that i was actually inspired by a dead tree i passed on the way home from a friend's house.

once the tiles are down, i take my ninja and jump around, seeing the height i can get from each jump etc.. making sure there is a good flow to it and modify when needed. also, this is where i add jump pads, bounce blocks etc.. to modify the flow and allowance of distance/height given to jumps.

once i'm happy, i add enemies in various positions. then after another round of testing, (and modifying tiles to accomidate the enemy paths) i'm pretty much done.

lastley comes 'le gold'

i try to make interesting shapes with it, make it pleasing to the eye and satisfying to pick up.

once the map is complete. i look for ways to modify the tileset that will improve the asthetics without damaging the flow. (for example: my latest map, ViRii... the cut out parts of the virus section at the bottom i added last and are what inspired it's name.)

then i give it a name that describes the way it plays, or it's look. (this is my favorite part of the process.)

allthough at times, my method can be slightly more abstract, i find this is generally a good way to work.

hope that helps

#69145 5 2005-05-24T15:51:12Z Sendy
Yeah I was going to say, always go through your E blocks and look for ways to add other tiles into them. You can chip bits away (to make it look more rugged, or to make symmetrical dips here and there), smooth edges (with either a rounded tile, or a curve made of triangle tiles), add notches with 5 tiles, and plenty more.

My creative process is just... Well I just stare at the screen and ideas come to me. Or I get ideas when I'm doing something else. I've been doing it for years and as I'm also a composer/artist, I've kinda trained my mind to be creative and think obliquely.

But the best way to think about level design, is to think of finding the 'sweet spots' in the game engine, and working on those. For example a sweet spot in N is the way the ninja interacts with the tiles, so good tile placement is one of the things that makes a great N level. Also, for some reason, most shapes that look interesting, are also fun to play on (or at least novel).

As far as objects goes they are usually placed after the tiles, but not always. Some levels that depend heavily on objects (eg, thwumps which may need to be used in a certain way), I build the objects alongside the tiles and modify both in a back-and-fourth manner.

Lastly, if you're stuck for ideas, copy. It's the oldest trick in the book. Just make a level 'based on' a level you really like on NUMA or N, and you should get some new ideas on the way. If you don't, you've got yourself a decent, but very unoriginal, map.
