RAY-HOUND



RAY-HOUND's got to be the craziest, uh... mouse game I've ever played. If it could get its own subcategory in the field of shoot-em-ups, I'd call it a "Magnet Shooter."



Basically, the premise is this; you control a pacifist hovercraft in a playing field composed of vector-drawn hexagons. Stationary enemies spawn in each level and fire the popular siphon-like lasers we all know from many Japanese shoot-em-ups at you. Your craft has no weapons, but can manipulate any lasers that come into your control field.



Use the mouse to navigate the hovercraft. Left-click to activate the field and steal any lasers inside; they are now harmless and can pass through your hovercraft safely. Keep holding left-click and steer the lasers back at enemies. Click again to activate more fields; whenever you let go, any lasers under your command stray off in their regular direction.

Hint: If you jerk the mouse quickly so that your hovercraft temporarily emblazens into a blue triangle, you can use this effect so that if you have lasers under your command, you can use this technique to quickly siphon lasers in a particular direction instead of having them slowly follow a circular pattern.



The game is based on time. Every laser your hovercraft is damaged by takes 10 seconds off the clock. Gain more time by beating levels faster, by destroying enemy hovercraft. According to the website (and Google Translator, heheh), so far the game is just in early stages, levels are infinite, and enemies spawn in random parts of the field.
