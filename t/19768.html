My favorite part of The Sunday N was probably Artist Commentary of the Third Kind. I love hearing what authors have to say about their own maps. In Issue 22 of The Sunday N, KinGAleX described the then-new section thus:
Quote: KinGAleX
The general premise is for an author to take ten of their maps (from NUMA or level packs) and provide a short explanation of say, why they like the map, a cool story behind the map (or anything to do with the map): basically whatever they find most interesting about these maps.
I found this idea really cool, and now that it seems The Sunday N has faded away, I cant let it die. Thats why Im encouraging you to post new ACotTK articles here. The rules remain the same: Pick ten (10) of your maps and write a little bit about each one. However, I do have some guidelines regarding this thread:

Ten maps means ten maps. I know some of you have a lot of maps that you want to talk about, but for the sake of variety, Im going to have to ask you to limit the number of maps you post here. Dont think you can talk about ten maps and then double-post with another ten maps. I want to mix up the authors in the thread, so please do not post more than one ACotTK article every seven (7) days.

Spelling and grammar are important. I want this thread to be as readable as The Sunday N was. Please, do your best as far as spelling and grammar are concerned.

When posting an article, please follow this format:
Quote: Sample ACotTK Article
Artist Commentary of the Third Kind, by <username>

<Name of first map>: Map description.
<Name of second map>: Map description.

Et cetera.

Attach a .txt file to your post with the level data for your ten maps in userlevels format: $Title#Author#ACotTK#Data#. Note that the Map Type is ACotTK.

If these guidelines are met, it will be easy (and hopefully fun ) to continue an organized Artist Commentary of the Third Kind. And dont think the old ACotTK articles will fade away with The Sunday N, either. I am working on an archive for all maps and their authors articles from The Sunday N. My progress is attached to this post. If you want to help, by all means go right ahead. Note: If you want to help, it would be best if you had Microsoft Office 2007.

And now, Artist Commentary of the Third Kind for all! I'll be posting my article soon.

Edit: Reworded some things so I don't sound like an all-important jerk. Maybe now this topic will be a bit more inviting. Still, I do stand by my guidelines. As with The Sunday N, organization and readability are a factor here. Enjoy?

Artist Commentary of the Third Kind by gloomp

Mr. LeWitt's All-Gold Challenge: I REALLY love modern and contemporary art, that's something you should know about me, and so I took notice when a few months ago Sol LeWitt, a talented artist who helped launch minimalism, died.
I made this map with no thought of LeWitt, but when it came time to submit it to NUMA, I decided to dedicate it to him due to the maps simplicity and hard, geometric feel.
Contrary too popular belief, there is more than one way to get back through the bounceblocks.

Rain in the Afternoon: I just love this. Plain and simple, might be the best map I've ever made.

Inspired by a Story, a Story of Infiltration: I really pulled out all the stops on this one. It's incredibly long and complex. It really might be too big, I've never quite decided.

. . . Stupid Hare: This is a good example of what good object placement can do to a bland tileset. The tileset is just a few clusters of 'E' tiles and the Metanet logo, not interesting. But chain those 'E's together, add some gold in the resultant chimneys, and stick thwumps at the bottom 'em, and you've got some mighty exciting gameplay!
And the title's another art reference, this time a recent Christopher Wool (Google him) painting.

Head of State: When you look at this, it looks like pointless clutter, when you play it, you discover what's cool about it.
I worked hard on the style of this, and thought over every object. I tried to fit subtle images of conformity and rebellion into it, such as the line of straight, perfect gold leading to death.

Matchplay: Preview of a map pack that I'll start releasing shortly.
I recognized that the tiles in this level were going overwhelm any attempts at maximalist object placement, so I tried to keep it simple, basic, an action map stripped down to the bare bones.
I think I pulled it off nicely, although the exit switch area could be a lot nicer.

Shekerboard: 03-3 in my map pack "A Beginning."
Why did I choose to include this one? Because it's so damn fun. I swear, there is not much that's more exhilarating and fun in N than rushing down a gauntlet.

Beyond the Call: From my Vehemence maps.
I remember this map taking a LOT longer than my other Vehemence maps.
The tileset is basically cleaned up scribbles on a spiral template.
It's mines that took a while, because with mine-jumpers, you need to playtest a lot.
I guess you could say it's 20mm-jumper.

In Aqutite: I didn't really know what I was gonna do when I started this map, just that I wanted to make an interesting and unusual map based off a room-like tileset.
When starting, I didn't really intend to make such a restrained map, it just sorta happened.
The thwump room really seems to clash with a lot of the map, but I like it, it sorta balances out the rest of the map. In fact, now that I look at it, all the rooms seem sorta full.
Oh well. Still works.

Somewhat Hard: I've decided to finish this off with my first map.
This remarkably managed to get a public rating of 3/5.
I think today that it's quite dull, especially with the launchpad, but back then I found it exciting and hard.
He he. Times change, huh?

---

I might have made a few gramatical errors, sorry if I did.
This was a lot of fun to do and I strongly encourage other mappers to do it.

Well, I like this idea very much, so I'm going to give it a shot.

Artist Commentary of the Third Kind by Ryzor

1: Pleasure in Pain
I made this level specifically because I hadn't made a race in quite some time, and felt it was the time to pick it up again. However, I looked at the races that were being done prior to my making this map and saw a kind of uniformity--nothing really set apart one from the other (speaking of course of the ones that actually had flow and were aethetically pleasing). So I decided, after making the first few turns, to only use 5, 6, 7 and E tiles in the making of the map. I tried to give it as much flow as humanly possible, while staying within the parameters I had set for myself. I also tried to be original with the enemies, but that was very tough...probably my favorite one was the gauss in the far right of the map.

2: Pitch-Black Progress
This map was made very quickly...it was completed and submitted after only 10 minutes of work. I wanted to add an element of simplicity to it, while keeping it fast-paced and fun, so I took those factors to mind when I made it. I used only 6 and E tiles, and made it fast and easy. I kept the enemies sparse but well placed, and used only the gausses to further the simplistic vibes the map was emenating.

3: Contagion
Fusion of two types of levels into one can be tough, and I've seen it done very well. So here I decided to do my own. It's essentially half action, half mine-jumper. The first part is fairly fast paced, but I didn't want it to exactly whiz by so I made certain parts require patience and delicate moves (i.e. the one-way with the mine at the bottom and the floorguards on regular doors). Then comes the jumper portion. There are a few enemies interspersed, but for the most part its you and the mines. As always, I made it artsy and slapped a fitting title on it (since I thought it looked like some tentacled (!) lifeform growing from a mechanical thing, I gave it the name contagion...).

4: Razorback
I've always wanted to do my guitar on NED (photo here) so I made some tiles that looked vaguely like it. But I figured that wasnt enough, so I surrounded the outline with more tiles in the style of flames (which were pretty damn hard to do with tiles, I might add!). But even then I decided that I wasnt done. So, true to my style, I inserted a red to yellow gradient (not the best, but it's large and therefore sufficient) inside the guitar outline. And there was a tribute to my guitar, the most smokin' metal monster out there. The hardest part, though, was working out the slopes of the guitar into tiles. Eventually I got it though.

5: Uzbek Dreams
I really decided to go off of the beaten path for this one. There are no tiles used (for me thats pretty much huge). I wanted to see AGD's for this (and still haven't) so I left it open, with various paths and techniques to try. It's not my favorite, but I really went outside of my normal boundaries by making it. It's a pretty challening map, too.

6: Boneyard
This map was really inspired by one of in_dub's tilesets (surpressed nature). I saw the cautionary-looking slanted patterns and decided that I had to incorporate them into my map. So, I started playing around with tiles and came up with this wierd crane thing. It looked like a crane you might see at a junkyard, so I continued the theme and ended up with a pretty open, fun map. I liked how it all seemed to fit with the tiles--the gameplay reflected them.

7: Puritanical Misanthropic Euphoria
I really like this map. I tried to be original with it, especially in gold placement (which is all on the corners of tiles). Maps like these have always been my favorite, and I really modeled it after alot of the best maps like it. The drones are pretty lax, but if you're caught in the wrong place at the wrong time, you're pretty much cooked. It switches between being open and confined as well, which I also like.

8: Own3d.
Here's a tileset that I had alot of fun making. Originally, it was just the teeth (I was experimenting with the formation of a 4 tile on the back of a 6-2 configuration, if that's understandable) but I made the dragon around it. Ever the pyro, I also experimented with different styles of flames to have seething forth from its maw, and decided on the style I used because it looked like it had the most velocity. But when I was all done with the dragon, the map still looked empty. Hence, the Castle of Own3d.

9: Hit the Floor
Next to survivals (and the obligatory test level) no jump races seem to be one of the rarest sorts of maps. This map was my attempt at one, and I like the way it turned out. I pulled out all the stops on propulsion (trapdoor, bounceblock, jumppad, thwump, etc.) while keeping it flowy. I really tried to do my best since there aren't many no-jump races, and it is actually pretty close to it.

10: The Physics of Fire
I modeled this level after a previous map (Step Up) that I did a while before this. I made this map one that has to be taken slowly. Unfortunately, it is cheatable, but other than that I love it. Placement is minimal but precise, and often times it had me hanging on the edge of my seat when I was playtesting it. I really tried to give every object a purpose, to make it seem like it was supposed to be there. One of my favorites to this day.

Artist Commentary of the Third Kind, by Pheidippides

Airborn Inferno: This is arguably the toughest map Ive ever made, and while it can be frustrating at times, I find it incredibly addictive. Its also surprisingly fast-paced if youve played it as much as I have. Looking back, there are things I would change about the map, but I still love playing it the way I originally made it.

Dysprosis: I just had to mention this one. This one was inspired by maximos Flight, which is probably my favorite map of all time. Everything just works so well here, I love it. Period.

Fragile Psyche: This was my first good race and my favorite race map of mine. Thorne, which I made right after Fragile Psyche, may have been bitesized, but I prefer this one. I like the flow of it more, and I personally think its more creative than Thorne. Weird, I demoed them both in 1288 frames.

Latent Heat: I think this map started my transcendence to the next level of mapmaking. After this map (and McP3000s failed Dynasty), my maps simply became much better. Also, I really enjoy the whole midair minefield aspect that the one-ways create.

Manifest Destiny: This is arguably one of my best maps from Twilight, and one of my personal favorites from the pack. Actually, it was only the second map I made for the project. And Im glad I made it that early Manifest Destiny really set the tone for my Twilight maps. As with Latent Heat, I feel that this map was another step up for my mapmaking.

Nonconformist: I wouldnt have made this map if it werent for Yahoozy. It was his idea to listen to an entire album and only that album while mapmaking. He posted about it on the forums, and I gave it a try. This map was a product of Gutterflower by the Goo Goo Dolls, one of my favorite albums. It only makes sense, then, that this is one of my favorite maps of mine. Order and chaos blended so well in the tileset, and I love the gameplay.

Shards of Twilight, Traverse: I wanted to talk about a map from Monologue, but its really hard to decide between these two. They were definitely the standouts of the pack. I love the tileset on Shards of Twilight, and the jumper-esque gameplay is choice. The gauss turrets work really well. As for Traverse, anything astheoceansblue calls so damn solid is definitely worth a mention. I love the chaingun up top, too.

Subverse, August and Everything After, Fallout Complex: I think I should talk about these next three together. I was really pursuing a certain (heavily-astheoceansblue-inspired) style in these three, beginning with Subverse. That was easily my favorite map from Dialogue, and one of the few maps where Ive used a laser drone really well. I had a few interesting ideas in August and Everything After, some of which I employed in Fallout Complex. To me, Fallout Complex is really the perfection of the style in Subverse, and Im immensely satisfied with it.
