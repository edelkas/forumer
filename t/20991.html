Quote: TS on Zenith 59-4
If there is one thing I love more than impossible challenges, it is screaming orgasms. This level is my sex-dream come true!

I did it.
For a long time, I had been saying,
After Zenith, I stop playing N.
But, to be honest, I was doubting that statement even as I was passing through 59-3.

[Will it be enough?] I was thinking.
[Will I be able to do it?] I was thinking.

Even as I moved my mouse over the button in userlevels, I was still doubting that statement.

And, then, it appeared.

I said Yes, this is it, and pressed spacebar.

As I was dying again, again, and again, I was contemplating on all the time I had spent playing this game.

I think that this game not only taught me how to play it with skill. This game taught me a lot about life in general.

No matter how many times you jump, you will always fall, and break every bone in your body.
No matter how much you run, the floorguard will still catch up.
At first, it hurts, but it gets a lot better when you keep on doing it.



Ive played this game when I was happy.
Ive played it when I was sad.
Ive played it when I was bored, excited.
Ive even played it at 7 AM instead of sleeping calmly in my bed.

The time I had spent on this game, it was the best time of my life.

And, parting with it, is sad, but necessary.

You know, it kinda reminds me of first love, now that I think about it.

Yes, it might have been love.
The feeling I felt when I played it might have been love.

Ive spent more time playing this game than I have spent out with my pals.
Ive spent more time playing it than I have spent with my girlfriends.

But that time wasnt wasted, no.
It was all goddamn worth it.
Every second of every minute of every hour of every day.

It was frustrating, it made me imagine throwing sharp objects at people.
But, more than that, it was something Ive spent months of total playtime on.

Through this game, I came to realize that no time is wasted. Every moment of life is so, so precious. It doesnt matter if you spent it laughing or crying.

It doesnt matter how many times you face the same problem.
You need to face it with strength, hope.
You have the strength you need.
All you have to do is press on.

So I thought Yes, this is it.

And I pressed spacebar.

Playing it was intense.

The level, it didnt exceed my expectations. It was perfect.

A perfect level to end it all.

I spent hours upon hours, just having fun with it.
Grabbing the bottom bounce-block after a fall from the top.
Smashing four rockets at a time against the gray walls.
I spent hours trying to get all the gold. And failed.

This level was beyond me.



All the time while I was playing it, in the back of my head echoed This it the last level Ill ever play.

And, with that thought, I had fun.
I had the time of my life playing it.
I died in more ways than it was possible.
I died more times than I died on the rest of the levels.
I spent hours trying to perfect a single route, then giving up and starting the next one.
All that time, I played with a smile on my face.
I didnt feel frustration for even a moment, because I honestly wished I would never beat this level.

And, after I thought I had squeezed all the fun out of this map I could ever manage to, I decided to actually beat it.

I chose the most insane route I could possibly think of, and executed it.

I was glad that user episodes werent implemented yet.
I didnt want to see you have achieved transcendence after I beat it.
I had already achieved it when I first saw the level.
This, this wasnt about transcendence.
It was all about ending everything with a bang.

I assured myself that I will not back down on this oath.
I will stop playing N not because all of the preparations I had made. I will stop because I want to.

I will not say that this is the best level Ive ever played.
I will say that this is the best level I ever played.
To the immense fun I felt when I played it, no other level can ever compare.

I remembered motherlode. I remembered the first time I played N. I remembered how I couldnt walljump to save my life.
I remembered the time when I reached 5-0, and couldnt beat it.
I remembered when I beat it, and then couldnt beat 5-4.
I remembered 9-1, 19-4, 24-4, 29-3, 37-3
I remembered LLCoolDave, and how he told me the way to beat 39-4. I remembered helping Alex beat it, the same way LLCD helped me.

Long ago, I was thinking that 83-3 will be the end of me.
Then I was thinking 85-3 will be the end of me.
Then it was 86-3.
I remember vividly how I felt when I beat 88-4 for the first time.

As I was playing this level, all the previous levels flashed in front of my eyes. All of the levels I beat before reaching this one.

I thought, that if motherlode was the beginning of it all, this is the end.
motherlode was the alpha, and this is the omega.
And I didnt regret it.
This was truly a level worthy of such a title.



And, after all those hours, when I finally fell down that shaft, I cried.

I cried, I said Yes, this is perfect.

And pressed Q.

Zenith .sol submitted.
N data erased.

Thank you all.

*bows*

#474942 33 2008-10-08T00:50:38Z Jiggerjaw
I remember the last time I played N
It was summer.
We were on a road trip. My mother and father in the front seat, and I and my grandmother in the back
I had brought my laptop, just to pass the time, and I opened up N version 1.5. I started, as I did all those years ago, on level 00-0. And I worked my way through, never jumping from column to column; beating the game in the order it was intended to be beaten. I figured I had just enough battery life in the machine to complete the entire game. But, my skillswere not what they used to be
I struggled nownot like I used to when I played. I had trouble with levels I would've breezed through in my primeall the same, I was clearly an experienced player, doing all I could to impress my grandmother, who seemed a bit aloof throughout the whole ride. I didn't care. I was on a quest.
Totally oblivious to the outside world, I was nearing the end of the 50's column. "Brenden," my mother called back to me. I ignored, though I had heard her quite well. I passed 59-4, and finally said back to her, "What, mom? I'm on a quest."
"You're grandma's feeling very bad right now," she said, "and that's why the air conditioner is on full." I glanced at my grandmother, who seemed to gaze aimlessly, distantly, without any expression on her face. I was disheartened. I convinced myself she was merely tired, and continued on into the 60's column.
I was moving very quickly through the sixties, as they're one of the easiest sets of levels, but I had trouble, as usual, with 69-2. When I reached this problematic level, I heard my grandmother cough. It was terrible. Almost as if she was choking on something evil in her throat
The road trip had to be put on hold, as we turned for the hospital. But, I knew I only had just over an hour of battery left, and, through my worries, I played.
Still en route to the hospital, no more than ten minutes later, I had beaten the level. My sickly grandmother seemed to take a turn for the better, though it was still clear she was in need of medical care.
I frantically switched, over the next twenty minutes, between reassuring my grandma and reminding her to breathe, checking my battery life, and besting level after level. As crazy as it sounds, it seemed, with each new level, came a slight improvement in her condition. Perhaps it was just me, justifying my lust for entertainment over comforting her.
We arrived at the hospital, and I was starting episode 81. I took the laptop in with me, knowing I was going to beat the game in record time. I had a meager 45 minutes of battery life left, and was determined to get through the last two columns before the laptop died.
By the time we got our brave patient to a bed, I had reached the infamous Bricklayer, level 83-3. Her condition visibly worsened while I struggled with this frustrating level, but when it reached its resolution, she stopped her rapid decline. I was almost convinced at this moment that my playing had some inexplicable correlation with her physical state.
As I neared the 88th episode, and the hardest level of the game, my mother informed me that Grandma had been diagnosed with throat cancer. "Ridiculous," I said. But somehow, they had missed it, and now, it was too late.
But I played on.
I got fairly lucky with the latter portion of the 80's column, and went through it with still twenty minutes to spare on my battery. I played, now, frantically, knowing somewhere within me that, if I didn't beat the gameif my laptop diedso did she.
I couldn't let that happen.
The doctor approached my mother with a concerned look on his face as I was waiting for level 98-3 to load. "Do you want us to pull the plug," he asked, "or keep her alive for as long as she's got? It's never an easy decision." I couldn't let them take her off life support. I had to try. I tugged at my mother's sleeve, and implored her to make the right choice. She humored me.
With only two minutes of battery life left, I reached the final episode. Tears in my eyes, I played, though shaking like three diabetics, with more grace than I had ever exhibited playing any game in my life. I drifted effortlessly through the first four levels, leaving 45 seconds for the final level.
I died. And I died again. And I died again. I watched the last three seconds drip out of my laptop's battery, the last three breaths, out of my dying grandmother's chest.
She died, TS.
I haven't played N since.
