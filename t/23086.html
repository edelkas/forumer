#450678 4 2008-07-04T03:25:46Z golfkid
Lemme take a stab at this. You're right that the total speed is faster when falling diagonally than when falling vertically. This is a rehash of physics here that you seem to understand already, but I'm gonna do it anyways. The straight vertical fall has a y-component we'll call A and no x-component. The diagonal fall has y-component of A and an x-component of B. Total speed for vertical fall is simply A, whereas the total speed for the diagonal fall is √(A + B), simply the Pythagorean theorem. Now, that said, I saw somewhere (I think its in the help section of the game) that the thing that kills you in a fall is the speed perpindicular to the surface you hit. So, even though the diagonal fall has the higher total speed, the vertical speed is still the same, so there essentially is no difference.

Another way of saying this is that during both falls you travel the same vertical difference, which would take the same amount of frames. During the diagonal fall, you happen to travel a horizontal distance as well. There is a principle in physics (that I can't remember the name of ) that says acceleration perpindicular to motion has no effect on that velocity. So, any horizontal movement you may make while falling doesn't affect the amount of time it takes you to fall.

Now, if you land on a non-horizontal surface, there will be a difference between the two falls, since you now need to take both vertical and horizontal effects into account. All this discussion does assume that the principles of real-world physics have at least a moderate influence on the game, which seems to be the case.

Hopefully this answers your question and/or gives you some insight on making a simple challenge. Sorry for anything I may have gone too deep on or not explained clearly enough, I'm a physics major in college so this stuff is pretty well formed in my mind and sometimes has trouble escaping from there in a meaningful way.
