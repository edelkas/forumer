Quote: R.A Salvatore @ Icewind Dale trilogy
I am dying.

Every day, with every breath I draw, I am closer to the end of my life.  For we are born with a finite number of breaths, and each one I take edges the sunlight that is my life toward the inevitable dusk.

It is a difficult thing to remember, especially while we are in the health and strength of our youth, and yet, I have come to know that it is an important thing to keep in mind--not to complain or to make melancholy, but simply because only with the honest knowledge that one day I will die can I ever truly begin to live.  Certainly I do not dwell on the reality of my own mortality, but I believe that a person cannot help but dwell, at least subconsciously, on that most imposing specter until he has come to understand, to truly understand and appreciate, that he will one day die.  That he will one day be gone from this place, this life, this consciousness and existence, to whatever it is that awaits.  For only when a person completely and honestly accepts the inevitability of death is he free of the fear of it.

So many people, it seems, stick themselves into the same routines, going through each day's rituals with almost religious precision.  They become creatures of simple habit.  Part of that is the comfort afforded by familiarity, but there is another aspect to it, a deep-rooted belief that as long as they keep everything the same, everything will remain the same.  Such rituals are a way to control the world about them, but in truth, they cannot.  For even if they follow the exact routine day after day after day, death will surely find them.

I have seen other people paralyze their entire existence around that greatest of mysteries, shaping their every movement, their every word, in a desperate attempt to find the answers to the unanswerable.  They fool themselves, either through their interpretations of ancient texts or through some obscure sign from a natural event, into believing that they have found the ultimate truth, and thus, if they behave accordingly concerning that truth, they will surely be rewarded in the afterlife.  This must be the greatest manifestation of that fear of death, the errant belief that we can somehow shape and decorate eternity itself, that we can curtain its windows and place its furniture in accordance with our own desperate desires.

I believe them to be wrong, though in truth, I cannot know anything for certain concerning what mystery lies beyond this mortal coil.  And so I, too, am but a creature of faith and hope.

Perhaps the greatest evil I see in this existence is when supposedly holy men prey upon the basic fears of death of the common folk to take from them.  "Give to the church!" they cry.  "Only then will you find salvation!"  Even more subtle are the many religions that do not directly ask for a person's money, but insist that anyone of goodly and godly heart who is destined for their particular description of heaven, would willingly give that money over.

And of course, the world is ripe with "doomsayers," people who claim that the end of the world is at hand, and cry for repentance and for almost slavish dedication.

I can only look at it all and sigh, for as death is the greatest mystery, so it is the most personal of revelations.  We will not know, none of us, until the moment it is upon us, and we cannot truly and in good conscience convince another of our beliefs.

It is a road we travel alone, but a road that I no longer fear, for in accepting the inevitable, I have freed myself from it.  In coming to recognize my mortality, I have found the secret to enjoying those centuries, years, months, days, or even hours, that I have left to draw breath.  This is the existence I can control, and to throw away the precious hours over fear of the inevitable is a foolish thing indeed  And to subconsciously think ourselves immortal, and thus not appreciate those precious few hours that we all have, is equally foolish.

I cannot control the truth of death, whatever my desperation.  I can only make certain that those moments of my life I have remaining are as rich as they can be.

I can't teach you anything sensible about religion because religion is inherently unreasonable. Science is about studying the workings of the universe by working with what we can know, but religion loves to take facts far and between and to make these convoluted bridges between them, on top of making shocking amounts of assumptions.
Aquinas knew what he was talking about when he said that Christianity was supplementary to reason and could not actually be called reasonable. His idea that religion "perfects" reason was essentially stating that when we can't find what we want to find through empirical observations, we can always add arbitrary assumptions to it to turn it into religion and satisfy ourselves with the (likely false) knowledge we've attained.

Cookie example describing reasonable vs religious views of reality:
You're in a room with no windows, and only one door that has no handle on your side. The room is entirely sterile and white, and there is an ambient light that has no source. You're sitting in the middle, confused about where you are. The door opens. An elderly chinese man dressed in traditional chinese formal wear bounces happily in and hands you a fortune cookie. Then he bounces back out and seals the door. You weren't able to see anything through the door while it was open.
Let's say you tried thinking reasonably. There really isn't a whole lot to know: you don't know where you are, you think you're the same age and that you're in your right mind, you have all of your limbs, you're wearing the last shirt you remember putting on, there are no blood stains on you, no new bruises, no new tears in you clothing, and no part of your body is more sore than the rest. You can deduce that the chinese man had gained the trust of whoever built and managed this room, or that he was the one who built and managed this room, but you can't say you know anything about where you are or why the room exists. You don't know if the fortune cookie is poisoned or not, but you have little else you can do in this room before you suffocate or die of starvation, and so far eating the fortune cookie and seeing the message is the only course of action that has a chance of changing your current circumstances.
Now let's say you got that far thinking reasonably, but like most humans you're uncomfortable that your presence in the room has no meaning that you can find and that you don't know what's going on behind the scenes or what will happen to you. So we go on to add some aribitrary assumptions. Let's see, which arbitrary assumption would ease your troubled mind most? Oh, I know! Let's arbitrarily assume that the chinese man had been in your place before, now feels sorry for you, and is communicating to you his plans to let you escape from this wicked complex of blood and torture through the contents of the message in the fortune cookie!
See what I did there? I just gave you a purpose, an aribtrarily assigned "good" on your moral compass, and irrational hope that you'll escape from the room as well as the assumed friendship of some power who knows more and is capable of more than you are. There is absolutely no reason you should make that assumption, but you can invent more assumptions of all kinds to prove that that chinese man is looking to help you escape. But it all started with that one ridiculous assumption.
The chinese man bounces in again, this time holding a pair of merchant's scales.
If you were thinking rationally, you'd watch what he does, and try to get around him and out of the room if he does something that makes you significantly more uncomfortable.
If you were thinking religiously, you'd rise to your feet, grab the chinese man's shoulder, and proclaim, "I thank you, mysterious chinese man, for aiding me in my escape."
Maybe the chinese man is about to free you, maybe he's about to torture you, and maybe he's just insane, or otherwise doesn't have any intentions. But if you think religiously you've already blinded yourself to the other possibilities. If it turns out later on that the chinese man is setting himself in a good position to double-cross you and that there's an english gent who actually wants to help you get out, you'd stick by the chinese man and repel the english gent, all because you've made a ridiculous assumption, convinced yourself that it was true, and are not stubbornly sticking with it.

So if you want to be rational, stick with hard science. Watch out for this religious science nonsense, because they like to pack on additional assumptions that haven't actually been established in the scientific community.
If you were to watch What the Bleep Do We Know with some scientist's commentary, you'd hear things like:
WTBDWK: "Our current view of matter is very lacking and fails to explain many phenomena we witness in our lives."
Scientist: "That doesn't make our current view of matter entirely wrong. That just means we're still fine-tuning it."
WTBDWK: "So we propose a new view of matter."
Scientist: "I'm looking forward to seeing which journals he cites."
WTBDWK: "We propose that matter is shaped entirely by the consciousness."
Scientist: "There is no scientific basis for that proposal. You're going beyond what science currently knows about matter."
WTBDWK: "So when you exert your conscious influence upon matter, it changes to your whim."
Scientist: "This has never been documented in any scientific journal anywhere, ever. It has even, as a matter of fact, been denied through countless experiments over the years. Google 'James Randi.'"
WTBDWK: "In this way, we establish that we can all shape the universe, that we are all chained gods."
Scientist: "Are you ser- Is this guy serious? He just took the plain ol' assumption that matter is defined by our consciousness and totally ran with it. All this shows is that when you make a stupid assumption that you can make a whole lot of other nonsense seem true as well."

So it comes down to this: why is it important to act rationally?
The nihilistic side of me wants to say that it isn't important because it won't matter anyway.
But the LaVeyan Satanist in me, which doesn't care why we're here but is focused on making the most of the time we are here, says that acting rationally is the key to enjoying oneself the most in the pithy amount of time he has to exist. And I believe that, because that seems rational to me.

So my advice to you, Viridovix, oh, so obsessed with the reason of science, is to look critically at these "scientific" texts you're reading and start off doubting everything. If you're interested in some revolutionary idea stated, demand proof and don't stop searching for it until it is explicitly proven. This vibration/soul crap is entirely assumption. These people should have won $1 million from James Randi years ago.

How do you make quotes look like everyone elses? (Name and date in brackets)

If you press the "Quote" button above someone's post, you will be taken to an Add Reply page with an extra textbox, which will contain the post you've quoted. All text in that second textbox will be formatted with the quoted user's name and the time his post was made. You can edit the text in that second textbox. Anything remaining in that second textbox when you click "Add Reply" to post your post will be at the top of your post.



If you want to do it manually:
[quote=name, time]Text being quoted.[/quote]
Just replace "name" and "time" with the name and time directly. The first comma character will be turned into a whitespace followed by '@'. So that text that I have above will look something like this:
Quote: name @ time
Text being quoted.
