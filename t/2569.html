Game: "Enemy Terroritory"

You're a spy that got himself put into Desert Storm. Unfortunately, you're mission was to assassinate the general and leave, but with as many soldiers as there are, you're gonna have to kill people off one by one to get out of there alive. Oh, and don't let anyone see you doing so, or you'll be shot on sight (like your own personal firing squad).

ET will be a FPS with a lot of Commandos-like gameplay. You'll be making and breaking bonds with the soldiers you work with. One area of the game will be at the base camp, where you can wander around (or kill when you see an opportunity), and talk to people to find out the latest gossip (if they know theres a spy afoot, they'll be alot more alert, so you'll think twice about killing anytime soon). You can sleep, which takes you to the next area of play: The battlefield. You'll be sent out on missions with your team, and you must find ways to kill them. The special thing about my idea: Realism. No crosshairs (cept for the ones on your guns), no run meters, no reloading leftover bullets into your current clip (wasting two shots and reloading will waste a LOT of ammo). This game will be realistic in gameplay to intensify the action.

Yeah, I sort of planned my rpg around both MMO and single player things at different times, and in the end they both ended up becoming very different games which just happened to mesh the same genres and happened to share a combat system. The MMO would have had a focus on fighting players, with everybody being divided into two "empires" as well as most of the npcs (including the normally neutral ones like orcs and goblins, which would have tribes on both sides). Instead of normal leveling, you would raise fighting skills by "purchasing" training with a combination of gold and "loyalty" points, which would be gained by killing people/monsters on the opposite side with more points from people and the most from high levels or large battles. Towns would be loyal to either side, but if you launch a big enough organized force to completely push the enemy out of a town you could take it over, realigning all of its shops (of which each town would have different ones) and thus denying members of your enemy's army of certain services (such as a helmet store) while giving you an extra outlet for those things (aka now you can go to two towns for a high-grade helmet). There wouldn't be any restrictions on the PvP conbat/conquest, except that large battles may have to be divided into several smaller, slightly overlapping ones. But if you pushed them back until one army only had a few cities while the other controlled the map, the game would automatically implement the "automatic 2-army preservation" switch and the losing side would be joined by something big (like minotaurs) until they got back a few towns. The idea is to keep them from being completely wiped out. Maniac, your idea sounds alright, but it's just about GTA minus the violence and fun things to do. List some things besides missions that you do after you finish the main game. Please include shopping cart racing/stunts.



As for my game, it was a simple RPG battle game that played somewhat like the PSX game Battle Hunter. Each player was dealt five cards out of a 52 card deck. The suit of the card was the card type, and the number was the card value.

Hearts = Defense (Damage that can be taken)
Spades = Attack (Damage dealt)
Diamonds = Movement (Move yourself)
Clubs = Movement+ (Move yourself or an enemy)

2 through King = 2 through 13
Ace = MAX

You placed your pieces on the boards (8x16 squares). After everyone places their piece (and are happy with positioning), the game starts. The first player either uses a card and draws another, drops their cards for new cards, moves one space, or waits. You cannot move diagonally. When you move next to a player (or move one next to you), you can attack. They must be adjecent to you, not diagonal from you. You pick a spade to attack with, and they pick a heart to defend with. If the attacker's card is higher, the defender's maximum card hold is reduced by one. When a player loses his last card, he dies. If the defenders card is higher, nothing happens. Both players discard the cards they used in battle and draw new ones. Aces are the MAX cards, which is the highest card in battle, and can place you anywhere in movement. Last man standing wins.

A little complicated, but fun when you get 8 people playing, and theres a moshpit battlefield in the center of the stage.
