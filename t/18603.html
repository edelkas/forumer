You're correct that the cross product in 3D becomes the "perp" operator in 2D. I think your problem is the calculation of the partial derivatives -- you have to take the derivative of the constraint function w.r.t _each_ particle involved.

So, if you have the particles p1,p2 which form the "stick" you're colliding against, and the particle p that's colliding against the stick, your constraint function is:

C(p,p1,p2) = Dot((p - p1), Perp(p2 - p1)/||p2-p1||)

I suck at vector calculus so I just expand everything to scalars:
C(px,py, p1x,p1y, p2x,p2y) = ( (px - p1x)*-(p2y-p1y) + (py - p1y)*(p2x - p1x) ) / √((p2x-p1x)**** + (p2y-p1y)****)

to make things easier to read, let len = √((p2x-p1x)**** + (p2y-p1y)****)

dC/dpx = -(p2y - p1y) / len
dC/dpy = (p2x - p1y) / len

so, dC/dp is your nc.
However, the gradients for the other two particles involved aren't so simple.. for instance:

dC/dp1x = (p2y-p1y)/len + (p2x - p1x)*( (px - p1x)*-(p2y-p1y) + (py - p1y)*(p2x - p1x) )/(len*len*len)
