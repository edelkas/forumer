#78746 5 2005-06-26T23:40:44Z Eldrial
well i'm a high rated author, so i'll tell my method:

usually, i just try to come up with a cool tileset, first usually using the [1] tiles. i just mess around with it, trying to get something that looks good. at the same time, i plan ways how the tileset can work together, so i edit tiles, move them around, copy them, etc. usually, i work with symmetrical tilesets. or at least i start with them. [i think about all my maps are symmetrical, or near symmetrical...] after that, i place objects where i think they would flow nicely. and i sometimes continue to edit the tiles to work better with objects i plan to use. that's about it.

#78841 10 2005-06-27T10:03:14Z LittleViking
I'm starting to feel like I'm the only one who doesn't built at least most of the tileset first. I start with whatever part of the map I want the ninja to begin at, build a chunk of the level, mess with the tiles to get some style, and move on to the next part. When it's all finished I smooth it out, work out the flow, ease up on the difficulty, and generally polish it up.

EDIT: I forgot to mention the most important parts of my map building. One: being different. You'll see it in everything I do, from the placement of a thwump to the tiles I choose. Little bits of my levels are as unique as I can make them, if only to give the player an experience they're not used to. Second (and less important), is filling out a level. You don't want parts of your level to be empty, but you also don't want it to become too long and hard. Fill in empty space with simple challenges that reward the player with gold.
