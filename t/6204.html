Check this out. I had a dream and wrote it down. Feel free to comment or whatever; I just feel like sharing it.

Quote
My dream.

It's strange that I had a dream like this. It reminds me of something my engineering professor said to our class the other day in lecture, speaking about how we help people and probably change people's lives so much. "Save the world. And if that fails, then save the part of the world you've been called to save."

I think this dream is important. I don't know why I do; I just do. Maybe it's because I remember it, even after waking up and going back to sleep. It's one of things that just came to me and I think it's important in some way, so I'm writing it down.

Like most dreams are, it was very confusing and it wasn't clear what exactly was going on or how I ended up in the situation I did. It's like a story with no clear beginning, it's just understood that that's how things are and there isn't anything strange about what happened up to the point of the dream. So I'll just start with what I remember.

I was with a girl. I'm not sure who she was, how old she was, or what she looked like. I don't know that she was anyone I knew. I just knew she needed my help. I was saving her.

We were in a corridor, a passage with walls and a ceiling and no windows. I had to help the girl through the corridor; it was important that I help her. It curved, and seemed to go on forever. On the right side of the corridor, there were places that were broken into little sections, like walls splitting a great open space into doorways. The corridor was grey and black, and it was like a hallway except for the spaces on the right, that was split like bathroom stalls. There were no lights, but it wasn�t dark. And there was more thing about the corridor. It was full of people.

There were so many people, all kinds of people. They seemed to be coming from nowhere, and each was doing his or her own thing. They seemed completely oblivious to each other, and yet so crowded, like people walking the streets of a city. They didn't notice the girl and I until we were right next to them. Some moved out of our way, and some I had to push out of the way.

That was the thing. The girl was scared, and possibly hurt, and all I knew was that I needed to help her get through the crowd. It was so crowded, like a claustrophobe/enochlophobe(fear of crowds)'s worst nightmare. They were pushing so much, and I felt like I was crushed between them, like a mosh pit gone wrong. That was the thing. They were hurting me. I couldn't tell how much they were hurting me, but I knew they were. It was like a dull pain all over my body slowly overcoming me. I was getting tired and hurt, and the girl was scared.

I kept pushing through the crowds as long as I could. Eventually I collapsed. I don�t remember if I fell, but I couldn't move any further. I felt the pain and the crushing feeling. I held the girl as close to me as I could. I knew I still needed to help her, and if I didn't help her, nobody would. I had to keep going. The pain went down to a point, then stopped. I felt the pain go away. I kept pushing through the crowd.

I helped the girl move through the crowd, which was strange, because people kept coming from the right, like we were pushing our way through the middle of a street crossing, and we were still in this passage. I kept having to ask people to move and push some out the way. I was still being hurt more and more, and I was tiring the more I pushed into the crowd. It was so hard, but I kept moving forward.

We kept pushing into the crowd.

The crowd never stopped. The people kept coming from the right, and I kept leading the girl through. We pushed and pushed and pushed. We made progress, I could feel that we were moving forward and getting somewhere. The corridor kept going on and on. I couldn't see the end of it, and I didn�t know if it would ever end.

I pushed as long as I could. The pain kept building up, and I kept getting more tired. I felt like I was being slowly crushed, but I wasn't being smashed between people. Maybe I was just tired. The pain eventually got to be too much. I collapsed again. This time was different. I knew that I had gotten her far enough. It was just like the time the pain went down, and then stopped. I squeezed the girl close to me again. I felt the pain build and build--then stop. This time was different though. The pain faded away and everything slowly went black as my breathing slowed.

Then I died.

The next thing I saw was my grave. It wasn�t really a grave as it was a coffin in a clearing. The whole scene was green and red and golden, like a spring dawn shining on a red metal casket. The sun's rays played into the clearing, lighting it. There was gentle grass all around, and some trees and vines, like a fantasy world beneath a weeping willow. It was clear only around my grave, I couldn't see outside of the clearing. There was only my grave there, and it had a cross and a ribbon on the coffin. At first I didn't know it was me inside it, but I eventually came to know it was me.

Then girl was there in the clearing. She laid white flowers on my coffin. She seemed sad, but I didn�t get to see her face and she wasn't crying. She stood over my coffin for a long time. She looked like she belonged in the scene; she had blonde hair. It was still dawn and the whole scene was bathed in a dream-like haze. Then I awoke.

Quote
I was with a girl. I'm not sure who she was, how old she was, or what she looked like. I don't know that she was anyone I knew. I just knew she needed my help. I was saving her.

This indicates that the dream is intended to focus on your feelings and intuitive self, the aspects of you which are unconscious and receptive, rather than willful or assertive. It could be said that it details your feminine side. That's who this girl represents. However, she is unknowable and indistinct. It is as if you cannot place who she is. This dream might analyze you trying to find and discover your deeper emotions or save your receptive side from an attack. Let's continue:

Quote
We were in a corridor, a passage with walls and a ceiling and no windows. I had to help the girl through the corridor; it was important that I help her. It curved, and seemed to go on forever. On the right side of the corridor, there were places that were broken into little sections, like walls splitting a great open space into doorways. The corridor was grey and black, and it was like a hallway except for the spaces on the right, that was split like bathroom stalls. There were no lights, but it wasn�t dark.

Corridors represent undesirable situations. They highlight the imprisoning intervals between being in places that you like to be. Life can be a corridor between two good events if you're feeling lost, bound, or depressed. A corridor that seems to go on forever represents an unsatisfactory situation that seems to have no solution--a perpetual state of limbo, if you will. The corridor had fissures in it. Glimpses into larger areas, which singified more freedom. However, you could only gaze upon this freedom. As of yet, you were still trapped.

Quote
And there was more thing about the corridor. It was full of people.

To be among a crowd is to be without personal direction. It is to feel that life is passing you by or that you are being bombarded with too many opinions, so you block them all out. It can also represent a desire to not stand out or be noticed. Let's continue:

Quote
That was the thing. The girl was scared, and possibly hurt, and all I knew was that I needed to help her get through the crowd. A crowd's primary purpose is to represent you feeling of people in general. People are crushing you. People are hurting you. People are threatening and damaging what the girl represents, your emotional nature. If the girl becomes injured too greatly, so too will your ability to experience emotion. You fear, perhaps, that society and the people in your life are desensitizing you. They are removing your emotions, or damaging your emotional self, even though they do not necessarily intend to. They overwhelmed you. They weakened you. You were unable to defend yourself against them. The people were so crowding of you, that even you became injured, in all that you are. That which you call yourself even came under attack. And you felt scared--and you saw your feelings in the girl.

You continued through the crowd, despite their effect on you. You labored on to find a safe place where you could, perhaps, protect the girl and what she represents of you.

And despite your lack of knowledge of a goal, you continued. Sometimes, we don't know what we're fighting for, anymore. There comes a point when we can only see what we're fighting against. Even though you had no visible reason, you felt the need to fight against the crowd, fight against society, fight against those around you.
