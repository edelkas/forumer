A website requires to be easily accessible by accident for the searchbots. Let me go through the general process of my understanding of it.

1) You put the webpage up. This page contains two words - "egg" and "yuppy" (this is a bit random, but there's a reason why I've specified this). Furthermore, you have specified two meta tag keywords - "island" and "mongoose". So, you upload this webpage to the internet. So far, so good.

2) The webpage remains on the internet, but there are no links on other webpages to it, or, there are links but on pages which aren't linked themselves. Searchbots can't track down pages if they don't have links, they won't try random letter combinations to see if certain pages exist, they just follow the internet's large link chain. So, at this point, no bots find you, and you are not on Google.

3) BAM! You're finally linked. On a website already monitored by Google, they link to your website. The link text is "tree" (random again, but I'm coming to this). So, the bots follow the link to your site, and record what your page has on it. Immeadiately, it records "egg" and "yuppy" as words on your page, "island" and "mongoose" as meta tags and "tree" as a word strongly. These words are then how your site can be found on Google - and every time someone links to you, they are adding words for this purpose, if they all use similar words, the effect is multiplied. If you get 50,000 people linking you with the word "tree" then you'll be very high under the search "tree" - the words on your page of course bear an influence, as do the metatags.

You can also directly link the bots to your page with the link iangb just gave - it's akin to another page with a link to yours.

For the record, the way you're linked does have a very strong effect, and this is best demonstrated by the number one result on Google for searching "failure" - it comes up with a biography of George W. Bush. This is because people decided it would be funny to make this happen, so they all linked to that page with the link text "failure".
