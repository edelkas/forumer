#91996 5 2005-08-17T20:23:35Z iangb
Three (and a half) counters to your counter.

0.5.) We got lucky. Get over it.

1.) The anthropic principle bit. If the universe had been unable to support life then we would not have been here to observe it and say 'this proves that humanity exists (or not) on the whim of God'.

2.) If there is a chance that humanity will exist given a certain set of circumstances and a universe does not have those circumstances, then no matter, just wait until the next big bang (as gravity eventually pulls everything back together) and hope you get a better set of circumstances. You may have to wait a few million million (repeated millions of times) centuries, but as there is no living creatur to observe it, the time doesnt matter. Time does not exist for you when you sleep. Eventually the circumstances will be correct. If there is a chance an event will happen, no matter how small that chance, in an infinite length of time that event is certain to happen (in fact, if the chance remains a constant then it will happen an infinite number of times. If you throw a dice for infinity then you will get 1000 sixes in a row eventually.

3.)The 'Life needs these circumstances to be created' story is a load of rubbish. Sure, humanity may have existed only under a certain set of conditions, but we do not know what would have happened in a different set of circumstances. A good analogy here is yourself. Imagine all the different possible DNA combinations you could have had given your parents (eg loads - a couple could have many many children, and none of them would be the same). Now imagine all the choices you have made, from what subjects you take to what colour pants you wear each day. The chance that you will have made those exact choices, will have had that exact DNA is minute, hugely small. Even more if you factor in the DAN of your parents, and of their parents, etc... But you still exist. And if you had made many of those choices differently, you (or a varied 'type' of you) would currently exist (admittedly dead if you made some really bad choices). It's like throwing a many-sided dice - there is a tiny chance that each side will come up, but it a certainty that one will. And we do not know how many 'sides' the dice of the universe has, and how many lead to humanity, or how many lead to other forms of life, or how many lead to nothing but the cold and the dark.

4.)(I know, I said I would give 3 important points, but this is related). There have been experiments using really powerful computers to try and model the big bang, and to show the outcome of changing the initial conditions of the early universe, and these have 'shown' that there are mant potential universes where, for example, carbon could not have been created, so life could also not have been created. But these experiments are flawed for several reasons. Firstly, even with all the computers in the world today you could not even model the behaviour of 12g of carbon - ~6*10^23 atoms. There simply isnt enough memory in the computeres to 'remember' each particle, even if you made each one represented by a single byte (8 ones or noughts). Secondly, even in those conditions life might still have appeared. Thirdly, and most importantly, the scientists assumed that each of the initial condition of the univers was unconnected, that you could change gravity without affecting magnetism. This is like marvelling that no matter where you touch a live wire you get a shock. So don't belive them.

I think that's all....
