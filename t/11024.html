In a series of graphic novels by Jhonen Vasquez called Jhonny the Homocidal Maniac, Johnny commits suicide at one point and goes to heaven.

In this episode when Nny visits both heaven and hell, Jhonen presents a very interesting version of what heaven and hell could be like.

Heaven was a plain grassy field, where the residents simply sat on chairs, nothing else. However, they were content as they had no desires or needs anymore. They were content to sit there for the rest of eternity.

Hell was just like earth. Continuing with Jhonen's trademark social commentary, hell was hell beause of its residents. They were not changed after death, so they still had unsatisfyable desires to be popular and conform. There were giant empty cities full of garbage, that the residents destroyed before moving on to build another city.

It's kind of hard to explain what hell was like there, but it really was a fascinating book to read.

Buy anyways, I wanna hear what y'all think heaven and hell would be like, assuming it exists an' everything. Personally, I think heaven would be something like an altered reality, like above. I think it would be alot like earth, but with major differences like total absence of evil, and the ability to watch what's happening on earth, like how God watches everything.

As for hell, I'm don't really have any ideas there.

#199020 10 2006-07-05T02:07:51Z Kablizzy
I'm in full belief these days that this is Hell. And of course, not the stereotypical Hell of pitchforks and demons and fiery pits, but a psychological, philosophical, emotional Hell. Because, really, when you ponder it at length, you begin to ask yourself, "What is the worst thing that could happen?"

And, of course, you think Eternal Damnation. But if you narrow that down a bit, you think Eternal Pain. But if you ponder further, you come to realize that the worst thing imaginable isn't pain, really. Not in the way that we think of Eternal Damnation. This is rather awkward to explain, but the greatest punishment wouldn't be pain, because there would be another level, a deeper level to the pain. Thus, if you think of pain as a punishment, and in terms of punishment, you think that human nature would dictate that one would learn a lesson from such pain. But the point of the Eternal State of Damnation wouldn't be learning a lesson, really, it would be the unending, everlasting pain.

Thus, the deeper level of pain would be lack of knowledge of said pain - ignorance. So, really, the worst pain imaginable wouldn't be the pain itself, nor the concept of pain, nor of damnation, but ignorance to the fact that said pain exists; under our bed, just at our doorstep, and around every corner.

Yet, we cry, "Why? Why?" when this pain manifests, not even coming close to understanding that the pain was there all along, simply looming as a shadow, always there, in light or dark. In darkness, even though one cannot perceive said shadow, it still exists. It simply requires the light with which to see and project it.

So, in my own mind, this is my personal Hell. For what? Why, I don't know, for I have no life lesson to learn. This is my punishment, this is my damnation.
