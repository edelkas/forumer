#142368 7 2006-02-11T22:36:41Z Tsukatu
I've been half-rambling about this in religious discussions, though I guess I can directly address it here.
I think it's great that people do nice things for each other, whatever their motivation. I don't care if God, Satan, or Epona tells you to be nice to people, so long as you're nice to people. But I divorce benevolence from religion. Most people think the two come hand-in-hand. I can be just as nice as a religious person (and from my experience, I tend to be nicer) without having any motivation other than my own benevolent will. Part of my beef with religion is the extranneous stuff, such as completely irrelevant or contradictory stories or other rulings to do things other than being nice. It's awesome that the Bible tells you to love your neighbor; it isn't that it clarifies that the neighbor must be a white male who doesn't work Sundays, isn't homosexual, doesn't wear clothing made of two different materials, isn't blind, etc. It doesn't matter who the person you're being nice to is - just be nice!
But what if your kindness is abused? Well lemme tell you this:
Recently I joined a Christian youth group. Well, not a youth group per se, because I only go to one of their functions weekly that involves the people actually in the youth group bringing friends, but still. I'm still quite secure in my atheism, and I have to admit that part of my motivation to come is in the form of a girl, but I have learned a bit from the meetings, and I get much more out of it than I thought I would. In particular, everyone in the youth group is very supportive of just about every other member. It hit me in the first meeting - these people have a really nice setup going on. Every individual feels love and support from everyone else in the group, and so is motivated to be nice to people himself. I haven't found that in any atheist community I've been apart of. Sure, they use Christianity as an excuse, but I don't care. Whatever reason they need to be nice, let 'em have it. I even put up with flagrant contradictions (as you should know, the Bible essentially tells you to kill atheists, gays, Jews, retards, and cripples, but this youth group supports 'em all). Anyway, the lesson here is that kindness will eventually win just about anybody over. If someone abuses your kindness, it just means they're insecure or that something is deeply troubling them. Keeping up your kindness toward that person (to a reasonable extent) will eventually make him happy, and people do some very generous things when they're happy.

Before this turns into a rant (probably too late), one more issue:
Consider the person who, after losing his faith in God, doesn't see a point in being nice anymore. This guy is the kind of person who needs constant supervision if you want him to be nice. He won't be nice unless he knows he'll be punished for being bad, and once he knows no one is watching, he'll feel free to be bad. I have no respect for these kinds of people. They deserve the ignorance their religion breeds.
