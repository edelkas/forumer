DOS will use your system's internal speaker by default unless you have a compatible (and ancient) sound card like me, (Creative Sound Blaster AWE64 ISA). ISA is a type of card bus before PCI and AGP.

Firefox, IE, Netscape, Opera, etc. are web browsers. HTML and Java are languages. Flash and Shockwave are plugins.

Your scheme on drives is a little off. A: and B: are reserved for floppy drives. C: typically is where your version of Windows is installed. D: through any letter can either be another partition. hard drive, flash drive, iPod, CD/DVD drive, etc. Not everyone's C: drive is larger than their other drives:



BIOS essentially tells your computer how to start it. Does it boot off a floppy, or a hard drive or a CD drive? What resources does your sound and video card use?

In the DOS days, you would have two files that told DOS how to operate: CONFIG.SYS and AUTOEXEC.BAT. Config.sys configures RAM usage and loads drivers into memory. Autoexec.bat basically executes programs in the batch file when you boot into DOS - similar to startup programs.

The Blue Screen of Death is still common. It was very common in the Windows 3.x/9x days. However, in NT, 2000, XP and Vista, they are not as common because they are more stable OS'es than the MS-DOS line. BSODs can still occur in NT:
