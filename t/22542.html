On those same lines, I define myself as my consciousness. I'm not conscious until I have a sufficiently complex and functional nervous system that would allow me to be conscious.
As such, I say my body existed well before I did. And depending upon what happens to me and my body, either my consciousness - and therefore I - will die before my body stops functioning (Alzheimer's, miscellaneous dementia, etc.), or I'll die at about the same time my body dies. Again, if you believe in something like a soul or spirit, or anything else that you think contributes to or is your personality, character, consciousness, etc., then you can feel free to believe that you existed before your body did.
...because at that point logic can be happily tossed out the window.

But if you believe as I do that there is nothing else that contains what is called "you" other than the arrangement and specific function of neural matter, then I think it's unreasonable to believe that "you" could exist before your body. Or after it, for that matter.
Sure, your genetic code exists long before you do (as well as components thereof, before your conception), but those plans haven't yet been realized.
If you understand object-oriented programming, saying that you exist simply because there are plans for the neural network that would then produce "you" is like saying that an instance of a class exists simply because there is a class definition, and that's obviously not the case.
