So from what I gather...
Marijuana alters your way of thinking, opening up parts of your brain you wouldn't normally use, and makes you see things from completely different angles. This effect is completely temporary, and there is no harm to your mind or rest of your body (more on this a little later). That is, you get that high, but when it's over there isn't any damage whatsoever, just some neurons that connected to parts of your brain that they didn't connect to before - not necessarily bad. You can avoid damage to your lungs by obtaining marijuana in pill form, and using it in moderation (very easy to do) removes chance of addiction. So basically, it's a positive effect without any negatives.

Of course, the main problems I still see with it are:
-Because it's illegal, and most Americans happen to be goody-two-shoes when it comes to drug laws, it's a pain in the ass to obtain, and you'll probably start being associated with some pretty sneaky people.
-Because it's illegal, you have to avoid authorities and parents. It doesn't seem worth the risk of being caught and fined or caught and disowned just to get that high. Plus, that makes it look addictive.
-While you are high, your sense of judgment can be much different (not necessarily impaired, but it very well can be), and you risk doing some pretty stupid stuff when you're high. So you'd have to be in solitude in a padded room in a straitjacket while you're high if you want to eliminate the risk of dying/doing something retarded/injuring yourself severely accidentally. And also, while high, you are a less productive individual.

But then again, two of those can immediately be eliminated with legalization of marijuana, which leaves only that last one, which isn't even that big of a problem. More people (percentage-wise, making it comparable) crash when sleepy than when they are under the influence of marijuana, which makes the case that it doesn't impact your judgment to a great degree in the slightest - no more than being sleepy, anyway. So if there is a benefit, and legalization takes away most of the cons, and the last one remaining isn't even significant... what's so wrong with marijuana? I'd appreciate statistics.
