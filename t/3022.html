I've been wanting to show you guys a piece of my writing for a little while now, but I haven't really had anything that really showed my writing style, because of my stolen computer. So in English class recently we were supposed to write a piece that focused on one moment spanning from 3-5 minutes. This is my outcome. It's somewhat morbid, but bear with it.

Quote: Moment Piece
I looked behind me for a mement, only to see the two snarling figures hot on my trail. All I could make out was their metallic-blue eyes peering through the bare, snow-dusted trees. How strange that those two things so evil could come from such beauty as the forest. The contrast was intriguing, and I almost stopped running just to stare. The two beasts gained on me, and the beads of eyes started to become much, much bigger. I could now make out their figures and knew they weren't ugly at all. In fact, they were devilishly beautiful.
They were two hybrid creatures, a mixture of what seemed to be a wolf and a stag. Their bodies were furry and slender, and they were quite agile; they were perfectly shaped and trained machines to attack. Their legs came out of their body very thick and wide, with obviously distinguished muscles, and were quite long, and as your eyes traveled to the foot, you would see the leg become thinner and thinner, finally expanding for the hoof. Their necks were very thick, a combination of the hair's fluffiness, and the very solid muscles to hold their long antlers up. As they ran, their fur was pushed back and rippled by the wind, creating an arrow-like effect. But the features that stood out the most were their eyes. They were as blue as the sky, and if you looked straight into them, you could feel them peering into your soul, evaluating and judging you, bit by bit. And they were vibrant.
As I ran, I started to tire out. I was barefoot and almost naked, and both the brambles and twigs on the ground and the cold wind cut into my skin and cause extreme pain. I leaned over and picked up some bark from the ground and attempted to cover myself. I shivered and breathed a white puff of breath. As it rose through the atmosphere it was like an angel ascending to heaven. Finally it expanded and dissappeared, and my visualization was lost. My hope was lost. I had no more strength, and couldn't go on. The beasts could do their worst on me, no matter the pain. I would leave this life of misery and grief, and face the unknown.
I looked at the ground rushing by me and tried to time my fall. I was already in so much pain, and I knew that if I didn't fall safely, I would probably die. I looked ahead of me, and saw a pile of snow near a tree. I didn't think; I didn't want to think. I closed my eyes and jumped.
I didn't even feel the impact. I just felt the cold snow rub against my open wounds. I didn't care. I would let them do their worst. I was limp, and could barely see anything but two blurry figures approaching rapidly, and I didn't care. I would let them do their worst. They slowed down, and cautiously padded up. They reeked of excrement. I didn't care. I would let them do their worst. They poked at me with their snots, and pawed at my arms with their hoods, making sure I wasn't a threat. I didn't care. Their hoofs were surprisingly warm and smooth; perfect blunt weapons. I would let them do their worst. They both looked me in the eye, and that was the last thing I saw. I didn't care. I would let them do their worst.
I was blind now - I had to rely only on my ears to know what they were doing to me, for now my body was entirely numb. My arms and legs were probably broken, or at least sprained. Something cool and wet brushed against my leg. One of the animals was licking my leg clean. The other was brushing the snow off me with his antlers. I felt relaxed; I felt ready. As one of them fitted its teeth around my legs, I smiled, and almost laughed. To die would really be a great adventure.
My right leg was gone. I couldn't feel it anymore, and certainly couldn't move it. It was the only part of my body that was in extreme pain. I felt the ground with my hands, brushing against one of the creatures' feet. I quickly pulled away, and kept feeling around. Right next to me was a tree stump, and I leaned my head back to rest on it. I wasn't in any pain anymore now - I could just feel the blood clinging to my skin. As my blind eyes faced the sky, I started to see something. I saw darkness. Utter and complete darkness. And as the two monsters ripped at my flesh, the darkness was ripped away by little spots of light, as if somebody was sticking twigs through a dark sheet of cloth one by one. Slowly, a little white spot appeared in the middle of my line of vision, and started to grow so subtly and slowly that it was unseeable.
The dot grew bigger and bigger, and became a star, then an eye, then the moon, and the sun. I could feel my body getting lighter. My torso had less and less to support. The little dot of light had become enormous, and was filling my whole view. My legs started to get warm, and the sensation traveled up my body until it reached my head. I felt quite comfortable. All of a sudden the nice warmth shot away and was soon replaced by a chilling trickle down my spin. I shuddered at the iciness, and started to quietly moan. It became louder, and was at a scream. I was screaming at the top of my lungs. I screamed for what seemed like hours, but was probably seconds. Finally, I lost energy amd collapsed. I stopped screaming. I stopped hurting. I stopped being cold. I stopped being. I stopped.
I wasn't alive when the beasts were done with me, but I could still hear them softly. They growled at each other and howled in unison as I drifted off to a better place.
Feel free to comment and make suggestions.

Anyways, here it is... but hell is it similar in design!


All around him, the sky was deep blue. Beneath him, forests and fields. Lush green below, perfect blue above. He was happy. He was flying. He heard laughter. He heard giggling. He was invisble, floating high above a free earth.
He was woken from his dream by dull metallic footsteps. He looked around. The sun was trying to clamber over the horizon. Night insisted on doing overtime. He was in a town. The street was dimly lit. As usual, he didn't recognize where he was. He hated that. For several months, he had been oon the run and whenever he woke, he felt lost. Again, he heard the heavy footsteps. They were approaching. One thought: run. He got up from the doorway that had been his bed, and ran. As he fled, he reflected on the past.
Twenty years before, when the earth resembled one huge city, the "robot age" started. It began with the creation of harmless, helful robots. At home, all was doone autoomatically. Humanity was getting lazy and... bored. To amuse the population, robot fights were organised. In huge arenas, robots fought 'til destruction. This opened a new market. Robots were made stronger, more destructive and intelligent. Fianlly, an ultimate robot, of German design, was created: the Psyborg. Its main advantage was intelligence and memory. It could find the weak pooint of any enemy. Its stealth capabilities also mad it invisble to radars...
Fred slowed down to a walk. The footsteps had vanished. The sun was low over the horizon, its golden rays burning his tired eyes. One word trotted in his mind: Freedom. Memories flowed into his head.
Then it happened. The Psyborgs decided to put an end to their cruel fate. Indestructible, these roobots were unstoppable. But then, the unexpected happened. The Psyborgs did not attack. No bloody wars broke out. The Psyborgs were programmed not to kill humans...
Fred stopped. He was tired. Tired of fleeing, tired of of hiding. The sun was well over the dirty roof-tops, glowing hot. The moon had fled. Fred longed to be Free. He remembered.
What followed was chaotic. The Psyborgs, realising brute force was unhelpful, used what they mastered best: intelligence. They overturned governments, they upheld kingdoms... Finally, they formed an international organisation: The Congress... The world was at their feet. They rewrote all the laws. Theirs were harsh and banned freedom. Everyone, worldwide, was confined to their home. Anyone not respecting the law was taken away. Killing humans was impossible, but torture wasn't... Most accepted. They were weak. Some refused. They were known as the "Freedom Stalkers". Fred was one of those...
"ID", murmured a deep voice. Fred looked up and saw it. The patrolling Psyborg had caught up with him. Fred had been thinking. He hadn't heard the pounding footsteps approaching. "ID," the voice repeated, mechanically. Fred smiled. He knew all was over. The Psyborg told him his rights. Fred wasn't listening. He knew he didn't have any rights. He was thinking of his dream, thinking of Freedom. The Psyborg aimed his stun-gun at Fred. Fred was far away, flying over snow-capped mountains, flying free. He heard sky-blue voices beckoning him to stay, he saw crystal smiles and twinkling eyes. The stun-ray hit Fred in the chest. An electric surge went through his body. He felt himself fade and tumble. Soon, he would wake up in New Alcatraz, hell... He started toppling over. His mind was full of happiness and freedom aching to break free of his body, of his fate. He hit the ground. As he faded, his last thoughts were:
"Free, free as a bird, but nowhere to go..."

A deep, damp stench rose from the mouth and turned the stomach of the young clergyman. For the past five cycles he had drawn pit management and rather than growing accustomed to the foulness of the task, the labour was growing weightier. And the smell! This, he had thought, he would at least find a resilience to, a lessoning of the nausea that rose burning in his throat threatening to strangle him with tears so often it would almost rob him of his balance and he would waver precariously above the sickening mass. The stench, that seemed to make a game of finding new ways to invade his being, was now eating at his very soul.

Adding to the burden.

Eating at his core.

He closed his eyes and uttered the words of faith the brotherhood had taught him as a child. They eased him and some weight fell away. His eyes opened and his gaze fell down wards. The water was still.

He breathed a silent prayer of thanks to his Lord and turned to make his way back to the temple.

It was not unheard of for a body to be not as at rest as one would hope and then came the unpleasant task of climbing down onto the lower platform to end the suffering of the poor soul. And with that came a great risk of infection. The masks the clergy wore were only sure at distance and there were greater risks. The previous pit manager, whilst performing a pity, had been taken hold of by something still retaining strength and pulled under the dreadful water. He had managed to wrestle free and climb back up to the platform.

It had taken only seconds.

His agony had alerted his brothers at the temple gates who had rushed to his aid only to witness a horror as reds and greens bubbled into a putrid soup and something terrible that used to be a face had cried out for an end.

A shudder shook the clergyman. He had been the first to arrive.

He tore himself from the memory as his hand reached for the winch to draw the cover shut, when he heard it, a faint splashing. Something was stirring below.

He rushed to the edge and stared down into the blackness of the pit.

The evening light had fallen swiftly and clouds had formed a blanket directly over the mouth. In the gloom it was hard to define any shape. He stared for a few seconds allowing his eyes to become accustomed and then he saw it. A silhouette of an arm was reaching up to the platform, then a head appeared and soon a body lay convulsing on the deck. The clergy man stood frozen. The fear rising again, he repeated the words of faith reaffirming his strength and slowly made his decent. As he neared the figure he unsheathed his blade and began to chant the prayer of passing, when a cloud drew away from the moon allowing its silvery light to be cast. The clergyman sank to his knees.

The body of a naked man lay before him.

The man was entirely clean.
