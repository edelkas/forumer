Ahem. The force due to gravity, F, = GMm/r^2. To find the amount of energy needed to move an object from some initial distance r[i] to r = infinity, we integrate the force due to gravity from r[i] to r = infinity. An object at r = infinity will experience no gravitational acceleration, since GMm/(infinity^2) = 0; therefore it will not slow down any further.

∫ GMm/r^2 dr between r[i] and infinity (if only I could stick the terminals in as well...)
= GMm ∫ 1/r^2 dr btwn r[i] and inf.
= GMm[-1/r] btwn r[i] and inf.
= GMm(1/inf. - 1/r[i])
1/inf = 0, so
GMm(1/inf. - 1/r[i]) = -GMm/r[i]. (It's negative because it's a potential energy.)

Now, if we have more kinetic energy then GMm/r[i], we have enough energy to move the object to an infinite distance and some left over - it will still be moving away, and no longer decelerating. In practice, of course, you're never an infinite distance away from something. However, since you have enough energy to keep going until you reach infinity (never) you'll just keep getting further and further away forever.

On your way to your aphid farm every day, you decide to measure the number of steps it takes to reach it.
