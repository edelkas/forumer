I've played n since 1.3 and I've always been a huge fan of user levels. I await level pack releases with glee but I never post to thank the authors who put them together. User levels have kept this game alive and interesting for me, especially after I finished the metanet levels. I recently decided to clean up some of my better maps and submit them here on the forums in a pack of sorts, in order to return something to the community.

I would not call myself a good map maker by the axes that this community judges map makers on. Still, I think I have had some cool ideas and implemented them decently. This is definitely not a pack of well-playtested, carefully measured and metered, implicitly/explicitly zoned and perfectly laid out maps - the kind of maps on which you hone your travel path for high scores (but there are exceptions...). These are the more playable/more interesting maps skimmed off the top of the results of me messing around with the level editor over the past year, and (most of them) refined to be completable and enjoyable.

They are not set out in episodes. I have grouped them into categories both in the data file and by letter in the userlevels list (b and c - terror and tricksy - remain mixed up in the file as a record of the order I made them in.) I do believe the tricksy (careful jumping, lateral thinking, timing challenges) maps are better than the action ones - which is strange, because I enjoy playing well-made action maps just as much as, well, those that would fall into other categories. I seem to have a harder time making interesting action maps, I suppose.

I would like to stress that these maps are very original, and I am very proud of some of the things I've put together. I haven't plagiarised any ideas here. This point would be irrelevant if I was claiming this to be a fun, coherent, "normal" set of maps, but again - the primary claim I'm making is that this is an interesting set of maps. Auxiliary to that is the fact that many of these are fun to play.

I hope this interests at least someone in the community. I would appreciate feedback, but my primary motivator is to give something back to the people who've fed my procrastination habit.

This seems a bit wordy for a mere 30 levels, but hey!

Regards,
Corps

I think you're right in saying that the "tricksy" maps are much better than the general action ones. There's certainly some potential here as a beginner map-maker, but there are certain elements that are generally frowned upon when making maps (just like me to pick out bad parts):
- Tight clustering of objects, particularly mines and gold. I know that the mines are generally there for aesthetic purposes, but they don't add anything to the gameplay. Same goes for the gold, but less so - those ones just look a little messy.
- Enemies or trapdoors behind bounceblocks or gold. Not a big problem here, but the use of them is pretty prevalent on Starry, starry night.
- Use of enemies by attrition: there isn't a great deal of this either, but the number of Gausses on Starry, starry Night strikes me as unnecessary. (It is fun to have a massive number of targeters following you, though)

Now that the generic negatives are out of the way, I'd like to comment on the maps specifically:
Escapism: Getting all-gold would be hard here, and it's generally a nice concept. For enemies, I would have always chosen a Laser and a Rocket, but Gausses suit the map fine. 3.5/5
Frolic: While the number of thwumps on the left side of the map initially seems pointless, they quickly become serious obstacles (and more solid ones than one-ways) when combined with the Gausses. Again, I think you could have done with fewer turrets, but it's not a big problem. 3.5/5
Opus terriblus: Strange, but really good in my view. The turrets seem a bit ineffectual. 4/5
Polymorphism: A bit hard, isn't it? 3/5
Regular: Can be tedious at times, but isn't bad otherwise. 3/5
Spoiled for choice: Classic concept, executed well. 4/5
Starry, starry night: I found this a bit dodgy. If you took out the hidden Gausses and the trapdoors, I'd give this 4/5. 3/5
A curious proposition: I don't know how to do this one... After testing in debug mode, I found that it's fairly good. But I can't escape the starting area. 3.5/5
Automotive concerns: The central jump feels nigh-on impossible, but the rest of the map is good. 3.5/5
Cirque du masochisme: Apt name. 4/5
Cities of the future: The maze-like start is almost tedious, but otherwise not a bad map. Feels a little empty enemy-wise. 3.5/5
Crater: You can force the same effect as that that can be done with locked doors with a little bit of level code editing (I'll tell you about that if you want). Also, the Bottom-middle-right tile section is annoying. But I like the use of that normal door; I'm always looking for maps that utilise that effect. 3.5/5
Floetry: Getting the Thwump launch is really hard due to the Drones, but otherwise a good map concept. 3.5/5
Komplex: Nice tileset. The gold cluster near to where the ninja starts looks ugly, but it's a fairly good mine jumper. 3.5/5
Low-risk: This is nearly identikit to many maps with the same concept behind them. Not my sort of thing. 2.5/5
Mekanism: I really like the style of this, even though it's a pretty common map idea. 4/5
Riches: Again, good tileset. It's another fairly good jumper puzzle, but the jump required to get out of the area with the exit door appears to be extremely difficult, if not impossible...
Rise to the challenge: Far, far too hard. If someone posts a demo I'll raise this score. 3/5
Simplica: An age-old Action map concept, but still good. 3.5/5
Strawberry fields forever: The ground-facing launchpads are irritating as always, but I assume that that's the point. 3.5/5
The depths of obscurity: Personally, I feel that this map could do with a Seeker Drone or two just to piss people off. 3/5
Touch the sky: Another jumper maps, but certainly a good one. 4/5
Tribute: I don't really like this sort of style, but it's pretty playable. 3.5/5
Mechanism: Again, a strange one. I don't really know what to make of this. 3/5
Limitless: Wow, great tileset. I actually really like maps like this. 4/5, verging on a 4.5/5.
Tedium: Pretty tedious, but has a good tileset. 3/5
Compromise: Again, incredible annoying trapdoors in the lower section, especially when you've gone the full-gold route. It isn't too bad, however. 3.5/5
Time Doctor Freeman: Having Gausses fire at you from two directions at once while you're mine jumping is slightly annoying. The way that you've only got a certain period before Drones fill the map is hardly new, but it works well. 4/5
Wahlqual: A nice map concept, executed well. 4/5

As you said, these are hardly the most polished maps in the world, but they don't have to be. Some of them have actually inspired me a little, which is rare considering my usual cynicism. This map pack is certainly worth people's time, in my opinion.

Thanks for that! It's given me some stuff to think about. A few points:

- I'm pretty happy with Starry, starry night. The difficulty of getting each patch of gold is intentional and the swarm of source-less targetters goes with the theme of stars :), even if it's frustrating in that it makes it hard to judge the direction the gauss will fire in.

- Overly difficult maps... well, I guess that's preference. I find ridiculously challenging maps to be a lot of fun... even if they sit uncompleted for ages, when you finally get the annoying bits down and complete them, the feeling of accomplishment is that much greater.

- A curious proposition: I really did hope that I had discovered a unique method of locomotion with the start of this map... I'm sure someone has seen it, though. You ride the launchpads up the wall and land on top of the mined column. To get onto the "track" you can either jump so that you land on the upper edge of a pad or jump up into the start of the track - the trick here being to ensure that you start touching the wall just as you enter the track.

- Crater: consider spending 5 minutes perfecting the jump off the wall into the spike pit thing. I can now consistently get past it very quickly.

- Riches: I actually made this jump easier for this public release. Just start the jump off the opposing internal wall as early as possible. N can jump suprisingly high off very little initial momentum. If you want more space, open the exit area before coming here.

- Mechanism: Note that this is in a separate category - "Esoteric". It's a customisable arena. I know that this is a common idea, but I've put a lot of effort into making it cpu-friendly and actually useable. This is probably the best-tested map in the pack as I personally use it often when I want to practise interacting with a specific enemy or combination of enemies. For posterity, I'll explain the map. Top row: the central 6 open turrets, and the 2 on each side open drone cages, with optional cage closing buttons as well. Bottom row: the right half are terrain chunks, and the left half are "boxes".

- Tedium: This was just intended as a tileset showcase, yeah. I ended up labelling Limitless as tileset too, since, while the level is decent, it's just so very pretty. Similarly I almost ended up putting Mekansm here.

- The last three maps are experimental. Compromise is a point of contention for me. I liked the idea but I spent months going in circles with this map trying to implement it. Also, the end is annoying. The other two I feel are unfinished, but I'm glad you found them playable.

Thanks again for the feedback.
