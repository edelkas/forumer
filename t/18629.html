#348939 6 2007-07-30T07:15:22Z Atilla
The damage system seems like it would detract from the game. My main concern is that it undermines what has traditionally been the main attraction of NSF - namely, that it encourages flamboyant, risky driving. There are already plenty of racers out there which encourage care, precision and consistency - we don't NSF to join them. As is, it's a nice bridge between the ridiculousness of Burnout - where crashes are so common, they become tedious and uninteresting - and the games which focus on more cautious and realistic driving. I, for one, would like it to remain so.

I've seen persistent damage in other racing games, and it's generally irritating. If people winning by bouncing off all the walls is a problem, then you really need to fix the game engine or track design, not just whack arbitrary penalties onto it. If you look at games with punitive damage, like the F-Zero series, hitting the walls slows you down enough that you would try to avoid it even without a damage system. If you look at games *without* a damage system, this is certainly the case. Furthermore, the shift from street races to proper tracks means you can stick sand patches, hay bales, piles of tyres and other obstacles in front of the wall, thus making scraping along it much slower.

On the shift from street races to track races, I approve with some reservations. On the one hand, it perhaps makes NSF a little more generic, although it must also be considered that many racers have street tracks these days. However, the shift to tracks will allow more emphasis on making tracks which are varied and enjoyable, rather than ones which actually resemble city streets. I actually found many of the specially designed tracks in past games, such as the URL races, Street X and Drifts, to be better than the street tracks. It will also get rid of those mysterious glowing forcefields, which basically turn the streets into a closed circuit anyway. Because of their nicely-curved shape, the forcefields are also particularly prone to being used as a "turning aid".
