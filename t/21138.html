Quote: heatwave21, January 25, 2008 01:54 pm
I say no.
Ever wonder why milk tastes creamy?
I have my doubts about those claims. The raw data are probably true, but they're being sensationalised. Somatic cell count is normally measured in cells per mlif you have a look on the internet, you'll find this is clearly the established standard. I imagine they used a figure of cells per litre on that website so they could have big numbers in the millions. Calling it 'pus' is clearly also an exaggeration. Somatic cell is not an synonym for pus; in fact, in the context of milk production, most of them are white blood cells (leukocytes). Mastitis increases the somatic cell count, but even milk produced by healthy cows has a somatic cell count in the tens of thousands per millilitre. A very low somatic cell count may even be a bad thing:
Quote: http://www.milkproduction.com/Library/Articles/Somatic_Cells_Function_and_Relationship_to_Milk_Production.htm
In part the answer depends on what is considered too low. The key to defending the udder against bacterial challenge is the ability to quickly transfer large numbers of somatic cells to the udder from the circulatory system. Healthy cows tend to have this ability. There is some evidence however that in very low cell count cows, a challenge by bacteria, especially coliforms, may overwhelm the immune system before it can respond and the result can be a very sick animal with clinical mastitis (5).
And, of course, these levels have no meaningful impact upon the creaminess of the milk.
