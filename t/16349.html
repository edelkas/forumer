Quote: zeel, 2007-03-13T13:08:27Z
...what is Bitsise...

Trusted NUMA members can receive a bitesize account, which gives them the ability to add the best maps to NUMA's Bitesize page. Getting your map bitesized means that it's, in every way, enjoyable and definetely worth a try. Bitesizing is completely subjective and is unrelated to the ratings.

Quote: zeel, 2007-03-13T13:08:27Z
...whats a DDA...
A DDA, short for "Don't Do Anything", is a non-playable map where you don't move the ninja, but he is transferred through the map by various propulsion methods. In some DDAs, you must hold the left or right key for it to work properly. Exciting close calls with hostile objects make DDAs interesting to watch. In brief, a DDA is a moving N Art.

Example of a DDA: http://numa.notdot.net/map/69898

Quote: zeel, 2007-03-13T13:08:27Z
...what makes a race a race?
Race maps focus on speed and the flow of the tilesets. You must keep the ninja moving all the time, and race maps have generally only one path which leads to the exit.

Example of a race map: http://numa.notdot.net/map/57335

#304957 4 2007-03-13T17:25:54Z usaswim
I was just like you when I was new to NUMA.

For acronyms, you can go here. Some examples are AGCD (all-gold completion demo), DDA (Don't do anything).

Here, you can find a great resource for good map-making skills.

To improve your grammar, go here.

Here is what makes a map what:

IMPORTANT:

Unfortunately, most new people here don't realize that you should never clump ANYTHING. That includes enemies and objects. Make the tileset look as good as possible. Also, do not hide trap doors that trap N in, so it cannot excape. Those are terribly unfun maps.
  • Survival - It should usually not include exits.
  • Race - Should be very flowy and should only have one path to follow.
  • Action - You must find the best path. There are many maps that fall under this category.
  • Incomplete - Of course, this is for incomplete maps only.
  • Test Level - This is for testing new concepts.
  • Tileset - non-playable. To make a picture using tiles.
  • N-Art - most are non-playable. For designs.This is an example of N-Art you must play.
  • DDA - Don't do anything or hold one button. There are many types, from a KRA (Keep rocket alive) to a HRDEKRADDA (Hold Right, Door Eerie, Keep the Rocket Alive, Don't do Anything). There is also a newer concept, which is Chase the rocket.
  • Puzzle - This is for complicated puzzles. Not as in a jigsaw puzzle.
  • Uncategorized - Category doesn't exist anymore.

Action maps are the fastest to make out of playable categories - if you entered Ned and just quickly want to submit something, action maps can be build pretty fast (but the total time depends on the playtesting). What maps are hardest to make?

Well, generally, a DDA is usually the hardest to make. It involves lots of testing and will also take the longest time. I highly recommend using a wide variety of propulsion. Here is a great place to learn about DDAs.

Test levels may take a bit of thinking to make too. N-Arts generally take up the most time. They require lots of experience and effort.

Which maps are easiest to make?

That depends on how much time you spend and how good you want the map to be. Survivals, tilesets, and action are usually the easiest.

Which maps are the most fun to make?

That depends on the mood you're in. I like to make races and DDAs. N-Arts are partially fun to make as well as tilesets. They may become quite frustrating, though. Action can be quite fun too.

Which maps are the most boring to make?

I'd have to go with survivals. They take up the least amount of time, and it is quite hard to come up with a good one. Puzzles can be a bit tricky and may not be as fun as you might expect.

Which maps are the longest to make?

N-Arts. They can take months. DDAs aren't far behind.

Which maps are shortest to make?

Preferably action or survival. Survival takes the least amount of time, and actions can vary from minutes to days.
