No-snap is really 1/24 snap, which is what we're using. (23*34)*(31*24) = 410,688 possible positions for objects. And as for the stacking, I'd consider two different objects on the same stop to be stacking. I'll make exceptions from stacking doors though, including the exit door. The switch counts as the position of it, and the door can be anywhere.

Now, and I didn't think about it before, the exit door has the most variation. Since I'll allowing stacking (because the switch represents the object, not the door), that means for every position the switch could be in, there are 410,688 variations (one for each possible position of the door).

So, even if this is the only object in the game, there would be 410,688^410,688 different combinations. This is even higher than the 10,000^500,000 rough estimate that got me that other number.
