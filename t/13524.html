This thread is me bitching about college. If you're likely to be bored by it, spare yourself the effort of reading through this.

"Oh, college will be a walk in the park. Especially your freshman year. No one really takes it seriously - it's just getting you accustomed to living unsupervised and fending for your own. It's basically 4 years of fun. It's a joke."

I'm going to Boston University, working toward a Biomedial Engineering degree. I'm in my first year now. I've been here two months.
I may just be pissy now because I've recently registered for classes for the upcoming spring semester.

Half of the majors the school offers are very straight-forward: you have to take four classes, each of which are worth four credits. That class normally meets 3 or 4 hours per week, which usually accounts for three 1-hour sessions on MWF or two 1.5-hour sessions TuTh, as well as a 1-hour long discussion section once a week. So we're talking at most 16 hours of being in class per week, earning 16 credits.

But me? I have a major in the sciences. That means I have to take science classes. Yay!
The School of Management, the College of Communication, the College of Health, the College of General Studies, etc., all of those bastards get their 3-4 hour classes.
Let me tell you about the classes I'm taking.
The first class on my list is an Engineering Core class. It's required for all engineers. It's four hours (two hours on Tuesdays and Thursdays), and almost a complete waste of my life. The first half of this semester, we went on field trips and wrote (in no more than 600 words, no smaller than font size 12 and double-spaced) about what we learned. It's kindergarten. This second half is our professor taking advantage of forced hours of teaching by teaching her students how to be test subjects for her personal research project at the university.
All in all, not so bad.
The next class is my math class. This one's also not very horrible. Three 1-hour lectures and a 1-hour discussion section per week. Four hours. Nothing really to gripe about, except maybe that discussion section (which I haven't gone to for at least four weeks now).
My easiest class is my writing class. It's three hours divided into two 1.5-hour sessions on Tuesdays and Thursdays. The professor is fascinated by big words.
So what am I complaining about?
Chemistry.
Chemistry has three hours of lecture divided over three 1-hour lectures on Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays. Chemistry also has a 1-hour discussion section per week, with a weekly quiz, which forces me to go. But why stop the fun there? Let's also toss in a special, reserved exam period and abuse it. We're not going to give tests in the lectures or anything. We're too good for that. Three hours, it is. Monday evening. Oh, but you think that's all? We haven't even gotten to labs yet. On top of all of that I just mentioned, we're going to stick in another hour for a pre-lab discussion, and then a 3-hour long lab in the middle of your week. Have fun scheduling your classes around it!

But even that's not so terrible. Chemistry's only taking up 11 hours of my week and worth the same as a class that takes a little more than a quarter of that amount.
What I don't like is how my schedule will be changing for spring.
Basically, keep that schedule I just told you, toss in a discussion section for that engineering core class (net +1 hour), and then replace the writing class with another infernal lab science. For both Chemistry and Physics, I now have lectures, discussion sessions, exam periods, pre-lab discussions, and 3-fucking-hour-long laboratory periods.

Wanna see my schedule?

I tried to give myself as much time off as I could, so I end up devoting entire days to nothing but schoolwork. I happen to like the tradeoff, but it's still an insane number of hours.
Oh, and if anyone wants to compare that to a high school schedule, imagine these hours with nothing but AP classes on your list.

I'm going to be spending more than 25% of my waking hours sitting in a classroom somewhere. My roommate is spending less than half of the hours I am and getting the same number of credits. He's an Anthropology major.
And I have to do this if I want to graduate in four years.

A few Fine Arts majors have actually tried to tell me off because they're forced to spend even more of their time in their classes than I do. Thing is, they're forced to participate in rehearsals, performances, and walking around in museums. That's not a mental strain. Even if it's an overabundance of creative input, it's still pretty pleasant compared to considering the operation of our known universe for the same amount of hours. I'm finding an integral by limiting myself to sequences (a very tedious and mind-numbing method of finding an integral) while you're watching Hamlet. Yeah, I feel sorry for you. Oh, what's that? You actually have to pay attention and take notes because you'll be quizzed on it later? That's far worse than my last test, which was three solid hours of frantic scribbling for which the class' average score was 34%.

Fuckers.
Oh, and I'm pre-med. That means that in my sophomore year, when I'd be taking a humanity and a social science of my choice (a nice 3-hour, 4-credit hourse), I'll be taking another lab science: organic chemistry. Yeah. Ouch.

I ended up doing pretty well though, but besides receiving an award for high marks, it was really all for nothing. No one cares about first year marks. I don't know how it is at your school, but at mine, if you fail a final they give you three opportunities to rewrite and pass before they kick you out of the program. Almost no one drops out in first-year.

From what I've heard from employers, most people don't even care about your marks, just that you've managed to graduate (which is an accomplishment in and of itself). Those that do look at marks will only go as far back as 3rd year. So basically, my first two years (and my best years, academically) were just to prepare me for the important final two years that actually "count".

Although, you said you were in biomed, right? Only a certain number of the biomed kids at my school can pass to higher-level courses due to restrictions on class size. So you're in the top 75, or you're out, more or less. It gets pretty cutthroat. I've heard of people sabotaging fellow students' experiments in the bio labs, just so that they can get have an edge when the lab reports are marked. I'd keep my eye on my petri dish, if I were you...
