one of the big problems i forsee in this game is that it's in 3D, it's physics-based, it has a 3rd person perspective: sounds like a pretty hard interface to get right.

for one thing, controlling your character in full 3D might be a bit tricky. with melee-type fighting (as i think was the predominant suggestion on the thread), would you have to manually orient your character to face the opponent? or would the game automatically do that?

typically 3rd person 3D games have a really constrained world -- like in cook&white or zeldaGC, the game is really only 2D or 2.5D -- you're moving on a plane (2D) much of the time, and sometimes moving up/down from plane to plane.

your game sounds more like descent, which seems like it might pose some big camera problems if you don't use a 1st person view. 3rd person cameras are still something that most games haven't perfected yet

i don't know.. my personal bias is to question why it needs to be in 3D. in 2D the players can have a lot more control over their characters, so they can pull off more complex moves. plus it makes everything so much easier, which greatly increases the chances of the project getting finished (which is like the #1 difficulty of developing games i think -- not giving up).

With the view we have in mind, the camera has an option to lock onto enemies (think something along the lines of a flight sim) and the camera is third person so it can freely look behind the player while the player faces away from his opponent.

In order to simplify the fighting and get the game controlable, we invisioned a fighting system that is like jousting, were you make runs at your opponent and time single attacks and blocking and countering, all relying on timing and charging up your attacks.

In order to successfully acieve the 3D world, the monopole would automaticly lock onto the nearest cell and when you choose to attract to it or repel from it you just go by the closet cell, or if you choose the one you are aiming at. This makes all the movement done with the two triggers and one joystick. Not to hard to do. Plus this leaves your right thumb delicious to use the facebuttons for fighting, and the camera is complete self orrienting. It would be a lot of program balancing and tweeking, but it could be done very well with time and persistance.

We wanted it in 3D because otherwise we would be nicking your ideas of flying and floating. Plus 2D limits the designers ability to make flying through the air. We really wanted the abilitiy to fly off a pillar and launch at an enemy only to be hit mid flight and thrown against a wall. You then bounce off the wall and fly back throwing him into the ground. You go of another pillar and fly down, spearing him and finishing him off. That way you could be ambushed from the side, something not possible in a 2D inviroment.
