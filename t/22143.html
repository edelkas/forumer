#427777 19 2008-04-12T22:29:04Z beginner2
My suggestion:

1) Get a vacuum cleaner.
2) Unplug everything from the back of the computer. Make a design of how it looked before if need be. Open the computer's case up.
3) Turn on the vacuum and hold the tube in your hand.
4) Place the tube, still holding it, inside your computer. Make sure there's no loose wires on the bottom metal plate of the computer. MAKE SURE YOU CLEAN UP ALL THE DUST!
5) Take the tube and point it at the power source fan. Take all the dust possible.
6) Plug in ONLY the power source, monitor, and keyboard. Turn on the computer, and enter BIOS. Look at the power settings, and at the fan speeds. If the fan is slow (under 1500 RPM, I believe), consider lubricating the fan and/or getting another fan installed on top of this one, to cool the computer down more.
7) If the computer is STILL experiencing problems, try getting a new power source, ~ 500W is good.
8) If it STILL experiences problems after all that work, run the computer in safe mode, completely scan for viruses. I recommend AVG anti virus and Norton to scan (2 anti viruses find more viruses than 1), then run an Ad-Aware scan in safe-mode. Delete ALL viruses, leave no trace. After finishing with each scan, turn off the computer (open command prompt, type this in: "shutdown -s -f -t 00"). Leave it turned off for 2 minutes, then enter safe mode, scan, etc.
9) if there are still problems, I'm stumped.
