#20724 1 2008-07-06T01:00:35Z scythe33
As Dave has proven to approximately half the forum, saying marijuana should be legalized is the new Ch t-shirt. Certainly, there's a point, but it's getting clich; take your arguments to your Congressman.
So, let's start with Salvia Divinorum. First off, the main reason it should be legalized is that it's already legal everywhere except Australia. Doing nothing is easy, right, guys? (Trust me, I'm very experienced in that area)
Or, on a more serious note, let's take into account that there's currently little information about it; there are either no cases of serious addiction/overdose or too few for me to find any. See, there are efforts in a few states, and in the UK to ban the stuff, even though there's hardly a case for doing so. One lawmaker was quoted as saying it "may be as dangerous as marijuana and LSD (slight paraphrase)", which to me is hilarious, because neither marijuana nor LSD have significant risks associated with their use. It is, in all fairness, a dissociative, in dangerous company alongside DXM-but just because it produces similar effects doesn't mean it has similar risks. I argue that there should at least be more research done before any action is taken to ban the stuff, and luckily, most scientists in the field seem to agree.

Ibogaine is a different, more complicated story. It's a psychedelic, and like most psychedelics not very addictive. Still, it's illegal-but that's not really the point here. Rather, it seems to stop most drug addictions right in their tracks. Even if not legalized for OTC use, it's incredibly stupid not to at least research the effect and hopefully, put it to use in rehab clinics. It should at the very least be rescheduled, perhaps to Schedule II (I still think that's not enough, but too much change too quickly causes too much public drama).
And your thoughts?
