Edit: I'm a planet nerd, so I looked into this a little more. For anyone who's curious.
Ceres is the largest asteroid in the asteroid belt. It makes up a third of the total mass of the belt. There are three other asteroids being considered: Vesta, Pallas and Hygiea. The four asteroids together make up half of the asteroid belt's total mass. Another interesting one is Sedna. Its orbit reaches out as far as 975 AU (almost 1,000 times further than Earth's orbit) and takes 12,000 years to go around the Sun. See its orbit here.

Yes. To elaborate, it's all to do with centrifugal and centripetal force. Centrifugal allows the planet to spin around to its heart's content, while it's partner centripetal force prevents the object from flying away. This works in the rotation on a planet/satellite's axis, and how it orbits a celestial body without flying off.
