#37011 2 2004-11-20T01:23:20Z brickman
A: be a member long enough to have an encyclopedic knowledge of the various bugs, tricks and effects
B: be creative and have lots of ideas
C: close calls a must
D: be flexible. Oftentimes opportunities appear that you wouldn't even think of on your own, rather less try to implement, and lady luck makes it work by accident when you're trying to do something different.
E: Time. Lots of time. If the ratio of time spent on DDA:length of DDA is under 1:10, it probably sucks. And longer ones like Kablizzy's and my latest one take hours.

Pretty much what Brickman said, my first DDAs were pretty bad, and I scrapped 'em before even posting them. My latest ones, though, I've put a lot of work into... and I mean A LOT. For instance, my last one took me almost 3 days, with spending roughly 3 hours a day. The thing that I try to remember when doing DDA maps is precision is key. #40194 7 2004-12-09T12:40:26Z iangb
Sometimes you can get lucky just by putting objects/tiles in an order that looks promising, then pressing play and seeing what happens. However, normally I start with a little bit of planning off-screen, for maybe the first 10 seconds or so (for a great example see More Thwump Power - the first decent, rise and loop was all planned on paper beforehand. Then, see where your ninja ends up and add another feature to shunt him (or her??) on a bit more. Keep going until you run out of ideas. Never be afraid to try something new - who would know if it went wrong!! If possible, try to get a section the ninja passes through repeatedly with different results - again the map is (quite) a good example. Try to keep up the pace by introducing enemies/close calls, but remember that if you add too many the lag will ruin the level. Thats all... I think. Have fun!!
