I don't know how much (physical, biological) evolution now applies to humanity now. The old story for evolution is that a single mutation gets passed on because that mutation makes the carrier more likely to survive, and breed. But with the rise of intelligence, the theory is no longer as applicable because everyone has a very high chance of survival anyway, so any special 'talant' is more likely to be ignored. Or worse, considered abnormal, and therefore removed from the gene pool.
What evolves now in humanity is culture. Political ideas, and the way we live are changing wi the generations, adapting to new circumstances. Communism was a social 'mutation' - one that was sucessfull and prospered for a short while. Now multiculturalism and global communication are becoming more 'acceptable', as attitudes adapt to the fact that if we constantly hate all other humans then the end result will be all-out war.

Anyway, on-topic. I think that for quite a lot of the population, that is true. They go through life with closed eyes and minds, never properly thinking about things, following the leader and believing what they are told. But for the people who do think for themselves - that is an evolving idea, a sign that society is slowly changing.

As for aliens - no, the universe is not infinite. It is a (roughly) spherical body, and outside it is... nothing. Not a big space filled with vacuum (*shudder* at those last three words, physicists), but nothing No space, no time. So really, there is no 'outside' the universe - yes the universe is expanding, but it is not expanding into anything. But the universe today is very, very, very big. Even bigger than that. So yes, I reckon that there are other life forms out there. We might not see them for a while - if a planet is on the other side of the universe (and we could somehow see it, which is a small problem) then the light we see from it today is light that came from that planet hundreds of millions of years ago. How much life was there on earth back then?
