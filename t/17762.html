My laptop has issues. I've taken it apart about 8 times now to perform random maintenance.
At its worst, back before the fifth repair or so, the touchpad didn't work, the wireless card didn't work, the cd drive didn't work, the monitor wouldn't turn on, the hard drive had bad sectors, no operating system was detected, and the internal temperature was hot enough to fry eggs after half a minute. After the seventh repair, I had fixed the overheating issues, fixed the wireless card, fixed the hard drive, fixed the operating system detection issue, and had the monitor working again, albeit with some irreperable damage, and the problems with the cd drive and touchpad remained unresolved.
Most recently, the monitor stopped working. I decided to chronicle my repair this time around because I realized recently that I own a digital camera that I may as well put to use.

Here we go!

My monitor without the front casing: [Picture - 32.9 KB]

After a whole lot of poking around, I more or less accidentally stumbled upon the location of the problem.
The doodad responsible was on that bit of board below the monitor.

Closeup: [Picture - 37.8 KB]

The last time I had monitor trouble, it was with that connection on the right being loose. This time the problem wasn't so straight-forward. I found out that the monitor turned on again after I applied pressure directly to the plastic. To investigate why the hell that was, I had to take off the plastic:

Plastic removed: [Picture - 54.4 KB]

The problem piece: [Picture - 45.1 KB]

It turned out that for some reason those connectors on the left were ever so slightly suspended above the contact points. Applying pressure forced them into contact.
