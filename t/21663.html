------
One could argue that God created the laws of the universe, and therefore isn't bound by them - but even that is not a sound argument. First of all, that is an attempt to make a deductive argument, and therefore already assumes that rules exist (the rules of logic). This is the kind of borrowed-concept argument that Obby and Exuberance are making. One cannot proclaim that God is beyond human capability to reason without attempting to utilize the concept of reason (and, as far as I know, both Obby and Exuberance are human). Therefore, they invoke the very means of pursuing the truth that they try to explain away.

Also, if God created the laws of the universe, he would have to do so by some means. There has to be a way for him to create it if it came into existence by his hand. If that means is random, then God did not truly create the universe, random chance did. If that means is not random, then there must be some kind of rules governing it (e.g. the rule that the universe doesn't come into existence randomly). Since the rules exist, either God created the rules, or he didn't. If God created the rules, then the argument reaches an infinite-regression of rule creating, which is problematic. If God didn't create the rules, then I think that shows that there are things he cannot do.

Basically, I think the only good definition for omnipotence would be "the ability to do anything logically viable." I think God could be omnipotent, but I don't think he could create square circles.

#413716 38 2008-03-04T04:44:57Z Atilla
There is a difference between "we do not know how to explain this using logic", "this is logically impossible" and "logic does not apply to this". If you do not know how to explain something using logic, there may well be a logical explanation which you are unaware of or lack the background knowledge or mental capacity to grasp. In contrast, if something is logically impossible, you know (or, at least, believe you know) how logic should be applied to the situation, and under the dictates of logic the specified circumstance cannot occur. If logic does not apply to something, on the other hand, then it is exempt from any logical reasoning, including this one, which is why everything gets confusing.

For example, we know of no explanation for the fact that electrons have a mass of 9.11 x 10^-31 kg. However, it's certainly not logically impossible for electrons to have that mass - quite the contrary! Nor do we believe electrons are exempt from logic. In short, "unexplained" is different from "unexplainable", and both differ from "does not need explanation".

Furthermore, there's a difference between something existing and its existence being relevant to us. If God is beyond logic and therefore incomprehensible to humans, then it's not clear that there is any point talking about Him since there is no guarantee that we can gain any understanding of Her, or indeed that It would wish us to understand Them. In such circumstances, ignoring God completely would seem to be as theologically sound as worship...
