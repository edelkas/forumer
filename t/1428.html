We just wanted to make a formal announcement that no, we're not dead [yet]
Also, we wanted to keep you all up-to-date on what's happening around here.


The not-so-good news:
we will be finishing 1.4 in the not-too-distant future, but it's not our current priority. 1.4 will be mostly the same as 1.3, with changes being more "improvements to the program"-type things, and not really any major features added.

For instance:
better editor
possible integration with numa
ability to play user-created levels/episodes in normal play
more unlockables, levels, etc.
more features for browsing highscores

In all probability there won't be any exciting changes such as new enemies, objects, etc.

Not that we wouldn't like to add things, but the reality is that (being our first big project) N is very messy and hard to change. it ends up being a choice between "spend X hours adding a feature to a new game" or "spend 2X hours adding the same feature to N."

We feel that N is pretty well as it should be; while lacking weapons, missing many puzzle-like types of objects which could be fun (like falling platforms and conveyor belts), etc. it still seems [at least to us] like more or less like a complete package.

Things could be added, but nothing major is missing.

We prefer to keep working on new stuff, both because it's more fun and interesting, and also because new ideas work better when they're the basis around which a game is designed, rather than crammed into an existing game. Once we release the source code, you can take matters into your own hands

We really appreciate all of your comments and suggestions for things which could be added to or changed in N, and we will definitely keep them in mind, both for our current projects as well as a more distant sequel to N (name pending: N 2: Judgement Day)....

Pushable objects, color-coded keys/locks, conveyor belts, weapons, water, ice, ropes, etc. are ideas we will remember and try to fit into any game which would benefit from them.


The good news:
since we're not currently working on N v1.4, we're working on other stuff!

Our last tutorial on N will be finished in the next week, and... we're almost finished a new game, Ultramagnetic

Wait!!

Before you get too excited, we should mention that it's a very different game from N -- it was started as part of a 2-week contest in Sept., and was designed to be our version of Pac-Man [But it's [b]not[/b] a Pac-Man clone! It'll be very different!!]

Many developers spend a lot of time preparing screenshots, websites, etc. for games that aren't even done -- we prefer to wait until we have something to actually show you before making a big deal about it

So when we have a playable beta version, we'll post here so you can help us test it.

----------------------------------------------------------

Some vague news:
there are 2-3 other game ideas that we're working on. We can't really tell you anything about them because we haven't written much code -- mostly we're trying to get various systems working so that we know what's possible.

The way we try to do things is:
develop some rough ideas for a game
develop technology which will enable us to effect those ideas
design the game by taking the original ideas and refining them using the new knowledge of what our technology can and can't do

When we made N, we had already developed the collision/physics system with a tile-based platform game in mind, so it was relatively easy to make a game on top of that. We're currently in the middle of working on a new engine, which will hopefully be useful for several games. Our main idea is to build the components one by one and use small games as tests of the components. Making these minigames is important since it will hopefully prevent us from falling into the "always-developing-technology-yet-never-releasing-games" black hole, which many developers unfortunately get sucked into

For instance, Ultramagnetic was the proof-of-concept for our new collision/geometry system.

One of our games in design (pending name: Wave Upon Wave of Killbots) will be a proof-of-concept for AI and physics systems.

Another game in design (pending name: Fields... Endless Fields, Where Human Beings are no Longer Born -- We are Grown) will be a proof-of-concept for our taking-over-the-world and enslaving-humanity systems


In the end we hope to have a nice engine. The idea is that instead of spending a lot of time building technology for each game, we spend a bit time building technology ONCE, and then using it for many games, so please bear with us!

We'd also like to try using a language which supports 3D accelerators, since many of our ideas are constrained by the limits of the flashplayer


In closing:
when we made N, we did it because we wanted to prove to ourselves that we could make a real video game, and also because there was a game we really wanted to play that didn't yet exist. We didn't expect so many people to share our taste in games

We think it's really awesome that so many of you are fans because it helps motivate us to continue making games. We're big fans of freeware and we want to continue the tradition of small teams making fun games using exciting new ideas, and then releasing them for free, for no reason other than the sheer delight of making and playing games!

However, the downside to all this is that we can only work on our games part-time, because in order to make money with which to live, we have to keep full-time jobs in the "not-making-games" sector

While we'd of course love to be able to quit our jobs and just make our games full-time as a living, this isn't realistic -- the only way that we'd be able to make a living creating games right now is if we joined a large games company (e.g. Electronic Aarts), or if we were contracted by a publisher or client to make games for them

In the former case, the games we would produce would contain almost none of our ideas, since we'd only be a tiny part of a large team, working assembly-line-style. Thus, it would be the same as working at our current jobs, because at the end of the day we'd still have to use our own time to get our ideas realised. In fact, it would be even worse than our current jobs, because we'd be working on games all day and thus would probably not relish the thought of going home and working on games some more. We'd most likely get burnt out and spend our lives as two of the many poor nameless automatons that crank out major titles like Spiderman 8: The Franchise Continues

In the latter case, the games we would produce would likewise contain almost none of our ideas, since our clients would be able to dictate what we should and shouldn't do. The game ideas that business/financing people come up with are likely to be either completely hilarious, or depressingly stupid, depending on how you look at it This only stands to reason, since their one concern is making a return on their investment; thus the measure of how "good" the resulting game is becomes equivalent to the measure of how large a profit it can generate, and not how fun, original, or addictive it is


In both cases, we wouldn't really be achieving our goal, since we wouldn't be realising our ideas, but merely helping to fabricate someone else's (probably terrible) ideas See the [ corporate ] section of our website for more thoughts on that.


What we're trying to say in this long ramble is that we're committed to making the absolute best games we can, because most of our favorite games/music/etc. have been made the same way: by people who are trying to realise their vision and create what they wnt to create, without contamination by marketing or business concerns. The only thing that should matter is how fun the game is, period.

We really appreciate the fact that so many of you enjoy N despite the fact that it wasn't on the cover of PCGamer and doesn't feature lifelike 3D graphics. We hope the reason you like N is because it's fun to play

We remember that, before gaming became a billion-dollar industry full of people whose only goal and only skill is making more money, creating fun games is [or at least originally [i]was[/i]] the goal of all game developers, as thinking of fun new ideas was their primary skill, and non-game developers weren't a part of making games.

If someday the developers once again regain control of their craft, then maybe we'll be able to make a living doing this Until then, we'll remain independent since it's absolutely the only way to make sure that the games we produce are good. Well... the only other way to do that would be to somehow become ghosts and possess John Carmack, in which case we'd have the power to follow our vision and just not care about the damn marketing people .

Aaaaaaaanyway,
we're still trying to figure out if there's some way we can make money from games without having to give up control of our ideas. Hopefully someday we'll find a way. If you have any thoughts, please let us know!

Thanks for reading this terribly long rant,
Mare and Raigan
