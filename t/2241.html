Hooray! I like this sort of thread, mainly because I have answers that sorta make sense.

Also, I don't think the "don't revive month old topics" rule should apply here at all - if someone thinks they have something new to add to a discussion, there's no reason why they should just drop it or make a new thread merely because the old thread got old.

Of course, spamming crap into old threads is a bad thing, but a lot of you did that anyway - you just said "no i dont" or "yes i do" with no reasons or reasoning attached, which is quite pallid and spammy. It's like you're not actually thinking about it at all, you just don't believe in reincarnation because. Full stop.

Anyway...

TheDailyLlama, w.r.t your post on how us being here is impossible: It's obviously not. The thing is, that sort of thinking is sort of self-defeating (not to mention usually leads up to the initial creator argument). I mean, you go on and on about how it's nearly impossible for us to be here, when the fact that we are is quite self-evident. For an answer (sorta) as to why we are here as opposed to an infinitesimal amount of nothingness, you might want to read Stephen Baxter's (I think) Manifold: Time. I think that most of what he says has been shown to be incorrect, but that involves physics and maths that make my brain hurt. His point is still quite interesting. (it's a really good science fiction book too, of course, otherwise I wouldn't have read it in the first place.) What he says, basically, is that this might not be the first universe there has ever been, nor the only one there will ever be. Even without delving into the whole multiverse thing, well, you'd basically have an infinite amount of time in which things can happen. And, though you might not know this, even chances that "are sorta below nil" become certainties when exposed to infinite amounts of time; that is, the probability of an event with a nonzero probability of happening at any given time happening as time goes to infinity is 100%.

Wow, that sentence doesn't make any sense. Ummm... How about, "everything that can happen will happen if you give it enough time to happen in"? I think that works better.

And, of course, that might be why we're here. We had a nonzero probability of happening, and there was a lot of time for us to happen in...

Which actually segways nicely into the reason I have for believing in reincarnation. Even if one were to discard the possibility of there ever being any other universes ever, well, this universe is still gonna last for a while, and it's my opinion that eventually someone or something will figure out how to transplant psyches across time, somehow. I have no clue how it could happen; you could die one day and wake up surrounded by Cthulu-like beings in a body which is not your own, or by the beautiful priestesses of Nymph 3 or something. The means and manner don't matter; the important part is that if it's possible, it might happen in the fullness of time.

Now, I realize that this isn't what most people think of when they think of reincarnation - there's no cockroach for a body involved, after all, and you get to keep all your old memories. That's a different argument, but I don't really think that sort of reincarnation is very fun.

Besides, the word "reincarnation" taken literally just means "being made flesh once more" - nothing about memories in that.

One could, of course, argue that you might not be you at that point for whatever reason, so I've looked up a quote from Neil Gaiman:

If you don't let me in, I will turn you into a demon half-face waitress night-club lady with a crush on her boss, and I'll make it so you've been that from the beginning of time to now and you'll never ever know if you were anything else and it will itch inside your head worse than little bugses." - Delirium, in The Kindly Ones, written by Neil Gaiman.

(the neat part is that Delirium is threatening to turn that being into the thing it actually is and... nevermind.)
