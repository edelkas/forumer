Better yet. Get a job. If you don't have something in mind that you want to do, pick up the most flexible degree you can find. Finance, accountancy, advertising, marketing, computer science, management, public relations, design. Those are all very flexible degrees and can esnure that you have a job relatively quickly once you're out of school. Don't make the mistake of aiming too high or biting off more than you can chew. You will get a job and then work up from there. No one has a "career" any more these days. You'll have a series of semi-related occupations as you age, which may or may not increase in wealth and benefits, but I wouldn't be quick to label that path a "career". Just focus on where you are, and natural events will lead you somewhere else which is better later on.

An MBA isn't something you get on the side so that the world thinks you can start your own company. To start your own company, you need to be born to rich people who also started their own company. Sure, getting an MBA will do you wonders, but consider if the extra work you do and extra money you spend on graduate tuition is going to be worth and increase in income. Most companies in the business sector don't scale up to magnificent levels just because you have an MBA. Most of that extra income will go into paying off extra loans, assuming you've properly invested your graduate expenses and have done well in all those classes. And you can't always count on that.

Anyway, in sum, you can't always love your job; but if you aim for a relatively generic degree to field you a large wealth of options when you hit the job market.

You can always go back to school later on, too, when your work and your lifestyle has made you more focused and given you some degree of expertise.
#342233 10 2007-07-12T21:29:19Z Hendor
I'm starting University this September in Kinesiology. At the moment, I think I am most interested in medicine. Tons of reasons for that: The money, the travel oppurtunities, the satisfaction of helping people... There was a pre-med course at my University, but I didn't have the 92% average to get into it. I had a measly 86%.

About high school courses... It does matter a bit in my opinion. In my last year, I took absolutely everything that a scientific university course could possibly require. My load was Physics, Bio, Chem, Calculus, Discrete Geometry, English and Kinesiology. My university course only required one math course and one of Bio or Chem. While my average could have been better if I took easy courses like economics or law, if I hadn't taken all the sciences that I did, I would have closed a lot of doors. For example, I'm thinking about minoring in Astrophysics. I would never be able to do this had I not taken both Calculus and Physics.

Of course, things in the States might be very different than up here in the Arctic. We don't take SATs. Most important thing really is to make sure you actually get into university/college. Don't get obsessed with taking everything, blowing up and not having the grades to be accepted.

Good Luck.


PS - As far as money goes, I lucky out hugely. My dad is on faculty at the University. I save $330 per course I take. As I'm taking 10 courses this year, I save over $3000 on my tuition. Also, I have a $1000 scholarship for entering with an average over 85. All told, I have to pay the University just over $1000 dollars, which covers both tuition and all other fees.
