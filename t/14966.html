This is a thread to describe how you see the overall style of mapmakers you find noteworthy for whatever reason. I'll start.

Atramentis's style is, I would call it industrial. His maps have a lot going on, and usually look cool, and there's an interesting path, but often that path is very narrow, and there's not a lot of mucking about to do.

atob/pariah: One of the best mapmakers ever by any and every measure. Very clean and open style, with no superfluous objects and lots of routes and options. Sometimes a lot of the paths don't quite work, or the best routes are uglier and less flowy than they could be, or the map is really touchy, making for a merely pretty good map. But atob's best maps are sublime, great at every skill level and amount of play, with difficulty as hard as you want with speed and gold and good routes for every skill level and practice amount.

tktktk's style is skill-oriented. His mind has been warped by one too many corner jumps and now, sort of like Neo, he opens the editor and sees corner jumps and chimney climbs. But, he has good taste, and that means that when he really puts his mind to it, and tests and tests, he can make a really really good (and hard) *coughframeworkcough* map.

Sweep's style is very creative. He makes a map when he has an *idea* and then he naturally approaches implementing it sort of sideways, so the implementation is interesting but sometimes a little off. But when the implementation *isn't* off, it's really something. Like that dolphin map, one of the greatest maps of all time.
