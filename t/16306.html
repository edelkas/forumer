#304433 13 2007-03-11T16:45:56Z iangb
It's a case of 'what was there before the universe', keron.

All we know for sure (allthough Macaddict would probably disagree with me on this) is that several billion years ago (give or take a few million), space and time as we currently understand them originated from a singularity (or near-singularity) event, commonly known as the Big Bang. 'Before' that (and now terms like 'before' get a little tricky)... the most popular theory is that there was void, in the truest sense - no space or time - not vacuum, but no actual space for a vaccum to be in, or time in which it can exist. However, another theory is that there was another universe before our current one, which suffered a 'big crunch' (gravity overcomes everything and the universe returns so a singularity state), triggering our own Big bang. That theory means that there need be no 'start' to time - it would just be a repeating loop, with a big bang/crunch starting the whole thing over again. There would be no 'uncaused cause' - the whole thing would just recurr.
It's also possible that each of the previous universes (should they have existed) had different physical constants, meaning that the life-supportingness of our universe does not make it 'special', it just makes it an inevitable consequence of an infintely changing universe.

Obby didn't like this because our current universe appears to be accelerating in it's expansion, and not slowing down. This suggests that either the crunch/bang theory is definitely wrong, or that there is still a lot we don't know about the universe. Personally, I'm going with the second option. I can argue a bunch of reasons with Obby if he wants, but right now I feel like I've typed enough.

Oh, and caero, with regards to "When I think of the Universe I think of the Universe itself, not the matter inside of it" - potentially, you are absolutely right. Have a look (warning: may get technical).
