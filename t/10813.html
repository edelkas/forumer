Quote: Keron Cyst, June 25, 2006 06:17 pm
Evolution dictates that if it isn't useful, it shall be thrown out.
No, no, no! That's not how evolution works! You're thinking of it from an "intelligent design" perspective. Contrary to the slogan, evolution is not "survival of the fittest", merely "survival of the fit enough". As long as an extra finger isn't killing you, it'll probably hang around - the species doesn't think "Hey, this finger is unneccesary. Let's get rid of it". Not all evolved features are optimal - otherwise no further evolution could occur. Many just happen by sheer chance. For example, the crossed air and food pipes of land animals lead to choking, and are clearly not the best design imaginable. But we have them because that was the mutation that first lead to air-breathing life. At the time this windpipe developed, there was no competition on land, so you could happily survive and thrive even with your ill-designed breathing apparatus. By the time the land was heavily populated enough for competition to play a significant part, the crossed pipes were prevalent. Of course, it's possible that a mutation with seperate eating and breating pipes could occur, but other factors would be more important since choking was not a primary cause of death. The numerical superiority of the crossed-pipe creatures would mean they would overwhelm the non-crossed lineage, because the advantage afforded by the latter was too small. Fingers are a similar case; we had to have some finite number of fingers, and it turned out to be five. Since we now have numerical superiority to the six-fingered mutation, and they recieve no significant advantages from their extra finger, we will form the majority until there is some pressing need for extra fingers (or, in a freak accident, a meteor wipes out 99% of the five-fingers and leaves the six-fingers unscathed). Since it's not actually killing them either, six-fingered people can (and do) exist as a minority.

As for 3... that's one of the worst arguments I've heard. Firstly, not only are cells not conscious, but evolution is not a conscious process. Fish do not suddenly decide "Hey, I wonder what's on the land? Let's grow some legs and see!" Nor does evolution have a definite end point or goal, or because being alive is in some way more enjoyable than being dead, so creatures want to live. One of the common misconceptions about evolution is that it is driven by some kind of imperative to reach a state which is "desirable" or "superior" in some way, which is entirely untrue; it is merely a byproduct of how the world works. Consider flipping a coin twice. You may end up with one head and one tail, two heads, or two tails. The first is the most likely; if you repeated it a million times, you would expect that there you would end up with one head and one tail more often than with two heads, or two tails. This does not mean that the macrostate with one head and one tail is somehow superior, merely that it is more prevalent because of the laws of statistics. This is also the priciple behind entropy and much of thermodynamics.
