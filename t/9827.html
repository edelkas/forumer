#8836 1 2006-05-20T04:31:01Z Teedle
Allright, this isn't a poll, because I want reasons and opinions and stuff more than plain yes/ no answers.

So, here's the thing.

I want to leave NUMA.

Now, I don't want to stop making maps entirely, allright? I'll continue to make them, but submitting them to NUMA holds little appeal to me anymore.

The reasons run thusly-

1. Sniping.
Sweet corpsified lord am I sick of seeing my maps go down from 5 to 3.5 in one swell vote, and drop down the ladder when they're bombarded by people who think that the maps deserve less than a 4.5, and thus figure the best way to ensure this happens is to give them a 0. I hate sniping with a passion.

In fact, Sniping has meant that this map (underground meteor, back from when I was new) is my second highest rated one in my collection (under improbability drive, which has too many ratings for sniping to really work), purely because it hasn't attracted the same attention that the rest of my highly rated maps get, mainly because it's such an old map before the days when I made some of the really good ones I have today.

Okay, you may say 'Ratings don't matter, just ignore them", but they DO. It was what drove me to try to improve myself on NUMA, and what made me addicted to the place- the competitive spirit, the creation of something from nothing and seeing how well your creation does, the sense of accomplishment when you spend a lot of time on a map and it gets a 4.5/5.

To me, ratings mean a lot. I know it's sort of stupid, but it's the way I feel. And it became the only real reason I continued making maps after my first one, 'towerful'. And ratings are screwed around with so much on NUMA at the moment, that they've become nothing but an endless source of frustration.

2. Maps that recieve low ratings.
From time to time I'll just create a nice, little, quirky level that I'm quite fond of, that isn't exactly brilliant but it sort of nice on a small scale.
I love levels like these. People on NUMA don't.

If a map isn't big, awesome, or outstanding in any way- if it is just a solid example of enjoyable gameplay- I find it tends to get a low rating and a whole lot of people abusing people who think that it's good.

There seems to be something sinful about giving people a high rating for a map that's merely enjoyable, and this annoys me to no end. I mean, sure, I could just ignore the ratings and submit it anyway (and, were I a better man, I probably would) but somehow, I tend to not like the idea of compromising my overall reputation and author ranking by making these maps.

I know this is flawed and dumb, but this is how I tend to treat submitting maps to NUMA, despite my better judgement.

3. Making episodes, levelpacks, and stuff is more fun.
And you know why? In making episodes, you can do far more than you can using NUMA for the same sort of system. You can make things far more complex and involved.
Also, I *love* playing around with difficulties both within episodes and within total columns, tweaking them untill I think they're just right. Whereas, on NUMA, difficulty is often
-Hard to justify
-Complained about (which doesn't happen when it's 'taken in context')
-Ignored entirely and totally insignificant

Also, in levelpacks, nice little quirky maps have their place. As do easy maps. And maps that aren't made on a huge scale, but made simply because they're sorta enjoyable, if not totally groundbreaking. And these sorts of maps- taken in context- tend to make a lot more sense and seem a lot more fitting.

Why I don't want to compromise.

Because, were I to do this, it'd feel slightly cheap having all of my best (or all of my NUMA maps in general) in the levelpacks, except padded out with 'filler' that wouldn't do well on NUMA. No, I think I'd prefer to move entirely to one or t'other.

The case for NUMA.

Here's my problem. Using my LA3^3 levelpack as an example, I can say for certain that less people played the maps as would have were they to have been submitted to NUMA individually. And very few people could be bothered playing them all, preffering to take their maps in small, individual doses from NUMA than in large doses in levelpacks.

To some extent, I feel the same way in relation to map packs and NUMA.

NUMA is simply... easier and more accessable, whereas map packs are a daunting task to start getting into. They're also a huge daunting task to make, and it's something of a letdown when almost nobody plays them.

So... have you any thoughts, opinions, or advice on any of this?
