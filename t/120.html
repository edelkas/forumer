Ninjas never had access to armor. In fact, they didn't have access to much. Do you think they used nunchakus because they were good weapons? No. If they could've gotten swords, they would've used them. Instead, they had to make do with farming implements (like the sai) and plow components (like the tonfa). The samurai who occupied their towns prevented them from owning any weapons. They had to create weapons from what they had. No, I just have a brain and a capacity for research. Nunchaku are considered relatively inefficient weapons even by martial artists because they are hard to use and ineffective against long bladed weapons - like, say, the katana you would expect a samurai to wield. Weapons such as the sai (which can be used as a sword breaker), bo (quarterstaff), tonfa and kunai were favoured. The bo, for example, is easier to wield, good for defence, and has longer reach than nunchaku, while kunai have a wide range of uses, are easy to conceal, and can be strapped to a staff to create an acceptable spear (contrary to popular belief, they were not generally used as throwing weapons). My statement was intended as a comment on the fact that many people misunderstand nunchaku and think they are some kind of ultimate weapon because they're all cool and exotic-looking, when in reality an unromantic, 6-foot long pole is more practical. I also point out that modern nunchaku are more effective than ancient ones, since they have been altered extensively because they are used exclusively as a weapon, and would be useless as a farming implement. While nuchaku were certainly used as weapons at some stage (although possibly not by ninja), they were by no means a weapon of choice, even among the limited arsenal of a peasant.

Oh, and sai and kunai are both metal, although they are not sharpened. Ninja did have access to some metal tools.
