#319754 6 2007-05-09T10:49:50Z Atilla
I, of course, am not American, but I'm answering anyway.

1. Do you think citizens should have the rights to criticize elected officials like the president, governor, or city officials?

Yes. The alternative is the path to tyranny.

2. Do you think burning the American flag in anger or protest is an acceptable form of free speech or do you see it as an unacceptable act?

Why not? The country is not so fragile it cannot withstand a bit of fabric being burnt. It's really just a way to make clear your extreme dissatisfaction with the state of the nation. Not terribly tactful, but hardly worthy of banning.

3. Do you think religious beliefs that people hold sacred should be open to comedy, criticism, or insults?

Yes. Religion is a matter of opinion, as are non-religious worldviews, and are therefore subject to criticism. From a practical view, making religion untouchable just doesn't work. For a start, you can just say "I believe God gives me the right to criticise other religions" and no one is allowed to disagree.

4. Do you think books that contain harsh language or controversial subject matter (sexual refrences, fantasy/witch craft, violence, etc...) should be banned from school libraries?

Witchcraft and fantasy, no. For start, that includes things like "The Wizard of Oz" and "Hansel and Gretel". I doubt many people have been turned into blood-drinking, Satan-worshipping murderers because of such stories. Sex, violence, and obscenities should be banned from primary schools, perhaps, but not at secondary school. People need to learn about these things as they mature, and literature can be an important aid to their personal development.

5. Do you think that speakers or groups, whose views may be unpopular or hateful should be allowed to speak their mind?

Unpopular, certainly. Hateful, I'm less happy about, but probably.

6. Would you support limiting the jokes people can tell if it would help to decrease insensitive comments about racism, sexism, and intolerance (No ethnic jokes, blonde jokes, jokes about lifestyles, disabilities, etc...) ?

Yes, though I would prefer to do so simply through social pressure rather than legislation.

7. Do you think that singers/bands should be prevented from writing or performing songs that have profanity or other controversial lyrics in them?

No. Sale of such music might be restricted for minors (as it already is, I believe) but other than that, no.

8. Should the internet be regulated so that dangerous, obscene, hateful, pornographic, etc... websites would not be permitted?

Somewhat. Not block that sort of material outright, but perhaps prevent access by minors. Sites about the construction of bombs and suchlike might be banned, since there's not really a reason for anyone to know that other than behaviour which is likely to harm others.

#337460 21 2007-07-01T13:13:40Z firestorm
I'm not american, but I'll answer.


1. Do you think citizens should have the rights to criticize elected officials like the president, governor, or city officials?
Of course, otherwise it gets like 1984

2. Do you think burning the American flag in anger or protest is an acceptable form of free speech or do you see it as an unacceptable act?
I don't see what the fuss is about burning the american flag. It's just some coloured fabric. You can see it as some symbol if you want, but all that's truly there is some coloured fabric.

3. Do you think religious beliefs that people hold sacred should be open to comedy, criticism, or insults?
Yes. It'd get ridiculous otherwise; you could probably say that many things count as sacred to some people, and then everyone would have to be really careful about any jokes they make in case it offended someone. People have to be able to take some flak from those who disagree with them, else is what they're standing for really worth it?

4. Do you think books that contain harsh language or controversial subject matter (sexual refrences, fantasy/witch craft, violence, etc...) should be banned from school libraries?
Fantasy/witchcraft? Witchcraft is similar to a religion, and I doubt they'd even try to ban e.g. christian books from school libraries (even though religion is a controversial subject matter). You can't ban some controversial stuff and not others, just because they'd be a bigger outcry depending on what they banned, so unless it's going to be 'free speech heavily biased to what the majority of the public wants and banning the rest', you have to allow it all. I do however agree that books with lots of violence/explicit sexual references should be banned from primary school libraries (not mild ones though; I read ones with mild references when I was younger, and didn't understand that it was a reference to anything much, until I was older anyway).

5. Do you think that speakers or groups, whose views may be unpopular or hateful should be allowed to speak their mind?
Unpopular? Of course; it's not about favouritism. Hateful? Well... yes, as long as the speech is peaceful in intent e.g. they're not trying to incite a riot.

6. Would you support limiting the jokes people can tell if it would help to decrease insensitive comments about racism, sexism, and intolerance (No ethnic jokes, blonde jokes, jokes about lifestyles, disabilities, etc...) ?
Hmm... jokes that hinge around some prejudiced point, maybe. Ones that have some truth behind them (e.g. about lifestyles, when a reasonable amount of people in that 'group' have that lifestyle) shouldn't be limited though, same about disabilities. I'm not really sure what I feel about this one. If I had to pick yes or no, I pick no, though.

7. Do you think that singers/bands should be prevented from writing or performing songs that have profanity or other controversial lyrics in them?
Of course not. Music is another art, and I somehow doubt that they're going to suddenly ban all paintings, statues etc that show nudity, which is simply another art with profanity in it. They can't pick favourites if it's going to be free speech (though paintings, statues etc aren't actually speech).

8. Should the internet be regulated so that dangerous, obscene, hateful, pornographic, etc... websites would not be permitted?
No, but it should only be possible to find them if you're actually looking for them, i.e. no adverts to them.
