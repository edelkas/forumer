The name's kinda sappy, but it's supposed to portray innocence, in a way. Please, comment and enjoy.


Angel Wings

Marten strode slowly but confidently to the cliffs edge. His dark and disheveled hair clung to his head from perspiration. He looked to the horizon. The sun shone brilliantly in the cloudless, liquid sapphire sky, and for a fleeting moment he imagined there to be another ocean mirroring the one that stretched endlessly out over a hundred feet below him. A warm breeze carried the aroma of fresh fish coming from one of the nearby cottages. Martens stomach growled at him resentfully at having been neglected for most of the day. Food can wait, he thought. This is much more important.

A boy and a girl from the village, each five or six years of age, were sparring playfully in the lush green grass not far away. Seeing Marten, they raced toward him cheerily and brimming with curiosity.

Whats that thing? It looks like a giant bird, the girl remarked.

She was pointing to a wooden contraption, long, slender, and sandy brown, that Marten clutched firmly with both hands. It did in some ways resemble a gull in flight, with wings of tightly stretched cloth. However, in place of the fowls body there was only a skeleton, the framework, with a sizeable hollow inhabited partially by various odd gears and mechanisms.

Yeah, shes a bird, agreed Marten proudly, And shell fly, too.

The pair stared at him for several moments with wide eyes that seemed to pierce the thin armor of his shallow confidence. In spite of the long months he had spent designing and constructing the bird in secret, doubts still throbbed painfully in the back of his mind. He pushed them away. She would fly, and so would he.

You think that thing will fly? retorted the boy. Youre just as crazy as your dad. Crazier, even.

Martens face immediately grew dark. His father was an inventor, but he was scorned by most of the village for not doing his share of the work. Only a few recognized that his creations were the only thing that kept them alive; the rest wrote him off as a daft and worthless tinkerer who consumed without producing. Thinking of this, Marten remembered his private vow to himself and to his father. He recalled the reason he had taken on this burden in the first place, and his resolve hardened.

Well, Ill be the one to show them just how crazy he is. Him and me both.

The sharp ache of adrenaline surged slowly through Martens muscles. He strapped himself into the bizarre aerial vessel, and took a few paces away from the precipice. Observing the sky again, he spotted a few speckled wispy clouds off in the distance. The sunlight streamed through them, painting them with whimsical pinks and oranges. He took this as a good omen, one of hope.

No turning back, Marten told himself. He took a few steps from the cliff, put on his most determined expression, and all at once sprinted at the void, attacking it before hesitations fingers could snare his mind in their cold grasp. As both feet left the ground, time appeared to slow, stop, rewind. It came to a halt at a long-suppressed memory of when Marten was but a toddler. His mother held him delicately in her arms. He giggled gleefully as she bobbed him up and down in midair. Without warning, her firm hold faltered, leaving him to hurtle to the merciless wooden floor below him. Just in time, her strong hands snatched him from harm, but as Marten snapped back to reality the memory was now playing out once again, and panic viciously engulfed him.

Marten plummeted sharply to the choppy swells below. Coming to his senses, he tugged wildly on two cords, jolting his craft to life. His trajectory flattened out, dangerously close to what seemed to be a vast expanse of death itself. A warm draft of wind caught the curved wings. To Martens astonishment, he began to ascend, though with painful sluggishness. Glints of silver waves caught his eye; they seemed infinitely more benevolent than they had been the moment before.

Looking to his right, he grinned as he saw the island, floating proudly and defiantly above water of ruffled silk. Martens isle was home to no more than a hundred people, and to their knowledge nothing existed apart from this odd mass of earth that gravitys stifling sheet of tyranny had overlooked. The lofty plateau was scattered with a variety of emerald vegetation and with small dwellings that nonchalantly spouted their gray smoke. At the edges, the cinnamon earth angled magnificently inward to form a jagged point at the base of the isle. Because there resided on the island no sufficient body of water, Marten naturally could not swim.

As he circled around, taking in his environment from this new perspective, it gradually dawned on him that there was little hope of ever rising high enough to return to dry land. Nightfall was making its approach, and he knew that the frigid air would be reluctant to support his already wavering craft. Looking helplessly to his island of refuge, he spotted two black vertical lines stretching into the sea, barely visible in the decaying light. Fishing ropes.

Marten worked vigorously at the controls, steering the bird sharply in the direction of his discovery. As he neared them he realized that he was hovering inches from the deep blue wasteland. It was impossible to reach his destination without plunging into it. He winced and braced for impact. As the icy doom devoured his body, eating its way from his feet up to his waist, he struggled for the line that swayed gently an arms length away. The waterlogged cloth, formerly his sole ally, now stubbornly held him back, and Marten was faced with a grim dilemma.

With no apparent alternative, Marten unfastened himself and paddled frenziedly towards the rope. After hauling himself from the water, he scrambled clumsily heavenward with no thought in his mind but of dry land. It took every kernel of strength in his body to propel himself to the towering summit. Having at long last arrived at his promised land, he sprawled onto the coarse grass, shuddering with cold and breathless exhaustion.

The two children who had shown such skeptical interest in Martens endeavors presently greeted him with enthusiastic shouts and mad frolicking.

Marten! We saw you! You were flying! they exclaimed together nearly unintelligible shrieks. They continued in their celebration for quite some time, but their excitement fizzled as they sensed Martens disheartened demeanor. He was looking away, weary and filled with a vague feeling of betrayal. He glanced dreamily up at familiar ivory stars with subdued acceptance.

Looks like the bird failed me after all.

Youre wrong, replied the girl. Her rounded face was saturated with an expression of complete conviction. She didnt fail you, and shes no bird either. Shes an angel. You rested your life in her hands, and she protected you. You were flying on angel wings.
