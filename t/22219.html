#19889 1 2008-04-11T05:56:34Z brocerius
I was passing through Laptopia today, when who did I happen to come across but a ninja! He rather stood out from the crowd, being about two meters tall, bright teal coloured, and made wholly of triangles, and at first I was almost too scared to go up to him. When I finally plucked up the courage to say hello he was quite friendly, and he agreed to give me an interview later in the day. The rest of my day seemed to drag on, and I waited listlessly for work to be over. I couldn't wait for the interview - I knew this was a great opportunity for me, and I was not a little apprehensive.

When, just before three o'clock, I was sitting in a caf at the local desktop I felt I had been jilted - it was mere seconds before three, and the ninja was nowhere in sight. Ninja's are known for their punctuality, and I knew if he wasn't there at three he wasn't coming. Suddenly, just as I was about to give up hope, I saw an indistinct shape leap from the stack of shortcuts opposite me - a towering collection of Microsoft Word pieces, where the administration of Laptopia worked daily - and fall, with practised ease, towards me. For a moment I felt scared - I knew that was too great a height for the ninja to survive. He rushed to the ground, and I had to close my eyes before he hit it. When I opened them, instead of the bloody mess I expected to see, there was a ninja standing, whole and hearty, in front of me.

'Sorry for scaring you' he said, with a smile 'shame you had to close your eyes - that was rather a nice jump I just made off the kerb.'

Phew. I forgot that ninjas have the inexplicable ability to cancel out all previous momentum simply by jumping off a wall, but even if I had remembered I wouldn't have thought he would make one so small - I realised this must be a rather exceptional ninja. No noob had I lucked into here.

I ordered a coffee, and he said he would have a hot chocolate. 'Coffee makes me hyper, so I tend to explode when I drink it' he said, smiling again. 'Shall we start?'

He had a friendly smile which he used often, and all my apprehension of the day vanished quickly. I decided to get a little background on him, so asked where he grew up - 'I come from a little IBM in the middle of nowhere. You won't have heard of it - the town was so small when I was a kid that the local council couldn't even afford to upgrade past Onepointone. Not that it made much difference to us - me and my friends used to get a thwump down to the local Version every day after school and play for hours. It was great - we were only noobs then, but we used to get a whole group together and run around Motherlode like maniacs. Technically you were only allowed to play the levels one ninja at a time, but nobody cared back then.'

So you started your training on Onepointone? 'I wouldn't call it training, heh. We were just kids, messing around, having a laugh - but yeah, I guess it put me in good stead for later, when I joined some local competitions and started playing for real. That isn't where I started playing though - back when I was in primary school we didn't even have a local version! Some of the older kids would jump a thwump heading to Ebaumsville, where you could play for free anytime, but kids weren't allowed to go on them without an adult, and my mother never approved of me playing. To be honest, I never like Ebaumsville - there's something inherently shitty about the place. And my parents would rarely take me there anyway - they always told me that I should finish my studies before I started playing, that it was important to learn the theoretical side of The Way before doing it practically, that being killed over and over again was bad for a developing mind and so on A bore, I can tell you. I was a bit lucky though - my dad had a secret passion for the game, and he used to let me come down to the basement with him if I finished my homework. He had quite a nice collection of NKRs down there, and he used to rig them up into wild levels, and send me into them. He didn't mind me getting killed - 'character building' he used to call it. We had a few floorguards in the back garden too, and I used to go out and play with them in the evenings. Those little buggers used to chase me till they were blue in the face! I soon learned how to outsmart them though, and the sight of me being zapped 50 feet into the air, which so alarmed my mother, soon became a thing of the past.' He finished with a hearty laugh, and took a sip of his hot chocolate. It was too hot, apparently, because he fanned his tongue daintily and put it back down. 'I think I'm going to have to nip round the corner for some gold - there's a top-up point just 30 second sprint down the street, so I won't be long. Sorry - I didn't have a chance to grab enough to last me before I came here. If I run out I'll end up back at home, feeling like an idiot, so I better get going.' With that he disappeared from sight with a corner kick off a corner that was, to all extents and purposes, not there. I began to wonder just how good this ninja was...

I was pleased with how the interview was going so far - so many fascinating things to ask about! He seemed to enjoy talking about his past, and was very open. I presume his confidence comes from having an infinite number of lives, and a frankly astounding strength. When you know you could crush the life out of most living things with a flick you probably don't need to be afraid of them - but I never felt in the least afraid of him. I know how strong the pacifist emotion is in the adepts of The Way, and this ninja clearly knew it backwards. His speed, his agility, his precision! It would be terrifying in anyone else, but with ninjas you feel at ease with their power. Its almost a tangible force, radiating from them; I feel stronger already, simply from basking in it!

I looked at my notepad - scribbled in the margins were 'NKRs - Ninja Killing Robots?', 'Thwumps - how dangerous?' and 'Ask about Ebaumsville!' I had plenty to work from, and his talk of competitions had whetted my appetite for more. I took a sip of my coffee, and laughed to myself at the memory at his effeminate tongue-fanning. An amusing little reminder of how human they actually are, beneath the steely muscles and titanium joints.

A teal blur was soaring towards me along the street, feet barely touching the ground. He arrived and deposited himself with a smile and a satisfied sigh. 'I feel like I've collected a million dollars! That was refreshing. What was I saying?'

I asked him about the thwumps, and the danger involved in riding them. This earned me another laugh, as he said 'Dangerous? Maybe to a toddler, but as long as you learn not to get to close to the blue side the things are pretty harmless. A friend of mine got crushed by one once - he fell asleep taking a slow one back from Ebaumsville, and when it got to the depot he woke up just in time to be minced! He reincarnated all the way back at the Ebaumsville Version, and had to get on another! Personally I don't like the slow ones. Don't have the patience. I'd rather take a runner, and get there in half the time!' A runner? 'Sorry - colloquial. The fast movers - the attackers, you might call them, even though they are almost purely functional nowadays - are called runners, because you've got to keep running to stay on top. I have no idea why - someone tried to explain it to me once, but it was too technical. You can either take them, or one of the walkers - a retreating thwump, in other words. They are more casual, and we ninjas can sleepwalk pretty easily, so some people take them if they are looking for a nap on the way home.'

I had heard about this phenomenon - since ninjas are so much more active than humans, their relative state of 'rest' might be called a leisurely walk for us.

I thought we would move on from childhood, so I asked what he did after he graduated. 'Actually, I didn't. Failed my exams, never got the theory down. By the exam years I had become a little obsessed - there is nothing in the world like the thrill of tap-dancing over mines, or stroking a passing rocket. Nothing compares to falling for a whole map, just to bounce of a locked door, into a tunnel, and back up to the roof before you can say 'Sweet Jesus that was fast'. Really, I became an adrenaline junky - I was tired of my small-town life and looking for something big, so I packed up my belongings, stole a bunch of gold from my parents, and legged it to Ebaumsville before I even took my exams. I had to go on foot all the way - I knew they would look for me on the thwumps - so it took me a while. By the time I arrived I was desperate, really low on gold. At one point I was on the street, begging for just a few pieces from passing strangers, living from second to second. If I died I would go straight to one of the Ebaumsville reincarnation spots, and I knew they would be being watched. I had to stay alive, so I joined some of the underground tournaments there, playing desperate games in illegal Versions. It wasn't satisfying, but it kept me going. And the fact that I couldn't let myself die meant that i had to become really good, really fast. The amount of levels I went through... unbelievable. I could never do it all again, not without dying at least a couple of times. But back then... I had such unbelievable force of will, I just kept on racing, kept on dodging those lasers and skipping between bullets - before long I became a legend in the underground rings. Ninjas would come from all over the 'Net to challenge me, and I defeated every one.

'Until, at last, came my dying day. I had been playing for weeks, throwing down competitor after competitor. It was rumoured that I was invincible, and by that time I had enough hubris to start thinking it might be true. But there were other rumours growing - a new Version had come out, and there were daring players springing up all over the place, some almost as desperate as me. I grew complacent, so when I heard that a young player, known only as Trib4l, was starting to dominate his way through the rankings I wasn't in the least afraid. Until he set me his challenge. It was only this; 'MTI.'

'MTI was new, unplayed. It had been finished by one of the most feared mapmakers of the time, one who had nearly killed me on many an occasion, and I had been secretly afraid of going for it. Everyone was desperate to see me do it - my friends, because they believed it would be my greatest run. My enemies because they believed it would be my undoing. I had been avoiding it for weeks, making up flimsy excuses. But now, it was unavoidable. I steeled myself, and set to training. I did mine-jumpers for days, ceaselessly. I became almost untouchable, bouncing off the ground on the pixels between mines, without dying once! It wouldn't bee believed if you saw it today, but back then there were no speedhacks, no cheats. We were raw, we were young, and we were dangerous. And finally, the day arrived. It was to be played in my Version - the Version I was champion over, the Version I had defeated so many young hopefuls in. It was to be my most glorious day - or his.

'We entered MTI. Neither of us had seen it before - and neither of us was ready for it. The first corridor stretched ahead, liberally bedecked with mines, glinting evilly from the floor. The spikes and straits of the walls looked dangerous, and the jumps we would have to do - formidable. And I knew this was only an inkling of the full horror of the map. I knew there would be many corridors, and many dangers ahead. I had heard rumours of course... such horrifying rumours, but I couldn't bring myself to believe them. Not until I saw it. But once my eyes had lain upon this first corridor, I knew that the worst was true. I knew thwumps and falls, mines and traps, spikes and slopes and death, death, death was ahead, waiting for me to greet it with a fresh, unknown fear in my heart. So little gold... so far to go...

'I looked to my left, at Trib4l. I felt the fear show itself in my face, and looked desperately for a trace of it in him. He smiled, the starting gun fired, the crowds screamed, and my long learned legs threw me forwards.'



I sat, enraptured by this god, this demon in front of me. His skin had grown dark, black, and I knew he had reverted to his true colour, that he had gone back, within himself, to that terrible time. His eyes were dark, and they stared unseeing into me, as he told of the journey through those perilous paths.



'We soared, side by side, over the first mines. Our hands reached out instinctively and stole the gold from the air, our legs pumped mercilessly at the tips of every spike. We barely touched a slope, flying from peak to peak. I was dazed, by the beauty and evil of the map, by the strength and agility of my adversary, and by the unfathomable speed with which we sped along the first corridor. We flew, together, under the drone, squeezing past its electrifying tendrils, its static making our bodies quail in remembered fear. We glided over the mines following, suspended by nothing but our determination. We flew towards the thwump, kicked oft its corner as it dumbly attacked what wasn't there, and fell. As we soared downwards, a moment of peace came upon me. Here was a moment of repose, a time to gather my shattered confidence and prepare for the onslaught ahead. I saw the thwump below me, and knew what I had to do, and knew that Trib4l knew it too. A shared moment passed between us, and for a second we were allies, not enemies. We were pioneers, legends, destroying this level together. The upcoming thwump passed us soundlessly, and sped towards the roof. It meant nothing to us, and our minds were focused once more on our landing, on setting the next thwump in motion and sprinting along with it. The ground met us, our angles were perfect, and at once we were sprinting towards the left wall. Gold was captured, the approaching thwump was dodged, and we were kicking our way up the leftmost corridor. Here, we were perfect. Our rise was swift; from ledge to ledge we leapt in a frenzy of pixel-accuracy that no future Faneca, no clux or naem would ever surpass. This was an unrecorded beauty, a perfection that would see no submission into the ranks of supposed heroes. This was excellence, and as we embarked upon the next corridor we knew that every witness here today would take something unique with them for the rest of their lives.

'I was too numb with energy to think, and the fear of the start had been replaced with the freedom, with the euphoria of knowing nothing but that your feet will guide your path. The Way was strong in me now, and if I had thought at all it would have been with a glimmer of hope that I could make it. But Trib4l was beside me, and his feet were as sure as mine, and his mind was as much a blank. He had joined me in the mindless plateau of perfection, and if ever I gained a pixel on him he would take it back in milliseconds. The thwump was no object, and I soared past it unhindered, smashing my fist into the glory of the exit switch. For a moment I was alone, and a flutter of joy crept into me - had he been caught? Had the thwump conquered my rival? Joy almost took me, until I saw a black figure accelerate past - he had let the thwump squeeze him! He had caught a boost of acceleration that I had missed! For the merest of seconds I almost failed - my feet missed a kick and I slowed to a mere sprint. I saw him drop down the next fall, saw the blackness in his eyes as he fell, and almost lost hope. My mind had lost its plateau, and I heard the roars of the crowd pressing into my ears, pounding my body. I was inches from abandon when I reached the fall, but I had been handed a gift from my young enemy - the thump he triggered had almost reached the top, and I dived forward with one last hope in me. I hit the bottom left corner of the rising thwump with such speed and precision that I was thrown down the fall, and onto the right angle that waited me with such speed as to almost kill me - my knees buckled for a moment, and bent.

'My eyes caught his shape ahead, delayed for the merest moment by the coming thwump, and my bending legs straightened to propel me on. I soared over the thwump, caught him, kicked off the next corner and I was ahead by a mere handful of pixels. I felt his anger hit me like a wave of pain, but I was back into my mode and nothing could stop me. My feet were flying, my mind set, and my form perfect. I could feel him pushing, smashing his feet off any and every available jump, but I was there before him and I caught them all too. We reached the next rise, and I knew this was the last - knew that one corridor lay ahead, and at the end of that was everything. At the end of the next corridor lay my life, my hopes, my future and past and present and all that I would ever need was to get there. The end of the next corridor meant eternal glory, and I would reach it. I hit the top corner with him close behind me. I smiled to myself, already assured, and glanced back at the challenger. A smile was set to take my face, but when I looked back he was not there. For the meanest moment I looked for him, uncomprehending, and then my eyes were back on the path. Except, there was no path. I searched, my eyes fought to see a way, my mind searched desperately, but I knew I could not make it - I was going too fast, I had abandoned myself to the foolish hope that there would be a way along this corridor, so I had dived headlong into it, but there was no route, there was nothing ahead of me but the blue glare of a thwump, glad to have trapped me at last. I hit the gap in the mines desperately, hoping beyond hope that I would be able to pass this monster, but long before I even pushed off the ground I knew it was too late. The world slowed, swirled, and became nothing but a great blue square, rushing towards me, with some daring tendrils of electricity already leaping for my fragile body. One caught me, the world flashed white, and I was gone.'



I breathed once more, the air coming out ragged and chocking. My heart was racing, and my pen lay abandoned on the table, next to two cold drinks and a notebook. He seemed deathly, as if he was truly reliving it. He shuddered once, and then a pale, blue glow seeped over his skin.



'He told me later than he knew. He knew the last corridor couldn't be approached with such speed, so he had slowed himself, and gone over carefully. He didn't need to rush then - he saw my body strewn in pieces about the last hall. The mines had been cleared by me, so he walked gently through it all, and stepped into the door. A glorious win, and I do not grudge him for it - I had defeated many a beginner on levels I knew. He was truly an excellent ninja.

'He invited me to join him. He was moving out of the underground, going to Metanet Forum, the ninja capital. I declined, but also left the underground. Ebaumsville is no place to live - a dirty, decrepit city. Rife with corruption, and the people there are frankly detestable.'

He smiled a little, trying to regain his lightness. I asked if he went on to play again.

'Of course,' he really smiled this time. 'I couldn't leave the game just because somebody beat me. The Way teaches you not to worry about winning to much, as long as you lose beautifully. I had begun to forget the teachings, in my madness in Ebaumsville. I am almost glad that Trib4l beat me. I think he saved me from myself, in a way.

'I moved to the New Mountain Arena - known as NuMA - and continued playing. It's an amazing place - a gathering of all the greatest mapmakers and players, working together to perfect The Way.'
I had heard of NuMA, from my studies into The Way, and felt honoured to be in the presence of one of its greats. I asked what was happening there now.

'There are always loads of things happening, new maps to be played every few minutes, and the greats are still there, mapping and playing away. There has been an influx of new players since the latest Version was developed, and its great to see so many young minds at work.'

I thought it was about time to wrap up the interview, but I had one or two things I wanted to ask before I went, firstly about the new craze that was sweeping the ninjas - religion.

'Hah, yeah, a lot of the new players are getting into that. Superstition in my opinion - The Way is religion enough for me. But a lot of the young ninjas are buying into it. It's the belief - how shall I put this... I think its ridiculous, but basically it is the belief that there is some form of Higher Power controlling our lives. That we are merely at the whim of some higher being, that everything we do, every map we complete and every step we take, is guided by His or Her Fingers. They believe that when a run is going well it is because They are Well Fed, Warm, and Comfortable. I think those are the three main supposed causes, although I won't pretend to be particularly knowledgeable on the subject. Also, when a run is going badly, or when you continue to make stupid mistakes on a level, it is because they are Uncomfortable, Hungry, or Their Attention is Otherwise Engaged. There is also something about having Cold Fingers, but I can't recall exactly what. I think it is supposed to make your reactions slower, or something. They also believe that each of us has his own personal guide, a single entity that directs our feet, and that some are better than others, which is why some ninjas are more adept than others. I don't hold much stock in the whole thing - I believe that my successes are my own, and no-one else's. But they don't do any harm by it, so I say let them have their stupid ideas.'

And lastly I wanted to know something about his personal life - does he have any romantic interests at the moment?

'Well, actually, I am married. I've got a lovely wife, called Alice, and two little ninja babies. I met Alice back in Ebaumsville - she was one of the hopefuls I defeated in my time. The story from there is long and complicated, so I wont bore you with it. Suffice it to say we are happily married, and bent on bringing up two of the next generations best players.'

With a smile, he produces a photo from... somewhere, and shows it to me - it is of him and a lovely blue ninja, who I am told is Alice, and their children, both black, called Jim and Bexley. I congratulate him, and thank him for the interview.

'It was my pleasure. If you are ever out and about, fell free to drop by my house - ISPN 126.5634.2342. Our local council has finally got a grant for the next Version to be put in - we are still on Onepointone back home - so when Onepointfive comes out we can maybe get together and have a run about MTI, for old times sake. I'll show you some of those jumps, if I can still pull them off.' With a smile and a leap, he disappears.





So, there you have it. An interview with one of NuMA's greats, and a champion of old. Hope you enjoyed it.
