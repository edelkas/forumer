Now yes, clifty has done the exact same thing, but I wanted to get deeper into the propulsion. There are several main types of propulsion, from the method of launch pads, to the expert use of a glitch (such as thwump death propulsion to glitched locked door propulsion.). First, is launch pads. I personally don't like launch pad propulsion, but it's okay if you want to use them. Simply point and click and you've got yourself propulsion. When you create a launch pad, a code is written into the text box. It reads 
2^X position, Y position, X speed, Y speed!
Let's focus on the final two numbers. They define the speed and direction of the launch pad. The first one is the speed at which it shoots you right (a negative number shoots you left), while the second is the speed at which it shoots you down (a negative number shoots you up). The exact speed is the number, times ten in pixels per step (or frame for you non-programmers ). That of couse means that a value of 72 will shoot you from the left side to the right side in one step while a value of 52 on the Y speed will do the same for vertical speed. These numbers can also be altered to give you more precise angles than 45 degree angles. So the possibilities are virtually limitless. Now for my second most disliked of all propulsion: Bounce block/thwump+gold. It is essentially sticking the ninja inside an object, then having it shot out. First you pet down a decent number of bounce blocks or a thwump, then at least six pieces of gold where the ninja is hitting it. It should look like this:

When the ninja hits the gold, the game engine is too busy with the gold to worry about the bounce block, so the ninja falls into it, like this:

When the ninja runs out of gold, he gets shot out, like this:

The thing that I don't like about that is that a good DDA maker can use it just like a launch pad (but that's all up to you). Next is regular thwump propulsion. All that it needs to be is a bunch of thwumps that manage to push you around, but that can be harder than it looks. Here's some tricks to help. If left facing or up facing thwumps are placed halfway inside a wall, they start reversing once you touch them and they never stop. This can be very useful. The same thing can be done with thwumps facing down or right, but they have to be moved one pixel out of the box. If thwumps are put like that, but there are no tiles in their way, they start moving once they are touched. If you do this with a down facing thwump it can help the player get to the bottom without getting snapped on the way down. Unfortunately thwump DDAs are often slow, so a lot of people squish the player between two or more thwumps to give it some gas, like this:

The other problem is that since it is so hard to manipulate, the player often gets stuck, and any enemies are really hard to integrate. This is a good thing because it shows great skill. The next, and probably my favourite, is trap door propulsion. It works kind of like the bounce block/thwump+gold propulsion, because the propulsion comes from N being inside an object. It is made by putting the keys to trap doors 1/4 or 3/4 away from it. It looks like this when it is put in motion:

Both push you in the same direction, the top one is if you come from the left, the bottom one from the right. This is used in many of my DDAs, in fact most of them. Next is normal door propulsion. The way to make this depends on your speed, but this is the concept. If the player has been collecting gold for 6 steps, then the door closes on you, which can be used to shoot you out. It looks a little like this:

Next is glitched tile propulsion. It basically involves a tile with the title of an asterix. It can shoot the ninja in many direction at many speeds. The problem is that it usually makes the ninja explode. Next is glitched door propulsion, which is a lot like glitched tile propulsion in that is can make you explode, but it can work. It's made by altering the third number after the ^ in a locked door's code. If you do the same thing to a normal door, and put a tile to the bottom left of it. When you hit the tile, you start falling into it, then get shot out of it. Finally, is the other locked door propulsion. First place two locked doors on top of each other. If the bottom one is opened then you can pass through it. Then, when the top one is opened, it closes just like a trap door. If you hit the corner of a thwump it can also shoot you to the side. This is propulsion because it gives you more momentum than what you had when you hit the thwump because the thwump would probably be moving in the opposite direction. It's like disabling the electrocution side. Post if this guide has been helpful, if there's anything that I've missed, you'd like to ask me about something or you think that I should add something else. Now for some tips. There's often a great deal of problems with trap door propulsion, so one-ways or thwumps can help. The trouble is usually that trap doors can't move you sideways unless you're already moving sideways. If the ninja is stopped then your DDA can become stuck and you have to end it early or resort to other propulsion. To avoid this, use one-ways or thwumps to stop the ninja so that it is not on an X snap. So in other words, a quarter off centre. Another good trick to avoid stuck DDAs, is to make hold left or hold right DDAs. Sometimes it's necessary to add in some tiles, though it's not usually advisable. If the ninja gets smacked into a wall or the roof too hard, a one-way inside the wall can very easily stop it. If you want a thwump to back up indefinately, but you find that you need to go through that spot afterwoods, you can use a fake tile. To make this, pick a tile that you haven't used elsewhere in the DDA. Replace the tile with it and put the same tile in the top-left corner. Then look at the level data and check what the first character is. Then find that same character and replace it with a space. Finally, delete the top-left tile. Here are some examples to look at, though some are horribly short. If you have any questions or things that I should add, don't hesitate to ask. Finally, I do oppose launch pad/gold propulsion, but you can use it if you want. You can start out with it, but try to use other propulsion once you get better. Good luck!
