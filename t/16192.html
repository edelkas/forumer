#14297 1 2007-03-05T08:14:35Z Tsukatu
Here's a letter I just sent to my College of Engineering advisors:

I don't think Biomed is my thing. I don't think Pre-Med is my thing.
My decision to major in Biomedical Engineering and to declare myself Pre-Medical feels more and more like a decision I forced upon myself because I like the thought of being a doctor. Who am I kidding, I just want the title without having to go through the work. I don't have any interest in biology or helping people, and my interest in artificially improving the human body is borderline fantasy. Even if I did force myself to work at a field that doesn't interest me, I'd only be doing it for the money, and that simply wouldn't be fulfilling.
Despite my constant efforts to delude myself otherwise, or give myself excuses to keep me focused on biomedical engineering, my behaviour throughout my first year at BU speaks volumes of undeniable truth:
I routinely skip Chemistry and Physics lectures, making myself promises that I'll go to subsequent ones to make up for it, and I know that I'll never keep these promises. I've been slacking off like crazy on Chemistry and Physics homework, saying to myself on each assignment that it's only one assignment out of many, and that one 0 on a homework isn't going to matter. I keep telling myself that I'll study before tests, and never end up doing it, getting by with high 60's and low 70's purely on my raw analytical talent. I'm genuinely worried that I will fail both this semester, and even more worried that I can't bring myself to do anything to ensure that doesn't happen. I don't think about Chemistry, Physics, or Biology in my spare time, and I'm not interested in reading news about advances in either. I'm not even amused by related humor.
Now let's talk about EK 127. [Metanet: This is the class on how to use MATLAB. It's basically a programming class.]
I've been to pretty much every lecture class, and am very disappointed with myself when I accidentally sleep through my alarm and miss one. I've never doodled in class or zoned out because my focus has always been on creative ways I can implement new functions that are taught. I'm enthusiastic about showing off what I know, and I'm driven to be at the top of that class. I finish homework far ahead of time and finish the projects in a flash, and continue to rewrite and redo the project to boast my understanding of the subject matter. Where I'm bored by my CH102 classmates' inability to understand units, I feel truly on top of the world every time I effortlessly implement a newly taught concept in a matter of minutes and see my classmates struggle for days to understand it.
I feel like a prodigy. I truly feel that I'm in my element.
But even more importantly, I do computer science related stuff in my spare time, and even instead of doing anything that relates to other sciences. I take breaks from learning about acid/base and redox reactions by learning some more PERL, or by getting lost in some Wikibook about networking protocol. Every other online bookmark I've made has to do with programming humor, Linux development, news about PC hardware manufacturers, and operating system exploits.
But I stick with the Biomed and the Pre-Med because I'm used to dominating at whatever I do, and because it's obvious that Biomedical Engineering is going to be way of the future. So I see my current major as the clearest path toward being a powerful, useful figure in the future. My pipe dream is to start my own biotech company. I don't even know why I still consider that motivation to stick with Biomed; it's never going to happen. With my study habits and complete lack of interest, I'd crash and burn a year or two into medical school, assuming that I receive the divine intervention I'd require to even get in.
I realize that I've been shrinking from Computer Science because the only future I could see for it was being a nameless codemonkey for a huge software company. In other words, the only product I saw of such a major was the life of a total loser, a failure. But I don't know, I've come to see things differently. My partially nihilistic outlook on life tells me that living what pointless life I do have by working my ass off and not enjoying it isn't nearly as worth it as adopting a more modest profession and actually enjoying life. I don't have to be the CEO of the most powerful biotech company in the world to be happy. That title sounds very impressive, but it also sounds like a hell of a lot of work that I am plainly disinterested in doing. Yeah, it'd benefit humanity, but I don't think humanity deserves what I could do for it if I devoted my life to those ends. I tend to simply hate people, in general.
Haha. I wonder how all of this would do as an admissions essay. Please don't boot me out of BU because of this. Haha.
And I'm sorry if I'm not making much sense; I have about six bottles of concentrated energy drink in me and haven't slept for three days.
And y'know what's also bugging me? The crowds I meet through my major. My classmates annoy me. I've met loads of people already, but I'm not interested in being their friend. They like to drink and party, and that's simply not the sort of person I am, and not the sort of person I like being around. To fill that portion of my life, I've only branched further into the online communities I'm a part of. As a result, I'm friends with a bunch of total nerds, and I wouldn't have it any other way. I just can't seem to find that sort of culture with the people in my classes and study groups. Every time I bring up the sort of thing that interests me, I observe how quickly the subject gets changed, and then see how long I can tolerate the mindless chatter before I explain that no, thanks, I don't want to come with you guys and smoke some weed, but thanks for the invitation and I'll see you guys around... and then I pack up my stuff and leave. But I'm confident that I'll find the sort of crowd I'm looking for with a Computer Science or Computer Systems Engineering major.
Now that I think about it... of every meal I've eaten in the dining hall (and I eat twice a day on my plan), I think about three of them actually had another human being sitting at my table with me. I've met loads of people... I just can't connect with any of them. And I've tried. I guess I don't know that it isn't something wrong with me, though. But still, I had loads of close friendships in high school and a very active social life. I am of strong character, I'm charismatic... I can be a social butterfly when I care to be, and I'm very, very pretty... so what the hell? I just want to meet someone I can relate to.
I don't know. Maybe I just need therapy, or Ritalin or something. It just feels like I've discovered that I'm on the wrong road in life, like something is just... wrong, and I can see what is right for me but for some reason push myself away from it.
I intended this email to be a declaration that I want to switch my major, but I think I should think about it a bit more before I do anything like that. Either way, I would really, really appreciate any input you could provide. I'm sure you've seen cases like mine fairly often.
And now I need to finish up a Chem lab in the next five hours. Sleep is for the weak.

Sorry for writing you a novel.
And thanks for reading. It means a lot to me.

-[my name]
[My BU ID]
