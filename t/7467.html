I took that one from Steven Pinker's book on natural language called The Language Instinct. There is an animal called a buffalo. There is a place called Buffalo so a buffalo from Buffalo would be a Buffalo buffalo. There is also a verb [i]to buffalo[i], meaning to intimidate.

Filling in some words that express meaning but are not strictly required we get:

The Buffalo buffalo that buffalo Buffalo buffalo often buffalo in turn buffalo Buffalo buffalo.

This guy has attempted to prove that you can repeat the word buffalo an arbitrary number of times.

#142777 8 2006-02-12T22:41:03Z nevermore
I have something similar to this. In fact, it really only works if said outloud, because it plays with the fact that "two," "too," and "to" all sound the same. Keep in mind that 2:02 should be said "Two two" in this sentence.

I changed my coffee break from 22 to 2:00 to 2 to 2:00 to 2:02 to 2:22 too, to be the same as you.

It's easier to understand if you think of it like this: the lunch break is a 20 minute break. The guy speaking's coffee break was originally from 1:38 (22 to 2:00) to 1:58 (2 to 2:00). He then changes it so that the break begins at 2:02, and ends at 2:22, so it's the same as whomever he's talking to. Because the person he's talking to's coffee break is from 2:02 to 2:22, the word 'too' means his will be the same as the other person.
Get it?
