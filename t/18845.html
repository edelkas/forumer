Cornbread
On Saturday morning, I endeavored to cook a batch of cornbread resembling that eaten during the Civil War. Lacking the presence of an authentic 1860s kitchen, I found the creation of such food no different than that of any other cornbread from scratch. As such, I learned nothing from my own experiencebut plenty from research.
The greatest setback for women in the 1860s would have been the dearth of an oven. Instead of pressing a few buttons once the batter was ready, they would have to start a fire in the stove hours before any cooking took place. Even once the fire was ready, it was difficult if not impossible to keep a constant temperature, and cooks would need to keep watch over the cornbread to prevent burns.
Also frustrating would be inconsistent measures. While wealthy families may have owned measuring utensils, most women during the war would have used a pinch of this and a handful of that. Recipes handed down from generation to generation were rarely more than a list of the ingredients, and those in the few cookbooks seldom gave amounts. Though experienced cooks were used to such situations, novices could often mess up and create monstrosities for supper.
Finally, Civil War women wouldnt have the advantage of Crisco with which to cook or grease pans. They would use raw pig fat, and become disgustingly slimy in the process.
Hands down, I had it much easier than a 19th century woman. Technology has brought me ovens and Crisco, which are much more pleasant than wood stoves and lard. The recipe I used had not only the amount of each ingredient, but instructions on what to do with it. While I was fine making cornbread this way, I wont be impersonating any Civil War era cooks soon!

System of Government

Basic Structure
The Cobalt Isles government is structured into a nearly complete democracy. All people at or above the age eighteen, regardless of religion, race, disabilities, or gender, are allowed to voice their opinions at the Meeting Hall and vote on the Administrator and all Moderators. All at or above thirty are allowed to run for either position.
The Administrator is in charge of everything in the Cobalt Isles.
The Moderators act as advisors for the Administrator and keep order in the Meeting Hall.
Other people vote for holders of each position, and have the right to impeach them.
The Meeting Hall
The Meeting Hall is the primary conference building on these islands. If anyone requires counsel, they may expound their problems to others in the Meeting Hall. At least five Moderators will always be present to aid them, and many citizens spend time there doing community service. If a problem is too important for the civilians to decide for themselves, a Moderator will add it to the nights assemblys agenda.
The Administrator will attend for the nightly assembly at the Meeting Hall from seven to ten. He will address any major issues that arose during the day and present any problems he himself encountered. Everyone in attendance may have their say before the Administrator makes the final decision.
Should an issue be irresolvable, the Administrator may hold a vote. This vote is not to determine the actions taken, but to show the Administrator the predominant feelings of his people. He has complete power over the ultimate conclusion.
Terms of Office and Elections
The Administrator of the Cobalt Isles has a two-year term in office. He may be re-elected up to two times, for a total of three terms and six years as Administrator.
Election season begins with the second October of an Administrators term. Moderators start observing contributors to assemblies in search of Administrational candidates, and begin discussing them with the public in November. On December first the five candidates are revealed and may open their campaigns.
On December twenty-nine and thirty, ten voting booths around the Meeting Hall open. Voters are given a slip of paper with their name on it and write the name of their chosen candidate below that. It is dropped into a box, and every vote is counted on the thirty-first.
On January first, the new Administrator in announced. A small ceremony is held and life continues much the same as normal.
All thirty Moderators have four year terms. There is no limit on a Moderators terms; however, it is traditional to serve no more than two.
The election of Moderators is much more informal. Starting every other time with the Administrational elections, everyone discusses the possibilities for upcoming Moderators. A decision is normally made by the time the next Administrator is put in office. If not, he will get the final say.
Balance of Powers
Though the Administrator has control over almost every aspect of the civilians lives, he is not all-powerful. To prevent abuse of his authority, civilians are given the right to impeach him. At any assembly, a person may give his reasons for wanting to remove the Administrator
from office. A vote is then held. If three fifths or more of the population want him impeached, he is relieved of his position.
Death or Impeachment
Should the Administrator happen to be impeached or to die while serving his term, the Moderators must choose amongst themselves for his successor. They may decide in whatever manner they wish as long as a new Administrator is ready for the job the very next day.
As a Moderator has now become the Administrator, his position must be filled. He is elected by public agreement, much the way the others were.
Creating or Removing Laws
A new law may be proposed at anytime. It will be treated the same way as any other issue and be addressed at the assembly. After some discussion, a vote will be held. If at least two thirds of the population support the proposed law, it is put into action immediately. The Administrator may override this decision, but it would put him in danger of impeachment.
If a law does not appear to be working, someone may propose removing or amending it. It is discussed and then voted on. Again, two thirds of the populations support is necessary, and the Administrator may override the decision.

J. R. R. Tolkien was a superb linguist, teacher, and artist, despite being best known as a writer. One of the most inspirational authors of all time, he has heavily influenced the likes of Christopher Paolini, J. K. Rowling, and Clive Barker. Tolkiens fabulous works give him the right to the title King of High Fantasy, as many proclaim him.
J. R. R. was born in Bloemfontein, South Africa, to Arthur and Mabel Tolkien. The couple had become engaged in England, but Mabel was too young to marry. Her parents forbid numerous meetings between the two, but they stayed faithful. Before Mabel was twenty-one and ready for marriage, Arthur had sadly moved away to manage a bank in South Africa.
A year later, Mabel came of age. Arthur promptly wrote asking her to come marry him, and she accepted eagerly. She boarded a liner and was soon reunited with her loved one. On April 16, 1891, the couple was happily wed.
On January 3, 1892, the Tolkien familys first child was born in the form of John Ronald Reuel. His first name comes from that of his grandfathers, and Reuel was Arthurs middle name. Ironically, he was most often called Ronald, which is of unknown origin.
Overall, life in the Tolkien family was going well. Arthurs business was thriving, and Mabel was becoming more comfortable in the foreign territory. Food was plentiful, and disturbances from the natives had settled down. Life was so pleasant, in fact, that Arthur and Mabel decided to raise another child. On February 17, 1894, Hilary Arthur Reuel was born. He grew quite quickly, and prospered in the harsh African climate. He was a much healthier child than his brother, whose condition had started to decline.
Becoming worried of his state, Mabel and Arthur decided that the children should be brought back to England. Not only was there better air and hospitals, but more relatives lived in the area. Because Arthur was needed in Africa to provide income, Mabel was forced to travel with the two boys alone. In April, the three Tolkiens moved back to England.
After a few months, Arthur came down with rheumatic fever, which was sometimes fatal. He held out through the winter, but he suffered a hemorrhage as recovering. Arthur died on February 15, 1896, when John was barely five. Soon, Mabel was able to find a true home for herself and the boys. They settled in Sarehole, a small countryside town. This quaint little countryside town and its eccentric inhabitants became the Shire and Hobbits in Johns later novels.
John was home-schooled by Mabel for a few years, then he entered public schools. He achieved some scholarships, and became well learned in some of the finer schools in England. The Tolkien brothers left Mrs. Faulkners for other housing, and Edith left to live with family acquaintances. A report on Fort Ross.

Fort Ross was built by the Russian-American Company to serve as a base for hunting sea otter and for growing crops to send to Russian settlements in Alaska. The fort is located in Sonoma County on Californias Pacific Coast north of Bodega Bay.
The 40 Russians and 80 Aleuts that started Fort Ross in March 1812 finished in September of that year. It was built of redwood and was surrounded by a wooden palisade. Connected to the palisade were two block houses with cannons that could control the whole area. Inside the palisade was the Managers house, the officials quarters, a barracks for Russian employees, storehouses, a well and a chapel added in 1824. Outside the palisade were houses for company laborers, Native Americans and Aleuts.
By 1820, the Russians had nearly depleted the sea otter population so they turned farming into their main occupation. Their farms were not very successful because of the fog, gophers, mice and lack of interest of the men who thought of themselves as hunters. Because of the farms failure, there was not much extra food to send to the outposts in Alaska.
In 1839, the Russian-American Company signed an agreement with the Hudson Bay Company saying that they (the Hudson Bay Company) would supply the Russian settlements in Sitka with foods. Soon afterwards, the Russians decided to abandon Fort Ross. They first tried to sell it to the Mexican government, but ended up selling it to John Sutter in 1841 for $30,000, which was never fully paid.
Sutter took the valuables and animals back to Sutters Fort. The buildings were used for various purposes until they became a national park in 1906.
