skyline:
The apostrophe is there to show that the worth is possessed by the pages.
Here's another example:
"I'd like a dollar's worth of cabbage."
It would be incorrect to say "a dollar worth of cabbage," because we need to show that the worth is a property of the dollar, and the best way to do just that is to use the possessive form - "dollar's."
The possessive form of a plural that ends in 's' includes the apostrophe after the final 's', as in the following example:
"I'd like two dollars' worth of cabbage."
This is saying that there are two dollars, and that the worth is possessed by the two of them collectively.
