This morning I think I broke my mothers nose on accident, we were in an argument and I was trying to push her face out of my face, and was going for her forehead, but I guess i hit her eyes/upper nose, and she thinks i broke it, and well, I dont really know. But anyways back on topic, up until yesterday I had no idea why she was this way or who she was before I was born, so then the perfect assignment for class (Bible) was assigned. the assignment was to write a story describing a member of you family, and was only supposed to be 1 paragraph long, but me being the geeek I am, ended up writing 7 pages (double spaced) and I was pretty proud of it, so here it is. I am considering using it for my common apps (for college), but of course in much greater detail. This is an extremely condesned version of the story.

Well anyways, I would like to take this post to hear about other people's family's, no names are needed, just a brief (or not so brief!) story would be great, I would love to read them all, and I will enjoy it too! I now have a great new respect for my mother, after knowing the hardships she went through before having me, and it has brought us closer together. I only wish to share this joy that I have found with my mother, so either go down and ask your mother about her life before you, or for you all to take a moment and examine how great you mother really is!

So without further ado, here is the story:

When I first set out on this journey to find a story, I honestly had no idea what I was going to talk about. I was truly perplexed, what it that made my family its quirky self? I called my grandmother who lives in San Diego and visits our house for the weekend every weekend. My grandmother told me her story wasnt nearly interesting enough for this homework assignment. After much badgering for her to give me any great ideas she said to go talk to my mother about her life before I was born. Honestly, I was not terribly intrigued by the idea, after over the years, not hearing very many great things, only that my mothers pre-me life was extremely exciting, but I did not know much more than that, nor had I ever questioned her about it, so I figured now would be a great time to ask her all about her life. What she told me was an hour long story and I shall try to abridge the version of the story she gave me because I have to admit, it was a fairly interesting life she had before me. So I will begin now telling you the story of her high school days all the way to my conceiving:

Her story began when she was in her senior year of high school. She graduated from a small high school in San Diego, about 10 miles away from SDSU called Patrick Henry high school. Her father (my grandpa) was fairly sexist so he told her that if she went to college she wouldnt be financially supported by him, and she would be cut off nearly completely, but that didnt matter to her because she had received a large scholarship from scoring well on the PSAT/NMSQT while in high school. This scholarship funded her completely through four years of college in any University of California School. She choose the University of California, Santa Barbara (UCSB), mainly because her father (my grandpa) did not want her to become a Nuclear Engineer, as he had been all of his life. UCSB had a great program for exactly this major, and because she wanted rebellion so badly she took this up as something she wanted to learn. So she applied for UCSB and was accepted (being a straight A student in high school) and began to major in Nuclear Engineering. At the time it was very rare for any woman to take on this major, as well as it was a fairly rare major overall, only being offered by 3 universities in the country.

In her sophomore year, she decided to study a year abroad as her scholarship covered a year abroad, so she decided it would be fun, and she should take advantage of the opportunity that lay in front of her. As she went on to through college she decided with a few of her friends to start up a branch of the pre-existing club Society of Women Engineers (SWE) which promotes both womens right and more specifically Women Engineers. This club is still going on today, after being started in 1977 with my mother as the first president of the Santa Barbra branch. This was in her junior year (3rd) out of 5 years she spent in college. She spent a year more in college because she decided to take a year abroad and although the stuff she was learning in Sweden was great and all, it did not fit in the strict schedule of items you have to learn in succession to become a Nuclear Engineer. The year abroad cost her an extra year that she had to pay through herself because her scholarship no longer supported her.

To gain some access to some money, she decided it would be a great opportunity to join the ROTC program at her school and commit herself to 4 more years of $600/month pay which was double what she was living off of at the time in the ROTC program, but there was a problem. My mother wished to be proactive at this time and the Nuclear Navy program she considered joining wanted to ground her because all the Nuclear programs was on the front line (which she had no problem with). At the time only men went on the front-line so she would be grounded to teaching for 4 years in Florida, and this did not even sound fun to her, so she decided not to take the position, because as you can see by her starting the SWE program she was fairly proactive about womens rights.

She decided she would be better suited somewhere else. The previous president at the time was Carter who signed a law that started affirmative action, which was a law that forced companies to have a certain amount of women to men ratio in a business to get a grant from the government which helped to a great degree. Now this was difficult from the nuclear businesses to comply with because here were so few women who were Nuclear Engineering majors in the entire country. The shortage of women was to such a degree that not everyone could get enough women to sign onto the affirmative action, so all women Nuclear engineering were in extremely high demand. My mother was starting her fifth year of college at this point and every Friday another business would fly her all around the country for the weekend on their tab to go for an interview, so she went to New York, Texas, Utah, Florida, etc. around the entire country for all these interviews because she was in such high demand for all the companies. She knew that she was only getting all these interviews and all these weekends stays around the country because she was a woman, and she didnt like this much and she decided not to take any of these offers up.

What she later found out about affirmative action would change her life: this law did not apply to any company who had less than 50 employees. So she found a company of this sort who obviously wanted her because she was well qualified, and not specifically because she was a woman. She found this to her liking, and on top of this personal bonus she gained, the company offered to match any salary of the top offer, knowing she was in high demand. She took a liking to this company because they didnt want her because she was a woman, but because she was a great engineer ranked 3rd in her class so this was a huge plus for her. So after she was done with her schooling, she took a job as a Nuclear Engineer, which slowly turned into a Uranium and Plutonium Broker. This job description included selling the pure uranium and plutonium ores that were mined around the country in hundreds of tiny mines. She worked at this job until a huge slab of pure uranium was found in Canada (which is still supplying Uranium to the world over 25 years later). The hundreds of tiny mines could not hope to compete with this huge mine, so she as a broker was run out of the business. On a stroke on good luck I guess it was, four cities in Texas ended up buying about 2 million pounds of uranium before they were ready to use it, thinking their power plant (which needs the uranium to run) would be completed soon. As it turned out the power plants would not be finished for another 10 years after that (late 1980s), so they needed a broker to come down and sell this overload of uranium. What happened was that their was no longer any buyer because of this Canadian mine, so she eventually gave up trying to sell it, so she again was offered a job to help with the actual building of the power plants. She did this for quite some time before she because bored with the business.

She then enrolled into law school, where she worked at the power plant by day, and studied law by night. When I asked her when she slept, she told me, Whenever I could! So after she graduated from law school, she moved back to California, where she learned to practice Californian law (which is different than Texas law), and she began at a law firm called OMelveny and Meyers. This particular law firm is in Los Angeles, California and is famous for representing large companies who are having legal troubles all around the country. Mostly it dispatches lawyers to around the country on extremely short notice, where they have to familiarize themselves with the lawsuit and work night and day until they come up with a defense for the large companies.

So finally one day, she was dispatched to work in Albuquerque, New Mexico. This was a fateful case that started just regularly, but would change her life forever yet again. The case was in the middle of trial and she was expected to stay in New Mexico for another 3 months, when suddenly the case settled in the middle of the trial, surprisingly and unexpectedly. Her bosses gave her and her co-workers the next amount of time off (up until the trial would have ended) as they had nothing else for those lawyers to do. Another reason why they did this is because the law office was in the middle of switching management because her boss had been elected as secretary of state and took many layers with him to become his cabinet.

My mother decided to become a little more spontaneous with her life and took the next plane to as far away as she could get. The next day she was on a plane to Nepal for a two week vacation of hiking through the Himalaya Mountains. Nepal is a small Asian country just north of India and west of China. There she met the man she thought she would spend the rest of her life with, her tour guide (called a Sherpa in Nepal). His name was Deepak Chettri (Dee-Pah-K Chet-Tree) and she acquired a visa for him (having connections with the Secretary of Defense and his workers) in about a week. This process usually works in only about a year, but it sure does help to have friends in high places! So they moved back to America, and exactly on their 80th day together back in the United States they became married. I was born very shortly after they were married and my father stayed around town for the first 3 months of my life, before me mother caught him stealing from her, and Deepak went back to Nepal. This was mainly because he conveniently began to start feel homesick after him and my mother divorced each other. Then my mother was still working at her prestigious law-firm OMelveny and Meyers.

When I was approaching one years old, they asked my mother to move to Alaska with me so that she could represent people who committed the Exxon Valdez Oil Spill, and my mother decided Alaska was not the best place for me to grow up, and she quit her law-firm knowing her working such long hours and moving at a moments notice was not the best environment to grow up in for a 1 year old. She quit her job at her law-firm and moved to San Diego, where she grew up for most of her life. She joined a law-firm who hired her in San Diego, and thats where I spent the first 6 or so years of my life. So thats the end of this story, and thats a family story if I have ever heard one!
