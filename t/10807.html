You're wrong because there's a fault in logic at work here. For instance, let's say that, in terms of atmospheres, there are (Hypothetically, of course), three types; Hydrogen-based, Oxygen-based, and Nitrogen-based.

Now, even if the universe were indeed infinite (Which it logically cannot be, unless it somehow steps outside the rules of coherent thought, which is also entirely possible, but we're only human, so for the sake of argument, here we are), then one out of every three planets would be either Hydrogen-based, Oxygen-based, or Nitrogen-based. Now, let's say that, when a planet forms, the Hydrogen-based planets form farther out, while Oxygen- and Nitrogen-based planets form closer to a given star.

Now, from there, we don't have enough evidence to postulate that exactly 33.33% of the time, a planet will be a Nitrogen-, Hydrogen-, or Oxygen-based planet. Why? It has to be one of the three, so it would have to average to 33.33%, right? Even if there were infinite space, the physical possibility of a condition forming does not follow into that physical possibility actually doing so.

Now, arguing that there is infinite space out there is rather shaky ground to begin on, as when the space approaches infinity, it becomes more and more exponentially possible that said Nitrogen-based planet will be out there somewhere, but it doesn't dictate that it *has* to. Even with the smallest, most miniscule impobability, it is still within the realm of reason that a given instance could or could not exist. With an unlimited, and unfathomable amount of space, though, it is almost certain that said condition will be met (Nearing 100%, as closely as it possibly can get), but it isn't a given.

Although, I'm not big on Infinitum Theory, so my dribble is merely speculation. S'how I see it, anyway.

#194662 18 2006-06-26T05:56:42Z Beo
@LV: The monkeys would not actually succeed in typing every piece of text that it is possible to write in a single keystroke as we're assuming they're typing on seperate typewriters and I'm pretty sure Shakespeare didn't write single-letter sonnets.

Your argument that the chance of something occuring in an infinite universe is almost certain but not quite is reasonable, kind of like the same logic that dictates we can get infinitely close to reaching the speed of light but not actually reach it, because doing so would require an infinite amount of energy. That's the thing though, in an infinite universe the chances of something occuring are the chances of it occurring within any given amount of space multiplied by infinity, which means there is an infinite chance of it occuring.

The universe is not actually infinite, rendering our discussion quite useless. However it is infinite in the sense that it is physically impossible to travel towards it's boundaries faster than they are exapnding outwards away from us.
