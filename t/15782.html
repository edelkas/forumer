Without an ultimate authority to dictate it, morality will come from social interaction.
In fact, all you need to develop morality is two people with a conflict of interests and some patience. If you bring together a cluster of people, you don't need to have word delivered from on high to develop "Grok no hit Thok if Thok no hit Grok."
In just about any "immoral" situation, at least one party comes away hurt and thinking that things need to be a little different. If the people acting immorally can see that and care that they don't get placed in a similar unfair situation, they'll treat people how they themselves want to be treated. Hey... that's the Golden Rule!
The Golden Rule is entirely instinctual to anything with even rudimentary emotion. Morality will come from interaction, and the only circumstance in which threat of eternal torture as a deterrent is necessary is when the parents are too afraid to hit their kid when the little bastard is out of line.

Naturally, different cultures will develop different specifics of their morality based on the intelligence and emotional sensitivity of the community's members, which God knows allows for some serious variation in those cultures' outlooks on morality, but they will all have the same foundation - the Golden Rule.

If you're thinking of an atheistic society, imagine a typical indigenous culture that has never been exposed to any mainstream religion and has only thought of tree spirits and animal spirits in passing. How many of those men, do you think, can bring themselves to just take a woman from that community and rape her, or to kill another member of that tribe for whatever reason?
And even the tribes with their own collection of ancestors and beliefs about nature spirits - they invented them, right? Was there a messiah who came down and had to tell them to be nice to each other? Their animism wouldn't exist if they hadn't stayed together and did their part for their community, and neither would they. They got together at some point, and they stayed alive because the Golden Rule is instinctual.

Right and Wrong are almost certainly objective.

On cultures: It is certainly true that different cultures have different standards. I hope you don't mind, Blizz, me quoting you

Quote: Kablizzy
In Indonesia, it is considered extremely rude to point with the forefinger. It is especially rude if the pointing is towards a person, nor will an Indonesian hand you an object with their left hand. When pointing the Indonesian uses his thumb. In Kenya, a slap in the face is considered perfectly fine, and in fact, courteous. In Thailand, it is an insult to walk into another person's home with shoes on. It is also rude to cross your legs or point your toes at another person. Equally, the head is the most important part of the body and should never be touched by another person. Children in most Western countries are familiar with being patted on the head and consider it a compliment. In Thailand it is an insult of the highest order. In every sense, those are perfect examples of subjectivism.

But in every example of these, the 'right' action is the respectful action. No culture values disrespect over respect, and a morals system (like the Bible's) should say that general ideals are right, rather than certain actions.

There are some exceptions. Killing is a specific action, and it is universally accepted as wrong.

Keep in mind, this in itself doesn't prove objective morals. It just destroys the argument that says that we have subjective morals because of differing cultures.

My argument for objective morals? Simple - We don't argue about subjective things. Nobody argues about whether or not I'm hungry (a subjective truth), but we do argue about whether or not I can chug a gallon of milk in an hour (I can't, but it is an objective truth that I can't). In the same way, people argue about whether a given action is right or wrong, but if it was subjective, this would be ridiculous! Imagine a conversation as follows...

Mac: I hate Windows, it really stinks.
PC: Really? I like windows.
Mac: Thats not true. You really dislike Windows inside.
PC: Umm, no.
Mac: But I dislike Windows! And since this is objective, you therefore also dislike Windows!
PC: But its not objective, its subjective!

Its also interesting that whenever something is subjective, we all agree that it is subjective. When things are objective, there is room for disagreement about whether it is objective or subjective.
