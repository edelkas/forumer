But that is beside the point. I'm writing this because I know there are a lot of cool people on this forum. I feel accepted here by a lot of people, you should know who you are, and I figure of all the places to post this, this is probably my best shot.

I have this weird social problem where it feels like unless I'm the center of attention, people don't like me. There's some part of my brain that needs constant reassurance that I'm valued by people (unless I know them well) and it's driving me absolutely crazy.

I think it's to do with my upbringing. I was always ridiculed and laughed at for being a feminine boy. My parents were both abused and in turn they abused me, mostly the male side of the family, but they played a lot of sick mind games with me to reduce my confidence. Not only that but I attracted 'best friends' who would turn out to just want to continue the abuse. Most of the abuse was verbal and psychological but physical and verbally-sexual abuse was also involved.

Incidentally, my parents have changed these days and are mostly very supportive.

When puberty hit me I went from an outgoing, hyperactive, slightly arrogant and fairly popular child to almost a loner. At college everyone saw me as outgoing and confident but I still felt awful socially, like I wanted to make more friends but something was stopping me. Like everyone else was getting ahead of me and I was stuck in treacle.

Recently I started female hormones and anti-androgens and my confidence and a lot of my old creative energy is coming back to me. I feel fantastic actually. What happened in puberty is I started turning into a man, which in my books was not supposed to happen.

I have nurtured myself back to the point where I can function and be emotionally mature, but I still dread mingling with people I don't know. It's like there's some paranoid part of my brain that I just can't shut down. Time and positive thinking has dulled it, but it's still there.

Nevertheless I hate the weak part of myself. I learned to have more compassion and that has helped. I realize that what I've dealt with would knock some people out of the game, because it's heavy stuff. When I used to tell my friends about it they wondered how I ever coped. But I don't want to go over past events, and the LAST thing I want is pity at this stage.

I have been fighting this and have gained much control. Several years ago I was almost catatonic. I had a breakdown in 2004 when the gender dysphoria got too much to handle.

I've tried mingling with people where I live but most people my age think I'm weird. Once they see how feminine I am I never hear from them again. I've gotten used to being a loner but the extrovert in me is suffering.

Does any of this sound crazy? To be honest part of me hates to admit it because of my pride, but now I've decided that sometimes you have to be strong enough to admit your weakness. I accepted that there was something 'wrong' with me years ago and it was only since the acceptance that I've grown better.

Now I just think, what the hell do I have to lose? If someone wants to laugh or poke fun, let them. I no longer care what people think. All I care about is getting better.

Anyway, thanks for being such a cool bunch of people - and I mean it. The limited contact I've had with some of you has really made a difference, silly as it sounds.

I know I can beat this and mostly it's being scared of something that isn't there. Some people think I'm brave to the point of craziness so I'm sure I can do it. I just don't know if I need any help or not.

Even if nobody responds, it's been therapeutic just getting this off my chest. Thanks for listening.

Alexandra

p.s. No religious answers please. Ta.

I feel exactly the same way.

I'm finishing up my first year in college right now, in a friggin' huge school (Boston University), and I don't really have anybody I can call a friend. Of the times I've eaten in the dining hall (twice a day, throughout the entire year), no more than three of those meals actually had someone else sitting at the table. The only person I really talk to is my roommate, and that's only the case because he's there when I'm visibly agitated or amused; if we were to meet in any other circumstance, I highly doubt that we would hit it off well.
And I'm not antisocial. I'm pretty strongly extroverted, I'm clever, I'm funny, I'm attractive, I'm charismatic... If I'm having a simple chat with someone, there is next to no chance that I come off as anything short of charming, delightful, and generally awesome.
That said, why am I so alone?
In classes and such, when I'm within hearing and speaking distance but not involved in an open conversation, I'm usually pretty good at joining it and leaving a good impression. People have very much enjoyed talking with me... but they tend to be isolated incidents, and I hardly ever get invited to anywhere. (In fact, the only times I have been invited somewhere were by less-than-desirable girls who liked me because I'm pretty, and who knew little more about me. I don't take well to that.)

I think, actually, that this is a result of my preference to surprise people. I love blowing people away. I find that makes for some more powerful first impressions. So when I'm new somewhere, I like to stay in the background and then completely take the spotlight from out-of-fucking-nowhere. I don't talk that much in class, and end up coming up with most or all of the brightest ideas when the class is broken into groups for discussion. I hold back during practices when I'm fencing, and when we actually start having official bouts with the rest of the class watching... the first time it happened, I skunked the guy everyone else in the class was afraid of, 5-0, and then beat the next guy, 5-2, before I was cycled out again.
So I think my problem is that I have this fantasy of being "discovered" by the right people, and I turn out to completely surprise them, and that makes for an excellent friendship. And it isn't working because it's a much larger population, and people don't so readily notice the quiet guy and wonder why they haven't talked to him yet. I need to find some way of being more intrinsically interesting.

So anyway, it looks like you're just not branching out fully, just like I'm not. Sure, we do make the effort to join conversations here and there or make small talk with people at school or work, but that's not really giving it your all, is it?
We're also both kind of weird. I won't challenge you for the title, but I do have a very convoluted sense of humor and odd demeanor that tends to clash with common folk after they get to know me. And that holds me back from branching out so much because I hate disappointing people - the goal is to be a pleasant surprise, after all. I like to think that I'm generally good at reading people when I talk to them face-to-face... heck, I'm very good at telling someone's demeanor, how that someone will talk, their intelligence, and how easy it will be to get along with him, all by observing a simple exchange of words they have with someone else... and it's a lot easier if I'm the one they're exchanging words with... so I can pretty much tell after meeting someone whether or not we'd get along. Unfortunately, that turns to be most people. I can still get along with them well if I stifle my quirkiness, but I hate having to do that.
