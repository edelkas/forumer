This seems to be my standard answer to most things, but Free will... may or may not exist, and it is impossible to say. At the moment, thanks to quantum mechanics, there is a level of uncertanty in the universe - put a particle in a box and you have no idea at any one time where the particle is in that box, or even if it has stayed there. It could be that at a quantum level the human Mind (where there is a capital M it's a M for Metaphysics) can influence the universe to manifest free will.
At the moment, there is no physical explanation for the workings of the human Mind (as an aside, never ever watch What the Bleep do we know? - a film attempting to 'prove' links between quantum and 'mind powers' - unless you watch it remembering all the way through that it is pretty much 100% fictional and equally bogus)

The alternative is that of a deterministic clockwork universe. Everything that makes up the universe follows the rules, so if you somehow knew the exact position, identity and velocity of each particle in the universe then you could work out all future states of said universe - including the workings of the brain. This alternative may well be the case.
Take an example, on the macroscopic scale - I wake up in the morning, and decide to have corn flakes for breakfast instead of toast. A bunch of factors go into me making my decision - that I've had toast for the last few days, that there was an advert for corn flakes on the radio just as I was waking up, that the box was closest to me when I walked into the kitchen. If the universe was replayed exaclty, from the time of my waking up, all those factors would still appy, and I would still choose to eat corn flakes.
If I had to pick a random number... any psychologist/amatuer magician (go Derren Brown!) will tell you that there is no such thing as random, when it comes to the human mind's ability to choose things. I instinctively allways choose "7" as a number (because of a book I once read) before remembering clearly that it isn't a 'random' number and chosing something alse - but my new choice has, no doubt, been affected in some way by previous events. That's how Derren (if you haven't heard of him, you should) does a lot of his 'psychic' tricks - by subliminally suggesting an outcome befor the person even has a chance to pick the 'two of clubs'.

EDIT: This lack of free will is by no means a bad thing. In a very real way, you are in control of yuor own destiny - if you lost the match it was because you felt lazy and thought you coud use determinism as an excuse to get out of it, or because the other team was better than you (think about it - if, for example, a bunch of pre-teens were to have a match against the UK football squad then, in an equally real sense, the outcome of the match would be predetermined - the preteens are never going to win apart from in very bad kids movies). Just because what you chose to do is pre-determined doesn't mean that you aren't making a choice.
And just because your future is predetermined, doesn't mean that it is predictable. The only way to predict the future outcome of a system with 100% accuracy is... well at the moment it is impossibe due to Heisneburg's Uncertanty Principle, but if you could somehow get around that then you could only predict the future of a system if you were completely removed from the system - otherwise you would have to read a prediction involving yourself, which is impossible (once you know your own future, you can chose to change it). As everything in the universe affects (on at least a minute scale) everything else, to predict the future you would need to predict the futture state of the entire universe, which means that you would need to be outside the universe.
Equally if you were to go back in time repeatedly to see if I would allways chose corn flakes, then that would break the system - it only applies if the universe is replayed exactly. Your presence could mess up my decision, even if I don't see you - for example you open a door to get into my house, meaning that it's a bit colder so I want some nice warm toast.

That's not actually true, as far as we know. If the physical world was not constrained to obey the uncertainty principle, many of our explanations wouldn't work. This interpretation has been upheld by numerous experiments and is agreed to be better by most physicists. Indeed, the wikipedia article Keron posted includes quite a good discussion about it, as does the one about the Copenhagen interpretation which it links to. In particular, the belief that the motion of particles at the quantum level is somehow determined by "hidden variables" - which you appear to be implying you support - has been shown to be inconsistent with the laws of physics as we know them. You may choose disagree with the Copenhagen interpretation, but you cannot claim it to be disproven with any degree of validity, and must therefore acknowledge that you have made a leap of faith in believing in a deterministic universe.

While I certainly agree with the first part, it's not necessarily fate or predestination so much as it is following a logical chain of events. The point that I'm trying to make is that defying the events and causes leading to an action is, by definition, supporting the idea that those actions can be predicted. Let me give an example:

1. You are in a completely white room. You have no memory of how you got there. There is a white table, a white plate, and a chocolate chip cookie on the table. The only sense you have is that you are not hungry. Do you take the cookie?

2. You are in a completely white room. You have no memory of how you got there. There is a white table, a white plate, and a chocolate chip cookie on the table. The only sense you have is that you are hungry. Do you take the cookie?

While a simple analogy, it holds all of the information that you need. Given the situation of a clean state, and a human operating entirely upon instinct of hunger, that person will act 100% of the time in a predictable fashion in each instance. More clearly, your choice isn't a defiance of a pre-set outcome; your choice *is* the pre-set outcome, based upon the previous things that have occurred. All of those influences that have an affect upon our actions and choices are the reason we make those choices. The reason we stop to give a certain homeless person change is based upon all of our past experiences, judgments, successes, and failings. If you stopped to give change to a homeless person in 1997, and was raped by that homeless person, given your past, you may or may not be inclined to stay away from homeless people in the future. But, given that scenario, I would put my money on the predictable outcome being that any person with a relatively normal upbringing will stay away from homeless people from that point forward. Given counseling, and a positive and optimistic outlook on life, that person may get over the inherent fear, and still give change to homeless people.

But go back and read that. That's exactly what I'm talking about. Those variables in our lives, no matter how small and seemingly insignificant, are precisely the things that we base our decisions upon. So, it's not so much a matter of fate, it's a matter of whatever happens in your life is a result of what happened in your past. Broken bones are a result of falling off four-story buildings, domestic abuse breeds long-term psychological problems. But on a deeper level that we can't understand, there are smaller factors that may tip the scales in a given direction. Say you're walking down a lonely street; on the boulevard of broken dreams. Every day, you walk the same route, until one day, a Tuesday, you wake up early, and decide to take the scenic route. You take an entirely new way to work, and your whole day shapes from there.

But what caused you to do so? Upon analyzing, you could say that the sun was shining that day. You could say that stubbing your toe in the shower had an impact. You could say that waking up early had something to do with your new route to work. You could say that it is the knowledge of the day being a Tuesday. But what caused you to wake up early on that Tuesday morning? You could have had a restful sleep the night before. The neighbor's dog, for once, was quiet and unassuming. The night before, you could have gone to bed early. Why? The previous day could have been tiring. You could have had too much to do at work on that Monday. Why? Perhaps it was the vacation you took last week.

See where I'm going with that? When you wake up in a bad mood, and you don't know why, I bet there's a reason. That reason may not be apparent or tangible, but it's there, I'm certain of it.
