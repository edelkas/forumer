Have you ever seen a franchise or style of gameplay that was cloned and copied so often you had trouble figuring out why people had liked it in the first place? Thats me with megaman. I mean, sure I like platformers, and shooting is obviously the most sensible method of offense when making a platformer, but I couldn't get into a game type where the whole levels were literally filler and all of the difficulty and skill was consolidated into instant-death platforming and crazy-tough bosses, while you'd be hard pressed to build a level with the existing components that a good player COULDN'T easily beat without either of the above despite the number of enemies. Not to mention that getting stronger with each level means the levels you put off become easier.
But then I came across Hardhat Rebellion 3, and I get it. Everything fits here. The above problems melt away and are replaced by great gameplay, attractive graphics and music, good level design, and good boss weapons. I can't really explain why its good, but anyone who likes or doesn't like megaman type games should play this. And to top it off, once you beat the first 8 levels it gives you a "weapon" that lets you skip the worst platforming bits or even all of them if you pour all the ammo into it.

Damaged Games
I can't speak for the other two games because all three are convinced that I have a gamepad, which I don't, and only the third lets you "skip" configuring it.

#108989 3 2005-10-17T21:59:26Z brickman
Well, the one thing I think is bad about the megaman games that seems to have snuck in here is the system of boss "weaknesses", which means that each boss gets a ridiculous health bar but takes great damage from one other boss's weapon, so if you have the right gun and know it he's easy but if not you're screwed. This almost negates the purpose of being able to select your level and also makes it a bit too hard to get in. On the guy's site you can find the weapon.list of who's weak to what. For the first I say pick either lava man or spike man, who I remember being relatively easy, or maybe laser man because the guy weak to his weapon has probably the most useful one in the game (but is too tough to take first). Whoever you choose, I suggest you try to get one or two energy ups first, by moving spawn points on and off screen if need be, and using them during the fight. Don't worry about how many you have to use, their importance drops after each fight and you'll have 8 or 9 by the time they're that important again.
Then again, I had 5 or 6 down by the time I noticed that list, though I lucked out at least once.
