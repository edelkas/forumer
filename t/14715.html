aight, guys, listen up. I just sent this in an epic e-mail to a certain special canadian, and decided to share a slightly edited version with you guys.

It's a true story. Well, almost. It's been embellished a little bit, admittedly- but it's pretty damn close to the true story. And I mean damn close.

CHAPTER ONE: You cannot boil the fish, sorry but

It was a dark and stormy night.

Actually, it was a warm and humid day, but it was overcast and humid and occasionally spitting the variety of rain that doesn't so much serve to cool things down or provide water as to provide a sort of secondary, slightly more intense humidity to the air.

This doesn't have much bearing on the story, but it does give you an idea of just how wonderfully uncomfortable the weather has made me; and how I was feeling inside the train, which is where the bulk of this story was set.

It all started around Flinders Street Station- where the goths have been misplaced (or possibly converted to) the emos, and where the clocks show times from around the world for some complex reason quite possibly involving terms like 'trans- cultural', 'society', or 'unification'. And a train which was leaving at neither the right time nor the right platform as, in a more perfect world NOT run by connex, it would have.

Turns out that connex where having even more competency problems than they usually do. The trains were in chaos, going where they shouldn't at the wrong times from the wrong platforms as the entire system continued to lag up as trains, somehow, against all reasoning, managed to gridlock themsleves. And it is against this backdrop- one of a world gone mad- where I lay my scene.

My friend and I part our ways, heading onto seperate trains to make our way home. He was the lucky one- his train went halfway through to the next station, paused for quite a while, and then the driver walked through to the other end of the train and they slowly rolled back to Flinders Street, spending only 1/2 hour or so sitting around wedged between two other trains.

Myself? My train paused for a while, then made its way down to Southern Cross station, where an announcement came over the loudspeaker. I didn't quite hear it, being pre- occupied listening to the Shout Out Louds on my mp3 player. So I took my headphones out, listening to the announcement to see what was up and why, suddenly, there were only a few people left in the carriage, making their way to the doors. Turns out we were supposed to get off the train and head to the next platform down, where the service would continue as THIS train was now going to go out of service. I stand up and head for the doors, when I notice there's still a guy sitting near where I was, fast asleep.

What could I do? Everybody else has ignored him, so I run back loudly saying "Mate, you have to get off this train now." and then giving his shoulder a bit of a push to try to get him moving so he doesn't wind up stuck on a train headed to god- knows- where which doesn't stop at any of the stations.

Now this is all well and good. But as I do this, I hear a beeping noise, and spring to the doors to watch them close, lock, and feel the train begin to move. The 'next station' signs flick to 'not in service', and I start to worry. I look back to the guy I was trying to wake, when I notice that- while not the stereotypical terrifying muscular type of guy- he was still rather heavily tattooed on one side, had a kinda extreme haircut, and just looked like a rather unpleasent sort of fellow. Insofar as you can tell based on looks.

But what with the paint- speckled pants, the facial piercings, what appeared to be a wiry kind of strength in his muscles, the facial hair, and the ridiculous amount of tattoos spinning their way painfully down the side of his body, he did look a little frightening.

During the time I'm contemplating this, the train is moving through the city loop, rushing past the stations when we near them and I see nothing but glimpses of slightly blurred faces looking at the train as it rushes past. All of this has been underground, and I notice as I try to text my friend to tell him what happened, that I have no reception. And then the train starts to slow down. And eventually stop.

In the middle of part of an underground system. Nothing is visible outside of the carriage but a slight suggestion of nearby concrete in the blackness. We are, insofar as I can tell, far, far away from any station.

Picture it. One sole man, standing in an empty train, hanging onto one of the green- painted metal poles, surrounded by blackness. The other carriages are ahead, and there's nothing in them but empty seats. Presumably, somewhere at the other end of the train, there's a driver. I, on the other hand, am at the exact opposite end- the last carriage. Then the engine of the train stops idling and seems to shut down entirely, and I keep on standing there, looking around, wondering just what to do. I flip out the phone and look at it again- no reception.

I hear a noise behind me, and turn around. It's the other guy. The one with large amounts of body art, slight facial hair, and his mp3 player still blasting out grunge music he had fallen asleep to- now audible in the empty carriage. He lets out another groaning sound, and opens his eyes. They're very, very bloodshot. I'm standing entirely still at this moment, looking at him from a few metres away. His eyes focus on me-

And the lights flicker. And for a split second, there's nothing but a blackness darker than pitch and some grunge music coming from headphones a few metres from me.

And THAT ranks as the most frightening moment in my life so far.

CHAPTER TWO: The unsatisfying conclusion

Yup, so, that's pretty much it. My heart's beating three times the usual rate at this point in time, and though I feel no particular inclination to wet myself, it seems traditional to do so at a time like this and I'm contemplating it. Anyway, the other guy gets up, swears a bit, and asks me where we are. I explain, and he asks why I'm still on the train. I say I tried to wake him when I got locked in too, and he seems pretty appreciative. He says he's been drinking a lot, and that's why he was pretty out of it.

Anyway, we find one of those red emergency buttons in the carriage. With a label saying "WARNING: Pressing this button will activate CCTV and emergency procedures", and while I'm wondering whether or not it's a good idea to press it, my new friends of the tattoos presses it for me and kindly spares me from an internal monolouge. It dials for a full minute or two before somebody picks up. (Huh, they sure treat their emergencies seriously.) Tatooman explains that there's still two of us on this train, and we're told it should wind up back at Flinders Street Station. Eventually.

So we sit back down, both of us sprawling over a full three seats each, waiting for the train to start to move again. The engine starts again (or possibly just starts idlying much more noisily) and eventually we casually roll back to Flinders Street, stopping at a platform I never knew existed, where nobody at all boards the train, very little light reaches, and there's little sign of life untill you reach th part near the bottom of the esculaters, where it merges with another platform and there are, thankfully, people.

I reach the light, and bump into my friend, who caught a different train, once more. And together we catch a different train which gradually makes its way back home.

THE END
