Take a minute, read the passage.

Quote
15 "Look at the behemoth, [a]
      which I made along with you
      and which feeds on grass like an ox.

16 What strength he has in his loins,
      what power in the muscles of his belly!

17 His tail sways like a cedar;
      the sinews of his thighs are close-knit.

18 His bones are tubes of bronze,
      his limbs like rods of iron.

19 He ranks first among the works of God,
      yet his Maker can approach him with his sword.

20 The hills bring him their produce,
      and all the wild animals play nearby.

21 Under the lotus plants he lies,
      hidden among the reeds in the marsh.

22 The lotuses conceal him in their shadow;
      the poplars by the stream surround him.

23 When the river rages, he is not alarmed;
      he is secure, though the Jordan should surge against his mouth.

24 Can anyone capture him by the eyes, [c]
      or trap him and pierce his nose?"

Now, note these things...

1. This beast apparently is an herbivore
2. It is gigantic, with bones like iron
3. It has a thick tail

What animals can you think of that are gigantic, eat plants, and have thick tails? Elephants have scrawny little tails, as do hippos, giraffes, etc. Whales don't eat plants. What we have here is a dinosaur, probably a brontosaurus.

Now, in order to explain this away, you must choose one of these options.

1. There was archeology over 2000 years ago, everyone knew about dinosaurs and their eating habits.
2. When it says 'tail' it means 'trunk'. Probably a clerical error.
3. Dinosaurs and people co-existed.
4. This text in Job was probably written after the discovery of the brontosaur in the 20th century.

Number one is ridiculous, and I think you know why.
Number two is a bit more difficult. But if it was a clerical error, don't you think it would have been corrected? Pointed out?

Also, verse 24 pertains to #2. Elephants were in use around this time, so they could be captured. A brontosaur, I'm sure, is quite a different matter to catch.

#4 - We have scrolls dating to before A.D. that have this passage in them.

As to the reason the Bible doesn't use the word dinosaur, is because it wasn't invented yet.

Also, Catholic Encyclopedia: Animals in the Bible:
Quote
BEHEMOTH, is generally translated by "great beasts"; in its wider signification it includes all mammals living on earth, but in the stricter sense is applied to domesticated quadrupeds at large. However in Job, xl, 10, where it is left untranslated and considered as a proper name, it indicates a particular animal. The description of this animal has long puzzled the commentators. Many of them now admit that it represents the hippopotamus, so well known to the ancient Egyptians; it might possibly correspond as well to the rhinoceros.

You should also look up badger, basilisk, cockatrice, demons, dipsas, dragon, griffon, hippopotomous, lamia, leviathan, mildew, phoenix, raven, ringtail, satyr, sea-monster, spider and whale, all of which demonstrate translation and clerical errors, confusion between various animals (and, in some cases, plants and/or inanimate objects), use of one term to refer not only to several species but to a mythical creature as well, use of a mythical or poetic description which actually refers to an ordinary animal, use of mythical descriptors to indicate the animal in question was unspecified, use of a word in a way which conflicts with its modern meaning (for example, 'mildew' now commonly refers to a mould-like growth rather than an animal) or use of an animal's name in a symbolic sense. Picking out a passage and claiming it is most definately and assuredly a dinosaur is probably not a good idea, given the trouble scholars and translators have had even with animals we know existed at the time.
