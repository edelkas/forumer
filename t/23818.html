Quote: Calcy, September 16, 2008 04:29 am
I agree with spoon, it never made sense to me to press down to look up and vice versa.

It makes sense if you think of the directions as 'forward' and 'back' instead of 'up' and 'down'. You tilt your head forwards to look down, and backwards to look up. When you use inverted, you tilt the stick forward to look down, and back to look up. Or, to think of it another way, imagine that you strapped a pencil horizontally across the top of your joystick, pointing away from you. When you push the stick forward, the pencil will point downwards, and when you pull it back, upwards. This also explains why people like me use normal with a mouse and inverted with a stick - we feel like we're pointing up or down, rather than tilting forwards or back.

It's also related to the control mechanism in planes, where pulling back on the controls points the nose upwards, and pushing forward points it downwards. Back in the Good Old Days ™ flight simulators tried to emulate the controls of a real plane by using a joystick with inverted controls. As a result, people who were into flight sims before FPSs - or at least FPSs which allow free look - typically use inverted.

This article is kind of interesting, though I'm not sure I agree with the stuff about introversion and extroversion.
