Several people claim that it was not the Titanic that actually sank, but it was her sister, the Olympic. In a book, "The Titanic Conspiracy", a theory that was outlined by Gardiner & VanderVat, claims that there was a scheme planned out by W.S.L. to defraud its insurers. White Star switched ships with the intention of losing the Olympic and reclaiming its losses.

The Olympic had a very unfortunate first year. In September 1911, she ran into a British cruiser, ripping her own hull and requiring costly repairs. White Star could claim no damages from insurance because the Olympic was clearly at fault. In a desperate attempt, White Star sought compensation in a hopeless lawsuit against the British Admiralty, resulting in a losing battle for White Star. A few months later, the Olympic dropped a propeller blade and again returned to the shipyard for repairs. While in the shipyard, the Olympic and Titanic were constantly rotated in and out of one of the available dry docks. The propeller's repair, which should have taken one day, oddly enough, stretched to five days. According to supporters this would have been the opportuned time to swap ships. Why? If the Olympic were indeed severely crippled, White Star would want to substitute the unsound Olympic for the new Titanic, sink her, and collect the insurance. J. P. Morgan, White Star owner and ruthless robber-baron, was claimed to go to great lengths to save an investment. In an intriging note, Morgan and friend, Robert Bacon, the U.S. Ambassador to France, canceled their bookings on the Titanic at the last minute. The authors explained why Captain Smith ignored warnings and deliberately sailed at full speed into a known ice field. He meant to sink her, but his execution was premature. He struck his target too soon. The expected rescue ships were farther west. Many eyewitnesses that survived the Titanic also claimed to have seen many objects on the "Titanic" (the Olympic) which were branded with the words, "Property of the Olympic."

There are hundreds of varied resources online that also discuss the aspects of this mystery, not just this book.

The authors go as far as to say that the real Titanic served White Star for 23 years until her retirement in 1935. Now what do you think? It sounds very interesting to me.
