I'm not convinced that my Chem Lab TF knows what she's doing. She sends us these emails with reminders as to what we're supposed to include and writes fun things like, "Make sure to include a) the mass of the original hydrous salt (mol) b) the mass of the final anhydrous salt (mol) and c) the volume of the H2O collected from the test tube (M)."
For those of you who aren't familiar with the units "mol" and "M," mol is basically a group of that item. In the same way that a dozen means 12 things, mol means 6.02 x 10^23 things. So she's asking something like, "how much does this apple weigh, in dozens? Not in pounds, or dozens of pounds, but straight-out dozens." Molarity is used for concentration, and is not the volume. It says how much stuff is in one representative unit of volume. Using an example with donuts and dozens, the concentration of donuts can be said to be the number of donuts per display box. So she's asking how many donuts for each display box instead of how big the display box is.
