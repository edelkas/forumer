Propaganda!

In this RPG, the players will take on the role of candidates running for election in a fake Western nation called "Propaganda", which is quite similar to Australia, America, or the UK. The objective of the candidates will be to acquire as many votes as possible from each of the five Parties in the nation. Like any decent election, this one will involve mostly cheating, lying, backstabbing, mudslinging, and perhaps some well-natured genocide. This will be the first time your candidates have ever been heard of in the nation. Not even members of your own Party (if you choose one) will have heard of your existence. This dynamic will make your game decisions all the more important as you proceed. Moreover, your candidate doesn't care about the country. It doesn't matter how bad you're making the nation of Propaganda. It doesn't matter if you're a complete hypocrite. The objective is to win, by any means necessary!

This game is intended as comedy. It's intention is to satirically poke fun at every possible race, creed, and rationale found in humanity. All the while, I ensure you that the game will offer plenty of chances for wild fun and Machiavellian power-plays, with a heavy dose of strategy. If you're easily offended, I recommend not joining up. If you're yearning to play a kick-ass concept game, I encourage you to give this a try (and help with playtesting).

Creating Your Candidate!

To design your character, compose a sheet made of these factors and place it in a Forumer QUOTE box with "quote=your user name". Do not stray from the choices listed after colons.

Pick a name. This data will not affect strategy.

Pick a gender: male or female.

Pick an age: young (26-45), middle aged (46-65), or old (66+).

Pick a tie (or business suit): red, blue, or gray.

Pick a Party affiliation: red, green, blue, white, brown, or independent. Your Party affiliation will allow you to start with four votes in the single Party of your choice. If you pick "independent", you will instead start with one vote in each Party. Check the next section for Party information.

Describe your character. This data will not affect strategy.



Let's Party!

The nation of Propaganda has five Parties which have somehow managed equal footing throughout the ages. The Parties are color-coded and the colors may or may not have any real-life significance. Each Party values different things and has some over-arching ideas for how to run this government. Knowing what each Party wants will help you more easily manipulate them into voting for you. Because you can only possibly get a certain number of votes from a single Party, and there are five Parties, it will be almost necessary to generate votes in more than one Party.

Here are the five Parties:

The Red Party[/color]
The Red Party is a labor movement that loves everybody being the same and having the same things. A statist Party at heart, the Red Party wants you to leave your decisions up to the government. Personal possessions and freedoms--like ownership of arms, property, or money--are relatively useless in their eyes. As long as everyone is working and working and working, everyone will share from the same communal wealth--which involves, perhaps, ramen noodles and a free clinic. Hopefully, the practice of "whistling while you work" will help distract Julia, the doctor, from the fact that she's making just as much money as the college drop-out whose vampire-based screenplay still hasn't been picked up by a big Hollywood producer.
Symbol:
Pro: Socialism, economic control, policy control, institutionalism, unimposing candidates.
Con: Religion, privatization, positive liberty, humanism, environmentalism.
Quote: "Go proletariat, go!"

The Green Party[/color]
The Green Party is an extremist, anti-government Party. If the Green Party ever comes to power, they will make it so that they no longer have the ability to exercise that power. The government should, first and foremost, keep their hands off of our money, says this Party. Just as important, though, is that the government keeps the hell off of our property, let us possess what we want, and lets us do what we want. We earned our possessions and we own our bodies, we're damn well going to use these things as we please! "What'll happen next? Will we start marrying llamas?" Yes. And you damn well should marry that llama and make an honest camelid out of her! Gragh! These Green Party yahoos are also in support of legalizing just about everything, especially the wacky tobacky.
Symbol:
Pro: Privatization, legalization, laid-back attitudes, humanism, positive liberty.
Con: Economic control, policy control, privatization, negative liberty, status quo.
Quote: "Legalize crime!"

The Blue Party[/color]
The Blue Party is a bunch of emotional smile-jockeys bent on doing what's right for everyone, at everyone's expense. The Blue Party is all about policy reform to make the world a nicer place to live for everyone. Unfortunately, many of these policies protect some people to the point of usurping others' rights. In addition to all this, they like to take your hard-earned money and give it to those guys that aren't working as hard. The balance between social freedom and economic control makes many of the Party's decisions contentious ones, even to the Party's own base. They are also in favor of socializing certain aspects of society, like education and medicine, enabling old and useless people to suckle at the udders of government for an extra forty years. Throw in environmental issues to waste your money on, and you've got the makings of the Blue Party.
Symbol:
Pro: Economic control, institutionalism, secularism, humanism, environmentalism.
Con: Privatization, policy control, predictable candidates, unilaterality, war.
Quote: "U-N-I-T-Y spells unity!"

The White Party[/color]
The White Party is delusional and backwards, though its members prefer the words "traditional" and "hardworking" and, for some reason, "middle-class" to describe their positions. This Party enjoys helping out the poor, defenseless status quo, which is constantly under attack by the less fortunate. Stupid less fortunate people! The White Party protects the rights of the people who share in the dominant religion, race, creed, and sexual orientation. Thankfully, they also help out those underpriveleged rich people by giving them tax breaks and the like. The White Party supports free use of wealth and property. There's often dispute among the Party on certain issues, because the Party supports economic freedoms moreso than social ones. The White Party tries to retain Propaganda's status as a world power, as well.
Symbol:
Pro: Privatization, policy control, religion, status quo, power symbols.
Con: Secularism, economic control, young candidates, humanism, institutionalism.
Quote: "One for all, but mostly all for one!"

The Brown Party[/color]
The Brown Party is all about imperialism, potency, and an all-powerful government. According to them, the best way to acheive happiness is by giving your life to the government: "We'll take care of the rest". The Brown Party wants to conquer the world. Internal concerns take second billing to external ones. For the most part, this government could care less what you do with your time, as long as your money goes to them for proper allocation. If in power, the Brown Party will invade other countries and create the Empire of Propaganda. Crazy much? The Brown Party knows that a nation of unwilling and unpatriotic citizens will be hard to manage in times of war, so they will institutionalize all necessary amenities and distribute wealth to people evenly. Most importantly: War, war, war. Let's effing do this!
Symbol:
Pro: Socialism, institutionalism, war, power symbols, unilaterality.
Con: Privatization, feminism, negative liberty, humanism, environmentalism.
Quote: "The future is us!"



Playing the Game!

The game will be broken up into seven rounds. Each round will have two phases: the Campaign phase and the Propaganda phase. At the end of the Propaganda phase on the seventh round, the election will be held and a winner will be declared.

Campaign Phase

At the beginning of each of Campaign phase, a number of issues will be presented. Each issue will have a number of decisions to select from. Each candidate will name each issue and give their decision on that issue. Then, after giving all their opinions, each candidate will select one of the issues and give a speech of less than 100 words on that issue, detailing their decision and, perhaps, why they decided it.

So, in public (in this thread):

Name each issue, and give your decision.
State a single issue, and give a speech of 100 words or less.

That's it. The decisions you make will determine how likely you are to gain a vote with each particular Party. Certain Parties might have different opinions of you based on your gender, age, and tie; these will also influence the likelihood of getting votes. If you are ambivalent on issues presented in the same round, Parties may not know what to think of you or whether to support you. For instance, the Blue Party may like what you said about socialized health care, but dislike what you said about tax cuts. Some decisions may even lose you a vote with a Party.

After this phase ends, I'll give the results and who gained how many votes with which Parties. Basically, I'll tell where everyone stands. and whatnot. Then, the Propaganda phase...

Propaganda Phase

During the Propaganda phase, each candidate will publicly submit two issues relating to government or politics--or anything else for that matter. Each candidate will also privately message me with the name of a candidate. Any candidate selected by two or more other candidates will be subject to a scandal and will, therefore, lose votes in a random Party (or set of Parties). It doesn't matter how many candidates select you during this phase if you have at least two; in other words, being selected twice is just as bad as being selected five times.

At the end of the phase, the candidates who have been scandalized will get a report of the votes that they lost (and what happened). During the next round's Campaign phase, a random set of issues presented during this phase will be used. So, if you need some help with votes in the Red Party, think of which issues you could suggest to help you out in that arena next round.

Votes

Each candidate has votes with the five different Parties. Depending of your affiliations, you'll start out with varying amounts of votes in certain Parties. Throughout the game, your number of votes in each Party will go up and down. Having votes in a Party does not deprive the other candidates of votes. In the end, the votes resemble support for you, rather than actual individual votes.

The minimum amount of votes you can have in a Party is zero; the maximum is seven. When you have seven votes, you can only lose votes from that party; when you have zero, you can only gain. Remember, though, that there are five Parties to receive votes from. Managing every Party's opinion of you is the best way to get votes.

Even though the votes are the most important, it's electoral power that matters when the final election takes place. For the most part, each vote represents an identical amount of electoral power (EP). However, if you have nearly all of the votes in a particular Party, that Party will grant you with more EP. See this chart to see how much EP you get from having each amount of votes in a single Party.

1 vote: 1 EP
2 votes: 2 EP
3 votes: 3 EP
4 votes: 4 EP
5 votes: 5 EP
6 votes: 7 EP
7 votes: 10 EP

After adding up how many EP you receive from each different Party, you'll have your election score. Whoever has the highest election score wins Propaganda!
