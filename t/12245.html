Anyways. I had to read half of "Sophie's World" over my summer (and am currently working on some other summer hw). Now I have to choose one philosophy from the book to agree with, and one to disagree with. I wanted to hear what you guys have to say on the subject. Here is my reasoning on what philosphy I disagree with. I encourage you to make comments on what I've written, good or bad. I'd like to know your opinion.

Disagreement:
Socrates once said that, He who knows what is good will do good (Gaarder 69). Although I do agree with this statement, I believe this is not always the case. When we do wrong it is because we dont know any better (Gaarder 69). Usually these people are young, those who have not yet learned what is acceptable in society and what is not. Among older men and women, it is sometimes the case that a person may have a mental disorder that renders them unable to distinguish right versus wrong. Yet in most cases these people can still make their own decisions based on common sense. So, if you know what youre doing is good, youll follow through with it. If you know what youre doing or about to do is wrong, you wont.

If doing the right thing will make us happy, who would do the wrong thing? Therefore, he who knows what is right will do right. Because why would anybody choose to be unhappy? (Gaarder 69). Here is where I begin to disagree with Socrates. Almost everyday we see and hear stories in the news about robberies and murder. Now Id assume that most everyone in the world knows that killing and stealing is wrong, so why do we do it? The most important reason is because of self-gain. Today, society teaches us that he who dies with the most toys wins. If you dont have the looks, the money, or the stuff, you wont succeed in life. Unfortunately, we all feel this at some point in life. This is why there are some people who will resort to killing, stealing, lying, and cheating just to get one step up the ladder. But we know it is wrong. Almost without realizing it, we are choosing to be unhappy.

Can we realize that we are making ourselves lead an unhappy way of life? Are they aware that these things are not rightor fair, if you prefer? (Gaarder 69). I believe the answer is yes. Imagine a small child, staring hungrily at a full cookie jar, waiting for the chance to plunge his hands into it and snatch away some treat. His mother reminds him he is not to eat a cookie before dinner. Im sure you can guess what happens. When her back is turned, the child discreetly steals one of the cookies, well knowing that he is not supposed to. He eats it, and after only a few brief moments of pleasure, he is racked with guilt. Of course, his mother notes the crumbs circling around the childs mouth, and proceeds to punish him for his wrongful decision. As we get older, simply taking a cookie before dinner will not make us feel guilty. Why, most of us would take another, if given the chance. But there are other situations we may find ourselves in quite often. Another scenario: A teenager is given two weeks to prepare for a big English test. Of course, he is preoccupied with videogames and other activities. The day of the test arrives and our student finds himself in a deep pit. The test is set on his desk, and sweat begins to run down his face. His eyes inch over to his classmate, who is sitting next to him. He proceeds to cheat on the test. Given that this student does not cheat often, later he will feel guilty. He receives an A on the test, and neither his teacher nor his parents find out. In fact, his parents give him special privileges because of his outstanding work. The teenager goes on to live his life knowing that he cheated, and was praised for his work undeservingly. Now every time he sees a test, he is reminded of that day and continues to feel guilty, although it may not last forever. Once again, many students who cheat often will not feel an ounce of guilt, so this is when we see the bigger picture. Although we may not know from experience, we can assume that someone who has just committed murder or robbery will get caught in a guilt trap. It could haunt them day and night, and could lead to the beginning of a downward slope. So we realize our wrong decision, which often leads to guilt. But why do we continue leading this life? Many of us may feel as though we cannot be forgiven for the things we do, therefore they continue to do what is wrong. Others because they want to get further in life, and may not see what they are doing until years down the road, when they suffer the consequences of their actions and realize that what they did cannot be undone. So if he who knows what is good, will do good, does that mean that every single person who does something wrong doesnt know any better? I think that He who knows what is right, has the decision to do what it right, may be more accurate. Because even though you know the difference between right and wrong, you may still choose to do wrong simply because of how you choose to live your life.

#221170 4 2006-08-29T01:25:48Z blue_tetris
You don't need to say "I believe" or "I think". It makes it too wordy and overpersonal. The fact that you're writing an essay tells the reader that everything on the page is what you "believe" and "think". Just remove that text.

It'd be even better if you could remove "I", "me", and "my" from the essay altogether. Attributing your statements to yourself isn't necessary and it makes it seem less professional. That'd be a huge overhaul, though.



Anyway, the sentence:

Although I do agree with this statement, I believe this is not always the case.

Would be better off as:

Although this statement has its merits, it's not entirely true.



And sentences like:

I believe the answer is yes. (and) I think that He who knows what is right, has the decision to do what it right, may be more accurate.

Would be better phrased like:

The answer is yes. (and) "He who knows what is right has the decision to do what is right" is a more accurate supposition.



Those sentences carry more authority, because you're willing to assert them impersonally and objectively.
