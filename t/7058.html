#135738 4 2006-01-25T16:07:38Z iangb
Nope. Google gives each website a score based on how many sites link to that website, weighed by the 'popularity' of each of those sites.

Word-wise, it looks for sites containing the word and sites that are linked to with the link as that word. So linking to NUMA like this: cheese! would give google a hit whenever someone searched for 'Cheese!', even though the site itself doesn't contain that word.

Big companies can pay to appear as 'sponsered links', and I think it costs the depending on the popularity of the word they want to be associated with. Eg "sport" or "bank" costs a lot more than "toenail".

During WWII there was a hare-brained scheme to use pigeons as guidance systems for bombs and missiles. The pigeons were trained to peck pictures of military installations; each time they pecked the correct part of the image, they would be rewarded with some bird seed.

Special bombs were prepared with a cradle to retain the pigeon placed in the nosecone. The pigeons had a little window to the outside world that was rigged up in such a way that pushing on different parts of the window caused the bomb's tail fins to change their angle such that the bomb moved towards the direction in which the window was pushed. So, with a pigeon pecking at the window, the bomb would move toward whatever it was they were pecking.

I believe that the dude behind this scheme was called Skinner.
