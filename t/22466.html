I've been diagnosed with high functioning autism, and among my symptoms are OCD.

It's not inhibiting for me, but it is noticeable and sometimes annoys me and others.

Among my behaviors:
  • All stacks of items must be of the same height
  • Cabinets and doors must be opened to the full extent that their frame permits. If they aren't, they should at least be at an angle which is a multiple of 45
  • Certain items, such as plates, newspapers, and books, must be placed where I cannot see them. Otherwise, I will not be able to concentrate on other things
  • My hands always feel wierd. I will go through a variety of odd motions, particularly squeezing my fingers, to make them feel normal
  • I often get a strong desire to squeeze something or push on it very hard.
  • I must always know the exact time. If I do not have my watch on, I will check the clock every couple of minutes.
  • I'm much happier if I have even numbers of things.
  • My hands always feel dry. As a result I will wash them constantly. If they are dry, I will not be able to concentrate and I won't want to touch anything.
  • Things set in rows must be properly aligned.
Along with those standards, sometimes random errors will just really bug me. For example, in my Mandarin classroom, there was a Russian dictionary that was shelved upside down. I was unable to concentrate until I turned it right side up.

Quote: shan1392, 2008-05-19T14:30:07Z
A stupid question. What exactly is OCD? Can someone define it in simple words?

I don't really know how to define it, it's almost like... depression? Mental something.

Obsessions are defined by:

Recurrent and persistent thoughts, impulses, or images that are experienced at some time during the disturbance, as intrusive and inappropriate and that cause marked anxiety or distress.
The thoughts, impulses, or images are not simply excessive worries about real-life problems.
The person attempts to ignore or suppress such thoughts, impulses, or images, or to neutralize them with some other thought or action.
The person recognizes that the obsessional thoughts, impulses, or images are a product of his or her own mind, and are not based in reality.

Compulsions are defined by:

Repetitive behaviors or mental acts that the person feels driven to perform in response to an obsession, or according to rules that must be applied rigidly.
The behaviors or mental acts are aimed at preventing or reducing distress or preventing some dreaded event or situation; however, these behaviors or mental acts either are not connected in a realistic way with what they are designed to neutralize or prevent or are clearly excessive.
In addition to these criteria, at some point during the course of the disorder, the sufferer must realize that his/her obsessions or compulsions are unreasonable or excessive. Moreover, the obsessions or compulsions must be time-consuming (taking up more than one hour per day), cause distress, or cause impairment in social, occupational, or school functioning. OCD often causes feelings similar to those of depression.
