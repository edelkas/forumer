#267516 2 2006-12-10T11:44:02Z sweep
You see, the problem with median is that effectively only one person's actual rating is of full worth. If you have 50 votes on a map, why waste all that rich, high-valued data on just using the person in the middle? The fact is, mean just has the highest value in terms of taking everyone's rating into consideration.

But, of course, there's the issue of sniping. Perhaps an outlier identifying algorithm could be used to solve this. Or, perhaps, people's attitudes to ratings need to change.

#267528 4 2006-12-10T12:04:19Z sweep
It's worth thinking about, but there are probably better ways around the problem. Let me illustrate something though, to show you why median isn't entirely appropriate.

Hypothetical situation:
You have five ratings, on what is essentially a terrible map. It receives two ratings from experienced members, who recognize this, giving it 0 and 0.5. Transversely, three relatively new members, including one who is good friends of the guy who posted the map, give the level 4.5, another 4.5, and 5.
The result? Median would judge the rating of this map as 4.5. The current system would give this map a rating of 3.1. The map deserves nothing more than 1, really... and median is so way off it's not even a joke.
#267530 5 2006-12-10T12:14:38Z astro
Averaging is a very difficult thing. You can never get it totally right. If you take the mean, as Sweep said, it just discards all but the middle piece of data. This fights sniping, but sniping is nothing but very low values placed unfairly; the mean doesn't differentiate between this and very low values placed fairly.
