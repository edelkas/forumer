Spoiler: [b-The Backstory-[/b]]
2051. The UN disbanded twenty years prior, and the world is in a state of general disarray. Local governments are the superpowers of the world, and city-states have replaces all previous borders. The mass-chaos all sparked from one incident ten years in the past.

The former United States, seeking the reserved oil masses within the southern portion of the Gulf of Mexico bartered with the Mexican president to allow a small portion of Mexican soil to be purchased for the U.S. at a hefty price. 1.7 Billion dollars was the price tag, and President Richard Mollins accepted the deal. A harbor city was built on this patch of land, roughly thirty-miles in diameter directly on the eastern coast of Mexico. This served as a main dropoff point for oil barges. Ironically, the U.S. hadn't bought the Mexican-owned oil reserves within the gulf, and had began illegally drilling it's resources underwater; secretly. Once the Mexican government found out about this, they immediately overthrew the U.S. settlement. After many, many weeks of harsh negotiations, the Mexican Government cut off trade to the U.S., and used the remaining $856 Million to build a series of impenetrable roadblocks on key trade-roads between Mexico and the U.S., severing commerce. This double-edged sword forced Mexico to call upon Central American countries for financial support in exchange for a part of Mexico's ploy in the matters.

The United States tore down these roadblocks in an effort of "Stabilizing Homeland Security and Equalizing Prospective Trade". Mexico was enthralled, and declared war on the United States with the military assistance of all of Central and South America.

The U.S. was struggling to maintain a homeland foot-battle against the Central and South American soldiers, and the U.S. citizens were either in disarrayed chaos or in a bloodlusted frenzy at the frontlines. Thousands died in the initial invasion of the border, including multiple hundreds in two bombing attacks on Los Angeles, California and Austin, Texas. The United States urged its citizens, under the plea of Congress, to retreat into the Northern States and Canada. The Central/South American forces continued a general sweep through America, albeit heavy resistance of troops fighting in civilian-massed cities. The difficult combination of retaining constitutional rights to Life and protecting the innocent from warfare at the same time brought upon a situation with no chance of winning. The people themselves were simply too much of a factor to bring about a successful resistance. Mexico eventually claimed the southern half of the United States as it's own, and fighting ceased.

The recovery of the United States was nonexistent. The states simply disbanded into City-States after the war. Each has it's own laws, constitution, and policies; and each had it's own leader.

Spoiler: [b-The Story-[/b]]
The story follows Tristan Hollander's fight for survival amongst a makeshift squad of battle-met friends, who are taking on the roles of a sort of "homebrew soldier" in the last efforts to maintain peace between the borders of two city-states. They lie on the territory of the City-State of Ashburgh, while the border of the City-State of New Johnsville lies not but one half-kilometer away from their outpost. A run-down motel their base of operations, they survive this desolate snowy wasteland between two near-deserted cities, caught in the crossfire of their political feuds.

-Where You Come In-
After every post of the storyline, post a suggestion for the plot. It need not follow any specific character. It can be as vague or as specific as you'd like. Direct quotes can even be offered. You may suggest any modification to the story as you'd like, as long as it's relevant. Any characters actions may be suggested. If no suggestions are supplemented, the story will continue as I see fit. If the only suggestion/s is/are completely asinine, I'll continue the story as I see fit. Partial suggestions can be accepted if not all of a suggestion is relevant.

Tristan walked over to a wooden table in the general lobby of the motel. His hiking boots sent an echo across the entire dusty room, reverberating off of every poorly-built piece of furniture near him. "Oh, that's lovely", proclaimed Tristan, as he crumpled up the letter and tossed it aside. "Who said they've got any right to control the food of one city, let alone two?"

He walked over to a window in the lobby, and stared outside at the blizzard for a moment. The snow wasn't letting up any time soon, and he knew this well. Over a foot had fallen in the past day, and ground-level doors were unable to open because of it. This made getting around the motel difficult for the seven remaining squad members.

"Craig, get your arse down here!" yelled Tristan. He had turned to face the door he entered, awaiting the entrance of Craig. "Hurry it up, will ya?!"

"Aye, mate?", Craig inquired as soon as he entered the room, faintly panting.

"Look at that note", Tristan said as he pointed to the crumpled letter on the ground. "Who in the four hells is 'Omasi', and why doesn't he want us taking food from wherever the hell we took food from?"

Craig walked over and picked up the note. He began to read it. "Omasi...?" he pondered while reading. "That's an odd name. Doesn't sound like it's from anywhere near here, mate. Maybe he's a cracked-out tourist?"

"Well, apparently he doesn't want us taking food anymore from his stock room. I can't remember ever having the squad get food from a stockroom, but we did raid a supermarket about three days ago in Ashburgh. We saw no one inside."

"Maybe he owns the place?" asked Tristan.

"So what do you suggest? Take up arms? See who the hell he is? Eat like a king?", Tristan rambled on.

"Nope" snapped Craig. "I say we do nothing different. Assuming 'Omasi' is for real, he'll come to us. Let's just keep working on those repairs, yeah?"

Craig exited the room, and Tristan stayed back. He faced the window once more and contemplated his thoughts. So much swirled around in a hellish hurricane of horrendous ideas that he simple tried to stop thinking. Hail chunks began to fall in extended intervals, and Tristan tried to count them as they plummeted to Earth. Anything to take his mind off of the day. It wasn't a good day at all. He had apparently woken up on the wrong side of the proverbial bed, it seems.

The jacket-clad tactician exited the room. He kept repeating to himself, "Must not show weakness. I'll bring them all down"; and this accomplished nothing more than the increasing madness of his psyche. He turned a corner to the left, and followed the path through the motel's hallways that he had assumed Craig has taken. Voices were off in the distance; serious voices, probably planning another raid plan to be presented to Tristan later on. The terms "flank" and "sweep" were heard numerous times, and Tristan was sure they weren't talking about flank steak or a chimney sweep.

"Tristan", Craig said as he poked his head out of a door leading to the hallway a couple of meters ahead of Tristan. "Aryn wants to talk to you. She says she can fix the generator, but there's a problem."

Tristan entered the room. "This problem involves risking life and limb, doesn't it?" he inquired. "Because if so, I'm only willing to lose three of you." The group chuckled.

"Nah" said Aryn, her brown hair flowing over her face as she looked up to speak. She moved it to the right of her face, behind her ear. It was naturally fine, and looked as if it had been washed hours earlier. "It's risky, but I know exactly what the part is, and where you can find it."

"So what's missing, exactly?" inquired Tristan.

"Oh, just a few mounting wires and brackets for an alternator."

"In English, please?"

Aryn sighed. "The metal things that hold a key component of our generator to the frame of the generator."

"Oh." said Tristan. "So why can't we just weld it on?"

"Because it's much more complicated than that. It's sensitive machinery. Put a torch in the wrong spot, and you've got a recipe for disaster."

"Story of my life" murmured Craig.

"What?" Aryn and Tristan both quietly inquired.

"Never mind. So, where can we find this bad boy?" Craig said, changing the subject quite quickly back to the topic at hand.

An eerie silence grew over the room as Aryn studied a large city-sized map in front of her. She rose her head up to speak again, and her hair once again fell in front of her face. She moved to the side once more. "There's a hardware store called 'Irons and Tires' that should have it. It has a well-advertised section on portable generators, including our model, from a few newspapers that were lying around. It should be there. I tried the CB on the local frequency, but to no avail."

"...Alright. How long will it take us to get there?" Tristan asked, a blank stare on his face.

"By foot, just over two hours." stated Aryn.

"Not bad. Who do you suggest for the trip?" Craig asked the general group before him, regardless of it's constitution of solely Tristan and Aryn.

"I volunteer myself" Tristan proclaimed. "I'd like to take you with me too, Craig. Maybe Vadeed too if we get injured. He's a medic after all. I think that should suffice and leave a bountiful pool of the squad here in case a weapon needs to be drawn."

"Good call" Aryn exclaimed. "Shall I pass on the word?"

"Nah. I'll track down Vadeed immediately, and we'll leave soon. I'd like to return before sundown - it's only two in the afternoon." Tristan said, a confident smile on his face.

"Lead on." Craig said, also smiling.

The two exited the room, and Aryn returned to her work. There was a closer destination to get the materials needed in New Johnsville, but the firefight that would surely follow wouldn't be worth it, she thought.

Tristan, Craig close behind, walked through the hallways searching for Vadeed. His sleeping quarters, room E5-17, was their destination. Upon nearing the door, a horridly rank smell filled the air. It was the last door in the hallway, only being met by a balcony perpendicular to the door. They both first inspected the balcony, and upon finding nothing as to what the smell was emitting from, they entered the room. A human lay face-down in a pool of crimson, no footprints or general distress about the room at all other than the corpse itself.

"The little bastard charged me, 'ey did." Vadeed exclaimed. "Callin' me a dirty halfbreed and all of that some-such."

"We're just glad you're alive. Who is 'ey, anyways?" inquired Tristan.

"Dunno. Said somethin' about working for the highest bidder. A simple slug to the chest is all that done 'em in." Vadeed said, smirking happily.

"Well, clean this up. We've got a raid of a hardware store, and you're coming along too." Craig proclaimed.

"Bah. Ruin my fun. I was goin' to torch him. But I'll throw him off the balcony if it pleases 'ya."

Vadeed dragged the corpse to the edge of the balcony and pushed it out of a hole in the railing, having been blown out by a haywire fragmentation grenade before the squad moved in. A fading trail of blood followed, and continued to trail until the edge of the balcony. The body bled once it impacted, releasing all clotting that had taken place earlier - if any.

"Done now?" asked Tristan, slightly annoyed by the body-shifting. One half-hour had already been lost to the event.

"Yes. You can lead. I 'avent half a clue in hell where we're off to."

"Alright. Let's go."

The group marched on. Lights in the hallway flickered often, only being illuminated by flimsy reserve power chargers below the complex, inaccessible to the squad. Litter, magazines, and old newspapers cluttered the halls, and made it difficult for the squad to move, but they eventually made it to a second-story fire escape. They traversed it down, into the fierce snowstorm outside, and began their journey.

They approached the doors. They were off of their automatic-opening slider bars, but that didn't bother the group any. A swift kick in the center by Vadeed sent them flying open, falling to their final resting spot on the linoleum.

"Holy shit. Was this hit by an air-strike or something?" asked Vadeed.

"No" replied Tristan. "If it was, the missile would have detonated on the outside. The crater's down here. Someone bombed this place from the inside."

"Well that's unnerving for the owner, but we just need our mounting brackets, and we're gone." said Craig, starting to look worried.

"Alright. Split in three, meet at the doors, behind the check-stands. Draw your weapons once up there and look for any outside movement." Tristan ordered. The group split up, as per request.

Vadeed took the left of the store. That section was mainly home appliances, so mounting brackets aren't completely excluded from possibility. Most of the merchandise was looted, and the display stands were all town down. With the exception of a few soaps from a broken display stand that Vadeed looted for the motel, which badly needed hygiene products, there was nothing of use over there.

Craig took the right of the store. Mainly composed of power-driven equipment and repair parts, this was an excellent spot to look for mounting brackets. Sadly, this section of the store was most heavily hit by the bomb, and was completely wiped out. All of the gasoline-incorporated vehicles and equipment were ignited by whatever flaming liquid had flown through the building, and they were all resorted to broken, charred piles of ash.

Tristan generally scoped out the center of the store. A few tie-down wires were lying around, possibly for a cardboard-box bailing machine. He put these in his backpack; in the largest open pocket. Wanding around some more, he found some clamps used for holding small components in refrigerators. While neither of these were what he was looking for, he continued on. Sadly, nothing else was in working order, or even in a condition to be looted. Trash, it seemed.

They all met up at the front in within the same minute, and all took out their weapons, as requested.

"I found some wires and some small clamps." said Tristan. "Nothing of true value, but we might make it work."

"Nothing useful found, but I got some soaps for back at the motel." Vadeed said; alert, and listening to his surroundings.

"I found nothing at all. The section that would most likely have the mounting brackets was torched." Craig said, smirking a little. "But this place did at one point have them, so Aryn's information was legit."

The group silenced for a little bit. They were looking around, outside, getting a clear and vibrant idea of their surroundings.

"Let's head home. We've done all we can." said Tristan, standing up from a prone position on the floor.

The group got up, assumed the three-point formation they traveled here in, and continued onward. The snow burning their faces as they were now walking into the frozen, harsh wind; this was going to be a long two-hour trip.
