#428052 5 2008-04-13T14:56:50Z Animator
No. I'll you why.

Say you saved 5 pennies a day, everyday. Now, multiply that by the amount of days in a year, and you get
$18.25. Now, say you continued such a trend, for, oh say, 8 years. You would have $146.00. That means, if you started saving from age 8, in 9 years, you would have some walking money so you'd hang with your friends at the mall. But this is besides the point.

The point is, if pennies didn't exist, then exact amounts (like $14.21) couldn't be calculated, because there's no penny. It's like taking out every hundredth of a decimal (excluding nickels, but that's another story). Since pennies go by the numeric value of .01, then any amount can be possible and calculated. If the penny -was- taken out, then this could only result in an increase of prices (since now, they can only go by fives. Ex: $13.51 could become $13.55) which might affect gas prices (ex. $3.21 might be $3.25). But, there may be another way: it might decrease because of lack of pennies (ex. $14.24 might go down to become $14.20, and with gas prices, $3.23 might go down to $3.20). Knowing our government, of course, the former (not the latter) might occur more likely.

So yeah, pocket change is important.

... No, it's not.

If you can destroy your government's currency to turn a profit, there's something wrong with your nation's economy.

Also, the penny cannot be melted down to make more money than one penny. But it does cost more than $0.01 to mint a penny. So the production of pennies is a waste. It's just not enough of a waste for a person to make a profit melting them.



The US needs to phase out the penny. So much time is wasted handling pennies--if we get rid of the penny, retail business will save in excess of ten minutes of worker wages per day. That's one or two dollars per worker per business per day in the US. That comes to billions of dollars of savings, easily, in a year's time.

The big issue isn't that the penny costs a lot to be minted. It's that pennies waste time, effort, and put strain on the system. The penny has been the lowest denomination of coin currency since the early 20th Century--back when it was worth the equivalent of about 25 cents today. Imagine that. The lowest thing they had back then was worth as much as a quarter! They were content to live like that.

Nowadays, we waste all sorts of time juggling small change just so we can round out two decimal places from our steadily depreciating dollar. Seems like that dollar might be worth a shade more if we weren't wasting all our time (and money) trying to divide it into hilariously small units.



Also, what LV said is important to remember: We already round out just to get our figures for payments and credits. When you receive an electric bill in the mail, you're probably charged $97.42101228. They round it down to $97.42 for you. But that's a bit precise. Amazingly precise, even. You could charge $97.40 and not be at too much of a loss. Better still is the idea that you can essentially forget about sales tax on cheap items. The nickle would soak up those taxes pretty effectively, making your money worth more to you--the private sector.

The real question is, what will the market do when they can't charge $14.99 anymore? $14.95 is still an option, but... it's so backwoodsy.
