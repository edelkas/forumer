I think I know what your problem is (after having to read part of the tutorial again)
Quote: The tutorial page
The formula for projecting vector a onto vector b is:

proj.x = ( dp / (b.x*b.x + b.y*b.y) ) * b.x;
proj.y = ( dp / (b.x*b.x + b.y*b.y) ) * b.y;

where dp is the dotprod of a and b: dp = (a.x*b.x + a.y*b.y)

Note that the result is a vector; also, (b.x*b.x + b.y*b.y) is simply the length of b squared.

If b is a unit vector, (b.x*b.x + b.y*b.y) = 1, and thus a projected onto b reduces to:

proj.x = dp*b.x;
proj.y = dp*b.y;
My assumption would be that you don't normalize the vector, so it just multiplies the acceleration a lot. You normalize the vector by dividing both its x and y to its length.
[smartass]
That's easy to prove: if you have the vector (x;y), with a length of ln = √(x*x+y*y), then the new vector would have ln2 = √ ((x*x)/(ln*ln) + (y*y)/(ln*ln)) = √((x*x + y*y)/(ln*ln) = √ (x*x+y*y)/ln = ln/ln = 1...

#313463 6 2007-04-15T18:30:10Z shoftee
Hrm. For starters, you don't project points on lines, you project vectors on vectors. I think that at some point you mixed those three up and it all went downhill from there.

To check if a circle is colliding with a line and with a segment are different things. If you're checking for a line, all you have to do is check the distance between the line and the circle...
(In an attempt to make my explanation clearer) I made a few diagrams:

This explains why (in your case) it's not just one simple check that needs to be done. These are three possible positions of the ball and the segment you're checking for collisions with (the fourth (and last) case is the one you had in mind). The red lines are what you are trying to calculate, the green ones are what you should be trying to calculate. (Red + Green = Brown)



The process of projecting a vector on another vector. The result is also a vector. I will use the same colors for the next few diagrams, too. (This diagram is actually just to show you what colors I'm using, though. The next few are the useful ones.)



When taking the point (x,y), you're actually taking the vector (0,0)->(x,y) and trying to project it on the vector (x1,y1)->(x2,y2). That's where your mistake is, too. What you need to do is take the vector (x1,y1)->(x,y) and project it on the line (x1,y1)->(x2,y2).




These two are essentially the same. It's just that in the lower one, everything is shifted so that (x1,y1) becomes (0,0). Your mistake was that you mixed the two up.



This last one is the case where the collision point is in the end of the segment. In this case, what you need to check is if the resulting vector lies on the line, or outside the line.

I think that pretty much wraps it up.
Hope you understand it (even though I highly doubt it)...
