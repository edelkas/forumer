#45553 3 2005-01-11T21:48:01Z brickman
Gold: I don't always add gold, although when I think about it I suppopse I should. The fact of the matter is, people have different levels of skill. A map thats very hard for one person may be of medium difficulty to another, and nigh-impossible for a third. Gold allows you to appeal to more of those people. People who can hardly beat the level ignore the gold and get a challenge thats reasonable for them, and then a slightly more skilled player will go in and grab some of the gold, going a little out of their way but not being able to take it all. Then a great player comes in and gets all the gold. These players each was treated to just the right ammount of difficulty, because the difficulty is variable depending on how much gold they're willing to go for. Without the gold, one would be satisfied and the other two would say its too hard/easy. Of course, gold is a bad thing when it is too easy to get. If going for the gold doesn't add challenge, then yes, its not needed. But it usually is.

Difficulty: once again, different skill levels. There are people who can beat almost any non-broken map on numa with enough effort, hereafter referred to as the uber-players. Some people, especially the uber-players themselves, make maps intended mostly for them. If you play those maps, you will fail miserably, because it wasn't for you. Meanwhile, there are poor players who can only beat a handful of maps. Many people, often those same people, make maps suited to them. If you play those maps, you will finish and say "wtf, what's the point? Its too easy". And you're right, because it wasn't made for you. Then, there are intermediate players, like you and me, and maps made for us, once again often by us. These range from pretty easy to pretty hard. Actually, there's a range in all three categories, but the intermediate is where its biggest. Each particular person has a certain difficulty "niche", a perfect difficulty, but its not likely that they will ever find more maps at that exact difficulty than can be counted on their fingers. Most will be off, by either a little or a lot. No map is the right difficulty for all people.

So then, why rate on difficulty? Unless its a three-second long map where you only need to make one jump to win, a map thats physically impossible, or a survival map with an easy infinity loop, difficulty is not a judge of how good a map is or whether others will like it. The only ones who I think have any right to say whats too hard are the top players, and unless you're one of them, don't.

#45628 5 2005-01-12T01:13:21Z tktktk
Yep, each item has its uses, whether originally intended or not. When M/R first made the game, they made the items with the assumption that they would be used the way they were meant to be used. Wrong.

Gold can be used not only to add two seconds to your score, but also to be aesthetically pleasing, for delaying platforms, mines, and launchpads, and to offer extra challenge in custom maps. The same goes for the other items. Do you think it was ever intended for a normal door to be a tiny, insanely-hard-to-jump-on platform? Or for thwumps to be used for a wall of death? Nope.

Let's face it, there really aren't all that many items, and if they were all used only the way they were intended maps would quickly become boring and similar. We adapt them, and use them to make the most of what we have. With gold, you can't exactly use it to help your time, so we used it for other stuff. Make sense?

As far as difficulty, if a map is truly well put together it will be fun for a player of nearly any skill, but it would be impossible to make it that way without a few optional objectives for the veterans.
