hmmm...

a rundown of my favorite vegetables cooked in the best ways, according to me:

Onions - When you make beef and add onions to it, it is really good. Wait until the onions are a ilttle burnt. This makes them crispy and they are covered in beef fat.

Sweet Potatoes (or Yams) - shave the skin off and then lay them flat in the bottom of a glass casserole or baking dish. Add a mixture of orange juice and brown sugar, and a little butter I think, but I'm not entirely sure, and also add cranberries. Heat them in the oven. I don't know how hot, but probably until the yams get hot.

Lemon Cucombers - Cut into slices and add salt.
Tomatoes - see above

Sweet Peas - Eat them plain. They are juicy and great. Wash them first tho.

Carrots - Plain or cooked, but if you cook them make sure they are soft.

Celery - I only like it if it has Peanut Butter and nothing else. My mom tried to make me eat it with cream cheese and raisins, but I would always trade those to the kids who had better food. For some reason they enjoyed my food more.

I think that is it... And stirfry would have to include pretty much all of these except the yams and cucombers.

#352250 27 2007-08-07T05:18:12Z Topolord
I always say onion works with anything. Think about it; onion+any meat=goodness. onion+any veggie=good, onion+any carb=good, onion even works with cheese for god's sake. CHEESE! You can eat it alone, om the side, in a sauce, or on top of another foodstuff. I even like it raw sometimes.

Garlic is basically a funky onion, so I credit all of Garlic's wondrous goodness to onion. It's tasty, healthy, and...f*ck, you just can't go wrong with garlic.

Avocado...is godly in Guacamole, aka with a load of salt and onion added. Guacamole is one of my favorite foods to make. oh, and don't forget that you can and should add garlic to guacamole too.

Cucumbers are a great snack, but they just don't have much to offer in the way of flavor: they're basically just water.

Pickles on the other hand, offer a tasty snack, and you can slice them and sandwich them if you need something to flavor your lunch. I am not a fan of mixing pickles with meat though, as the pickle flavor is over-powering.

Tomatoes...(okay, it's a fruit, whatever.) had best stay in sauces. You can't beat a good tomato sauce, but a plain tomato is, just like a cucumber, mainly just water. no taste. unlike a cucumber though, they have the thickness to make a great sauce when cooked down.

Beans and broccoli are good, but simple. They just don't offer too many possibilities. You steam or boil 'em, maybe you add some onion and garlic some olive oil, whatever.

Spinach. Cream it.

Zucchini and squash are great when they are just soft all the way through, but too often they are mushy and unappealing.

Pumpkin has many uses, pie foremost among them, and it's great for those.

peas and carrots. the latter makes a tasty snack, and is good caramelized, the former is best when buttery or in a tomato (and likely onion) sauce.

Oh yes, Artichokes. They're best alone, with butter.
