There are a lot of things I see wrong with how discussion is handled here, so free of my beliefs and simply as a Mod I am going to lay out some simple instructions to keep in mind.

1.) Do not argue with the intent to prove the other side wrong, because it's futile. Discussion should be based on gaining understanding and knowledge of the other side's opinion, and your own. Statements like "you are wrong" or "I am right" are not productive or beneficial to the furthering of understanding so refrain from using them as much as possible.

2.) Do not generalize or make assumptions unless you clearly point out you are making one. Common ones I see are all athiests are the same belief wise, all Christians are under the authority of the Vatican, intelligent design is mutually exclusive of evolution, etc. You know when you do it so please be mindful and try to limit these things. I picked the ones I listed because most of our arguments stem from or involve religion, they come up the most often.

3.) The internet is not an end all source of knowledge. Providing a link, even multiple links to sites that support your view point does not validate your view point in any way other than offering other people's opinions on the issue. Feel free to use links as they are very useful in showing people who know more about the issue discuss things more in depth than we can, but do not use them as "I have a link therefore I am right" because that isn't the case with the internet.

4.) Acknowledge the points others make. If you make a topic switch, first either concede the point, or say in your post that you have nothing further to say on the issue and wish to bring up this other point, or if it pertains, explain why. Simply posing issue after issue without countering the rebuttals of the other side does nothing for you and wastes the time of the counter argument. Please at least aknowledge they made a point, whether or not you agree. It's healthy for debate and discussion.

5.) Repeating points you have made prior in larger letters or different colors is not tolerated anymore, if I see it I will edit it, end of story. Try rewording your answer to explain what you mean more thoroughly or elaborate what you think they are not agreeing with you on and not just shoving till they stop shoving back and leave.

6.) Pose your objections to the other side as a question and not a statement. It makes it easier for them to know exactly what you want answered and removes confussion and arguments.

7.) Avoid, as best you can, hypotheticals and "what if's" because 9/10 are circular in nature and do not advance either side, but simply are used as stalling techniques. If there is a valid time to use one, make it clear you are using a hypothetical some way and understand that by using one you are traversing dangerously into circular logic and unhealthy debate since most "What if's" can be countered with an equally pointless hypothetical in return.

8.) If you have seen something in the other side's argument you agree with, point it out. Showing places where two sides mutally agree is a great platform for beginning further discussion. "Since we agree x is so, then what do you think about y?" and so forth. Be warned though, don't assume the other side agrees unless they say so explicitly.

9.) Do not assume the other side has less standing than you on the matter, even if they do. For the sake of discussion, take each point as if it were presented annonymously. This is because even though we have different levels of understanding we all can still make valid points and counterpoints.

10.) Explain your ideas fully, yet concisely. Rambling is as unproductive as not saying anything at all. It upsets and distracts from your points. But on the flip side, make sure you cover as much as necessary for the other side to make a valid rebuttal to your point that is topical to what you said.

11.) Complete sentences/grammar/etc. are important if you want your idea to be listened to, don't expect respectful thoughts on your points if they aren't respectfully presented.

12.) Re-read other's posts to ensure you fully understand what they are trying to say. I've seen it happen a lot when there is a mis-read and an argument ensues, please avoid this as best as possible.

13.) Understand we don't know everything, and if a time arises when both sides admit they have nothing else to share on the issue agree that debate is over and move on. Don't get to a point where the same points are posed over and over. Ensure debate continues to move forward through an issue, and most importantly, stays topical.

14.) Do not be snide, jeering, or anything but collected. Present all your points in a non sarcastic tone and present them free of any dissent towards the other side. If I find this is becoming a problem warnings will be issued.

15.) Other people, despite their belief systems or history, can and do have valid things to say to you. I've said this in other words earlier but it's very important to point out. Regardless of religious/politcal/sexual affilitation people can still have something to teach you. That is the case 100% of the time. People, even those you don't agree with, know things you don't. So thoughtfully consider what they have to say before you dismiss it as foolish.
