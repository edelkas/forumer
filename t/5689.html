#115524 9 2005-11-16T22:38:01Z brickman
Bodies not sticking out of walls is not physics. Being able to destroy debris in your path does not require physics (though everyone seems to assume it does). Jumping up and then falling back down with some damage if you fell too far isn't physics. Grenades aren't even really physics, at least not in the sense I mean. Look at duke nukem 3D. In there you could toss pipebombs, take damage for jumping off buildings (and damage that depends on the surface you land on no less), use jetpacks to enter through windows or fly around between roofs, go swimming, ride elevators and subways, drop people on conveyor belts and have their body get carried along, blow up walls or in one case level an entire building, and so on--all without a physics engine any more complicated than "If object is not resting on ground, make object fall". You could even set up explosive tripwires, bounce shots off walls, and shrink and then step on your enemies or yourself, and set up decoys to distract enemies in a firefight. All without physics. Really, the lack of physics didn't create any barriers at all to what the program could do--what few real barriers there were were caused by the rigid sector system instead. However, it ran fast and well, was fun enough that there's people who still think fondly of the features noone bothered to copy from it, could set up fairly realistic and interactive environments if you wanted it to, and never, ever had enemies standing behind solid walls that you couldn't see because the game needed to take shortcuts just to keep up with its massive drain on resources. No, I'd like to see more games rejecting physics, not trying to "improve" them.
And BTW, N already is affected by physics more realistic than you'll find in many platformers. He has real momentum which can change directions as a result of terrain, has gravity that kicks in gradually instead of all-at-once, can move fast enough to smash himself against any surface but needs a boost to get above a certain speed threshold, and handles sloping and even curved surfaces in a very believable way. Physics affecting n do translate into better gameplay. However, his enemies have no physics effects at all, because that does not create better gameplay.
