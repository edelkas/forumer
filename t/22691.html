So, which NUMA do you like more - old or new?
Spoiler: What I thinkPersonally, I like the old version more, for several reasons:

1. The old NUMA captured the simplistic atmosphere of N much better than the new one does in my opinion. I'm not saying that it doesn't look good right now (it looks great), but I prefer the old skin. I'm actually fine with this now.

2. Point ratings - It is now impossible to rate a map 2.5/5 for average. You can only rate in whole-number intervals now. 2/5 is "below average," and 3/5 is "above average." What happened to "average?" Also, people often dock off 0.5 points for a small flaw on a map that doesn't deserve a 5, but is better than a 4. That is now impossible, and I don't like that. Also, the rating displayed on the maps is only by whole number intervals, too.

3. Featured maps - I'm not particularily bugged about this one, but what was wrong with Bitesizing? "Featured Maps" just doesn't sound as nice as "Bitesized Maps," not to mention that "1 Featured Map a Day" limit. I'm fine with this now, too, except for the one-a-day limit. There just may be a flood of great maps one day.

4. No more top-rated page - Sure, there's the "Hot Maps" link, but that doesn't really take the place of it well enough. Classics like "On Route 666" and "Afternoon Dragon 01 Step 04" aren't going to get enough attention now, in my opinion, since now they're almost impossible to find now.

5. The "Hot Maps" page - I like the idea, but now it's kind of hard to tell what maps really are the newest, and people could snipe other maps to make sure their's is on the top. Edit: Also, This places even more of an emphasis on ratings, which people don't like, right? See my posts below for a further explanation.

6. The disappearance of the top-rated authors page - I liked that page (maybe some of you didn't), because it provided links to the most appreciated authors on NUMA, not to mention that I had hopes of reaching that place some day. Oh well.

7. Also, how do you put more than 5 tags on a map?


This isn't to say that I don't like NEWMA. It's still a great place, and I really like some of the new features, such as the "NOT" operator in the search, the avatars, the rennovated profile page, the "Active Authors" page, and the immediate links to previous maps by the author, but I still like the old NUMA more. It would've been great if those could've been added without a change of skin and a complete rennovation, in my opinion.

#439229 9 2008-05-26T02:07:41Z Atilla
NEWMA all the way.

1. The old NUMA captured the simplistic atmosphere of N much better than the new one does in my opinion. I'm not saying that it doesn't look good right now (it looks great), but I prefer the old skin.

The old NUMA looked dull and somewhat amateurish by modern standards (no offence meant to the designers). With web authoring tools becoming more powerful and more widely available, and with presence of a generation that has grown up on the 'net, even small websites have become more stylish and professional in appearance over recent years. NUMA's new look reflects this change. I find it much more attractive and stylish than the old.


2. Point ratings - It is now impossible to rate a map 2.5/5 for average. You can only rate in whole-number intervals now. 2/5 is "below average," and 3/5 is "above average." What happened to "average?" Also, people often dock off 0.5 points for a small flaw on a map that doesn't deserve a 5, but is better than a 4. That is now impossible, and I don't like that. Also, the rating displayed on the maps is only by whole number intervals, too.

Good. Most people only use five rating increments anyway - 0, 3.5, 4, 4.5, and 5. And, really, people aren't perfectly accurate rating machines. It's not worth getting hung up on 0.5 increments for the map average because at that degree of precision differences in the way people rate are just as likely to effect the overall rating as any aspect of the map. Yeah, there are occasionally times when you might want to rate something 4.5 instead of 4, with good reason, but in general I don't think it's a problem. Just recalibrate your ratings to 3, 4, 5 instead of 3.5, 4, 4.5. If you want to be daring, you could even use the whole range of ratings (statistically, the average map gets well over 3.5 4, so perhaps we need to increase our expectations).

3. Featured maps - I'm not particularily bugged about this one, but what was wrong with Bitesizing? "Featured Maps" just doesn't sound as nice as "Bitesized Maps," not to mention that "1 Featured Map a Day" limit.

Eh, I think the reviews and the fact that the featured map pops up on the homepage is nice. If nothing else, it should drastically reduce the number of times people wail "Why was this map bitesized?!" Also, I think decreased volume is a good thing - I've seen people who bitesize every single map their favourite author produces, even though most of them are not exceptionally good for that author or for NUMA as a whole. One per day is perhaps a little stingy, but I understand that this limit is not set in stone (if you look at the home page, it states that featured maps will "initially" be restricted to one a day). Once everyone's settled in and the appropriate privileges set up, I wouldn't be surprised if it increased.


4. No more top-rated page - Sure, there's the "Hot Maps" link, but that doesn't really take the place of it well enough. Classics like "On Route 666" and "Afternoon Dragon 01 Step 04" aren't going to get enough attention now, in my opinion, since now they're almost impossible to find now.

Well, I for one haven't used the top-rated page for quite a while, because I have already seen the overwhelming majority of maps on it. The ones that haven't been there as long and don't have 8000 votes tend to flop all over the place due to sniping, so I'm not sure it was a very good indication of... well, anything, really.

Also, I believe both the maps you mention are immortalised in the Dronies Hall of Fame. There are methods other than the top-rated page for this, is what I'm saying, and I honestly think that a hall of fame works better for this sort of thing.


5. The "Hot Maps" page - I like the idea, but now it's kind of hard to tell what maps really are the newest, and people could snipe other maps to make sure their's is on the top.

Personally, exact chronological order doesn't bother me overly much, as long as one map doesn't stick at the top for ages. I grant you that sniping could be a problem, but it's already somewhat of a problem. So is the large number of poor maps submitted, which few people are interested in playing, and this does something to address that. No net loss, I feel.

6. The disappearance of the top-rated authors page - I liked that page (maybe some of you didn't), because it provided links to the most appreciated authors on NUMA, not to mention that I had hopes of reaching that place some day. Oh well.

I didn't like it in the first place, because I considered it terribly inaccurate as a reflection of map-making ability or how likely I am to enjoy an author's maps. No loss for me, but I can understand that others might feel different.

To scythe:

The Newest page was a method I came up with, and pitched to Arachnid, for allowing users to post more than two maps in a given period without having those maps throttled out of the browsable database. So, authors can post as many maps as they want without it clogging up NUMA too much. To check out new maps, just go to active authors. The organization there is just about identical to that of the Newest page on old NUMA, except that all of a single author's maps are docked under that author.

Also: The top-rated page was horrible! Ugh! That page didn't display the ten greatest maps from NUMA's 100,000 map history. It simply didn't! Why weren't formica, Sendy, or other vets at the top? It's because rating habits evolve over time--and so, recent map ratings are only valid compared to other recent map ratings. Hot Maps will hold good maps for long enough that anyone who visits NUMA will be able to see it on the top page, and favorite it or at least be exposed to it.

In short: Active Authors replaces Newest; Hot Maps replaces Top Rated. They don't replace them with the precise same function, but they replace them better. They are more navigable for finding what you're looking for. And if you're still looking for the best maps of all time, you're not going to be able to find them by ratings--the consideration for the best maps of all time is way too subjective.



I think most of the complaints about Nmaps.net will be from people who enjoyed the competetiveness of ratings. That's one aspect of NUMA that won't be coming back.

#440219 40 2008-05-29T12:28:56Z Atilla
They're not anti-ratings so much as anti-ratings abuse, or perhaps anti-whinging. It's died down a bit recently, but there was a time when we were getting half a dozen OH NOES I HAVE BEEN SNIPED threads a week. People would spend hours writing huge, hysterical rants asserting that their map having a rating of three was a sign of the impending apocalypse and furthermore NUMA has destroyed their entire life, they cry themselves to sleep every night, and will seriously consider a suicide unless everyone gives their next map fives. Speaking of which, some people started putting complaints about snipers in the description of every map with a rating below 4 purely because this would result in a flood of fives to "counteract the snipe" (I've seen this happen when the only vote on the map was a 3.5 from me). Then, we had the fad of resubmitting every map which didn't get the rating you want and claiming it was sniped, which cluttered up the newest page with maps we've already seen. There was a big multiaccounting scandal a while back, too. And, of course, there have always been the idiots who snipe and multiaccount and otherwise abuse the voting system for petty revenge or to "win" the competition for top-rated maps.

In short, the "ratings don't matter" line gets trotted out so often because in the past, excessive focus on ratings - and particularly on the competitive aspects of ratings - has lead to more complaints and forum drama than anyone would wish to see.
