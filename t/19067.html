#358585 8 2007-08-23T06:56:15Z southpaw
haha, this is where the Allegory of the Cave comes into play.

Ignorance is defined by Socrates as when a man who has seen shadows all his life is shown what is "true" in a sense, and yet that man denies it. At first he would rather believe the shadows on the cave are the truths in his life. But if he is returned to his shackles and stares at the walls his mind will think of the objects, the real objects shown to him, and he will not be able to deny that they were not of a real sort, just as the shadows are real.

At first he was content knowing that the shadows on the cave were the "truth." How can you know you are blissful if you have never known anything else besides your knowledge up to the point at which you decided to be ignorant? Because an infant doesn't decide they are ignorant from day one. There comes a point where they accept all the knowledge they have received, which comes much later in life, and they decide they are content with what they agree with and sometimes try to change the things they do not agree with. Therefore, nobody knows true ignorance because we are all taught different things and are shoved into situations we have never encountered. True ignorance would be someone who has seen only "shadows" their whole lives, never being told what is right and wrong, and making their own judgement on things. This has probably only occured a few times, and it was before humans were ever dealing with philosophy, probably when humans started to emerge on this planet is my guess.
