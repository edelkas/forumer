@BWD: Who is he supposed to PM for a thread like this? It's a public discussion of the NSA in America. I could see a few of his threads that could have been better as a PM (the one about taking screenshots of N and the one about warns), but many of them are discussion and debate threads like this.

Anyways, not everybody who disagrees with the NSA's practices is covering up a crime. The Constitution and Bill of Rights in the U.S. guarantee us certain rights. The Bill of Rights hasn't changed in over two centuries and it really seems to be working well for us. However, some would argue that the government today is ignoring the Constitution and breaking our guaranteed rights. Specifically the Fourth Amendment which bars the government from searching and seizing private property without first getting a court-issued warrant that outlines exactly what is to be searched, what is expected to be found, and pre-existing evidence that the person committed a crime.

The PATRIOT Act allows government officials to intercept and monitor data transmissions over the internet without getting a specific warrant for it. The Act essentially gives officials the right to sieze private data and then sift through it later. They don't need to be looking for anything specific. They don't even need a specific place to search. To give an analogy, it's like having the police come into your home and taking record of everything that's there and everything that's changed. Every day. While you're not there. For as long as the surveillance lasts.
