#454537 8 2008-07-23T05:07:19Z Atilla
You don't consider "So-and-so attempts to conquer the world/galaxy/universe" a cliche? It's exactly the same as the fantasy cliche, "So-and-so attempts to conquer the kingdom/world/planes", but with lightsabres instead of magic swords and psionics instead of magi. Indeed, popular sci-fi like Star Wars and other space operas are often the epitome of the "epic war" scenario.

"Machines Go Bad" is also quite the cliche, and sci-fi has the "traipsing though dungeons" thing going too - instead of the Escape from the Demon Lords of Ur, it's Escape from the Crab People of Protolon Five. The fact is that fantasy and sci-fi are quite similar genres and share many of the same tropes, to the extent that it can sometimes be difficult to decide whether a particular work is sci-fi or fantasy (some are termed "science-fantasy" these days). Isobelle Carmody's Obernewtyn series is a good example - I'd call it sci-fi, but to the casual glance it looks like fantasy.

Also, there are plenty of fantasy works which involve neither dungeons or epic wars. Neil Gaiman's Anansi Boys is a good modern example, as are several of Gaiman's other works. Many Pratchett books also avoid standard fantasy plots - Reaper Man and Going Postal are good examples. Also, don't forget that fantasy also includes fairy tales and wacky Japanese stories about a fox and a tanuki placing bets on who is better at shape-shifting.
