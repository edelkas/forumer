So, I saw Cossack's post in the Ron Mueck thread. I totally disagree.

He used two very visible examples of modern art: Marcel Duchamp's "Fountain," and Damien Hirst's "the Physical Impossibility of Death in the Mind of Someone Living." While these are both excellent examples of modern art, it is a misconception that these works were created with arrogance.

When Duchamp signed a urinal and declared it art, he was trying to test the word of the organizers of an exhibition that claimed they [paraphrasing] "would exhibit all work submitted." [/paraphrasing]
Keep in mind as well that he was a member of Dada, so he was seeking to create "anti-art."

When Hirst made P.I.D.M.S.L, he was trying to make a lasting impression. He wanted the viewer to feel something, and judging by the controversy, it worked.
Last summer I had the honor of seeing it in person, and let me tell you, do not judge it until you've seen it in person your self; it holds an awesome weight.
(as a side note, Cossack referred to P.I.D.M.S.L as a "a shark cut in half," which is inaccurate.)


I've opened this thread for a discussion of modern art, so feel free to expand the scope beyond what I've covered.

#392911 12 2007-12-24T06:17:35Z Goatman
I'm an artist myself, I recently had my debut exhibition. I have several opinions on art, several things that annoy me, all that jazz.

Firstly, just addressing cossack here, money means absolutely nothing to most artists. Take a graffiti artist, say someone like Banksy. He doesn't run around doing his various stunts (eg. sticking his art in prestigious galleries, scrawling messages in elephant cages, etc.) for the money. If anything, he does it for the publicity, the fun and the love of art. And I think most artists would agree with this philosophy (unless of course they are a painter in which case it's likely they are prestigious bastards. Good artists, yet still prestigious bastards).

Secondly, the thing that probably affects me most, as a fourteen year old, is exhibiting. There are many skilled artists out there who simply do not have the money or the place to exhibit. I was lucky to stumble upon a small gallery around the corner from my school which charged me nothing, others aren't. Galleries (that are good) are hard to find, and often won't except more modern art forms such as installation or new media.

Thirdly, art is overpriced. Take for instance a small graffiti gallery in Melbourne, Until Never. It sells some of its sketches there for $300, when I could make something in ten minutes of the same quality. And I'm no Van Gough. It's all overrated, overpriced, and lacking in originality, skill, concept and quality. Just because some accomplished artist makes a piece, doesn't mean it should sell for three million dollars.

Yep, that's about it.
