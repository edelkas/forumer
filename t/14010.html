#259703 5 2006-11-25T23:34:16Z Twistkill
The game is great in its own right. Below is an in-depth, but not completely thorough, overview of the game.

+ indicates a good change. - indicates a bad change. = indicates it could go either way.

= Crew. The crew feature is a unique change. It's not very complex or particularly exciting, but it can make the game a little easier in certain races. There are 3 types of crew members you can hire:

(Crew continued) A blocker can temporarily slow down opponents who are catching up to you. Drawback: They can sometimes mess up and miss your opponents.

(Crew continued) Drafters speed ahead of you, and if you follow behind them for a few seconds, you get a boost of speed when you pass them. Drawback: There aren't too many long straightaways necessary for a good draft.

(Crew continued) Scouts find all the shortcuts possible in a race, and they know the best lines through corners. Drawback: Er... I've honestly never used a Scout before. Sorry. I'll get back to you on this one...

(Crew continued) You can only have 3 crew members available, and you only take one of them with you into races.

- There is no more reason to have the cops chase you anymore. There is no bounty or milestones to achieve, and the cop chases aren't really as exciting.

+ Canyon races. Look in the Boss and Canyon races section for details.

+ Urbanization. The city theme makes a welcome return. It is night-time when you play once more, and overall it has a better feel to it.

- The tier system. The tier system for cars isn't very good, in my opinion. There are 3 tiers, and each tier is better than the last one. You start with Tier 1 cars. When you upgrade it to the max, it's still only better than average. Tier 2s are great when upgraded to the maximum, and Tier 3 can be upgraded the best, but the problem is that it is awkward. I wish there could just be cars like in Most Wanted, they're all great when performance is upgraded to the max... all you would need is the cash. Unfortunately, this is not so.

+ Classes. The class system for cars is a welcome addition. There are 3 classes of cars, and the 3 tiers go along with them, as well: Muscles, Tuners, and Exotics.

(Classes continued) Muscles have the best acceleration in the game, as well as a hefty top speed. They are the best in the straightaways. Their main problem is cornering. You need to slide into corners early to be able to make them correctly, but if handled properly, a Tier 3 muscle can be the best car in the game. (The best is the Chevrolet Corvette)

(Classes continued) Tuners are all about handling. They make tighter turns than the other 2 classes, and their acceleration is pretty good, but their top speed is a little slow. So, don't slow down too much when you're entering a corner and leave your opponents in the wall. The best Tuner... I believe is either the Lancer Evolution 9 or the Nissan Skyline.

(Classes continued) Exotics are kinda mixed. They share a little of the turning ability of the Tuners, yet they share some of the Muscles' speed. They do, however, take skill to drive. If you can outrun your opponents in the corners, and you can find a good line down a straightaway, you can beat almost anyone. I believe the best is the Lamborghini Murcielago.

(Classes continued) So you see, all cars from the 3 classes handle and drive differently, and this is a welcome addition to the game.

= Customization. The Aftermarket system is basically unchanged form Most Wanted, (NFSU 2 had the best customization I've ever seen) but the unique Autosculpt system is kinda neat. Only special parts are used with the system. You actually shape the bumpers, hoods, wheels, etc. that you want. Unfortunately, there aren't enough options for bumpers. Body Kits in the Aftermarket system are still my choice. It all depends on what you prefer.

+ Territories. This is a cool change, and although it's not very in-depth, it works. There are 4 territories in the game, with several zones in each territory, and there are 3 or so races in each zone. When you beat 2 out of 3 races in a particular zone, you gain control of that zone. If you gain control over all of the zones in a territory, you come to the boss race for that territory, (Explained next) and only if you beat the boss will you claim ownership of that territory.

Mostly + Boss and Canyon races. There are only 4 boss races in the game. But, they are more exciting. When you beat all of the zones in a territory, you face the boss. The first race against the boss is a 1-on-1 in the city. Beat him there, and you go the Canyon race. There are 3 canyons, and which one you race in depends on the territory you're trying to take over. It's all downhill.

(Boss and Canyon races continued) In the first race against the boss, you have to stay close to him/her as he/she races down the canyon. A meter displays how close you are to the boss. The closer you are at any particular point, the more points you accumulate. If you start to fall behind, your point accumulation slows down.

(Boss and Canyon races continued) In the second Canyon race, it is the opposite. You are in front and you must try to keep as far ahead of the boss as possible. Whatever points you accumulated in the previous race are what you start with. The farther ahead you are at any particular point, the slower your points decrease, and likewise, if he is sticking on your ass, you lose points faster.

(Boss and Canyon races continued) If, in the first race, you somehow manage to pass the boss for 10 seconds, you automatically win both races. However, if the boss passes you in the second race for 10 seconds, you automatically lose.

(Boss and Canyon races continued) Some of the turns are on the edge of the canyon! If you make these turns too wide, you may hit the edge of the turn and fall of the cliff! You must restart the race from the first Canyon race if this happens.

+ Graphics are even better than Most Wanted, even for the PS2. The cutscenes are amazing. (If that is somehow possible. Damn EA and their haxoring abiliteez)

Phew... This is not a throughly exhaustive overview of the game, but it is quite detailed in its own right. Overall, this game is quite fun, and it is very challenging when you get to the last boss race. The racing action is still clean and exciting, there are several changes to the gameplay, and the graphics are slightly improved.

#259786 12 2006-11-26T04:20:29Z George
Well, it only took me a week to beat it. I'm currently on 94% career completion.

I think that Need For Speed Carbon disappointed me. Graphically, it's a step up, but I think the career mode was way too short. I think they could have expanded the plot a lot more.

With the overall look, I wasn't much of an Underground player, but was with Most Wanted. I preferred the day time racing rather than night time. I'm also pretty sure that the world was bigger in Most Wanted. I also didn't like the fact that there weren't many places for you to test out top speed in Carbon. I only reached speeds of 300 km+ (186 miles) a few times in the whole game.

The difficulty curve on Most Wanted was very good, especially with the large impact bounty and milestones had. Carbon was just a walk over through the first three bosses, and only got a little harder on the last boss. Ironically, it only took me four tries to beat Darius on the canyon duel.

Some welcome features I thought were the new customizations availiable, the introduction of tiers, and crew members. Personally, I like the tier system because in Most Wanted, I stuck with my Lexus IS300 throughout the whole game and had a truckload of spare cash. This one makes you work for new cars.

The crew members are okay, because if you carried over some skills from Most Wanted, you can win without the use of crew members through most of the game.

Carbon also introduced the new Drift race, which replaced Drag from Most Wanted. I think that they should have included both of them in the game, because there wasn't too much variety. Circuit and Sprint was virtually the same, Speedtrap was a walk over with some nitrous oxide, and checkpoint was easy as well. If they incoporated both Drift and Drag, Carbon would have been a much better game.

I still can't believe that I've only had a total of six pursuits with the police after completing the game. The police AI is incredibly stupid, because even when I had Heat 3, I could avoid the police in a matter of seconds. Back in Most Wanted, my longest pursuit was something around half an hour.

Overall, I get the feeling that EA made this in a rush, it took me around a month to finish Most Wanted, whereas Carbon's career mode was just a joke.
