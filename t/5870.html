#121260 15 2005-12-08T04:50:14Z ImALoserBaby
Okay, since no one's actually answering your question (About where you should start), I'll answer.

Pong.

Making anything even close to N is an incredible achievement for ANY Flash programmer, and according to what you've said, you've never coded anything before.

And no, I'm not just basing this on assumed information-I've used Actionscript for more than three years now, so I know the ropes, at least.

Start with Pong, or something else that's really simple. The biggest mistake that new coders make is being over ambitious, and getting disappointed that they can't achieve their out of reach goals.

Wait a little while before you start with your ninja game.

But if you REALLY want to start, here's what I suggest figuring out. These are merely guidelines, so don't follow them word for word. And they're also only suggestions, so don't think that I'm telling you that this is the only way to make your game.

1. Collision detection and reaction. You've said you've read up on Metanet's tutorials on this, but you should avoid using their methods-they're far more powerful than you need. Simple math or even hitTest() will do if your platforms are all rectangular (For the other programmers out there, I'm gonna say that a tile based engine is also out for now). Some more math is needed for slanted ground.

2. Basic simulated physics. Jumping is the most important gameplay element. Slowing down is a very close second. Gravity, and friction, respectively.

3. Trigonometry. If you're making a shooting game, you'll need to be able to understand everything out there about angles-especially these three functions: Math.atan2(), Math.sin(), and Math.cos(). The Pythagorean Theorem is also needed in many cases.

4. AI. AI is always a tough thing to implement if it involves anything even slightly complex, but the AI in N is very simple anyway, so if your game is similar, not much will be needed here. Even so, it can cause lots of problems.

5. PLANNING. A game like this needs to be well thought out, or else it's bound to fall through somewhere in the process of creating the game and coding at the same time.

There are lots of other things needed, but those are what you'll really need to have down to tackle this project.

Oh, and I just thought about this-If that came out as condescending, it wasn't supposed to. It's kinda hard to talk flatly and not sound that way on the internet...
