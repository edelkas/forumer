Q: What is "1337 / g33k-t4lk?" I hear about it so often on the Internet
A: G33k-T4lk is a kind of Internet-language used by nerds, script kiddies (simple virus programmers), etc. It regards using any symbols except actual letters to resemble letters. Some are static (there's only one way to resemble it), but it really depends on the person. In G33k-T4lk, "1337" = "leet" = "elite," although most say those who speak in G33k-T4lk are morons who have no life
Here are some inflexible, always-the-same translations:

E = 3 (I don't know anyone who has E for something other than 3; maybe the Euro sign)
D = Almost always |); can be [), etc.
H = |-|
P = Almost always either |> or |*
R = |2 (if you slightly bent the 2 diagonally, it looks like the right side of an R)
S = 5
And so on. It really depends on you; letter symbols are really flexible if you can picture it right (e.g. A = 4 or /-\).

In "minor g33k-t4lk" - as the above is totally hardcore - only a few replacement symbols are used, like "t3h" = "the," & "j00" = "you." For some reason j is pronounced as a y I don't see why, because a Y is just as easily put into symbolization: `/.

Example text:
1 |200|_ _|00! = I rool joo! = I rule you!

You can find the Wikipedia's extensive information and history on Leet here.

Q: Why do karate people grunt when they punch/kick?
A: (Thanks to ShadyTrader for this bit of info) Well, yelling might demoralize the enemy or make bystanders think you're crazy and flee, and it tightens your abs and other torso muscles so hits to your chest or stomach wouldn't hurt as bad Plus (this, thanks to wilderness), as you attack, your body tenses, and on impact, you release it. The exulsion of air increases your focus and adds weight to your thrust, kick, jab etc. Furthermore, (epigone edit) the 'grunt' being referred to here is actually called a kiai (pronounced key-eye), which is a Japanese word that roughly translates to 'spirit yell.' The idea is to internally focus your energy and release it in one explosive force of will. Want to know more?

Q: How do I take screenshots?
A: On Windows, press the Print Screen button - located above Insert in most keyboards - to copy a capture of your screen to your computer's clipboard. Alternatively, Alt + Print Screen takes a capture of your currently selected window. Then, open any image editor (even Paint will do), and paste the capture with Ctrl + V. Remember to save the result and you're done.
