Quote: My Beef With Theism
If I were forced to pick one thing in the world that I despise, Id have to say its religion. Nothing makes me feel sorry for and dislike the world as much as religion. It wasnt always this way. I remember a time when I was deeply religious and thought that religion was the best thing for someone. Then I turned nine.
I was once Russian Orthodox. I was very devoted to my faith. I would pray every night, I would talk to God, and I would credit Him for making me as great as I was (I thought very highly of myself in my younger years). But as I grew older, I began to question my faith. Was it really God that was making my life blissful, or was it simply that the circumstances of my life naturally and conveniently made me happy? As soon as I realized that happiness can come from something besides God, I was torn between atheism and theism.
To solve this problem, I decided to look into proving God. It was just about as hard as it sounds. The evidence I saw for God was the fact that the majority of the world believed in Him and that the Bible affirms his existence. I tried to look at the Bible first, but ended up dropping the pursuit, mainly because I couldnt read Slavic (and the Bible was written in Slavic in my church) and because I didnt have the patience. So instead, I compared general majority opinion and the general agreement that God exists.
Now, I think abstractly (relative to the majority of the world, which thinks concretely), and its very easy for me to prove something to myself definitely in a short amount of time. For most of the year, when everyone else in my second grade class was swinging on the monkey bars or going down the slide repeatedly, I was sitting on the corner of the sandbox thinking about God. By the end of that year, I had absolutely no doubt in my mind that God was about as real as Santa Claus or the Tooth Fairy. From what I remember (which isnt much), it was a lengthy series of conditional ad absurdum arguments about God, combined with the fact that a positive assertion (like a theory) needed clear proof to be taken seriously, that made his existence entirely impossible.
When the day came that I made the definite conclusion that God did not exist, I was very concerned that I might be wrong and that God would hate me or something, but as time went on, my growing wisdom only provided me more affirmation that God does not exist. And so I went into third grade a proud atheist. I had no problem with others believing. I just assumed that their parents would tell them what I believed to be the truth or that the kids would figure it out for themselves. It didnt surprise me that I was the only one to realize this truth, since I went to a public school and was consistently at the top of my class.
My alarm at the blind faith of the world grew substantially when I went to a religious private school, realizing that the school was created by adults that believed in God. To me, this was just the same as an adult that believed in the Easter Bunny. The masses that I had to sit through, all part of going to a Catholic school, made me realize that this was a serious issue and that people actually believed what I considered to be baloney. It was also most likely what made me think less of theists intellectually.
As I moved on, my beliefs became an excellent candidate for the most influential force in my life. As I picked up odd bits of wisdom with age, my atheism was being constantly reaffirmed in new ways, from completely different angles. By the time I reached 8th grade, I had denounced the Bible as the worst crime on human integrity in the history of man. Worse yet, which made me sick to my stomach the moment I realized it, people in the world took it literally without any qualms or second doubts.
At one point in my life (its hard to pin down), I also realized that there are other religions aside from Christianity. I looked into them with interest, but rolled my eyes when I saw that most of these religions contained god figures. Christianity, Judaism, Islam, Hinduism, theyre all the same to me. They all believe in inconsistent gods that have no hope of existing. I stopped caring about nuances between them. I dont care whether God/Elohim/Allah/Brahma tells us to eat red meat or not, its still some god figure trying to direct human lives for absolutely no reason. Buddhism and Zen stood out in this crowd, however, since they believed in balances of forces rather than god figures, but I ended up turning my head from them as well when I realized they came from human need to explain something and in his haste forgetting to apply reasoning, instead relying on the fact that things that sound mystical and super-neato are mistaken (even by themselves) as some ultimate wisdom. To me, it was all about the phrase, gee, wouldnt it be cool if...
But what really gets me going about religion is that even those masses that I consider brainwashed and inferior dont act consistently with their beliefs. Jesus taught Christians to turn the other cheek and to love their enemies, right? God wants peace, and Christianity promotes peace and benevolence, right? Youd think so, because clergymen and the Bible and any Christian will tell you that a Christian ought to be kind and merciful and peaceful and loving. This is pounded and etched into the skulls of any Christian. If this is all true, why is a Christian 40 times more likely (statistically, based on prison censuses) than an atheist to commit a violent crime, such as murder or serial rape? Why do Christians lead the nation in divorce rates, drug usage, spouse-beating charges, child abuse, child molestation, and alcoholism? What about the crusades? The witch-burning trials? Why have I, to date, received four death threats and five threats of massive bodily harm for solely theistic reasons? And why have I only received practically unconditional friendship from any atheist Ive met?
I guess my real problem is with the people. The weaker masses. I can freely admit, even if I regret it, that Im an elitist. My experience with theists has only shown that theism is for the weaker, less intelligent, more violent masses, and that there is a reason that strength of faith is directly proportional to how violent and ignorant the society is. I can fully understand why someone would believe, including fears of loneliness and lack of purpose. Humans want to feel friendship, and they want to be needed. Faith, from my perspective, is really just a measure of how desperate someone is for purpose and companionship that they suspend reason.
Im an optimist. I want the world to be intelligent, prosperous, peaceful, and efficient. Im only met with overwhelming opposition when I dare mention my beliefs. Because religion is directly attacking my efforts to better mankind, I reserve a special place for discriminate dislike for it within my heart.
