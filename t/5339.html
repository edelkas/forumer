#110554 7 2005-10-26T01:46:14Z Daddaluma
Here's a bit of a longer one involving some logic. I'm not quite sure where it breaks down either, but I know there's something wrong somewhere.

So in some ancient civilization some guy is sentenced to die.

He is put in a room with 5 doors marked 1-5. He is told that behind one of the doors there will be a lion which will jump out unexpectdly and maul him. They tell him he has to open all the doors or they'll torture him to death instead.

So he reasons this out. They told me that a lion will jump out unexpectedly from one of these doors. Well, if I opened the first 4, then I know that the lion is behind the 5th door, so it won't be unexpected. SO the lion cannot be behind the 5th door.

Well, since I know that the lion cannot be behind the 5th door, then if I open the first three doors with no lion, then the lion will have to be behind the 4th door. But then it would be expected, so it can't be behind the 4th door either.

Well since I know it can't be behind the 4th or the 5th doors, then if I open the first two with no lion, then I will know the lion is behind the 3rd. But then it won't be unexpected. SO the lion can't be behind the 3rd either.

And so on until he rules out all 5 doors.

He then is confident that there is not in fact a lion behind any of the doors, so he begins to open them. And he is surprised when a lion unexpectedly jumps out frombehind door number 2 and mauls him.

What was the problem with his logic?
