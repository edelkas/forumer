You know what I'm sick of?
I'm sick of this pretentious invasion of language by other languages.
You know, when some asshole insists on writing his name in English with characters from a foreign alphabet, or demands you use the pronounciation of his language for a character in your language that looks similar to a character in his.

I said that as generally as I could, so I'm sorry if it's confusing.

But here are some examples:
"Jose"
Question: How many letters are there in the English language that make the sound we normally start off "Jose" with? I can count one - 'h'.
Question: How 'bout 'j'? Does 'j' make the same sound as 'h' in any English word? No, I don't believe it does. 'J' only makes the 'h' sound when it's in another goddamned language. But we're speaking English, so no, Jose, unless you write your name "Hosay," I'm calling you the plural of 'Joe'. You're Joes, bitch.
But "Jose" is sneaky - what about this flagrant "Garon" shit? Does '' look like an English goddamned character to you? When do you say '' in your A-B-C's? I'm rhyming your ass with "archon" unless you write it Garson. And don't use your silly little accents; English words have all kinds of wacky pronounciation rules, so you're free to tell me to put stress on a different syllable, but don't spell it "Garon".

Here's the deal, folks:
If you have a name from a culture that uses a different language, it is your responsibility to intelligibly represent your name phonetically in English when you're writing your name in English. It is not polite to sporadically switch to French or Spanish in the middle of an English sentence, so keep your shit consistent. When writing English, you use English characters, and don't think you can get away with using similar-looking characters that have different pronounciations, Juanito (or should I say "Hwanito?").

My name is not Constantine - my name is Константин, which is actually pronounced noticeably differently from "Constantine," but I have taken the effort to represent my name in English characters using English pronounciation to make a sound that is as faithful to the original phonetically as I care to make it. I do this whenever I'm writing in English. Notice that I do not use Russian pronounciation rules - I do not write my name Konctantine, Koнctaнtiнe, or use characters that aren't identical to English characters at all like и - Koнctaнtин. I don't sporadically switch to Russian when I'm speaking in English.
When some Chinese dude writes his name, he writes in phonetic English, using English characters and their English pronounciations - you don't see Chinese names in English spelled with random Chinese characters.

But "Jose" is ridiculous. If you believe that the best phonetic English representation of your name is "Jose," then your name is pronounced "Joes." There is little more to it than that. "Juan" is "Jew-on." When I ask for your name in English, I am asking for a reply in English, bitch. Don't you switch up languages on me in the middle of a sentence, because then you're just being a twat. Our George changes his name to Jorge for your ungrateful asses, but you need to meet us halfway. Otherwise, you're calling your language better than ours by refusing to use our barbaric, unenlightened tongue to represent your precious name.

Moving beyond names, even, I don't go to "ballets" because that's a nonsense word in English. It sure does mean something in French, but we're not speaking French. My sister does "balley," but I have no idea what "ballet" is referring to. Similarly, I enjoy "buffeys," but don't rightly think I've ever been to a buffet. Ever been to Illinois? I haven't, but there's a state in the US with a similar name - Illinoy. Have some Merlow while you're there, and maybe some kesadias.
"Oh, but it's a Spanish food, so we use the Spanish spelling - quesadillas." Yeah, well we call it borscht, not борщ, and we say chai, not чай.

Enforce logical language!
