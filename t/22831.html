I've been in Cambridge, MA for the last few days for my brother's graduation from MIT (my brother is Gordon Freeman). The commencement ceremony was about 4 hours, unless you wanted any seating whatsoever, in which case it was closer to 4 hours of ceremony + 2 hours of waiting.
Out of all members of my family, I'm chosen to arrive early and reserve seats. So I arrive at 8:30 AM or so, about an hour after they started letting people in (commencement began at 9:45 AM), and I managed to get some decent seats. The weather forecast for the day was "slightly cloudy," so I only brought a light jacket in the case of light rain. About half an hour in, it started to rain very heavily. My jacket, despite the word "weatherproof" written on the inside, turned out not to be so weatherproof. Wondering who the hell thought that a commencement ceremony held outside in Boston was at any point a good idea, I ran to get one of the ponchos being handed out by the staff. By the time I get back to my seats, they're of course drenched with water, and the rag I used to wipe them off from the last night's rain was thoroughly soaked and useless. My poncho was just short enough that my butt was uncovered by it when I sat down. Figuring I'm already soaked anyway, I did my best to clear as much water as I could from the seat before sitting down and soaking my ass. The rain stops falling at around 9:20, and my family shows up at 9:30. They're mostly dry, whereas I'm thoroughly soaked (especially from the jackasses in front of me who kept tilting their umbrellas back, dumping a bunch of water onto my lap). No one behind the third row can see anything anyway because 70% of the crowd decided that bringing umbrellas wasn't discourteous.
The graduates begin marching down the center aisle at around 10:15, led by some official in fancy robes who was carrying a huge golden mace (the Mace of Knowledge? not a very reasonable item to represent higher learning, is it?). And this line of graduates was enormous. They just did not stop coming. Their number was somewhere in the hundreds. Once everyone was in their seats, we got a few speeches from various people, all pretty much saying, "be good people." The only insight I gained from those speeches was that the guest speaker, Mohammad Yunus, was eleven times more awesome than I previously thought. These speeches lasted something around 45 minutes. Then it was time to name all of the graduates. Even though they had two people going at once, one naming undergrads and the other naming graduate students, it still took until about 2:00 PM for them to finish. And my family had to stay around to the end because my brother's major and name put him second-to-last in the order. Then we went around to receptions, and that was just fine.

tl;dr...
Graduation ceremonies are intensely boring. Every family in the audience only cared about one or two graduates, and each graduate only had five people at most in the audience who knew who he was or cared that he was graduating. And yet the entire audience was subjected to the naming of every single one of the hundreds upon hundreds of graduates. This all just seemed unnecessary to me.
So I'm thinking, when I'm in my final year, that I'll make some unchangeable plans for the day of graduation and ask that my diploma simply be mailed to me. Instead of spending my happy day waiting for hours, hungry, in a rain-soaked, fold-out chair, wearing a dress whose color is leaking into my clothing, and messing up my shoes in the mud, I'll invite my friends and family over and have a private graduation party. That way, all the people who don't care won't have to endure me and my family, and I and my family won't have to endure all of the people who don't care. I can't think of a single reason to go with the commencement ceremony over a private party. Not a one. And this extends to all ceremonies where the audience and the participants are only invested in 0.001% of the entire event.
