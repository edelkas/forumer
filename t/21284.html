#405213 10 2008-01-29T03:58:58Z LittleViking
I've never used Gimp more than a little bit, so I fired it up now to give it a shot. It really was just as powerful and easy-to-use as Photoshop. There was never something I wanted to do but couldn't because it wasn't there. It was all there.

Or... almost, anyway. I couldn't find an option for scaling up brushes, believe it or not. It's such a simple option, you'd expect it to be there. But all brushes have one size, and if it's not the right size for you, you have to make a custom brush. (Edit: I see that Gimp 2.4 has scaling options for all brushes now. I just need to upgrade.)

Other than that, I couldn't find anything I'd want to do to an image that Gimp can't handle.

GIMP, Fireworks and Photoshop are all good for what they do. GIMP is effectively a version of Photoshop not made by Adobe, which lacks quite a few features but gives a great outcome.

Fireworks is moreso on the web imaging scale, like small icons and headers, which can be exported into Dreamweaver instantly.

For me, and my experience over the past year and a bit, I prefer Photoshop. The CS3 version has blown me away after the reformat, and is a heck of a lot better than CS2.
