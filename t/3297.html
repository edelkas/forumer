#69069 21 2005-05-24T04:01:12Z great_sea
Well... I still haven't decided, but if I did vote no, this would be the reason:

First of all, you guys are putting way to much worth in the comments. Some of you say that you would rather get feedback on how to improve and what people like/dislike about the map than to get that map rated. In reality however, probably 80% of comments that are already on maps provide almost no _real_ feedback for the author. Most of them are simply demos, or give feedback such as "good job," "It was so fun," "I liked so-and-so part of the map," or "really boring," "not fun," etc. This is the majority of any sort of constructive criticsm that is going around, and most of this isn't much help to the author, besides stating the obvious.

I know that there are some people who do leave in-depth feedback, but this is still a slim margin in comparison with the majority of comments. Unless _alot_ of people start to step it up a notch, only leaving very serious in-depth feedback on maps, I think it would be useless to make comments mandatory in order to vote. Like I said, the majority of comments which people are already givng are composed of some one- or two-line trite remark which really only tells whether or not that person liked the map, or what they gave it, or something about their demo.

It seems to me like instituting the mandatory vote-comment rule would simply make less people vote or comment on maps. I guess this could be a good thing, because the only people voting/commenting would be the ones who leave good feedback/vote fairly (although that is not necessarily true...), but I think that it would cut off too many of the less-commited members of NUMA. (Many of whom are a vital part of the community).

Of course, this is all speculation. In the end, it all depends on the reaction of the people who make up NUMA. How would the general populous respond to such a change? More or less activity? More or less dedicated members? I don't really know. There are pro's and con's for both sides... but at this point I am leaning toward voting "no." But maybe not.
