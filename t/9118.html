Extract: "To remove or take out"

Thus, the act of recording shouldn't be misconstrued as "extracting" because, obviously, when all is done and the sound is recorded, N still remains whole and 100% intact and unchanged with no sounds missing, "removed", or taken out. With this in mind, how could anyone truthfully claim that the act of recording is extracting when at no point is the N audio "tampered with, removed, or edited."

Decompile: First of all, at least to my knowledge, although I welcome correction where correction is merited, decompile isn't a word, thus making it impossible to ever decompile N in any way, shape, or form. However, I know that were I to base my entire point on this "fact," people would get pissed off, which might happen anyway, although I have been trying to go about this whole thing in a non-confrontational or "I am better than you" manner, so I will make another point. Before using an audio program such as the freely downloadable Goldwave, N, in its entirety should be complete/whole. During the entire process of recording, N and any files that belong to or are a part of N, should and will remain untouched, unmodified, unmoved. Once the recording is done, everything about N should be exactly the same as it was before the recording process. With this in mind, I don't see how anyone could even start to "claim" that N was decompiled in any way during the aforementioned process.

Disassemble: to take apart something into its constituent pieces.

Recording sound with Goldwave requires no actions that could even be compared to taking anything apart. N, nor N's audio files, even need to be touched, let alone taken apart, thus making the whole "disassembling" point moot.

Here is one final note for those who seem to not be able to pay attention/comprehend what has been said in prior posts in regards to this matter, at least as far as I can tell, extracting sound from N and recording the sound from N are two completely different and incomparable things, okay?

My point was that you responded to the wrong part of the post, although I appreciate that this may have been unclear. You should have said that you do not believe recording the sounds would be considered decompiling, disassembling, or reverse engineering the software.

"Decompile" is a term used in programming, and is the opposite to compiling (converting source code in one language to object code in another). Look it up on Wikipedia if you want more information. You might also wish to look up assemble/disassemble, which have technical meanings distinct from their everyday use.
