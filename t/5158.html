Okay. This post harkens back to one of nevermore's previous suggestions. It was so brilliant that I needed to expound upon it.

I have an idea for the three levels of NUMA. Basically, they would be member levels. You could be a neonate, a veteran, or a sage. There would be a system for easily narrowing down a search for maps by neonates, veterans, or sages (or any combination thereof). The newest page stays--except maps by neonates are in brown (bronze) font, maps by veterans are in gray (silver) font, and maps by sages are in gold font. Bronze, silver, gold. Make sense?

The system to advance ranks would not need to be moderated. It would be based on a combination of raw map count (including only rated non-test, non-incomplete, and non-uncategorized maps) and average map rating. Why? So that members couldn't just mass-produce maps to gain status; and so members couldn't prune out all but a few good maps to gain status.

Here's the system I propose:

Neonate: beginning phase

Veteran: at least 20 rated maps and an average rating of at least 3.0 (I think if you read through the authors list for expert authors, you'll find a set that is decently-sized, fair, and appropriate. Remember not to allow one's frequency on the boards or high scores table to make you wonder if this is an appropriate system. This set is for devoted map-makers only. The set probably includes between fifty and a hundred members.)

Sage: at least 40 rated maps and an average rating of at least 4.0 (This list is surprisingly good and complete--it shows a broad range of talents that are at various ranks on the "all authors" page. As of this post, there are about 15 members in this group--spanning multiple pages in the all authors area. I think this works very well. Take a look at the "all authors" page to see all the legends who are included.)

Here's why this will work:

The main problem with the newest page is that it is not cleaned up so that maps by quality authors can be found more easily. This will help that work.

The second problem is that status is not well determined by the "All Authors" area, because average ratings can be easily manipulated by deletion of maps (to produce a "musical chairs" effect, which I'm sure everyone has noticed). The authors page will be sorted by these new groupings, first, then by average map rating. Raw map count should show that a member doesn't just make good maps, but does so consistently, which should be a factor of status.

Status will allow us to show which maps accurately have a higher chance of being good, because the authors have had a lot of experience at making good maps. Experience must be a factor of this system to display reliability. If you don't think this will work, take a look at the "All Authors" page and look who would be included. Those who are close to being in a higher group will strive to produce a number of good maps, which will be an incentive to push map-makers in the proper direction.

Also, I think all of these things would provide NUMA with a new feel--which it could use about now. The coloration would help with visual deliniation of quality.

Members that are novice have names that are in a brown (bronze) font. Members that are expert have names that are in a gray (silver) font. Members that are elite have names that are in a gold font. Again, for visual deliniation to help find quality maps.

Elite members could have "stars" which would denote super-coolness. These stars would be awarded for maintaining elite status (4.0 rating) with a large number of maps. Here is the gist:
40 rated maps, no stars (LouDog004, for instance)
60 rated maps, one star (nevermore and myself, for instance)
90 rated maps, two stars (formica, for instance)
130 rated maps, three stars (Sendy, for instance)
180 rated maps, four stars (maximo, for instance)

Just another neat incentive for map-makers to produce more maps at the same or higher quality as their existing maps.

#107348 34 2005-10-11T20:30:32Z Adam Dobay
Hello everyone,

Let me start by saying that I like the idea of blue_tetris very much. Having said that I must point out one problem I see, which is again because of the "human factor" that has come up in a number of topics here.

My proposition is that if this system is realized any time in the future, then in respect for those people who have not made many maps yet but are neither spam-noobs nor considerably bad, there should be more categories between the category which the absolute beginners go into and between which the long-time experts go into.

The reason for my proposition: I think that while setting a line between "novice" and "expert" mapmakers is a good basis, over time there will be less and less chance for newcomers to be able to get into the higher category regardless of their mapmaking skill. There is currently a too large gap between the two categories while there are clearly more than just the noobs and the experts.

Why will it be hard to get a lot of rated maps? Currently, many good maps do not get rated because they get lost in the insanely high number of maps appearing every day.
There are multitudes of maps posted every day to NUMA. I haven't counted, but 50-100 a day might be accurate, probably Arachnid could tell. Some of those are enjoyable maps made by intermediate levelmakers, some of them are very bad levels made by noobs and some of them are close to the equivalent of spam. Among these levels, there are very good ones by people who haven't made many maps but make good maps and have some self-criticism not to post maps they have made and think they're bad.

So why is this a problem? I've witnessed more than once before that a map that I have spotted on the Newest list and voted on still has one vote because it got down two pages in the "newest" list in less than fifteen minutes, and thus it is not surprising that it never got past one vote.
The result: even if someone makes a good map, it may go unnoticed because of the map posting overflow, so currently, in the end it is not the quality of the map that counts but rather chance: if you're lucky enough to stay on the newest page for long enough you may get a rating because more people get the chance to see it. Or if you're lucky enough to have someone online at the time who will take the time and energy to look at some of your other maps, you may get more votes.
And I see this on my own levels as well, as while two of my levels were rated 3 and 4 and others have been commented about being good, I have not received a rating in 2 months for any of my other 16 levels. At this rate it will take roughly two years to get 20 of my maps rated, and then I can get into being expert.

So, what can be done about this? I think that the main problem is that good but unseen level makers get sorted in the exact same category as the spamming noobs. So I think that if blue_tetris's idea is implemented, there should be at least one more category, for people who have very few ratings (eg. as low as 3) but the ratings of which are over 3. As spammer noobs either don't get rated or get low votes, this could filter those out. Then, there could be another categories, for people who have 5, 10, 15, etc. levels rated, depending on how many others are needed.
With this system, you could filter the people who have already made a couple of good levels and you can also help their work by voting on them. This way, maps may not get lost as easily as they do now.

Another idea would be that there could be a limit to how many maps you can submit to NUMA without voting for the levels of others, but that idea doesn't strictly belong here so I will make another topic for it and elaborate it there.

Another comment: Some of you proposed that 3 votes should be enough for experts. As Arachnid has pointed out in another NUMA topic, he made the minimum 5 because under that, one vote could change the rating by a whole ninja. So, it may not be really accurate even though experts vote better than noobs.
