#88074 19 2005-08-03T22:06:01Z crazychimp132
No, that's not the idea.

I'll give a very basic example. I've come up with much more complex applications than this, but for the sake of explanation this works well.

A level, A, is made with two exit doors- one on the far right and one on the far left. This level's data is displayed on a website, and beneath it is a blank box for the user to submit replay data through. The user copies the level data and plays it in N, recording the demo taking the right exit. The player submits the replay and the website processes it, figuring out which exit the player took. The player is then presented with new level data of the level meant to be to the right of Level A. This level, B, can link back to A or continue to the right, linking to another level. The website keeps track of the level the user is currently on and what each exit door links to. It would not be very difficult to program that, and in this way it is possible to have several levels all linked together, in different directions.

As I stated before, I've thought of much more complex applications, taking advantage of how level data is formatted. I have also come up with a way to make it easy for people to develope their own sets of levels. There are a whole lot of possibilities.
