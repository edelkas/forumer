#20294 1 2008-05-21T01:06:07Z DemonzLunchBreak
My grandmother is going to die soon. That is, her heart will stop beating and her body will decompose. I know this. I know because she has been severely ill for two and a half years. She had an infection in her brain which created an abscess. The doctors managed to treat the infection, but the pressure in her head severely damaged her brain. She is minimally responsive, doesn't recognize anyone, and cannot talk, eat, or even sleep on her own. She used to smile when I came to visit her, even after the illness struck. Now she stares blankly. Her sickness has effected her immune system. She has developed a strain of pneumonia that is resistant to multiple drugs. The only medication that works against it anymore is a powerful antibiotic that makes the pneumonia go away, but brings my grandma close to dying.

The pneumonia comes and goes. Every few months, she'll get it again. There's not very much to be done about it. Even the nuclear antibiotic only makes the sickness go away for a while. I found out a couple hours ago that she got sick again, except that this time, my family and the doctors have elected not to treat her. This means that her death is guaranteed, as far as I can tell.

I've been struggling with thinking about this stuff. My first reaction was denial. Not outright denial - I knew that what my parents were saying was true. It was denial of thought about it. I went into emotional lock down. I couldn't really think about anything that was happening.

Now that I've had some time to think, I want to open up about it. The truth is, I don't feel very sad about this new development. I feel very sad that I'm reminded of the horrible situation, because it really is terrible. But as far as I can tell, my grandmother died more than a year ago, and her body is nothing more than a painful reminder of the amazing person she once was. And if she is still really in there, somewhere, then every day must be torture for her, and so death is merciful. There really was no chance that she would get better after that kind of brain damage and that length of time. I hate that it had to come to this, but I support the decision of my family to let her go.

I don't really know why I'm posting this, but I have an urge to express these thoughts, so I guess I'll do it. If you guys have had similar experiences, or if you have questions or want to discuss this kind of thing, go ahead. I don't want this to turn into a Terri Schiavo style debate. Don't go down that road, that's all I ask.
