Now, as far as I'm concerned, science and religion are NOT opposed to each other. Certain theories or observations may sometimes contradict Holy Text X, but that doesn't mean science and religion are by their very natures opposed; even close friends disagree sometimes.

What some people fail to understand is that science is simply a system for predicting and modelling physical systems based on empirical observations. No more, no less. It cannot be used to prove or disprove the existence of the supernatural or spiritual because it deals only with the physical. That said, it may be used to disprove beliefs about the physical world which are associated with certain beliefs about the spiritual; the traditional example is the belief that Earth is at the centre of the universe.

People also often forget that science is based on observations and evidence, and there is always the chance that accepted theories will change based on new observations and better evidence. Scientists to not claim to be arbiters of ultimate truth, and what is proven today may be debunked in fifty years (or, more likely, modified until it works). Take gravity, for example; it used to be thought that Newton's theory of gravity was all you needed to know. Then Einstein came along with relativity, which explained what Newton's theories did not. But that doesn't mean Newton's ideas were foolish or stupid. They work very well for everyday objects, and are still commonly used. Similarly, classical mechanics don't work on a quantum level, but are still fine for everyday calculations. Furthermore, scientific theories descirbe what is currently thought to be the most accurate or most likely explanation, rather than some kind of ultimate truth.

As such, don't go around saying "I'm not religious because I believe in Science". That's treating science as a religion, which it most certainly is not. "Believing in" something implies that you have faith, and faith should not be part of science; as I said earlier, it is a system which is rooted in evidence and observations. Scientists even calculate the odds that their experimental results were a fluke, and how much they can expect results to deviate from a prediction due to inaccuracy of equipment, minor uncontrolled variables (gravity is slightly different depending where you are on Earth's surface, for instance) and so on. You can believe a specific theory is correct, or believe that the scientific method is a good way of doing things, but don't go around capitalising science and saying you Believe as if it's some kind of monolithic entity you can throw your faith behind.

Also, please refrain from saying "Oh, scientists disagreed/changed their mind once, so they're unreliable", "This one experiment went wrong, so the whole technique is unreliable" or "It's only a THEORY". Firstly, it is good that scientists change their minds; it means that when something doesn't fit the evidence, a better explanation is found. The newly accepted hypothesis is likely to be more reliable and more accurate than than the old one. Constantly changing and refining accepted theory makes science more reliable, not less. Secondly, please learn the correct technical usage of "theory". In science, a theory is an explanation which has been extensively tested and verified by many independent sources (and the details of the experiments conducted by said sources have been examined by still others, and the experiment has been shown to be repeatable). Theories are therefore among the most reliable parts of scientific knowledge. This extensive testing process also catches out flukely results, due to an error in the experiment, problems with equipment, misinterpretation of results, and so on.

Finally, don't get in a huff because scientific theories disagree with your personal beliefs. Scientists are just reporting what they observe, and as I have stated earlier, scientific explanations are restricted to the purely physical. If the most likely physical explanation for the facts is not the same as your spiritual explanation, then just consider that their explanation works as best it can within the restraints of science. Even if it's not actually true, it lets them predict a few useful things, and that's the important part. At this point, someone will no doubt complain that scientists deliberately ignore the supernatural, and therefore promote atheism. No, they do not. Science is restricted to the physical because it is useless otherwise. If supernatural explanations are allowed, any observation can be explained as the whim of a deity, and there can be no predictions; after all, what man can fathom the mind of a god?

So, in short, science describes the physical world, and religion the spiritual, and for the most part, they don't intersect. Scientists are not trying to determine how much an evil act weighs (how many can you perform before your heart outweighs a feather? And what kind of feather is it?), or to calculate exactly how hot the fires of hell are. Nor do religions attempt to calculate the efficiency of a Carnot cycle between resevoirs at 600K and 300K. Occasionally, things wander into an area which some think is physical and others think is spiritual, and then there is a clash of opinions; however, this by no means implies that science and religion are fundamentally opposed.
