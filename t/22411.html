let me see what I can remember...

1.) no kicking or punching places which are off-limits (which can mean anything). Usually the rules are no kicking or punching to the knees or below the knees (tripping is allowed in some cases though), no attacking the neck, and arms don't count.

2.) If you are on the ground you can sometimes score a point on your opponent depending on the strike and where you hit. Usually though being on the ground means that you cannot score but your opponent can score on you.

3.) No stepping out of the ring (same thing goes here)

4.) No elbow smash's or knee kicks.

5.) Contact of a strike depends on the match, though usually it is kiss-touch (pulling your strike just after it makes slight contact).

Seriously, people, don't make wild statements like that.

TRUE, Tae Kwon Do does award 'black belts' to 9 year olds, but that sort of black belt means absolutely nothing. Black belts earnt through the kid's programs are not at all the same as the adult ones.

Anyone who has achieved 2DS black belt status over the course of at least 8 years and has studied with an ATF certified school can pretty much kill you in a fight. Tae Kwon Do olympic-style sparring has rules, but for day-to-day 'street fights' there are no rules, and students (at least at my school) are trained to fight for their life in any way possible.

The bottom line: Tae Kwon Do is amazing at the advanced level. It just looks flashy when kids do it.
