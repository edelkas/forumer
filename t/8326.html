Kablizzy Posted: March 04, 2005 10:54 am
Quote
Turn Ons:
Symmetry. I'm a symmetry freak. Make it symmetrical, and chances are, I'll love it.
Tradition. I like maps that stay within the accepted boundaries of map-making. The exception to this, of course, is unique ideas that are executed well. Boss Maps? No. Bug-only maps? Christ, no. Beyond that, I'm pretty lenient, so long as it's done well.
Simplicity. I can't stress how vital this is. I would rather have a map with one homing rocket than ten.

Turn Offs:
DDA maps where N dies, uses an excess of jump pads, or an excess of gold delay.
Asymmetrical maps. Not so much, but when a switch isn't centered, and it feels like it should be, it gets on my nerves. ESPECIALLY when it happens to one of my maps, grumble...
Levels where the mapmaker thinks it a superkewl idea to make one map with gausses, replace the gausses with homing rockets, replace the homing rockets with laser drones, so on and so forth until they have 5 of the same exact map with different enemies.
Maps where you have to delete the outside tiles. Come on, just no. The exception is James' DDA where N was vaulted 170 miles straight up, that was pretty cool. If the DDA hadn't lasted 11 minutes, it would have been downright awesome.
Maps that use hidden gausses, laser drones, et cetera. The one exception to this is hidden thwumps, but not by much.
Maps that aren't playtested properly. This is just a simple requirement. What kind of audacity do you have not to test if something works or not? A door bug here and there when you don't know about it, but I like playing through my levels prior to submitting, and I think I've only done this once or twice.
Unneccesary Map spam. I can see two, maybe three maps at a time, but once you get into a page, or two, or three, then you've got too many damn maps at once, and you need to take it outside. Because having that many maps in one day means that you've either been in a diabetic coma or your maps are crap.
Boss maps. I'd have to say this is the least of my pet peeves, but I still don't see the point, since the vast majority are awful.

Here are mine, nothing real special:

+ Rockets. I love rockets, and if you can implement them in a way that doesn't feel overused I will love your map too.
+ Overall polish. This is basically my first impression. Everything should look neat and organized.
+ Symmetry. Not just for symmetry's sake, but in a way that both looks good and helps the gameplay.
+ Color variety. Blue from drones, red from mines, and yellow from gold make a level look visually pleasing.
+ Stylized. The kind of map that you can look at and play without knowing the author and say, "Oh yeah, so and so made this one."
+ Gold. Maps just tend to look empty without it, and it gives you an extra challenge.

- Rockets. Yes, this is sort of a contradiction, but there are sooo many rocket maps out there, and if yours doesn't do anything new with 'em it's just another map among hundreds of others.
- Bugs. I really don't like them, unless they're used in just the right way.
- Unnecessary items. My biggest pet peeve, if there's a single mine or one-way that doesn't need to be there and isn't for aesthetic purposes, I hate it with a passion.
- Frustrating areas. I know, nobody likes them, but for me they can ruin the entire map in some cases.
- Chainguns. Eew. My least favorite enemy, unless you can use them in a way in which they don't result in cheap deaths.

Beo Posted: March 15, 2006 02:03 pm
Quote
Good:

- Good Gold Placement, I hate sloppily placed gold and overuse when the gold isn't properly placed. If you're going to use Z-snap, be extremely careful.
- Mines, maps just feel empty without at least a few.
- Planning, what I mean is that you can't make a tileset, slap some enemies on and call it a map. Have drone pathing that makes jumps that little bit trickier, a carefully placed floorguard to complicate an otherwise easy map. A nicely placed mine may force to take a completely different route
- Flow, maps have to have some kind of flow, even if they're action. This word also covers another design aspect, the pacing of the map. Make sure that it's not fast-paced and actiony one second, then slow and careful the next. If your map revolves around quickly changing playing styles, make sure you switch between the two at a steady pace, not at inconsistent intervals. Try to make your map flow between different gameplay styles.
- A variety of objects, it annoys me when maps have gold, mines and rockets [sometimes gausses.] If you're going to do that, make sure it's perfectly streamlined and you can't improve it. It's much better when more gameplay elements are woven together, to avoid mundanity
- Creativity, Freshness, come on, it's no fun playing the same map you've played hundreds of times before under a new disguise. try and innovate, or at least keep it fresh and blend styles together. Puzzle maps are great.
- Intelligence, I don't like playing maps mindlessly. Please make me at least think about which path to choose, how to execute a tricky jump, etc. Don't add hard enemies and think it's fun. Maps where you can choose your own path out of many, where there are several good ones but lots of bad ones so there's still strategy, that is, the freeform maps, are great fun.
- Aesthetics, please keep it neat, clean and stylish. There are endless tileset possibilities, don't stick with the one tile type and overuse pattern and shapes. Keep the style consistent, don't just abruptly change styles. You can make the map flow from one style from to another, just do it carefully and seamlessly

Bad:

- Bad Planning, maps where enemies are just slapped on and not thought about, with no attention to pathing and strategy
- Cluttering, where objects are bunched together and the maps looks ugly because of it.
- Linearity, make there be different paths, unless it's a puzzle where the whole fun is figuring out the one possible path to take.
- Be careful with chainguns, their main function is to force you to take refuge behind something or to keep moving as there is no real way to dodge them. In maps where the tileset and objects are designed to take advantage of this, then they're fine but avoid carelessly placing them anywhere.
- Plainess, use different kinds of tiles, enemies and gameplay and vary your maps because no one likes maps with one type of thing overused. Try to make it exciting and colourful. Never find a cool shape and copy it loads of times. It's just boring and ugly.
- Difficulty Spikes, as someone so aptly put it. When the map suddenly gets harder, or when the difficult bit is at the end and the player has to redo the easy parts over again to get to the part they're having difficulty with. 'There and Back' maps are annoying long, try to avoid any levels being excessively lengthy.

I'm just gonna say the overriding themes there is very broadly stated:

Simplicity - how i wish that stated with an "f"

and now I'm just gonna quote something I always give people who ask me how to make maps

Quote
1. The most important rule of mapmaking: playtest, playtest, playtest! Make sure every part of your map is both beatable and fun. Even if it is beatable, it may be just too frustratingly difficult for most people, which is not a good thing.

2. Make sure every single item has a purpose. Right down to the last piece of gold, ensure that everything in your map has a reason for being there. If it doesn't need to be there, take it out. It's okay to use gold and whatnot for aesthetic purposes, just in moderation.

3. Ensure that every part of your map goes with the others. In other words, let each part share the theme. For example, this theme could be an enemy type, an interesting style of tileset, or making use of a specific ability. Use this theme throughout the map. You don't have to do this, but most good maps do. And if you can find a concept to base a map on that hasn't been used before, that's all the better.

4. Develop your own unique style. If you look at one of maximo's or LV's maps, you can immediately tell who made it. You can use symmetry, item placement, enemy types, and much more to aid you.
