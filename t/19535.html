#17336 1 2007-09-27T03:23:43Z Kablizzy
Hey all, and welcome to the Metanet Orchestra. Use this thread to discuss any errant concerns, pose questions, and so on.

Objectives -

First and foremost - We'll be doing music for a number of different projects, the first of which being for the Legacy-produced Knytt Stories level. As such, the vast majority of what we're going to be doing for this is going to be strictly instrumental. Almost all of the music will be ambient in nature, and it should mostly fill the role of background music - With the exception of intro/cutscene/ending credits, most of the music will be filling the role of classical pieces, but we'll also be doing a lot of jazz, some electronica, a few rock pieces, and we're going to try and make each of the pieces unique and innovative - That's what we're looking for here. Innovation.

Secondly, we'll be doing some individual pieces so you all can get a good idea of where we want the project to go. I would like for you all (Those of you who can, that is) to download Knytt Stories, if for no other reason than to hear the music and understand where we want things to go. For thsoe of you who can't download Knytt Stories, I can put all of the music and ambience together (They're .ogg files, so they may need conversion) in a .zip file and host it for you guys to hear.

Anyway, for this stage, each of you will be doing at least one individual song to specification. Classical Musicians might be seeing a lot more action during this stage, since the individual pieces will likely be piano- or classical types, and most will be pretty easy to pull off.

Next, we're going to be dividing ourselves into three groups - Composers, Musicians, and Editors. The composers will be overseeing the individual projects, directing what direction each song takes. The Musicians will be working closely with the Composers to create these pieces, and once each part is finished, the editors will piece everything together, add theme and ambience, and make everything a finished product. These tracks will likely be more complex, but will still focus upon background music, but these can be a lot more abstract and will probably end up being our theme music for the level.

So, third, we'll probably be putting all of these songs into a compilation Metanet album, so these songs that we're going to create will not only have to work for Knytt stories, but they've got to be relatively standalone. For those of you who were around for "It's Dave!", this will be pretty similar in form.


Tools -

Knytt Stories - Knytt Stories is perhaps the main thing you want to be familar with, at least in some form. If you have the ability to play .ogg files, then simply go into the Knytt Stories download directory (If you put it where I put mine, it'll just be C:\Program Files\Knytt Stories), and search through the data files, under "Ambiance" and "Music". The easier wa to go about this is to open up the level editor, and cycle through the music (To do this, when you open the level editor, you can either load an existing level, or create a new one. On the left, you should see "Music," "Atmos A," and "Atmos B." Left-clicking on the numbers beside these cycles through the music and ambience tracks. Holding down the music/ambience buttons will play the track, or setting the number of music/ambience that you want to play and holding down the "play all" button will play the selected music track and selected ambience all at once - To let you hear how things mesh together.

Now, if you have a Mac, sorry - Knytt Stories is PC-only. But as I said above, I can compile the .ogg files from Knytt Stories, .zip 'em up, and host them somewhere.

OverClocked ReMix - Fantastic place to find inspiration, and to get a feel for what I want out of video game music. Are there any games from your youth whose songs you can recall, even now? That's the type of stuff I want. I can still remember almost the entire Chrono Trigger Soundtrack, and Final Fantasy 6 sticks with me to this day. This has happened with a lot of games over the years, and I want to create a similar theme. The tracks on OCR are available in a number of ways - You can get them through the site individually, you can download the torrents (Which are HUUUUUUUUUGE, but well well worth it), or you can head over to Last FM and download the songs individually from there. You can also browse their site for a specific game's music, and see what the artists have done there.

SoundSnap - SoundSnap is one of those places where people congregate and upload a bunch of different sounds for musical purposes. Now, you may be asking, "Why don't you just use some of the 30,000+ files there?" Well, I want original stuff from you guys, and I want it to be a lot more unique than the common stuff that SoundSnap has. So, I warn you in advance - use this sparingly, and only if you absolutely have to.

FindSounds - FindSounds is an internet search engine specifically tuned to finding sound clips on the Internet. It's very useful for the types of things that we'll be doing. Beyond that, FindSound Palettes does the same thing, except it allows you to edit music and categorize it - But I believe that Palettes costs cash, so use this at your own risk. Again, I'd like for us to come up with our own unique stuff instead, but we can always augment it with stuff from here.

Of course, there are other sites, like the Freesound Project, and ccMixter, and there are some great audio editing programs out there (Of which I only really know of Garageband and Audacity, I'll let you guys post the ones you use), although I've heard that some of these programs deal in copyrighted material (Namely, Garageband - If this is false, quell my fears), so let's be careful with what we use, alrighty?

Now, let's have a bitchload of fun, and welcome!

I checked out the Apple website and there's an FAQ page for Garageband that addresses this:
Quote
Using royalty-free loops in GarageBand with commercial work
---
Learn about the usage of royalty-free loops in GarageBand on commercial songs, multimedia presentations, videos, and animations.

The GarageBand software license agreement says:

"GarageBand Software. You may use the Apple and third party audio loop content (Audio Content), contained in or otherwise included with the Apple Software, on a royalty-free basis, to create your own original music compositions or audio projects. You may broadcast and/or distribute your own music compositions or audio projects that were created using the Audio Content, however, individual audio loops may not be commercially or otherwise distributed on a standalone basis, nor may they be repackaged in whole or in part as audio samples, sound effects or music beds."

So don't worry, you can make commercial music with GarageBand, you just can't distribute the loops as loops.
