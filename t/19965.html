#17754 1 2007-11-01T03:48:08Z Atilla
Just thought of this today. It's a blend of those thumbs up/down systems (Is that Dave I hear cheering?) and the favourites system which was tried a while ago. I'm just throwin' it out there to see what people think. I'm not sure whether I'd support it at this stage - I need some more time to think of potential problems - but the concept seems to have some merit.

Rating system:

There would be three ratings, "Good", "Average", and "Poor". Users would also be able to favourite maps just like they can now.

Now, ratings would be calculated as follows:

1. The map must have at least 5 votes.
2. We calculate (Average + Good) - Poor. If this number is less than 0, the map is deemed "Poor".
3. We calculate Good - (Average + Poor). If this number is less than 0, the map is "Average". Otherwise, the map gets a "good" rating.

Obviously, a lot of maps will be "Good" under this system. Will there be a way to sort them? Indeed there shall. We shall use the ratio of favourites to "Good" votes. If lots of people think a map is "Good", but nobody wants to favourite it, then it's a fair bet to say that it's enjoyable but not outstanding. If one or two people particularly like it and decide to favourite it, it probably has a little more merit. If just about everyone who likes it at all favourites, they're calling it "Good" because they all think it's brilliant. We can use a similar system for the remaining levels.

The top-rated would then be sorted as follows:
1. "Good" maps by ratio of favourites to Good votes.
2. "Average" maps by ratio of Good votes to (Average + Poor) votes.
3. "Average" maps which are equal under step 2 sorted by ratio of Average to Poor votes.
4. "Poor" maps by ratio of (Good + Average) votes to Poor votes.

Advantages of this system:
1. Every vote still plays a part.
2. You can't make a map's position swing wildly with a single bad vote. It's only possible to push a map into a lower category if it's already borderline. Furthermore, the lack of extreme voting options makes it more difficult to make ratings change drastically even within a category.
3. There is some mechanism from sorting good maps from great maps.
4. Although the way maps are sorted is a bit complicated, the ratings themselves are pretty intuitive.
5. The limited number of ratings (3 + favourites, rather than 11) would make it easy to just display the actual vote tally, if people wanted to see it.

Example:

Map X has 3G and 2A votes, and has been favourited by one person. This map has a "Good" rating and an F/G ratio of 1/3 (0.333). Map Y, which has been around longer, has 6F, 19G, 10A, and 5P. This map has a "Good" rating and an F/G ratio of 6/19 (0.316). Map X will be higher on the top-rated.

Now, say that someone gives Map X a "Poor" vote. How will this effect things? Not at all. Map X still has the same F/G ratio, and it still qualifies for a "Good" rating, so if that was a sniping attempt it was completely ineffective.

But wait! What if the "Poor" vote is geniune? Shouldn't every vote count? Well, in a way it does. You see, that vote made Map X borderline. The number of Good votes and (Average + Poor) votes is now exactly even. Another Average or Poor vote will tip it into the "Average" class. More good votes will give it a bit of insulation. This prevents a lone idiot messing things up - a map can't pushed into a lower rating unless the majority is on your side. Nor can it be promoted into a higher rating against the majority.

So, Map X now has 1F, 3G, 2A and 1P. Someone thinks it's good but not amazing and gives it a "Good" vote. This causes the F/G ratio to drop to 0.25 and Map Y is now more highly rated. Eh? Giving it a good rating makes it move down? It seems odd at first, but if you think about it it makes sense. What's with the one person who favourited it? Clearly, not many people agree with them - just as not many people agreed with the one person who gave the map a "Poor" rating. Perhaps the mysterious favouriter just likes this kind of map far more than most people, or maybe they're friends with the author, or perhaps they were just in a good mood that day. For whatever reason, if lots of people are giving the map "Good" but not favouriting it, it means they think the map is good but not great. It's rather like giving a map a 4 when it has an average of 4.5 - it's still a good rating, just not as high as it was.

But what if people come along and favourite the map, vindicating the first guy to do so? That works too. Say two people now give the map a "Good" vote and favourite it. This gives us 3F, 5G, 2A, 1P. The map is now up to a .6 F/G ratio. The rating has improved as more people favourite it, just as you would expect.

Potential problems:

1. It's possible to get wacky categorisation in some circumstances. For example, a map with 3 good votes and 3 poor votes would be considered "Good". However, that's not likely to be a huge problem, since that sort of thing is unlikely to occur unless there's a war between snipers and multi-accounters. Not to mention that a single Average vote will fix that problem. I think you'll get anomalies like that in any rating system; the problem with the current one is that they occur in a way which gives the dishonest minority too much influence. At least with this system, they can be eliminated by a couple of normal votes, without that equally-dishonest anti-sniping garbage.

Another anomaly is that favouriting a map without giving it a Good rating can boost its standing more than it should, so long as the map is in the Good category. However, this can only happen a limited number of times before it falls into the Average category. It could also be addressed by only allowing users to favourite a map they have stated is "Good".

2. You can still cause ratings within a category to wobble a bit. Again, I think this is inevitable to some extent, and that it would be less under this system than the current system.

3. This system is a bit more complicated than the current one. Fortunately, the voting portion is pretty straightfoward, so it shouldn't confuse people unless they want to know just how the maps are sorted.





Thoughts? Problems? Issues?
