#350370 4 2007-08-02T21:04:02Z southpaw
b_t is saying that the story shows there is no ultimate truth.

What I took away from the story was much simpler in that we are all ignorant in one way or another. We believe things that may not be real, yet we still think they are. Then to be shown what is real and what isn't we have to be forced in some way to see the truth, or the "light of the sun." This can be blinding at first and we don't want to believe what is shown to us at first because we are so accustomed to our own beliefs. But as our eyes become adjusted to the light of the sun so does our knowledge.

Socrates does also say in this story that there are two ways your eyes may be blinded at first: coming from the dark into the light, and also coming from the light into the dark. People can make you believe things that are not true (take Hitler for example). The overall message is agnosticism, but underneath this message there are more profound ones I believe.


Such as this one: What is Socrates saying in the ways of teachers and educators? Does one truly have to be forced into the "light?"
