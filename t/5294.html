That's what I thought was a good explanation too - and there are lots of recent theories about it. But now I'm not so sure...

Let me deviate a little, into the strange realm of special relativity. One of the eqations of special relativity states that time slows down for someone who is moving relative to the observer. This has been proven - atomic clocks have been set to the same time, then one has been flown around the world in a jet. When reunited, the one that went around the world was a few nanoseconds behind the one that was stationary.
Now this confused a lot of physicists, because of the 'Twin Paradox'. Say we have a pair of twins, imaginatively called A and B. They are both in empty space, with A's velocity relative to B as 0.9 times the speed of light. A then reverses direction, and returns to B at the same speed. When the twins meet again, the physicists saw a problem. From Bs point of view, A went away and returned and so should have aged less, because A was moving very fast. However, from As point of view it is B that has gone away and come back, so B should have aged less. Obviously they cannot both be younger that the other one. So there is a bit of a paradox, which spelt bad news for the theory.
This paradox was solved by the fact that one of the twins has to experience acceleration to change direction, so special relativity can no longer apply to that twins point of view - it is the one who will have aged less.

Anyway, back to the main problem. If the universe is circular (you can go in a straight line and return to your start point) then twin A could leave and return to twin B without either twin experiencing acceleration, so the paradox re-appears. A tricky problem.

Any comments on that little bit of randomness?

#111854 36 2005-11-02T01:59:43Z Atilla
The "halves" paradox is created by faulty logic. Your position is space is continuous, not discrete. Think of it like a graph in the cartesian plane. There is a single, precise point in time where you are exactly at the end of the street, like the x-intercept of a graph. At any other time, you are to one side or the other. By choosing to only walk half the distance, you are deliberately choosing not to cross the intercept, but to approach it from one side in progressively smaller intervals. There is no reason why you cannot reach it. The length is finite. You are just electing to travel smaller and smaller distances so you will not reach it. It's like saying "Hey, it must be impossible to walk a metre, because if I don't move I never reach a distance of one metre from my original position! Even if I keep failing to move forever! Wow!" or believing that anything you can't (or choose not to) see doesn't exist.

Although she is slower than Achilles, Achilles is entirely unable to catch up with her, because she continues to have moved each time he catches up.
