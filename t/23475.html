FEDERAL PRISONS MAKING MONEY

COMPETING WITH OFF-SHORE FIRMS


In the early years of the 21st century, "going off-shore" was a way for many big corporations to increase their profits by 20-to-40 percent by ditching their higher-paid U.S. employees for low- wage workers in India and Malaysia, and millions of Americans lost their jobs.

Now, however, many of those corporations, especially in the fields of telecommunications and banking, are bringing those jobs back to the United States, lured home by even lower wages for a dependable, enthusiastic labor force with wages ranging from 23 cents to $1.15 an hour in Federal prisons some state prison wages start at 12 cents an hour.

Indeed, those low wages and dependable workforce are the key selling points UNICOR i.e. Federal Prisons Industry, Inc. makes to its prospective customers on its web site and in its 2007 annual report to the U.S. Congress.

UNICOR, which saw a $140 million increase in net sales and a $40 million increase in its profit in 2007 over 2006, and is poised to grow even more with the projected increase in Federal prisoners in coming years, boasted in its report to Congress that in November 2003 it had only 23 inmates working in "directory assistance," whereas now it has 1600 inmates working in five call centers and had just serviced its 100 millionth phone call.

Prisoners handle everything from directory assistance for major telephone companies, to credit card transactions, airline reservations, etc.

In the "services" section of UNICOR's web site, the for-profit government agency informs prospective clients:

"After years of providing quality goods and services to federal agencies, UNICOR now has the authority to partner with private sector firms who are sending the work off shore, or in lieu of sending the work off shore. Companies expanding into new areas of business can also take advantage of UNICOR's unique outsourcing alternative. With more and more contact center work being outsourced, UNICOR can provide contact center support at a highly competitive rate, and do it right here in the USA.

"Imagine... All the benefits of domestic outsourcing at off shore prices. It's the best kept secret in outsourcing!

"Now, private companies and government agencies can outsource their contact center work to UNICOR. In a nutshell: You provide the software, telcom, and training, and we'll provide the facility and labor. We have demonstrated our capability with real world results:

"Our DA [directory assistance] contact centers employ over 1,500 workers, 7 days a week, handling all manner of Directory Assistance calls. With an AHT of 35 seconds, an Abandon Rate of under 2%, and an ASA of under 5 seconds, our workers are comparable with any in the private sector.

"Our Outbound B2B contact center has performed data scrubbing, lead generation, and profiling for some of the top companies in America.

"When you outsource to UNICOR / Federal Prison Industries, the benefits are very clear. We offer:

"Skilled operators

"By taking advantage of UNICOR's Contact Center capability, you can let us address your simple, high volume, first-tier customer support functions, while your employees focus on the more complex, high-value operations....

"Whether you need outbound B2B or inbound contact center support, UNICOR believes that we can be the ideal partner. We have the experience, we have the quality control, we have the cost-effective labor pool, and we have the facilities nationwide to offer a highly competitive alternative to off-shore outsourcing.

"Let's face it, outsourcing off shore can be a hassle. There are language barriers, varying monetary exchange rates, time differences, and simply visiting your contact center can involve a transoceanic flight. When you outsource with UNICOR, your contact center is located in the United States, and those issues disappear. Your company will enjoy all the benefits of a domestic operation, with the cost savings of going off shore. When you partner with UNICOR, your company is providing valuable job skills to federal inmates while it repatriates jobs back from overseas. It's truly a 'win-win' situation for all involved. As one satisfied customer, an unnamed CEO highlighted in UNICOR's 2007 report to Congress, testified:

"Absenteeism is the bane of the contact center world. UNICOR has effectively eliminated this issue from the equation. The agent pool is more mature, better educated, and harder working than what one would find in the U.S. domestic market."

A 'NEW DEAL' PROGRAM

When President Franklin Roosevelt signed the legislation establishing the Federal Prisons Industry (FPI) on June 23, 1934, to utilize Federal prisoners for agricultural, roadside and military labor, FPI was prohibited from selling its products to the general public or private businesses, for the obvious reason it would undercut private sector companies.

The 1935 Ashurst-Sumners Act (18 U.S.C. 1761(a)) and the 1936 Walsh-Healey Act (41U.S.C. 35) controlled the sales and shipments of prison goods, prohibited the sale of prison-made products across state lines and outside the country, and limited the value of the products manufactured.

In 1979 those laws were changed with the Justice System Improvement Act of 1979 (Public Law 96-157, Sec. 827) and more or less repealed by The Crime Control Act of 1990 (Public Law 101-647 and Title 18 U.S.C. 1761)), which authorized the Prison Industries Enhancement Certification Program (P.I.E.C.P. or PIE Program), which gave the go-ahead for prison industries to sell their products to individual companies, corporations and the general public, when previously their sales were restricted to state and federal agencies.

Today, UNICOR, or Federal Prisons Industry, Inc., is in the manufacturing, printing and publishing, textile, electronics and recycling businesses. It operates state-of-the-art printing, binding and mailing plants in three locations across the country Hopwell, Va., Sandstone, Mn., and Leavenworth, Ks., and its clients are not only federal agencies, but also high-profit defense contractors.

According to UNICOR's web site:

"The Services Business Group currently operates three state-of-the-art printing operations located throughout the United States. Our primary goal at each location is to provide high quality process color printing and complex bindery services. Our finished products are top quality and meet all customer specifications. We have years of experience in meeting and exceeding our customer orders.

"Our strength lies in printing the kinds of jobs most government agencies require. Our versatility and flexibility allow us to handle most print requests, whether it's for a million envelopes or a small run of administrative manuals, and just about everything in between!

"When you work with UNICOR, you're not sending your job to an unknown entity. "Our Printing Departments specialize in high-quality commercial printing. With a choice of multiple presses using web and offset printing, every job, from a 3-1/2" x 5-1/2" label to a 40" target, or from a 4-1/4" x 5-1/2" notepad to a 200 page book, can be completed with the highest print quality through this highly efficient and cost effective process....

PATRIOT MISSILES

PRISON MADE

Prisoners in UNICOR's electronics division even make components for the Patriot missile, which is manufactured by Raytheon, the Defense Department's fourth largest supplier of military goods.

According to UNICOR's web site:

"The Patriot missile is the world's most advanced ground-to-air defense system, and is designed to defeat advanced targets, including aircraft, tactical ballistic missiles and cruise missiles. UNICOR/FPI has successfully implemented surged production to supply electronics and electrical components for Patriot Advanced Capability (PAC-3) missile propulsion, guidance and targeting systems.

"We also are an important supplier of wire harnesses and cable assemblies for virtually all ordnance. This equipment is produced to rigorous military standards to perform flawlessly despite being subjected to long storage, vibration, shock, and broad swings in temperature, humidity, altitude and pressure.

"Many of our electrical and electronic components are used in guided missile seekers, aptly named for their ability to seek and destroy hostile aircraft or incoming missiles. In addition, we manufacture and distribute electronics for missile launchers, propulsion systems, antenna mast groups, guidance processors and warhead detonation....

"UNICOR/FPI supplies numerous electronic components and services for guided missiles, including the Patriot Advanced Capability PAC-3) missile. We assemble and distribute the Intermediate Frequency Processor (IFP) for the PAC-3s seeker. The IFP receives and filters radio-frequency signals that guide the missile toward its target.

"The Patriot Advanced Control (PAC-3) missile is launched from canisters, many of which have UNICOR/FPI-manufactured cable assemblies linking the ignition and control systems...."

UNICOR's textile and furniture divisions make everything from beds and mattresses and for hospitals, university dorms and military installations, office furniture (desks, chairs, file cabinets, shelves), helmets, uniforms and vests for the both the American and Iraqi armies, bed linens and curtains, institutional uniforms, boots, etc. Its industrial division manufactured 1.7 million HVAC filters purchased by hospitals, airports, office buildings, military installations and auditoriums and its "Fleet Management" division customized 4,300 law enforcement vehicles, such as those for K-9 Corps, detainee transport buses and vans, and rugged vehicles used in border patrol, etc.

According to UNICOR's official history (see:http://www.unicor.gov/about/organization/history/)

"To compete effectively in a shrinking Government marketplace, UNICOR regularly shows its products and services in trade shows around the Nation.

"UNICOR conducted market research; increased its participation in trade shows around the country; opened marketing centers, field office display areas, and a product showroom at BOP headquarters; and set up product distribution centers to respond more quickly to customer needs. Stressing 'total customer satisfaction' as a goal, UNICOR established a customer service program, expanded its quality enhancement program, and provided extensive training on quality improvement to hundreds of line staff and managers. Although its' marketing activities were modest in comparison to those of private industries, UNICOR felt they were essential for retaining its customer base in a time of increasing competition. Fortunately, these customer satisfaction initiatives and limited marketing campaigns were effective in generating enough sales to support adequate work opportunities for Federal inmates....

"In short, UNICOR endeavored to follow the modern marketing and management principles in the 1980's and 1990's that had proven successful for private sector enterprises.... Because of these measures, UNICOR was able to increase production annually by 4 percent between l987 and l990, even though Federal procurement in UNICOR's product lines decreased by 40 percent during the same period...."

One of UNICOR's goals, according to the 2007 Report to Congress is to increase the number of Federal prisoners engaged in manufacturing or services from 18 percent to 25 percent of the total inmate population; the current number of prison-laborers is 23,152, who work in 110 factories in 79 locations. The number of Federal prisoners involved in prison factories, however, is just a small percentage of those working in state prisons a number which is growing as the prison system is privatized and for-profit businesses continue to invest in this country's prison-industrial complex, which now houses some 2.3 million people at a cost of nearly $70 billion a year. And while the taxpayer is billed for the cost of constructing the prisons and paying the guards, the for profit-corporations who build the prisons and the manufacturing plants inside reap the profits.

The question of blowback arises. Just as it is common knowledge in New York, Los Angeles and other cities that criminal gangs are often run from prison, what will happen when those inside America's prisons start to share our credit card information and Raytheon's missile knowledge with their friends on the outside?
