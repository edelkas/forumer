#45871 5 2005-01-13T00:10:47Z Daddaluma
I would highly suggest a revision of the scoring categories.

Personally, since N is completely geared toward speed, the time category should be worth more than the other categories. I would say just double the amount of points each place receives (i.e. 10 for first, 6 for second and 2 for third).

Style and Entertainment should be second most important. Perhaps 1.5 times it's current amount of points.

Creativity should be fine the way it is.

Completion is a ridiculous category, and shouldn't be getting equal weight with the other categories. First of all, it's definition needs to be reqorked and very clearly stated so we don't have judges (maniac) voting for horribly incomplete maps. Also, this category is mostly useless unless the map creator specifically creates things to do that don't need to be done ot finish the map. Usually, going for all the gold will increase your score. Usually. There are of course times where it won't, and then this category has some merit, but not for every map. There also needs to be some set criteria for how to officially rank maps that are "fully" complete. However, any way I can think of is already covered by other categories (you could rank them by speed, but that is covered by time. You could rank them by how much you liked them, but that will usually be the covered by style and entertainment.)

So I suggest eliminating completion for maps that it serves no purpose on, and then on maps where it does serve a purpose make the points 3,2,1 for first, second and third. Or if the map is really difficult to fully "complete" then you can up the points in this category for that week. A set unwavering point total for this category just won't work because it's so map specific.



Also, I would suggest allowing people to change the 2 demos they submit right up until the last second of the contest. Each person can still have 2 entries, but they can improve upon their two entries as much as they want. This will remove the punishment for posting early in the week, and allow more of a competition (like on the high score boards) to take place.
