Well, it's kinda hard to answer, because I'm lazy, but here goes.

I'd suppose they used AS 1.0 objects simply because they were in a hurry. But I'd have to leave the details to Mare and Raigan themselves .
About the other stuff...
While the ninja is alive, it's simply a circle (collision-wise). Of course, they still keep track of where the body-part positions are. Each limb consists of three particles, and IK is used to connect em properly. When the ninja dies, they replace the "circle" movie clip with the particles, which make the actual ragdoll that you see. If the death is "exploding" then they disconnect the particles, and make new ones where needed (like at the elbow or knee). Clear enough?
You also might want to check out Jakobsen's article on verlet physics, if you haven't already. That could help you quite a bit if you're interested in ragdolls.
And about your little project: well, it's pretty good for a ten-day-old project . Keep it up

#202150 5 2006-07-13T14:15:50Z Raigan
hi,
yeah, we actually have two sets of graphics: the logic works as TS explained, with the "alive" graphic being the whole ninja, and the "dead" graphic being individual limbs. Embedded in the alive graphic are invisible movieclips which tell us the position of the limbs, so that when death occurs we can setup the limbs in the identical pose.

about classes: when we wrote N actionscript 2.0 had just come out, and we had been using 1.0 since flash5, so we stuck with what we knew. there is a small performance penalty for using getter/setter functions, which can be overcome if you just make the variables public and access them directly.. not a great OOP approach though

you can use Movieclip.localToGlobal() to get the worldspace transform.

good luck!

raigan
