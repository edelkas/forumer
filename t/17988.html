(Assume that you are guaranteed to receive the money and that you will be unable to retrieve your eye.)

So I was mindlessly surfing earlier today when I came across this questionnaire that was supposed to determine whether or not I would go to Heaven or Hell. It said some pretty silly things like, "the Bible says that all of mankind has broken the first commandment, and by claiming that you didn't, you're also lying" (even though you could truthfully believe that you hadn't broken the first commandment).
But anyway, it ended the questionnaire with questions like "based on this, do you think you're Innocent or Guilty?" The "Innocent" led to a page that said "INCORRECT," and the "Guilty" was the right answer. The next question was "so are you going to Heaven or Hell," and of course the only right answer was "Hell." The final question, which prompted this thread, was "are you concerned that you're going to Hell?"
I of course answered "no," and I got this page: [link]

All along, I was reading the explanations it gave for each question and was answering honestly. I found that most of their real-world parallels involving judges and Jesus paying my bail kind of silly, but I was still answering many of their simple questions to myself honestly.
Now read the first sentence in the link I just gave - "would you sell one of your eyes for a million dollars?"
My answer was "probably, depending upon how it's removed."
Then it said I wasn't in my right mind. ;_;

So now I extend the question to you (and this is completely unrelated to Christianity):
Would you sell one of your eyes for one million dollars?

Bonus question:
Would you sell both of your eyes for ten million dollars?

Please do not discuss religion in this thread, unless you worship a beholder or something.

---

My answer:

Hell yeah, I'd sell one of my eyes for $1 million. So long as it's properly, surgically removed (in an American hospital by American doctors) and I can receive full hospital care until I recover, I wouldn't even care which eye they took.

The way I see it, the cons are:
No depth perception: This is the biggest problem, obviously, but much of what I want to do in my life doesn't require depth perception. I really don't need to see how far away my computer screen is if I can still read the code I'm writing. It might be a little frustrating if I'm trying to assemble something, but I don't think it would be frustrating enough to matter. Precise depth perception really doesn't matter in the majority of tasks you will perform in your lifetime.
Otherwise, failing to throw things accurately won't really affect me because I already suck at throwing things accurately, and using a firearm won't really be affected negatively because of its point-and-click interface (not to mention my second eye won't skew my aim - I'll be forced to use the sights without complications).
No driving: Ouch. That actually would suck. Then again, $1 million gives you a chauffeur, right? Otherwise, I usually walk or take the subway anyway. And if I continue to live in Boston or any other populous metropolis, I'll probably continue to do so.
Social suckage: I really don't think this is enough of a drawback to matter. Sure, every now and then my glass eye may wander or my eyepatch will fail to impress, but that's trivial for me.
Blindness on the appropriate side: That would also kind of suck, but it'd still be natural to turn your head farther toward the blind side when, for example, looking both ways before crossing the street. Things will be able to sneak up on me easier on that side, too, but that really doesn't happen often in life.

The pros, however:
A legitimate excuse to wear an eyepatch: Hell yes.
A glass eye: ...can be thrown at people and gives me a few more party tricks, as well as distinguishing me from other people.
$1,000,000: This in and of itself greatly outweighs the cons. Having this money will, among lots of other things, allow me to quit my day job and live without having to work, which would not only ease the whole "living at an inconvenience" thing, but exceed it in its emotional benefit.

But about those cons:
Bionic eye: My generation will live to see it (punny!), and my $1 mil will let me buy it. I can totally survive with an eyepatch and/or glass eye until something actually functional is developed, and by then my panel of personal accountants will have turned my $1 million into a far larger fortune.

End result: After a substantial amount of time being at a minor inconvenience, I'll have a fortune and will be able to see better than you can.


And as for the bonus question - would I sell both of my eyes for ten million dollars?
HELL NO.
Having one eye is a slight inconvenience, but being blind would suck. $1 million is good enough of a launchpad to get a larger fortune; the $9 million difference wouldn't matter in the long run.
