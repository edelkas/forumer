#13663 1 2007-01-23T05:36:50Z maximo
Okay, first off I don't in anyway claim these as unique ideas (I'm not hear enough to know who else said what) but I still pop around enough to have some ideas on the matter.

This proposal is a rather large scale renovation of sorts, affecting the top authors, ratings, newest page, etc. so whether or not it's practical for it to be implemented I wont argue and don't honestly care, this is just ideas.

First off ratings: anyone with half a brain knows not to care about ratings because they mean so little about actual map quality, so my question is simply why keep them? The system I think would work (better/more efficiently/what have you) is a simple thumbs up. If after playing a map you enjoyed it, give it a "Yay" (as I'll refer to it from now on), if not, do nothing (more on "do nothing" later). the amount of Yay's a map has correlates directly to the number of people who have enjoyed the map enough to recommend it to other players, or at least the people who think the map is worth looking at.

Now in addition to that there should be in my opinion a thumbs down (or "Nay") option. This isn't to say you didn't like the map, but that it was horrendous and not worth playing, this isn't a button for "i didn't like this map" it's for "this map is horrible/noobish/spam/etc." more on how I feel this should work in the next section. For maps you don't care for, or just didn't really enjoy, you do nothing.

Top rated pages next: Obviously this screws the hell out of the current system based of highest scores and percentages. I think the new table should work as follows though. The top rated page is broken into 3 categories: past week, past month, and all time (with the possibility of past 24 hours if deemed appropriate). The maps with the most Yay's for the given time block are listed in descending order. This in my oppinion gives the advantage of a frequently changing top authors page, giving new authors a chance to be seen, and letting numa cycle and be more fluid than it is now (Mount Doom is still up there). Although Mount Doom is probably worthy of the spot, when you are surfing for new maps seeing a 2 year old map isn't want you want to see quickly. This allows quicker scanning of numa and a higher chance new authors will be recognized (or at least in theory).

Now I also dwelt on the idea of having every 5 nay's on a map minus one off it's score, in the off chance a map becomes boosted unfairly or something of that sort, and sort of a way to check the system but i'm not sure how it would function. Making sure it was 5 and not one eliminates the problem of snipers because if they wanted to hurt a top rated map they would have to multi account to no end, and that would easily be picked up by our new wonderful mods (meant in all seriousness). I wasn't sure about this idea because the top rated page would refresh so fast it wouldn't really affect anything but the all time list. It's up for discussion for sure.

Newest page: A small addition to this page if this system is enacted should be if a map has 5-10 (not sure what would work) more Nay's than it does Yay's it should be omitted from normal viewing. There should remain an option at the top of the page to "show all maps" which un-hides these maps, but the default view should leave them out. This is a great way to limit the amount of spam, or fluff maps that pollute the newest pages these days. I think a simple solution to the otherwise dreadful task of milling through the newest pages for something worth while.

Author of the Week or Year: This is simple and I've heard the idea bounced around favorable before, but the author was chosen by hand, which I think after about 3 weeks is tedious. This system could do it automatically by placing a link above "top authors" with the name of the user with the most yay's per map's submitted on their maps for the time period given (in most cases a week). The math is simple and if I baffled up my explanation I'll try again: For each author divide the number of yay's on maps they submitted that week by the number of maps they submitted that week (giving you a percentage of yay's/maps essentially). The top author's name is then displayed on the numa navigation bar linking to that authors maps. This is another feature I feel would help direct new users to map makers to emulate, allow new mappers to emerge and be known to the older members, and just generally add something fresh to numa each week with a little competition involved (competition always increases quality).

For author of the Year, at the beginning of each year a .txt of that users maps over the past 12 months is compiled is userlevel.txt format and downloadable. Just a fun way to reflect over the past year I think. Just me dreaming mostly, but I think it would be interesting in the long haul. This could be shortened to 6 months or maybe even 3 and make it a quarterly honor (author of the season maybe) so it cycles more often.

An author of all time can be calculated to replace to current top authors page, but I don't think that would be necessary with a system that rotates every week.

Map packs: Another addition I would like to see is a place to submit full .txt files of map packs that are downloadable in their entirety (not map per map) on numa. The scoring of these could be handled identically to individual maps with a simple "yay" i liked this pack. Pack of the (insert time frame) could be promoted as well. This idea is obvious and doesn't need much more explaining.

The main bar I see in changing the system is old maps wont get rated again, and I strongly disagree. Mount doom came out of the grave to move back to the top spot, going from 90 votes to 120 in the last 3 months. Old maps that are worth voting on still get played, and the old maps worth being in the "all time most yay's" list would find there way up there again. If they don't then they weren't worth remembering in my opinion and don't deserve the spot (go back 10 pages on numa's top maps now and see how much of them you actually remember, not many I suspect)

So now I'm done and I'm leaving if up for discussion. I don't know or expect any of this to happen but I think they would be nice improvements to what was, and could be again, a great system.
