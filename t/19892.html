The Basics
Quote: Wikipedia; with some edits
Most of the game is set in outer space along a vast chain of miniature planetoids and other space matter, though many levels consist of huge areas to explore.

Mario is capable of jumping or launching from planet to planet in order to gather items and defeat enemies. Gravity plays a significant role in the game, as each planet has a gravitational force that prevents Mario from drifting off into space, which allows the player to effortlessly circumnavigate rounded objects. The varying degrees of gravity also have an effect on Mario's ability to jump, and can be used to pull Mario toward certain planetoids while in mid-flight. Like previous 3D Mario games, the main objective is to collect Power Stars, which are awarded by completing tasks or defeating enemies. The game's central hub is set in the Comet Observatory, which is divided into six themed areas. Distributed among these six worlds are 40 "galaxies", or levels with 1 to 6 stars each. Obtaining these stars allows the player to unlock more in each themed galaxy.

Mario's health consists of a power meter made of three sections, which can be restored by collecting coins. The bottomless pits featured in previous Mario games have been replaced by black holes, which are stationed beneath the surfaces of certain planets and spread throughout outside space. As always, Mario has a range of jumping abilities, such as his trademark triple jump, wall kick, ground pound, and side somersault. Some of the moves that were absent in Super Mario Sunshine have returned for this installment, such as the long jump. Mario's melee move is a spin and it allows him to attack enemies, break crystals and activate Launch Stars and Sling Stars that shoot him across the galaxy. The Wii Remote also comes into play to collect Star Bits, aim and fire them at enemies, and also to activate Pull Stars that pull Mario between planets.

Camera is great. Not perfect, but great. I had no major issues and very few minor ones. Definitely a big step up from other 3D Marios, and most 3D games in general, for that matter.

The long jump and the spin move are both really useful. Long jump for crossing large gaps and traversing planets more quickly, spin move for stunning enemies and also as an extra little top-off to your jumps in case you misjudge one.

The pill-shaped planet with gravity fields in the Good Egg Galaxy had some fantastic level design. I spent a good five minutes just playing around inside it.

The first planet in the Space Junk Galaxy is really fun to jump on with the long jump. If you do it right you can go around the whole thing, and then some.

The disk-shaped planets in Battle Rock Galaxy were also fun to long jump on; I'd jump off one, swing around and end up on the opposite side of another.

Sweet Sweet Galaxy (the one with conveyor belts with shapes cut out of them) was fantastic. Just fast-paced, intense platforming. Loved it so much that when I beat it I immediately went back and played it again.

Loopdeloop Galaxy (the manta ray surfing one) was awesome. It's hard though, and I think the reason for that is because the waves are choppy and occasionally you get thrown up in the air a little bit, which at first tended to make me overcompensate on my turns. It's ridiculously fun, though, I was never genuinely frustrated.

Rolling Green Galaxy (the ball rolling one) was also awesome, and easier than the manta surfing. Some great level design here as well, with lots of different routes available for collecting stuff.

The boss battles I've played so far have for the most part been fairly easy, although still fun. The first Bowser encounter, on the other hand, was genuinely difficult.


That's it for now. As far as closing comments, I think the phrases "It's amazing" and "You should get it" sum up my feelings pretty well.
