I live in the foothills of the Appalachians in a small town in Connecticut. Home to about 8,000 people, it's pretty cozy. But the benefit of living here is the climate; while it gets extremely hot in the summer, we get the full New England effect of the leaves changing colors in the fall, and a cold winter with a lot of snow. There's a lot of hills and forests which are great for mountain biking. The thing I'd rather have, however, is a warm, tropical climate. From my house, I can see Hartford and all of it's buildings, which is pretty cool. The nicest part is that I can see all of the fireworks in the different towns from my room on the Fourth of July. Although we go to the beach sometimes, the water is always freezing.

I'm in the village of Palmetto Bay in South Miami, Florida. I don't have any pictures, but nothing really happens here that's worth taking pictures of. Oh, except after we got a little affected by Hurricane Wilma. Oh, and my parents have pictures of post-Andrew wreckage. Besides that, nothing really happens. Wait, I remember that last year, some teen went around, going into cars. My mom left hers unlocked, and it got all messed up in there. The person even looked through my bookbag. The only bad thing about living here is that the power grid is always changing. After Wilma, we expected to get power back since we live right near the Miami Children's Hospital, but the power grid got changed because of construction, so our part of the street didn't get power back, and everyone with power was rubbing it in our faces by putting up Halloween decorations that were huge.
