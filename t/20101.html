The other day I was thinking about perception, mostly because of the thread started from the question of "If a tree falls in the woods, and nobody is around to hear it, does it still make a sound?"

I don't want anybody to answer this question in this thread, as there is already a thread for that.

On to my point: As I was thinking about reality and watching the sunlight on the floor of my living room I realized something, and that is we are all living in the past.

I thought about this. People have always told me we live in the "present" but as I thought about it more and more there was no possible way that we are perceiving the present. Here's why.


1. Light: Although the rate is a rather small number, our eyes perceive things essentially in frames. For instance, a lightbulb flashes on and off constantly at very rapid speeds. Our eyes are unable to register this flicker, and therefore I assume we see in "frames". Even then, our eyes also have a delayed reaction. For the time it takes light to reach our eyes and the time it takes our brain to process this is a very small amount of time to us, but it shows that things we are viewing have already happened. We are constantly seeing things at a time slightly behind what has actually happened.

2. Touch: The same thing applies here. If you touch a hot pan, there is a slight delay. Even though the time is near instant there is still a delay, so you have essentially touched the pan before your brain registered you have. You have performed an action and your brain is slightly behind in registering it, therefore you have perceived something that has happened in the past.

3. Sound: This is the easiest one to explain, mostly because the farther away you are from a sound the longer it takes for you to hear it, if you hear it at all. The soundwaves have to travel to your eardrum and then your brain has to register this and determine what kind of sound it is. So the sound has already happened while your brain is processing it.

We need not focus on taste and smell because I'd just be repeating myself.

I find this idea quite interesting myself. I just wanted to hear your thoughts. I'd be happy to explain more, but I've just been reflecting and expanding on this idea within the last week.

What it comes down to is this: our bodies and minds are in the present. We truly are always in the present. But we cannot perceive this. It is impossible because of the delay from our brain having to receive a message, determine what it means, then send a reaction. Our perception of what is "the present" is effectively in the past.

Same with a computer. As I type there is a slight delay from the time I hit the key to the time it shows up on the monitor. There is also a slight delay from the time I hit the bottom of the keystroke to the time my brain has made this realization.

What may be more interesting is that our brain has done one of two things. Just so that I can relate to it and explain the idea easier, let's say you are playing piano. Either your brain waits for the message that you have hit a piano key and sends a message to lift the finger but the delay is so quick you don't realize it; or your brain has predetermined the length of the stroke and has sent the message to lift the finger before the message that the key has been hit is received.

I think the former seems more probable, while the latter is more interesting to think about. But I may be wrong.
