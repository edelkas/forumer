An SUV is a vehicle with a specific use. If you carry enough large cargo and need to take it places that require a powerful vehicle often enough, then a SUV is the appropriate vehicle to get. But this is only if you need to do these things often enough that you need to buy an automobile for it.

Do not forget that this is your first car as well. A large vehicle will cost a fortune for insurance and the gas milage is so horrible that driving such a vehicle will ensure that your pockets will be empty so that your gas tank will be full. I speculate that you must not appreciate how much these things cost.

A large vehicle has dangers as well. Everybody knows that having a high center of gravity is a dangerous quality of higher vehicles, but there are other dangers as well. An inexperienced driver will need to see more area around their car to perform parking and other small space maneuvers. An inexperienced driver will be more prone to be involved in a minor fender-bender with a very large car.

And yes, SUVs have a significantly higher output than other vehicles. This is bad for the environment and all of that. Although there are many other causes, increased consumption of gasoline is one of (though a bit minor) the causes of the increased price of fuel.


My suggestion? Get a very practical vehicle. I would suggest nothing other than a mid-size or compact car. Small cars are much cheaper to insure and cut the cost of gasoline significantly. They are much easier and smoother to drive as well. Because your parents are paying for this, they may think very highly of you if you suggest a used car. There is nothing wrong with buying a nice used car. Partially for the reason that there is a chance that you might bump into something or scratch your car being an inexperienced driver; I know that I have caused quite a few small dings and scratches in my car because I have backed into a few things. But it is not as significant of a loss because my car is a little old. I would be very concerned if I had a dent in a brand new car.

I am sure that there is some psychology to this, that a person like a large powerful vehicle because they are not a large, powerful person. I actually only know a few people who actually need to own a truck, but yet a vast majority of people in my community drive only trucks. You may want to think about issues like that as well.

I guess that was a little long, but it took that much to tell you what I think. I hope that helps you realize that SUVs are a vehicle that really should not be so popular because it's use is very specialized, not practical.

#110653 25 2005-10-26T19:04:08Z The_Juggler
I asked Dr. Tetris about the psychology of owning a larger vehicle than everybody else and this is what he said:

Quote
It's a status symbol. Like many things in life, owning it serves no purpose but to show that it can be owned. We assume that possessions should be practical or entertaining to be worth purchasing. However, every time you make a purchase, you buy not only what is physically present, but a series of social and psychological benefits attached to that product or service.

Take a new video game console. Buying the video game console will allow you to play its games and have enjoyment. Moreover, however, buying a new video game console will privelege you to the social gratification of being part of that console's culture. Every product creates an illuminatus of sorts--that is, a group of people who know something the rest of us don't know. People who own golf equipment know a more refined sportsman's lifestyle. People who own an SUV know the feel of glory (even if the feeling is entirely intangible) that owning something larger than everyone else can provide.

Marketing is 10% product and 90% psychology. That's why people buy brand name products. Are Nike shoes better than Reeboks? No. They both look attractive and are quite durable. So why do Nikes sell more? The brand name. It's not about owning shoes--it's about owning Nikes. Why does McDonalds make more money per business than Burger King? Again, the psychology of business attracts individuals to buy certain intangible components of the things they are buying anyway. Marketing has convinced people they need to buy McDonalds, even when other options are available--parhaps subconsciously.

So, why buy an SUV? Because:

It makes a person part of a group of upstanding and middle-upperclass Americans--the type of folks who enjoy baseball, hot dogs, and apple pie. Anyone who sees the person in this vehicle will understand that the person knows what it is to be part of the SUV dream.

People are going to buy a vehicle anyway and the extra price of the SUV is worth the experience of owning an SUV.

The person who owns the most stuff, the most expensive stuff, and the biggest stuff wins life. Buying SUVs helps stake that claim.


So, there you have it: Buy the biggest, hulkiest vehicle that you can afford because it makes you look rich, powerful and better than the rest. Forget everything that I said about owning a modest, practical compact car. Sissies drive compact cars, real men own enormous gas-guzzlers and run over little compact cars!
