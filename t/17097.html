This is to address the "Lord, Liar, Lunatic" argument, concerning Jesus Christ, of Nazareth. Or, rather, why I think it sucks. I just felt like posting about it, because this argument really annoys me.

The argument:

1. Jesus claimed he was the son of god
2. To claim such a thing, one must be insane, lying, or the true lord.
3. Someone who lies about something so fundamental to their existence must be a bad person.
4. Jesus was not a bad person
5. Jesus was not a liar.
6. Someone must be extremely mentally unstable to think themselves the son of god, when they are not.
7. Someone who gives sound advice is not mentally unstable.
8. Jesus gave sound advice.
9. Jesus was not insane.
10. Jesus was not insane or lying.

.: Jesus is the son of the Lord, and/or the Lord himself.

The problems:

1. Actually, this is open to much interpretation. He could've easily meant that we are all "god's children," and so he referred to god as "father." Also, the Bible is not reliable as evidence. The entire premise of this proof is based upon the assumption that the Bible is true. The Bible can't be true cover to cover, because it's full of contradictions. The Bible is not reliable because it has been mistranslated. The Bible is not reliable because there are historical errors. The Bible is not reliable because of regular violations of the laws of physics, with no empirical evidence to support these violations.

2. This is a false trichotomy. There are other options. He might have been misquoted, for example. Also, none of these situations are mutually exclusive. Gods can lie, or be insane. Liars can be insane. All three could be true.

3. This is not always true. 'Good' people can make mistakes. Besides that, good and bad are opinions, more or less.

4. Again, subjective.

5. *Bzzzzzzzt* Wrong. He could have been.

6. True to a degree, yet certain parts of the brain can be almost completely depleted while others remain fully functional.

7. See above.

8. Well, yeah. Most of it.

9. Not necessarily true.

10. This is just a repetition, for the sake of rhetoric and clarity, so I don't think I need to address it twice.

The conclusion is completely inconclusive. This entire argument is logically weak, and I'm tired of fundies *cough* *macaddict* *cough* trying to use it to prove Jesus Christ. Yeah.

That leaves us with the only possibility, as far as I'm concerned. Jesus was the Son of God.
