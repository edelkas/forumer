Ah, I take it you haven't seen my little friend...

...Ahem. All kidding aside, it is indeed terribly elitist, but it would serve many purposes:

First, we would need a set criterion for current voters. I'm thinking (And this is completely arbitrary, mind) that users with above 500 votes on other maps would be a good start. If we wanted to get technical, of those 500 votes, I'm certain that Arachnid could implement a system where that given user's average rating given will have to be more than a 2.5, or something along those lines.

A second criterion (If that didn't work), would be number of rated maps (Say, 25 or 50, perhaps). In this form, we could set a limit for those people who have had their maps rated, and stuck around Numa. This would display not ignorance or apathy for the ratings system, but a knowledge of the ratings system on a level that they don't so much care about the rating a map receives so much as simply making the map. In the same vein, we could grade, in addition, by that user's average map rating. In doing so, a user who has a higher rating would likely be less inclined to rate others' maps down.

If that doesn't seem good enough, we could even collect data and judge by number of maps submitted (Say, 50 or so), and even by time on Numa (Anywhere up to a year would seem like a good criterion to judge upon).

An ever more drastic step would end up simply hand-choosing members who have given out higher average ratings, I'm certain that Arachnid logs that type of thing (And no, I would definitely not make the cut for most of these, I'm a harsh judge on maps, particularly these days, and I barely have peaked some of these criterion).

So, on top of this, we could even go one further step, and implement the above (Tweaked of course) criterion for voters, and *then* look at their average rating, on top of all that. Or, alternately, we could even just take a look at everyone's number of 0 ratings, and oust someone with a percentage of 0 ratings amongst ratings given (Hidden, of course).

From there, each new member that signs up would have to prove their ability to rate maps fairly, and leave good, constructive comments. We can put a limit on their time in the community, on their number of comments left on other maps (And yes, what about bad comments left? But if someone is detrimental enough through 250 comments left that they wouldn't warrant the ability to vote, then they would easily be removed prior to the time when they would join the community.

This is a really easy, simple solution to the problem that is Numa. No, it's not a cure-all, but it is far better than anything that we can do short of abolishing it altogether.
