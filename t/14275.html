#292165 11 2007-01-31T15:36:54Z Groxx
Most laptops will intentionally under-clock (ie, slow down) their processor as things heat up. It's a safety feature, as laptops don't have as effective of a heat transfer system as desktops, and saves battery life. That's my best guess.
Either that, or it's just something computers do. They collect junk in memory while a program is running. Flash isn't the most efficient programming language either, so it contributes to the problem more than most programs.

To test the first possibility, do some gaming in another program to max out your system and heat everything up, and then start up N. If it starts out using 100%, that's almost definitely the reason. If not, it's probably just collecting junk, and I'm not sure what to do in that case.
