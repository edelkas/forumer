I mean, yes, there was calculus to each of God's decisions. He never chose an amount randomly. But just because something isn't random does not mean it's significant. If I go to the store and pick up 6 hotdogs, and I did so wittingly, one does not need to try to extract the meaning behind the decision.

If you try to take meaning from every minute detail of the Bible, you might miss out on the elements that are actually worth taking as metaphoric.



Numerology was a common hermetic element of texts, too. It may have become a common theme in the Bible because early Jews wanted the Bible to bear magical qualities like other texts of its time. The waters of the Flood cleared on the first day of the tenth month. I could go on until 30-something, (pun!) but I'll stop here. You get the idea.

Read through Numbers sometime to get absolutely inundated with significant values, most of which don't get used as often later in the Bible. Some, however, get picked up to stay!

We divide our weeks up into 7 days (no surprise how that came to be) and we have 12 months (again, no surprise). But that is only because the church influenced the Gregorian calendar.

Now take a look at Aztec calendars:
Quote: http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec_calendar
The tonalpohualli ("day count") consists of a cycle of 260 days, each day signified by a combination of a number from one to thirteen, and one of the twenty day signs. With each new day, both the number and day sign would be incremented: 1 Crocodile is followed by 2 Wind, 3 House, 4 Lizard, and so forth up to 13 Reed, after which the cycle of numbers would restart, giving 1 Jaguar. The cycle of twenty day signs, however, would continue until 7 Flower, after which it would restart and give 8 Crocodile. It would take a full 260 days (1320) for the two cycles of twenty day signs and thirteen numbers to realign and repeat the combination 1 Crocodile.

This their 260 day ritual cycle, or day count. Notice that they count 13 numbered days, something that would have never been condoned by the Church, seeing as how Friday the 13th wasn't exactly a good day. The Aztecs also had a calendar of years that went for 365 days, something they figured out on their own, which I find amazing.

Quote: http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiuhpohualli
The Aztec solar year was divided into 18 "months" of twenty days each, called in Spanish veintenas, with an addition of an extra five days at the end.

So you see, the numbers for the Aztec faith were given by the people, even though they had strong beliefs and fears of their gods. I'm sure if we were all Aztec peoples we would view 5 as a bad number because the last 5 days of the year were called "nothing days" and if a child was born on one of these days they would consider this child to amount to nothing in its life. Whether these numbers came from the divine words of their gods is debatable, but just to show you that numbers are arbitrary and only affect those who know of them and have been influenced by them.
