#245974 2 2006-10-26T05:03:14Z blue_tetris
You certainly should have kept his secret--never come out for someone else. That'd just be foolish (on your part) and certainly harmful to your friend.

You probably should not have urged him to come out, either. You couldn't possibly anticipate the results. Only he could know what could come of that. (By the way he was acting, though, he sounds a little histrionic and probably would have come out anyway after telling you.)



If he came out himself, he shouldn't feel bad at all. If people give him shit about it at school, he needs to tell a teacher or someone that this is happening; because that's illegal. There's no shame in being gay. He shouldn't be ashamed. You shouldn't be ashamed of him. If you really think that he's being made fun of, tell him to talk to an adult about it.

I wouldn't be quick to assume he's come out yet, either. People in high school are likely to use "fag" and other homophobic terms to make fun of people, without even considering their sexuality. Try not to bring the idea of his sexuality into the situation, because it might not be important. The only thing you need to worry about is: He's your friend, and people are making fun of him. That should be enough for you to know how to handle it. It doesn't matter that he's gay. Don't even bring it up.



Secondly, that girl should never have said he had a gun. She probably just saw him in a car and wanted to assume the dramatic. Just keep in mind that she probably knew he didn't have a gun and just wanted to escalate the situation. Some girls do that.
