This is not quite where I was trying to get to, so let me see if I can explain in more detail.
First of all, here's what I mean by a vector:
Quote: Wikipedia
In vector calculus, a spatial vector, or simply vector, is a concept characterized by a magnitude and a direction. A vector can be thought of as an arrow in Euclidean space, drawn from an initial point A pointing to a terminal point B.
Now, what i was thinking is that your proposed tile merging is somewhat similar to closed vector shape unions. Basically, the end result would be tme same as putting two tiles on top of each other. So after that thought, I figured that the tiles themselves could be represented as vector shapes.
Let me try to illustrate this:

Basically, now the tiles are broken up into their individual vectors, and when they are joined, they form the resulting shape. That's what I mean when I say tile merging is only composed of joining sets of vectors. The first tile is a set of vectors, the second tile is a set of vectors, and when they are joined together, they form a new set of vectors.
Then I take it one more step further, concluding that if this were to happen in this fashion, then why not just make N purely vector based? The terrain could have predefined sets of vectors that simulate the feeling of tiles, but with the new N, the terrain wouldn't be limited to tiles (kinda like Soldat's terrain).

Which is why it's more easier in just having pre-programmed 1/4 tiles (unless M&R will switch to vector based instead of tiles).

#356300 41 2007-08-17T06:15:31Z mattk210
well, N's physics can't handle colliding with 2 tiles at once, because of the central concept it uses: projection. when an intersection is detected between the ninja and a tile, the ninja is shifted out to a point where it no longer intersects. With tile merging this point may however intersect another tile, and projecting out of that would lead to an inaccurate calculation.

You don't need to understand that but what I'm saying is that 2 tiles can't 'magically' merge. A completely new tile needs to be created unless N's physics are completely modified. it's a lot less hassle to manually add new tiles than to derive new tiles from 2 overlapping original tiles. (Which I think is what ZeroTruths is trying to say)

I hope that made sense
