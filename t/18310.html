#341374 6 2007-07-10T15:49:25Z The_Juggler
I can tell you that you will not regret buying a Wii. I have played PS3 (in the store) and Xbox360 and I am very glad that I went for the Wii.

Everybody who said that the motion control was a cheap gimmick was quite wrong.


My advice to you about games is this: Don't buy any cross-platform games. Games that are ported to the Wii are just the same game with the motion control features just "tacked on" to make it kind of like Wii.

I have DragonBall Tenkaichi 2 - it is a really fun game, but it hardly utilizes the controllers; it would be just the same as if I had it on PS2.
I have also played Marvel Ultimate Alliance, another port, and it is the same. The Wii controls are just tacked on after the game is finished, and they are entirely optional anyway.

Twilight Princess is an excellent game - get it - but it does not really utilize the controllers much either. Another cross-platform game, the controls are mostly optional and don't really add anything to the game. It is just like playing it on GameCube.


I expect WarioWare Smooth Moves and Mario Party will be much different though. Being only for Wii and designed specifically for the controller, they should really take advantage of the motion control and make it a great game.

I'm just waiting for Smash Brothers Brawl - it's killing me.

A friend of mine has been playing with hacking the wii remote using bluetooth transmitters and some hacking programs.

If you have a bluetooth connection device for your computer, it is fairly easy to get your Wii remote to control your computer's mouse, and the motion controls to perform button presses for the computer.

For the sensor bar on the Wii you can just use 2 candles, or anything else that emits infrared for that matter. You can even use candles to control your Wii, the sensor bar is only 2 infrared emitters.

Quote: Donfuy, July 12, 2007 05:24 pm
for those who have Zelda: The Twilight Princess.
The idea that I have from that game is that we control the sword or something in one hand, and in the other hand we have the "Nukchuk" (it reminds some kind of god...), and with it we control Link's body... is that true?

In Zelda Twilight Princess

The game uses the same game engine as Ocarina of Time and Majora's Mask. It is greatly improved in many ways, but it plays in the same way. I was a bit disappointed, but it is a good game engine. It has been used for other games as well.

The nunchuck - which is a very degrading word for martial artists - has a joystick and is used to move the character around, exactly the same as any other zelda game.
* You shake the nunchuck forward to do a shield bash
* You wave the nunchuck from side to side to do a sword spin attack
* You shake or jiggle the remote to swing your sword
* You jab forward with the remote to stab with your sword
* You shake the remote while carrying something to throw it (this is optional)
* You use the pointer to aim when using the bow and arrow

And that is everything I can think of, all other actions are performed with a button press. Aiming the bow and arrow is the coolest because it feels the most like actually doing the things the character does. You aim the remote at the screen, press the trigger button, draw the remote back, and let go of the trigger to launch an arrow!

But believe me, other games for Wii utilize the remote much more effectively. And I imagine newer games will be even better.
