#412344 5 2008-02-26T20:15:57Z Skyline
Just as a historical background, the words 'under God' were added to the Pledge of Allegiance back in the mid-1950's after the idea of this annex was developed by the Knights of Columbus (a Roman Catholic organization), who first recieved the inspiration to do so through Lincoln's Gettysburg Address, in which he said 'under God'. The bill to change the Pledge was introduced to congress several times, and it finally passed after the current president, Eisenhower was convinced to accept it.

As for my own personal opinion, I don't think it is worth changing. Even though I'm an atheist myself, considering how this phrase has been so thoroughly integrated into society, especially public schooling (children are taught this version of the Pledge in early years), that it would take an impractical amount of effort. Considering the vast majority of the population in the U.S. does follow some sort of theistic religion, in the end, 'under God' really does cater to the majority of the citizens. Of course, there's always the option of simply not reciting the Pledge every morning (which is what I tend to do), or if you don't want to go that extreme, omitting the words 'under God' when you say the Pledge.
