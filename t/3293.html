#69003 2 2005-05-23T21:11:54Z shoftee
Well... technically, it should be possible to use the SAT. You just have to calculate the normal vectors of the square and triangle. How? Well if you have a triangle for example, the normal of each of the triangle's edges would be either the right-hand or left-hand normal of that edge, depending on the orientation of the triangle. If the orientation is CW, then you should use left-hand normals, and if it's CCW - right-hand normals. If you have problems determining the orientation, just ask.

Hope I've helped.

Hi,

After I used a pencil and a piece of paper for 10 minutes, I discovered that you actually don't need the orientation . That means that you just pick one of the two(left or right hand) normals and calculate the projection length of the edges onto it. There might be a way to skip some of the edges (maybe using voronoi regions), but this will have to do for now...

And don't worry, it's not so complicated.
