I just had an idea for a (in my opinion) pretty awesome RPG game.

Here's the premise: It's an online game. 1-3 people join as 'heroes' who choose a class and a name etc. they would have either have a first or third person view (presuming the game is 3D) with keys mapped WASD style with attacks mapped to the mouse with left click for weapon attack and right click for specials (magic, setting traps etc.) with scroll being able to cycle spells.the I key would access a diablo style inventory screen where you can arrange items, equip different ones etc. and other rpg key binds (C for character info, M for skills etc.). the HUD would have health and mana bars along with the currently selected spell icon next to them in an oblivion-esque style. Or it would be a looking down style view like diablo, titan quest etc. with left click as a generic command button (move to that spot, attack monster, open chest, etc.) and right click as a button for using skills, the HUD and key maps would be the same apart from no WASD.

1 player would join as a 'dungeon master' and would have RTS style gameplay and view, being able to set traps in the dungeon, create units and 'spawn them' in to play (out of protaganist site unless the DM builds a 'teleport node' which lets you create units in player site until they destroy it and not where prot have already explored unless they have missed a 'teleport node') they can also edit any floor of the dungeon below the protaganists and empty of players. Treasure chests would be randomly spawned in corridors and side routes built on to the DMs map after creating so as to balance gameplay (in otherwords DMs can't heavily secure chests and stuff it's all done by the computer). The DM gains resources to build units and stuff by killing randomly spawning AI 'heroes' thay try to get through the dungeon (so there is no way to just mindlessly mine resources the DM has to actually work to get them)

1-3 players would also be able to choose to be an antagonist (mainly just negative class choices as the protaganists like dark knight instead of night, neromancer instead of cleric etc.) and essentialy just work the same as the protaganists but these gain experience from killing AI heroes rather than monsters and aim to stop the protaganists. also there would have to be some form of lives or something to stop antagonists and protagonists death matching (perhaps antagonists being restricted to certain depths of the dungeon and starting out more powerful so that they are more like boss characters)

any players after the initial 7 would be able to choose to control any of the monsters that the DM creates and can either fight like smarter monster spam or spend time gaining experience off of AI heroes and gradually becoming 'unique' enemys at level 5 or so where you can name your creature and choose a class (like blood thirster or something) main difference between antagonists is that they start at minimum level and all skills are pre-defined.

#359823 2 2007-08-27T02:01:44Z Grassman7z7
Sounds good, and a lot like games already available.
The problem is that a game that reliant on both Multiplayer and Player Balancing wouldn't go over too well with a Developer.

The person building the Dungeon, the DM, would have to be limited in some ways, and rewarded in others.
What if he just made crummy dungeons, where all the "phat lewt" was easy to grab? Then you could just pimp out your character and massacre anything in your path. Obviously, you would want to be able to go to other Dungeons with your Hero, and adventure there. What fun is it for all three parties (Protagonists, Antagonists, and DM) if some dude can hop on the server and totally own everything?

Setting a level requirement/limit could help, but you could still easily "twink" your hero by having a friend build a Dungeon filled with chests that you could easily get to.

The Protagonist would be the easiest to work with, I suppose. Just like every old RPG ever made. I think the interface that best fits would be either "Oblivion" by Bethesda or "Might and Magic: Dark Messiah" by whoever did that. With level requirements to join a dungeon, things could still be fair, and work out rather well.

Antagonist would be another problem.
Obviously, you wouldn't want to hang out in a dungeon and wait for people to join so you can be killed by them kill them. You could end up waiting for a long time, and get little reward. AI Heroes would work, but then you have to figure out how you control their power and the like. If it were up to the DM, once again he could power level someone if he was so inclined, and you could dungeon hop (Once again, all that work to (If done so legitimately) level up, you would HAVE to allow players to play on other dungeons).
Although being an antagonist would be fun, developing a fair, balanced game would be extremely tough, and likely easy to exploit.
And what about incentive for people playing as creeps to guard chests, other than the fact that it makes heroes more powerful. Wouldn't they want some sort of reward?

So, the best way I can think of to prevent exploitation would be "League" style maps. The only way to be able to Map on these servers would be to have enough kudos and then apply to one of the developers. Then he would judge you, and you would receive "professional" status, and be able to DM these "League" dungeons. Then, you could join these maps (Anyone could) and play, but you cant carry over any characters that aren't from league games. Just to be fair.

If the game were to be limited to LAN games, or just on an overall smaller scale, then exploitation would not be an issue. However, finding people would be a pain. Thats where servers come in handy, or something. Theres really a lot that would need to be done for anything like this to become a reality, and unfortunately, I doubt that there are any game developers, publishers, or anyone, who would be willing to work on a project this demanding.

Sorry to be such a negative guy towards this (I've had tons of ideas about games much like this, so dont think I'm being biased or anything), but eventually you have to realize how much work would have to go into something this big to keep things even remotely fair/plausible of an idea. Remember, all these issues come up for wide-scale multiplayer, and this wide scale multiplayer (Although maybe not something you or many others really want) is what is necessary for people to want to make and sell this game.

But its a good idea, regardless.
Maybe more of an open-source environment would be willing to help...
I do hope I didn't leave anything out in that schpeal...
