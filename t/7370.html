#141205 14 2006-02-09T06:20:23Z The_Juggler
don't count out GIFs either. They may be mostly for animation but thier filesize is around JPG, but with near PNG quality.
Both are a good choice though, it is all in the algorithym

BMPs are large because they store the color of each individual pixel separately (get it - bitmap - a map of bits) This gives them quality but thier filesize increases as the image size increases.
JPGs store the data differently. If much of the picture has the same color, it will store each pixel that has that same color as one entry. This dramatically decreases filesize (and similarly decreases quality) even in very large pictures because the amount of pixels is not as important.
I don't know the specifics on GIFs, PNGs and other formats but I know that they are similar to JPG in the way they store graphics.

Peter Bauer, Almighty Emperor of Everything Having To Do With Graphics (Help Desk Director at the National Association of Photoshop Professionals, Adobe-Certified Expert, and member of the Photoshop World Instructor Dream Team), says the following:

Quote
JPG: JPEG, as it's called, is actually a file compression scheme rather than a file format, but that's not important. What is important is that JPEG throws away some of your image data when it saves the file... use JPEG only for copies. When should you use JPEG? When sending images to a photo lab that doen't accept TIFF files and when sending images (perhaps by e-mail or on CD)... When saving JPEGs, the lower the quality setting you choose... the smaller the file but also the more damage to the image.

GIF: GIF is more appropriate for items like Web buttons and banners... that it is for photos. If you save a photo that's more than perhaps 100 pixels x 100 pixels in size, you might see some degradation of the image quality as similar colors become on color. When you save an image as a GIF, it can contain no more than 256 distinct colors. JPEG and the other common file formats can have thousands of different colors.

PNG: PNG comes in two types: PNG-8 (which is a substitute for GIF) and PNG-24 (which is a substitute for JPEG). PNG has a couple of advantages for Web designers, such as support for transparency, but not all Web browsers can display PNG graphics. Generally speaking, it's safer to use JPEG and GIF.
