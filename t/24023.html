I'm bored enough to care about this.

"I would never abuse any of my powers, as, my mind would stop me."
This is not a valid grammatical construct.

When "as" is used to mean "because" or "since," there is no comma after it. Would you put a comma after "because" in that sentence? After "since?"
I've heard you say this phrase aloud, and it sounds just as awkward spoken. I know you make a pause after "as," but pauses like that are neither accurate indications that a comma should be used nor sensible in the first place. It sort of seems to me that you use "as" so frequently because you think it sounds more sophisticated or intelligent, but when you overuse it, and overuse it incorrectly, it has exactly the opposite effect.
This use of "as" is not used anywhere in any literature I've seen. You made it up, and continue using it even though it has never been validated by any other literate English-speaker.

In fact, in most situations wherein you use "as," even if you would have excluded the comma that follows, using "as" is more awkward than "since" or "because." Even a frickin' semicolon would work much better.
Assuming that the above sentence even makes sense, the following are far more acceptable:
"I would never abuse any of my powers because my mind would stop me."
"I would never abuse any of my powers since my mind would stop me."
"I would never abuse any of my powers; my mind would stop me."
But the one you actually went with doesn't make a lick of grammatical sense.

Even your um-uh-like-um's are more tolerable than this.

Anyway, I wanted to address this habit of yours because I think you may have gone a little too long thinking it was valid English.
