Eh... nerds are drunk off happy solidarity, but often with groups of people very much like them. Everyone is an individual. Cliches are useless, but stereotypes are even more so. Today, being a geek is a good thing... yet I find myself being called a nerd by some of my popular "sports cliche' friends, usually whenever they need something done on a computer, slide projector, etc. My dad's an Apple Computer Specialist, so I kinda grew up "nerdy" but not just, like smart-ugly-awkward kid, but more like uniquely imaginative artistic reader nerd. I was the elementary school wild card.

As far as I've experienced, elementary school is the one place where we have not yet developed our full set of prejudices, and thus I developed very few real social skills before junior high and yet considered myself good friends with all those at my elementary school. I essentially TRIED to be as weird as possible through all of 7th grade and half of 8th. No one convinced me to stop, or to try and conform, but I became a different and less nerdy and more sophisticated person with time.

So, basically, I don't think you should try to be popular, or unpopular (as I did without quite knowing it). Your reputation as a student, person, and friend IS important, but your inner self and how you show it to the world matters in too many ways to relate. So if you ARE a nerd, be a nerd. Geeks, be geeks. Sports kids, embrace your jockiness. But don't go over the edge, and don't swim under the line. You'd only be hurting yourself.

That's what I think of it anyway.
