If it weren't done by committee, it would be intelligent design.

*Ahem*

ANYWAY, as we all know, the human species is riddled with design faults. As the Committee for Human Redesign, it is our job to create a better one. It must be a carbon-based lifeform with DNA, be able to reproduce, and be physically feasible (ie not be too heavy to support itself, be able to acquire and process sufficient food, etc.). It should also be sapient, sentient, able to communicate, and capable of building temples and worshipping the Committee.

Now, there are some bits of humans that work reasonably well, so I think we should start from the current model, identify the problems, and attempt to fix them rather than trying a redesign from scratch. Suggestions for enhancements and optimisations on those components which, while passable, are not suitable for a product of this calibre will also be welcomed. Bear in mind that the finished product is not required to resemble the previous model - if there is good cause and it is physically plausible, a full restructure is to be welcomed.

This thread will probably be moved to either intelligent discussion or messageboard games, depending on who gets the first few replies in and thus sets the tone. Hopefully it will be the former; I want to see some biologically viable alternatives.

And just to get you started, here's a little list:

Problems:
*Air passage crosses digestive tract, leading to unneccesary suffocation. Recommend seperation of tubes. Also to be considered is a membrane which can be lowered to cover the breathing hole and act as a filter, allowing homo superior to survive in otherwise fatal atmospheres and preventing inhalation of dust, etc., but this may be impractical.

*Limbs and organs do not regenerate if removed. Reccomend altering, as in case of lobster claws, so that any missing component can be rapidly replaced. In current state, important organisms can be permenantly crippled with relative ease, which is a unpleasant to the victim and a burden to others of the species.

*Important organs (digestive system and reproductive system) are in an exposed position on front of organism. This makes them vulnerable to predators, given human's tendency to stand upright. Possible solutions include relocation, inclusion of auxillaries, implementation of rapid regeneration, better blood control, and placement of bones, scales, thick fur, or chitinous plates to protect these delicate areas. More bones or armour will probably limit mobility, so a redesign of the torso to allow protection of all organs is probably the best bet. Scales and fur are also feasible, though they would be less effective.

*Teeth rarely fit correctly. There are too many of them, and the shape of the mouth and face is poor. Possible solutions include redesigning the skull, and replacing some or all of the teeth with other mechanisms, such as mandibles.

*The sinus are always blocked or infected or something. The head needs better plumbing. I suggest a complete redesign, which will also help accomodate the seperation of the breathing and digestive tracts.

Improvements:
*Unnecessary body parts (appendix, tonsils). Remove them.

*Introduce dual-lensed eye, for zoom function? It could possibly be achieved by placing a larger sphere with a lens around the current eye. By distorting to become elliptical, the distance between the lenses - and thus the level of magnification - could be altered.

*Sensitivity to infrared radiation. There should be an eye-like organ, or possibly an addition to the normal eyes, which could detect infrared radiation of the wavelengths emitted by a objects which are approximately 300K in temperature. This would allow homo superior to see without external illumination, especially other creatures (which would be ~310K). Noise from the organism's own body heat could be filtered by having an insulated group of receptors to provide a background reading. Similar (albeit mechanical) systems are already used.

#156786 25 2006-03-22T22:45:57Z Tsukatu
I have a few things regarding sensation (vision, especially):

If we're going the species engineering approach and we're theoretically planning on creating this species (as opposed to wondering how a human could've evolved easier), I'd really appreciate a customizable lens for the eye that could be adjusted with a special tool made for the job. The lens could balance out an irregularly-shaped eye, eliminating the need for eyeglasses, contact lenses, and laser eye surgery. I guess there's a case against that in saying that glasses have a certain sophisticated look or something, but in this case we'd make one kind of lens (nice and flat) and treat glasses like a hat or something.

I also think that the light spectrum we see now limits our perception drastically. Other animals can see into infrared and ultraviolet spectra, so I don't see why we shouldn't be able to. Modifying the eyes themselves to this degree would be a little complicated, so I think the best solution would be to make another few pairs of eyes, either next to the eyes we already have (and we can do with reducing our eye size - they're much too big as it is; we'd have to flatten them up a bit to retain our current vision quality, but it's not like anyone but a surgeon is going to notice that the back of someone's eyes aren't as far back into their skull or anything) or on a different part of our face altogether (I prefer the former).
The case I can see against this would be that a person would be confused by this flood of new senses - to which I say, no, you would be confused at these new senses because you haven't grown up with them. How do you think we handle the five senses we do right now without flinching? (My Psych book actually makes a good argument for a sixth sense that has to do with your spatial orientation, as a combination of the cochlear fluid in your ears and information sent back by your limbs telling you where they are.)
I guess we'd also look kinda freaky having three eyes in each eye socket, but whatever (one for the visible light spectrum, one for ultraviolet and a bit beyond that, and another for infrared).
Keep in mind the benefits of having these extra eyes, though. Ultraviolet light has a much smaller wavelength than visible light, which means that fewer obstacles can get in the way. We'd be better able to see through substances considered impermeable by visible light. As for infrared, that's far more useful than UV light. We'd be able to see people through thin walls, tell how hot a pipe or bowl of soup is just by looking at it, etc. In terms of playing paintball in the woods, using infrared vision would be like using a wallhack.

An eye in the back of your head? I dunno, but we should consider it. How 'bout one on top? It might be making too many sensitive areas on the head, but then again, what kind of environment do we plan this new human to live in?

Either move the light receptors cells in the existing eye closer to the front of the eye, or give me a tapetum (the thing in the back of the eyes of some animals that bounces light back into the receptor cells, giving them a second chance to pick up the light (as it is, 90% of the visible light entering your eye is blocked or otherwise doesn't hit a receptor cell), also giving those animals a creepy glow when you shine a light at them in the dark (cat)). We'd still need a pupil and an iris to limit the amount of light allowed into our eyes, but we should be able to control that consciously.

The way we gauge distance of a sound is by seeing the difference in time that the sound reaches the ears. If it reaches your right ear before your left ear, you know the sound is coming from your left, and the time difference tells you how far away it is in that direction. This means, however, that we can't tell how far away a sound is that is anywhere on the plane running straight through our faces (in front, below, above, or behind our heads). To fix this, most people turn their heads. This might be a trivial point, as some people might not see turning the head as an inconvenience, but putting a muscle to control the position of the ear would spare you the trouble as well as allow you to more rapidly adjust your ear to find the distance of the sound.
That or put a third ear under the eye on the back of your head.
