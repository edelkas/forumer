To be truly benevolent, you should act to help people in the most efficient manner possible as well as, and this is important, anonymously.
Here's the best way you could have handled the situation:
You see granny struggling with her groceries. The benevolent thing to do here is to help her get those groceries into the car to save her the work, and to do it quickly. So what you should have done was get out of the car and sprint up behind her. Make sure you try to run as quietly as possible, though without compromising your speed, so that she doesn't hear you and doesn't turn around to acknowledge you. This makes you anonymous. Now, were you in granny's shoes, you'd be mighty suspicious of some punk who sprinted up to you to help you with your groceries because most people aren't so benevolent. You ought to spare her the worry and the possible emotional stress to her poor old heart, and you also want to make sure she has no idea who you are (anonymity), so the benevolent thing to do in this case is to, just as you come sprinting up behind her, give her a powerful elbow smash to her occipital to knock her out cold. She'd probably be uncomfortable laying on the pavement until she wakes up, so you should get her into the driver's seat of her car. But wait, there's that kid in the back seat, and he might get a look at you so he can track you down and credit you for your benevolent actions! Can't have that! Before you move past the backseat window, grab a hard object and smash the glass, not hard enough to break it but definitely hard enough to make a sizeable crack through which it's impossible to distinguish your features. Put granny in the driver's seat, give her enough money to cover repairs on the car, close her door, load up her groceries, and head back to your car.

There! You've successfully helped an old lady load her groceries into her car, and done so anonymously! You've also given granny a well-deserved period of rest and zero net emotional stress and monetary worth.
